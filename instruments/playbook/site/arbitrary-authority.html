Arbitrary Authority

Station 32 of 50 on the Control over your life line.

Code C 32Stage: ControlStandard tactic T277

What is Arbitrary Authority?

Arbitrary Authority is when decisions that affect you follow no stated rule, so nothing you do reliably changes the outcome. The same effort is praised one day and rejected the next.

It can sound like

  • It’s my call, and today the answer is no.
  • Last week that was fine. This week it isn’t. Don’t ask me why.
  • You’ll know it’s right when I say it’s right.

Also called Effort-Outcome Mismatch, Uncontrollable Outcomes

Signs it might be this

  • You have tried several ways of meeting the expectation and none reliably works.
  • Asking ‘what would make this a yes?’ gets no answer, or a different answer each time.
  • Others in the same position see the same unpredictability and have stopped asking.
  • Outcomes seem to depend on whether you are in favor that week.
  • When you point to an earlier decision, you are told that does not matter now.

Signs it is probably something else

  • The criteria are written down somewhere, even if they are hard to meet.
  • The decider explains the difference between two outcomes when asked.
  • The changes follow new information or a stated change in policy.
  • The person admits they were inconsistent and works to be steadier.
  • Your sense of randomness comes from one surprising result rather than a run of them.
  • The decider is willing to set out criteria when asked, and sticks to them.
  • Unclear decisions can be reviewed by someone else.
  • Inconsistency is admitted and corrected.

Questions to ask yourself

  • Could you write down the rule this decision followed? Could anyone?
  • When you ask for reasons, do you get reasons, or reminders of who is in charge?
  • Have you seen the same input lead to opposite outcomes more than once?
  • Do other people in the same position describe the same pattern?
  • If you did everything perfectly, would you know it in advance?

What it is

Arbitrary Authority is when decisions that affect you follow no stated rule, so nothing you do reliably changes the outcome. The same effort is praised one day and rejected the next.

Permission is given and then withheld for the same request. Some call this an effort-outcome mismatch, or uncontrollable outcomes.

Every person in charge makes some judgment calls, and not every call can be explained in advance. The pattern shows when no rule can be found at all, and asking for one is treated as a challenge rather than a fair question.

Why it matters

When effort and outcome stop being connected, people stop trying, or keep trying in an exhausting way. It becomes hard to plan, learn or improve.

Over time the person in charge holds more power, because only their mood or choice decides.

How it shows up

  • The same piece of work, request or behavior getting opposite responses at different times, with no reason given.
  • Rules that exist only in the decision-maker’s head and shift without notice.
  • Requests for criteria met with ‘It’s my call’ or ‘You’ll know it when you get it right’.
  • Rewards and penalties that seem to track the decider’s mood, favor or convenience rather than anything you did.

What you can do

Pick what fits. You do not owe anyone a confrontation.

Words you could use

“I want to meet the standard, so I need to know what it is. Could you put the criteria in writing, or tell me who else can review this?”

Ask about it gently

Ask a plain question: ‘Can you tell me what would make this a yes next time, so I can aim for it?’

Set a clear limit

Ask that criteria be written down before you put in more effort: ‘I’d like the standard in writing so we’re both working to the same thing.’

Step back

Where you can, limit how much you depend on this person’s decisions, for example by choosing another class, team or route.

Get support

Compare notes with others in the same position. Talk to a mentor or trusted person outside the situation for a reality check on your work.

At work, school or with services

Keep a record of requests, responses and dates. Ask for the decision to be reviewed through a formal route, and seek advice from an employee representative, student adviser or independent office.

Using this label fairly

  • Calling every judgment call arbitrary can deny the real role of expertise and discretion. The label fits when no rule can be found or explained, not when you disagree with the reasons.
  • Demanding a written rule for every small matter can wear down people trying to work fairly under pressure.

How it differs from look-alikes

Negative Reinforcement Traps link relief to compliance, so they are predictable in their own way. Arbitrary Authority breaks the link between what you do and what happens.

Phobia Indoctrination / Catastrophizing exaggerates one consequence, and Strategic Chaos / Crisis Manufacturing floods you with emergencies. Guilt-Tripping and Moral Framing work through your conscience.

Financial Control and Location Tracking restrict resources and movement. Moral Licensing / Strategic Philanthropy uses a good name as cover.
