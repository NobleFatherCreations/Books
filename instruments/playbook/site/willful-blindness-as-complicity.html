Willful Blindness as Complicity

Station 24 of 31 on the Work, school and systems line. Code S 24

What is Willful Blindness as Complicity?

Someone who could see the harm doesn’t look and doesn’t ask, then says they didn’t know. The harm goes on, and nobody answers for it.

It can sound like

  • I don’t want to know the details, just make sure it doesn’t become a problem.
  • Nobody has ever formally told me, so there’s nothing for me to do.
  • I’m sure there’s an explanation. Let’s not go digging.

Signs it might be this

  • The information was easy to get, in a file, an email or a conversation away.
  • The person has a role, such as supervisor, parent, board member or landlord, that would normally mean checking.
  • Specific questions or documents are avoided, delayed or left unopened.
  • Reports go unanswered, or are acknowledged and not acted on, more than once.
  • The same person claims ignorance each time an issue arises, about issues that others around them already knew.

Signs it is probably something else

  • The person had no access to the relevant information, and it was not part of their duties.
  • They investigated promptly, even if the outcome was not what you wanted.
  • Their delay has a clear and stated reason, such as an ongoing formal process, and they keep you updated.
  • They asked for more detail and acted once they received it.
  • They were genuinely not told, and responded quickly once they were.
  • They looked into it, told you what they found and acted on it, even partly.
  • They had no access to the information or no authority over the situation.
  • Once informed clearly, they responded within a reasonable time.

Questions to ask yourself

  • Could this person have found out easily, and was it part of their role to look?
  • Did I or anyone else give them clear information, and what happened to it?
  • Do they avoid certain topics, documents or meetings, in a way they do not for others?
  • Has this happened more than once, with different issues?
  • Did they act at all, even slowly, or has everything stayed the same?

What it is

Willful blindness is when a person or organization has strong signs of harm in front of them. They do not ask the next question, open the file or speak to the person involved.

Because they never confirm, they can say they never knew. It shows in the gap between how easy it would have been to find out and how little was done.

This is usually someone whose role would normally call for a closer look. Not knowing is common.

This is a particular, repeated kind of not looking. It cannot show what anyone was thinking.

Why it matters

When the person best placed to look declines to, harm carries on with nobody formally responsible for it. The person being harmed has no route to anyone who will act.

Being told ‘we had no idea’ about something you reported can add its own injury.

How it shows up

  • Clear warning signs that no one follows up on, even though following up is their job. Examples are repeated complaints, injuries, missing money or a pattern in the records.
  • Steering clear of the one question, document or conversation that would make inaction harder to defend.
  • A later ‘I didn’t know’ in a case where the information was within easy reach.
  • It is seen in families, workplaces, schools, congregations, clubs, landlords and boards, wherever someone is expected to supervise or protect.
  • Different from active cover-up: nothing is destroyed or denied, and the not looking is the whole behavior.

What you can do

Pick what fits. You do not owe anyone a confrontation.

Words you could use

“I have told you about this in writing, on the dates in my message. I am asking for a written reply that says who is looking into it and what the next step is.”

Ask about it gently

Put the facts in a short, dated message and ask a direct question: ‘On the 3rd I told you about this. What has been done, and who is looking into it?’

Set a clear limit

Ask for a written response from a named person. ‘Please confirm in writing who is dealing with this and when I will hear back.’

Step back

If one person keeps stepping back, stop repeating yourself to them. Go to the next person with authority, an ombudsperson’s office (an independent complaints office) or an outside body.

Get support

Talk to someone who is not part of the system, such as an advocate, a union representative or a lawyer. Bring your dated notes so they can see the sequence.

At work, school or with services

Use the formal route in your organization: a written complaint, a safeguarding lead, a regulator or an ombudsperson’s service. Keep copies of what you sent and what you received.

Using this label fairly

  • Accusing someone of willful blindness can unfairly blame bystanders who had no knowledge. That happens when they failed to act on something they never had a chance to learn.
  • Using the label to insist that a person must have known, based on hindsight, is a misuse of it. It treats what is clear now as if it had been clear then.

How it differs from look-alikes

Resource Sabotage removes what you need, such as funding or support, and then blames you for the result. Willful blindness removes nothing and simply does not look.

Emotion Control tells you which feelings are acceptable to show. This pattern is about someone declining to receive your report, whatever you feel.
