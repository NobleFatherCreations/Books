Stochastic Harassment Direction

Stirring up a crowd against someone

Station 12 of 19 on the Turning people against you line. Code P 12

Safety first

Harassment waves can move from messages to threats, doxxing or visits to a home or workplace. Take these signs seriously: your address being shared, threats naming you or your family, or someone appearing in person.

Keep dated copies and report each wave to the platform. Take the file to the police or a victim-support service, who can advise on protective orders.

What is Stochastic Harassment Direction?

Someone keeps painting one person as a villain to a big audience. They never tell anyone to act, yet strangers start harassing that person.

It can sound like

  • Posts that name the person, say what a disgrace they are, and tag their employer or school.
  • Followers’ messages that quote the speaker’s exact phrase: ‘You are exactly what he said you are.’
  • The speaker’s reply to complaints: ‘I never told anyone to contact her. I only said what she did.’

Signs it might be this

  • The same person returns to the same target again and again, each time with sharper language.
  • Waves of harassment follow the speaker’s posts closely and quote their phrases.
  • The speaker names or tags the person’s employer, school or home area when it adds nothing to the point being made.
  • When harassment starts, the speaker disclaims it but keeps going.
  • Personal details that the speaker never posted begin to circulate among the audience.

Signs it is probably something else

  • The speaker explicitly condemns the harassment and removes or edits the post.
  • The criticism is about a public act of a public figure, and keeps to that act.
  • The backlash began before the speaker said anything, from other sources.
  • The speaker has not referred to the person again since the first post.
  • The person named is a public official or business criticized for what they did in that role.
  • The speaker moves on from the topic, or states plainly that nobody should contact the person.
  • The criticism concerns something the person did in public, and names no private details.
  • The reaction is limited to ordinary replies and does not reach the person’s work or family.

Questions to ask yourself

  • Did the harassment arrive after a specific post, and does it quote that post’s words?
  • Has the speaker returned to this person more than once, and with escalating language?
  • Did the speaker say anything to calm their audience when the harassment began?
  • Is the person being named in a way that connects to a public role, or to their private life?
  • Would the criticism have been just as clear without pointing the audience at their workplace or home?

What it is

Stochastic means ‘driven by chance’. Nobody can say which listener will act, only that with a large enough audience, some probably will.

Here, someone repeatedly names a target and describes them in a way that stirs up anger, often mentioning their workplace or town. They never give an instruction.

Then a wave of messages, calls or visits arrives. One harsh post means little.

The pattern is repeated portrayal, followed by repeated waves that cite the same words.

Why it matters

The person singled out takes harassment from many people at once. The portrayal that set it off stays easy to deny.

So there is no single act to report and no one to hold to account. The whole world can seem to have turned on you.

How it shows up

  • A repeated, inflammatory description of a named individual, often with their job, school or neighborhood attached.
  • An audience large and angry enough that a few of its members acting is close to certain.
  • There is no direct instruction, only a framing that makes harassment seem justified, so the speaker can say they never asked for it.
  • Waves of messages, calls or complaints that arrive soon after each post and quote its wording.
  • It is about foreseeable effect over time, not about one person’s anger or one heated post.

What you can do

Read the safety note at the top first. If speaking up could make things worse, skip the wording below and talk to someone you trust or a local support service.

Words you could use

“Will you read through these messages with me and help me work out who should see them? I’m keeping a dated record and not replying to any of them.” Say this to a trusted helper, never to the sender.

Ask about it gently

If you think you may be part of the audience, ask yourself in private what the speaker wants you to do. Then ask whether you would say it to the person’s face.

Set a clear limit

Do not reply to the harassers or to the speaker in public. Block, mute and report from your accounts, and keep screenshots with dates and links.

Step back

Lock down your accounts and remove personal information from public view. Ask trusted people to help you watch the messages, so you are not alone with them.

Get support

Tell a trusted friend or family member what is happening. Ask them to help you keep a record of each message, with a note of when it arrived.

At work, school or with services

If it reaches you at work, tell your manager or HR early and give them your record. Ask the organization to handle calls and emails about you.

Using this label fairly

  • Treating any pile-on after a post as proof of planned incitement can shield real wrongdoing from public criticism.
  • Someone who is being criticized may use the label to cast all criticism of themselves as harassment.

How it differs from look-alikes

Punishment by proxy hurts someone you love to send you a message. Here the pain lands on you directly, from many strangers.

Information cascades spread a belief because agreeing is safer, and no one aims them at a person. Reputation laundering hides the source of a claim.

Here the speaker is out in the open, and the deniability comes from never giving an instruction.
