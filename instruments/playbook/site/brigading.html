Brigading

A crowd sent from elsewhere to swarm you

Station 25 of 25 on the Online and technology line. Code O 25

Safety first

Look for threats, a home address, a workplace name or a family member’s name in the wave. If you see them, treat it as a different level of problem from rude replies.

Save evidence before blocking. Report it to the platform and, where threats look credible, to the police.

Ask a friend you trust to read the messages for you and flag only threats or personal details.

What is Brigading?

Dozens or hundreds of people flood one person with replies or reports within hours. Usually a post somewhere else sent them, and nobody could answer them all.

It can sound like

  • Within minutes of a screenshot being shared in another group, your mentions fill with replies that use the same phrases.
  • Go and look at what this person said, posted in a group chat, followed by a stream of new commenters.
  • A run of one-star ratings on your shop page within an hour, none from a buyer.

Signs it might be this

  • You can find the post or thread that points people toward you, or someone sends you the link.
  • Most replies land within a few hours and then drop off.
  • Many replies repeat the same wording, screenshot or claim.
  • The responders have no history with you, and several accounts are new.
  • Replies are about a clipped or altered version of what you said.

Signs it is probably something else

  • Replies arrive over days, from people with differing views, and some engage with what you actually wrote.
  • A large account shared your post but asked nobody to respond.
  • The replies include people you know and the wording varies.
  • The criticism points at something you did say.
  • The volume is close to what similar posts from accounts your size get.
  • The spike fades within a day and the replies are varied.
  • The source is an open public conversation, not a call to action.
  • Some people in the thread correct the record on your behalf.

Questions to ask yourself

  • Can you find a post, thread or group where people were told to come to you?
  • Did the replies arrive in a narrow window and fade quickly?
  • Are people answering what you wrote, or a clipped version you never posted?
  • Do many share the same phrases or the same screenshot?
  • Do the responders have any history with you or with one another?
  • Is the volume unusual for an account your size?

What it is

Brigading is a surge of replies, reports, downvotes, ratings or messages sent to one person or page by people who were pointed there. The pointer is often a post, group chat or forum thread elsewhere.

It also happens on review pages, polls and comment sections. What separates it from a pile-on is the shared source and the short window.

Each participant may do something small. The weight comes from the sum, which no one can answer one by one.

Why it matters

Volume arriving at once removes any chance of answering the way you could answer a single message. The platform itself becomes unusable for as long as it lasts.

Notifications pile up faster than anyone can read, and a calm reply is buried.

How it shows up

  • A sudden spike of hostile or mocking replies, ratings or messages in a short window.
  • A visible source, such as a post, screenshot, group chat or forum thread, that points people toward one person or account.
  • Mass reports, downvotes or one-star ratings, sometimes on a shop, book or app page.
  • Participants who may not know the person and have seen only one screenshot.
  • Sometimes an old post pulled back up and sent round again, so a quiet account is suddenly busy.

What you can do

Read the safety note at the top first. If speaking up could make things worse, skip the wording below and talk to someone you trust or a local support service.

Words you could use

“Here is the original post in full, with what came before it. I’m not answering individual replies.”

Ask about it gently

If you were misquoted, post one calm correction with the full original attached and answer no individual replies. People who arrive later can then see the record.

Set a clear limit

Use tools that need no announcement: restrict replies, filter keywords, mute the thread and block accounts as they appear.

Step back

Step away from notifications for a day. Ask a trusted friend to read them and tell you only about threats or personal details.

Get support

Save dated screenshots of the wave, the rallying post and any threats. Send them to the platform’s report tools and, if threats or personal details appear, to the police and an online-harassment support service.

At work, school or with services

If it may reach your employer, give your manager or HR a short heads-up with screenshots before they find it themselves. Ask who should handle any press or messages.

Using this label fairly

  • Calling any heavy criticism ‘brigading’ can dismiss people who disagree with you in good faith. The label needs evidence of a shared call to converge.
  • If you accuse a named person of organizing a pile-on without a link or screenshot trail, that can start a pile-on of its own.

How it differs from look-alikes

Sock Puppets are one person posing as many voices, while a brigade is many real people. Doxxing publishes private details such as an address.

A wave can include that, but a brigade needs no private information, only a link to follow. What sets Brigading apart is the shared rallying point: everyone arrives from the same place, at about the same time.
