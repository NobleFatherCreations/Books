Apology Without Repair

Station 18 of 31 on the Dodging blame line. Code B 18

Safety first

An apology followed by repetition can be part of a cycle in controlling or violent relationships. In that cycle, remorse after an incident is followed by a calm period and then another incident.

The harm may involve threats, hitting, destroyed property, control of money, or fear of how they will react. If so, contact a domestic violence advocate, who can help you plan for safety without requiring you to leave.

In immediate danger, call emergency services.

What is Apology Without Repair?

They say sorry, sometimes beautifully, and then do the same thing again. After a few rounds the sorry starts to feel like a way of ending the conversation.

It can sound like

  • I’m sorry, okay? What more do you want from me?
  • I said I was sorry. How many times do I have to say it?
  • I’m so sorry. I hate myself when I do this. (which leaves you comforting them)

Signs it might be this

  • The same offense has happened three or more times, each followed by a similar apology.
  • The apology never describes anything they will do differently.
  • You are expected to accept it that day and move on.
  • When you bring up the repeat, the old apology is quoted back as proof it is settled.
  • Comforting them about how bad they feel becomes your job.

Signs it is probably something else

  • The apology names a specific action and one thing they will do differently, and you see it.
  • Between incidents there is visible effort, such as reminders set or help sought.
  • When it happens again, they bring it up before you do.
  • The gaps between repeats are getting longer.
  • They let you take your time before you accept.
  • Specific steps follow, even small ones, and some of them stick.
  • Setbacks are admitted before you find them.

Questions to ask yourself

  • What exactly changed between the last apology and today?
  • Did the apology name what happened, or only say how bad they feel?
  • Is the same sentence being used again?
  • When I mention the repeat, do I get a conversation or a reminder that I already forgave it?
  • Is there a plan I could see, like a step, a date or a helper?
  • Am I looking after their feelings afterwards more than they are looking after the problem?

What it is

Apology without repair is a sorry that works as an ending instead of a beginning. The words may be warm, specific and even tearful, but nothing is different afterwards.

The same thing happens again, and the same sorry is offered. One apology not followed by instant change is normal, because change is slow and uneven.

The pattern is the closed loop. The apology stands in for the repair.

Raising the repeat is met with ‘I already apologized. ‘ This does not show whether the person means it.

Why it matters

Each apology closes the incident without changing anything. The same harm can recur while, on paper, it has been dealt with every time.

The person on the receiving end is left holding a record of apologies and no change.

How it shows up

  • An apology that is complete in itself, with no plan, change or follow-up attached.
  • Often arrives at the point of highest tension, and its effect is to lower it.
  • Can be sincere in the moment. The pattern lies in what follows, not in how it sounded.
  • Gets recycled: the same offense, similar wording, sometimes the same gift or gesture.
  • Turns the next complaint into the new offense, because ‘I already said sorry’.

What you can do

Read the safety note at the top first. If speaking up could make things worse, skip the wording below and talk to someone you trust or a local support service.

Words you could use

“Your apology is real to me, and so is the pattern. This has happened a few times now, so what I need isn’t another apology. Can we agree on what will be different next time?”

Ask about it gently

Ask what the apology comes with: ‘Thank you for saying sorry. What will you do differently next time, and how will I know?’

Set a clear limit

Say that words alone are no longer enough on this issue: ‘I appreciate the apology. Could we write down what will be different?’

Step back

Stop giving a quick ‘it’s fine’ to end the discomfort. You can say ‘I heard you’ and leave it unresolved while behavior makes the case.

Get support

Keep a short dated list of the repeats and the apologies and show it to someone you trust. A pattern can be easier to see on paper than in the moment.

At work, school or with services

Ask for the apology to come with a written next step and a review date. This could be an agreement on how it will be handled next time.

Using this label fairly

  • Counting apologies as proof of bad faith is unfair to people working on change slowly. The label fits a closed loop, not a first or second relapse.
  • It can also be used to refuse any apology, treating sorry as worthless in advance. Sometimes an apology is all that can be offered.

How it differs from look-alikes

A Non-Apology Apology regrets your reaction, not their action. Here the apology can name the action in full and sound sincere, and then nothing changes.

Denial disputes what happened, whereas here it is admitted. Minimization makes light of the damage.

Here the damage may be fully owned and the same thing still happens again.
