# CAT10: Specialized Exploitation Composites — Entertainment & Music Industry

## Category Introduction

[SPEAK] The earlier categories describe stages. This one describes people and places. It gathers the situations where several mechanisms are braided together around a particular person in a particular corner of the business: the teenager whose gift has become the family income, the parent who is both guardian and signatory and who is gently told to stop asking questions, the young athlete-turned-performer, the whistleblower who saw something on a tour, the imprint built around one charismatic figure, the manager who is secretly the label's friend, the circle where gifts, favors and favors owed make up the whole economy.

Some of these look nothing like exploitation from the outside. A child performer's schedule is packed, and the packing is called opportunity. A wellness retreat, a recovery program, a sober companion on tour: support that can be real and can also tighten control. A generous mentor opens doors and gives gifts, and the generosity makes a ledger. A leader is magnetic, and the room is arranged so that disagreeing feels like disloyalty. The exceptions are always special: this contract is just for you, don't tell the others. The performative ally says the right words on stage and signs the opposite on paper. And it can loop back on itself, so that every attempt to leave or complain becomes proof for the story that you are the problem.

Because these composites borrow from everywhere, the clearest sign is the population and the setting. Who is being targeted? A minor, a person on a first deal, a crew member who needs the next booking, someone in recovery, someone who witnessed misconduct and signed a confidentiality agreement? The protections you need depend on who you are in the picture, and so do the people you should turn to. Genuine family involvement, real duty of care and sincere mentorship exist, and many are exactly what they appear to be.

Where a child's safety, sexual or other exploitation, threats, a medical emergency or a danger to life is involved, this is no longer a matter for careful conversation. Go to emergency services, child-protection authorities, medical and crisis professionals. In every other case, the guidance stays the same: write things down, ask for clear terms, keep an independent adviser, keep people in your life who do not depend on the arrangement, and treat any structure that cannot bear outside eyes as information.
