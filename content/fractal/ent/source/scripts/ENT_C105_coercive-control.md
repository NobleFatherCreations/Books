# C105: Coercive Control — Entertainment & Music Industry

## Safety Note (read first)

This script describes a pattern that can involve threats, surveillance, financial control and sexual pressure within an intimate and professional relationship. If you are in immediate danger, contact emergency services now. If you are unsure whether you are safe, contact a local domestic-abuse or crisis line, or a trusted person who is not connected to the relationship or the business, and plan carefully before changing anything. Do not confront the person, do not announce a decision to leave, and do not delete or disable things in a way that could alert them. Take care with devices and apps, since a change can be noticed. Sexual pressure or coercion of any kind is a matter for specialist support services and, where you choose, the authorities. Do not handle it alone. Resources and legal options are jurisdiction-specific. [LEGAL CLAIM — JURISDICTION-SPECIFIC]

## Cold Open

[SPEAK] You're a twenty-eight-year-old singer, and you are in the kitchen of the house you share with the man who manages you, reading the calendar on the refrigerator door, which is also the calendar on your phone, which is also the calendar in the shared app that tells him where you are. You met him when your career was a rumor. He believed in you before anyone else did. He was patient, and funny, and good at the things you are bad at, which turned out to be most of the business.

For two years you were a team. He drove you to every early show. He read every contract, aloud, in the car, with a voice like a good teacher. When you signed the management agreement, it felt almost like a formality: the two of you in the living room on the first night in the house, a bottle of wine, a page that said he would handle bookings, finances, press and "all aspects of the artist's professional life." You laughed at that phrase. All aspects.

Now there is a schedule for everything. He sets your wake time because the day is built around the tour. He chooses your meals around a plan from a nutritionist you have never met. He suggests who you should see on days off, and he is always so reasonable about it. You are tired, but good at being tired.

You have not seen a royalty statement in a year. When you ask, he says it's with the accountant. You have not seen the tour budget since March. He says the numbers are boring, and that you should be saving your energy for the voice. Your own account has a small allowance paid in weekly. He calls it a draw. It covers groceries and not much else.

Somewhere in the second year your sister stopped calling as often. The last time she came to the house, he wasn't rude, only quiet, and afterwards he mentioned that she'd seemed "negative" about the project. Your old bandmate wrote to say she was in town. He said she'd probably want something. You said you'd call. You didn't.

The location app was his idea. It was for safety on the road, he said, after a scare in a city you didn't know. Your phone has been a small blue dot on his screen ever since. Last month you went for a walk and stayed out an hour longer than you'd said. When you came back, the kitchen light was on and he was sitting very still. He wasn't angry. He was hurt. He said he had been calling, and that it made him think about how things used to be, and whether you still wanted this. He said he didn't know what he'd do without you, in a voice that frightened you more than shouting would have.

Last week he asked for your phone password, gently, to "help manage the DMs" before the release. You gave it. It felt smaller than arguing.

Tonight the tour calendar says you've got a six a.m. flight, a radio appearance, a meet-and-greet and a soundcheck, and nothing else until midnight. You have done this for forty days. You have not been alone in a room with the door closed for weeks. In the bedroom, he is waiting. He says it's been a while, that you've been distant, that he's been patient. He says it softly. He doesn't need to say anything else.

On the refrigerator, under a magnet shaped like a guitar, the management agreement sits in its clear plastic sleeve, with a yellow sticky note he wrote months ago that says "Remember, I'm on your side."

## The Name

[HARD CUT] "That's Coercive Control."

## Definition

Coercive Control is the sequence in which one person, who holds both an intimate and a professional position over another, regulates daily life, information, money, relationships, location, devices, emotions, energy and intimacy until refusing costs more than complying, using behavior control, information control and financial control to convert the artist's need for autonomy across every domain of daily life into the comprehensive loss of autonomy, safety and often physical wellbeing. It can function inside a management agreement that hides the household, and the household that hides the agreement. The person who benefits may control the artist's career and the artist's life. It is hard to detect because each act can be explained as care, strategy or love, and because the person inside it may be proud of the partnership. Intent would require additional evidence, and this script does not diagnose anyone.

## Verbal Breakdown

[SPEAK]
1. **Behavior Control** — wake times, meals, clothing, who you see and what you do on days off are set by the schedule and by one person. This functions by turning a career plan into a daily rulebook, so that autonomy shrinks one reasonable instruction at a time. Concern rises when the rules apply to private life as well as work, and declining them carries a cost.
2. **Information Control** — royalty statements, tour budgets and contract versions are "with the accountant" or "too boring." This functions by making one person the only source of the facts about your own career. Concern rises when your questions about your own money or contracts are deflected or treated as distrust.
3. **Financial Control** — income is routed through the manager, and the artist receives an allowance or draw. This functions by making basic needs depend on one person's goodwill. Concern rises when you cannot access your own accounts, statements or funds, or need approval for ordinary purchases.
4. **Isolation** — a sister, a former bandmate and old friends become "negative" or "wanting something." This functions by dissolving outside support through small, engineered frictions, so that fewer people can notice a change. Concern rises when contact with family and friends declines after comments from the controlling person.
5. **Location Tracking** — a shared location app is introduced for safety and becomes constant. This functions by making every deviation visible and therefore accountable. Concern rises when tracking is not mutual, cannot be turned off without consequence, or is followed by questions about where you were.
6. **Device Access Demands** — a request for the phone password to "manage DMs." This functions by removing digital privacy, so private conversations stop being private. Concern rises when access is requested beyond what the work needs, or when saying no is treated as hiding something.
7. **Emotional Blackmail** — hurt, quiet disappointment and statements like "I don't know what I'd do without you." This functions by making the cost of independence an emotional catastrophe the artist is responsible for. Concern rises when your wellbeing is linked to the other person's reaction, and self-harm hints or threats appear, in which case this is a safety matter.
8. **Ritualized Exhaustion** — forty days of early flights, appearances and no solitude. This functions by depleting the energy needed to question, plan or leave. Concern rises when the schedule leaves no private time, rest or independent decisions, and the exhaustion is described as the job.
9. **Sexual Coercion** — persistent pressure about intimacy, framed as patience and "it's been a while." This functions by making refusal costly in a relationship where the other person also controls work, money and housing. Concern rises when saying no brings sulking, pressure, punishment or professional consequences. This is a safety matter, and support services are the right route.

## Why This Order

[SPEAK] Behavior control comes first because it begins as help, and help is accepted. The schedule feels like care. Information control then removes the facts you would need to judge it, and financial control removes the means to act on that judgment. By the time the artist might object, they no longer know the numbers, nor hold the money.

Isolation follows because it takes away the people who could notice a change. Location tracking and device access demands then make the isolation airtight, since the remaining private spaces, the walk and the message, are no longer private. Emotional blackmail is the lever that uses all of this: when you have nowhere else to go and no way to check, the other person's distress becomes your problem.

Ritualized exhaustion keeps the system running, because a tired person defends nothing. Sexual coercion is last because it depends on everything before it. Refusal costs more when it is also a question of housing, income, career and the only remaining relationship. The conversion point is isolation: until it is complete, the person has someone to ask. What would weaken the sequence is an outside relationship, independent access to money and records, and a safe plan made quietly with professional support.

## Individual Level

[SPEAK] The person may begin to apologize for ordinary things. Over time, they may stop making small decisions, avoid mentioning other people and check how a message will be read before sending. They may feel they are overreacting, because each act has a reasonable explanation. They may lose sleep, weight and confidence. They may feel loyalty and fear in the same moment. Some will defend the other person to friends, because the partnership was real.

## Institutional Echo

[SPEAK] In this sector, the same pattern can be carried by a management agreement that covers "all aspects" of an artist's life, a business structure in which the manager also holds the accounts, a tour schedule with no private time, a team that treats the artist's phone as a company asset, and a culture that treats the manager's partner role as an asset to the brand. [COMMON INDUSTRY PATTERN] A label, agency or promoter may see only an effective team. Dual roles are a conflict-of-interest risk, and independent advice on the management agreement is especially important when the manager is also a partner. Whether a management agreement can be enforced, and what rights an artist has over their own accounts, depends on jurisdiction. [LEGAL CLAIM — JURISDICTION-SPECIFIC]

## Civilizational Echo

[SPEAK] The myth is that the artist needs someone to handle everything so that the art can flourish, and that a devoted partner makes the best manager. It sounds like love and care. The language is gentle: "I just want to protect you," "just focus on the music," "we're a team," "I'm on your side." The public story is a power couple. The private loss is a person's autonomy, money, friends, privacy and body. A culture that admires the manager-partner as the secret to an artist's success has little language for noticing when it becomes a cage.

## Observable Indicators

- The manager is also the partner, household head and holder of the accounts.
- The singer cannot see royalty statements, tour budgets or contracts without being told they are too boring or not needed.
- The artist receives an allowance and cannot access their own funds.
- Contact with family or friends reduces after comments about them being negative or wanting something.
- A location app is used by one person only, and an unexplained absence is followed by questions or distress.
- Requests for passwords or device access go beyond what the work needs.
- Statements such as "I don't know what I'd do without you" follow any independent decision.
- The schedule leaves no solitude, private rest or independent day.
- Refusing intimacy is followed by sulking, pressure or work-related consequences.

## Evidence To Preserve — Additions

- Keep what you lawfully hold in a safe place the other person cannot reach, such as a trusted person's account or a secure location, rather than on shared devices.
- Copies of the management agreement and any amendments, with dates.
- Dated notes of schedule demands, refused requests and consequences, written where they cannot be found.
- Records of what money you can and cannot access, including bank statements for accounts in your own name.
- Messages in which you were pressured, threatened or punished, preserved without altering the device.
- Take care that any step to preserve evidence does not alert the other person; ask a specialist advocate how to do this safely.

## False-Positive Check — Specific

Many managers and partners in this industry share a calendar, a house and a location app by mutual choice, and many tours are punishing for everyone. A mutual, switch-off-able location share, an agreed schedule and an open ledger are not this pattern. Genuine duty of care, especially around health or a frightening incident on the road, can justify temporary precautions.

Concern strengthens when: the arrangement is one-way, saying no to any part of it brings distress, punishment or pressure, and outside contact, money or privacy shrinks over time.

Apply the Universal Benign Explanations and concern tests in the Sector Standards.

## Recognition Cue Cards

**SPOT IT — The Switch-Off Test:**
Ask: "Can I turn off tracking or refuse to share my location without a penalty?"
Concern rises if: I cannot, or the answer is a conversation that frightens me.

**SPOT IT — The Ledger Test:**
Ask: "Can I see every statement, budget and contract about my own career?"
Concern rises if: I am told no, or that it is too boring.

**SPOT IT — The Phone Test:**
Ask: "Could I say no to sharing my passwords and be fine?"
Concern rises if: saying no would be treated as hiding something.

**SPOT IT — The Friends Test:**
Ask: "Who have I stopped seeing, and when?"
Concern rises if: the answer follows comments about those people.

**SPOT IT — The No Test:**
Ask: "What happens after I say no to something personal?"
Concern rises if: the cost is anger, sulking, withdrawal of work or money, or pressure that does not stop.

## Chain Breakers

- Talk quietly to a domestic-abuse or crisis service and an independent advocate before changing anything; they can help you plan safely.
- Identify one trusted person outside the relationship and the business, and agree a simple signal for when you need help.
- Keep copies of important documents and ID somewhere safe that you control.
- Where you can do so safely, open an account in your own name, and ask an independent advocate how to do it without being noticed.
- Take independent legal advice on the management agreement from a lawyer with no tie to the manager, arranged away from shared devices.
- Do not announce plans to leave, confront the person, or delete the app without advice, because changes may be noticed.
- Plan any exit with professionals, not alone.

## Escalation Triggers — Specific

**LOW → MODERATE:** you cannot see your own statements, your schedule leaves no private time, or you notice that you are avoiding people or topics to keep the peace.

**MODERATE → HIGH:** tracking or device access is demanded, funds are withheld, contact with family is discouraged, saying no to any part of the arrangement leads to punishment, or the management agreement is used to keep you in the relationship or the household. Seek independent legal and specialist support.

**HIGH → URGENT:** there are threats, stalking, blackmail, physical violence or intimidation, sexual pressure that continues after refusal, withheld travel documents or phone, confinement, threats of self-harm used to control you, or any fear for your safety. Contact emergency services and a specialist support service, tell a trusted person where you are, and do not confront or negotiate alone.

## Safer Response Language

- "I'd like to have a copy of my royalty statements and the tour budget for my own files."
- "I'd like an independent lawyer to look at the management agreement. I'll arrange it."
- "I'd prefer to keep my phone private. I'm happy to send you my schedule."
- "I'm seeing my sister on Sunday. I'll be home by evening."
- "I'm not comfortable with location sharing all the time. Can we use it only on travel days and make it mutual?"
- "I need a day with no schedule. Please keep it free."
- "I'm going to speak to someone I trust about this." (use with care, and only if safe)
