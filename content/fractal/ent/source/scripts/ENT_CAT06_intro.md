# CAT06: Social Warfare Composites — Entertainment & Music Industry

## Category Introduction

[SPEAK] In music, everybody knows everybody, and that is both the beauty and the weapon. A career is a web: the booker who takes your call, the engineer who recommends you, the playlist editor, the other band on the bill, the friend who knows a friend at the label. Reputation moves through that web by whisper, by group chat, by who is suddenly not tagged in the post. This category begins when an artist, a songwriter or a crew member steps out of line, asks a hard question, talks about leaving, or says something out loud, and the web begins to respond.

It rarely looks like an attack. It looks like silence from people who used to reply. A rumor that you are hard to work with, passed along as concern. A mutual friend who tells you what someone else supposedly said about you, and who always seems to be in the middle. A bandmate approached privately, then another, until the group stops talking about the thing that mattered. A sudden wave of strangers in the comments, a call to pick a side, a story that has been told so many times that it has already become what everybody knows. Information may only flow through one hub, one person who is close to everyone, and the picture of what everyone thinks comes from that hub alone.

The sector has the fuel for it: scarce slots, tiny rooms, a lot of people who fear being the next one cut from the bill. Loyalty is treated as a virtue and as a test. Gratitude means silence. Burn a bridge here and you will not work in this town. Some of that is simple professional caution, and a falling-out between collaborators is not a campaign. The pattern concerns the coordination, the third-hand claims, the timing right after a signal of dissent, and the pressure to publicly choose.

What protects you is a life that does not run through one circle. Keep real friendships, peers and advisers outside the label, the team and the scene. When someone tells you what everybody is saying, ask who, and ask to hear it directly. Be slow to declare loyalty in a hurry. Write down dates and sources. The people worth keeping will still be there when you take your time.
