# C201: The Body Audit — Entertainment & Music Industry

## Safety Note (read first)

This script touches on body image, eating and physical health. If you are restricting food, feeling faint, losing weight you did not intend to lose, or having thoughts of harming yourself, please contact a doctor, a qualified eating-disorder or mental-health professional, or local crisis and emergency services. Do not wait for a better moment in the schedule. If the person affected is under eighteen, the concern also belongs with a parent or guardian outside the production and the appropriate child-welfare authority. Nothing here is medical advice.

## Cold Open

[SPEAK] You're a music-show presenter, twenty-nine years old, and you have wanted this since you were a teenager introducing songs to your bedroom mirror. You earned it the slow way: regional radio, a web series, two years of guest slots where nobody remembered your name. When the network offered you the weekly show, the executive producer said, "We just want you to be you." You heard it as a promise.

The wardrobe department is on the second floor, past the green room, and for the first month it feels like a gift. Rails of clothes arrive in your name. The stylist hums while she pins a hem and calls you "my girl." Nobody has ever dressed you before. You think: this is what it feels like when a place believes in you.

The first remark is so small you almost miss it. "Ooh, that's brave, with your arms." She laughs, and you laugh, because you are not a person who can't take a joke. The next week it is, "We'll go a size up on camera, it only ever adds." Then, "Lovely, lovely, just maybe not sitting down." Each one arrives with a smile and a pin in her mouth. None of them is an insult you could repeat to anyone without sounding petty.

Then comes the camera test. It is a grey room with a tape mark on the floor and a monitor that shows you to yourself in a way no mirror ever has. A producer and the stylist stand behind a desk, and afterwards you are handed a sheet. At the top is the fitting sheet: bust, waist, hip, thigh, upper arm, each with a column headed Current and a column headed Reference. Below it are the camera-test notes. *Face carries weight on the long lens. Reference line cleaner through the midsection. Jumpsuit pulls at the hip, reference did not have this issue.*

You ask who the reference is. The producer says, "Oh, just the look we had before. Don't worry about her." You have never met her. Nobody will tell you her name. She is a body you are being measured against and cannot see, and you notice that the thought of her has already moved into your head and taken a chair.

After that the sheet comes every fortnight. You are asked to initial a small box at the bottom marked *Noted*. You initial it because everybody initials things, and because the box is next to the line that says your costume fittings are scheduled for the following week.

In a planning meeting you hear yourself discussed in the third person while you are in the room. "Can she carry the jumpsuit by episode six?" "The partners want the look to sell." Someone says the show is a shop window for the clothing deal. Nobody is cruel. They are talking about a rail of clothes, and you are the rail.

The word the floor crew uses for fitting day is "the audit." You've heard it so many times that you stop hearing the joke.

You begin to arrive early to the camera test having skipped dinner. You stop taking the craft-table lunch because a runner once glanced at your plate and said, kindly, "Good for you." You carry a cereal bar in your bag like contraband and eat it in a toilet cubicle. When the monitor shows your face you look away first, before anyone can.

For three weeks you have been dizzy during the long link segments, the ones where you stand and talk for six minutes without a cut. You have a doctor's appointment you keep rescheduling, because a doctor might say a number, and then the number will be on a sheet. So you tell the floor manager you're fine. You ask for a chair between takes and apologise for asking.

On Thursday the executive producer finds you in the corridor and tilts her head. "You seem quiet. Everything all right?"

"Never better," you say, in your on-air voice, and you smile, and she smiles, and the voice works perfectly.

## The Name

[HARD CUT] That's **The Body Audit**.

## Definition

The Body Audit is the sequence where small remarks about a performer's body, measured against a comparison nobody will show, turn the body into a standing deficiency and a work object, until shame sits where self-regard used to be and the person stops saying what they need. It uses Negging, Comparative Devaluation, Objectification, Shame Installation and Need Concealment to convert the wish to be accepted as you are into lost physical confidence and a reluctance to seek care. It is hard to detect because every remark is small, wardrobe and camera work really do involve measuring bodies, and the harm shows up as private behaviour: skipped meals, hidden symptoms, unmade appointments.

## Verbal Breakdown

[SPEAK] The component tactics, in order:

1. **Negging** — The affectionate half-insults: "brave with your arms," "it only ever adds." This functions by lowering confidence in small increments, each wrapped in warmth so that objecting costs more than absorbing. Concern rises when the remarks repeat, cluster around the same body area, and always arrive when you are pinned, fitted or on a tape mark.

2. **Comparative Devaluation** — The unnamed predecessor, the "reference line," the look "we had before." This functions by setting you against a standard you cannot examine, challenge or even see, so the failing is real in your mind and unverifiable on paper. Concern rises when a comparison is written down but its subject, criteria and date cannot be produced.

3. **Objectification** — Talk about "the body" and "the look," third-person discussion with you present, the show as a shop window. This functions by relocating your value from your skill to your fit, so being a person with a view becomes an inconvenience to the look. Concern rises when decisions about your body are made in meetings you are not invited to.

4. **Shame Installation** — The fortnightly sheet, the initialled box, the nickname for fitting day. This functions by making the judgment routine and recorded, until you deliver it to yourself and no one else has to. Concern rises when you monitor your own body before anyone has spoken.

5. **Need Concealment** — Fainting spells explained away, appointments cancelled, food eaten in secret, "never better." This functions by teaching that needs are evidence against you, so the person removes themselves from the people who could help. Concern rises when someone hides a symptom or a meal because of what it will add to a sheet.

## Why This Order

[SPEAK] Negging comes first because it needs no justification. A single remark is deniable, affectionate and almost free to deliver, and it softens the ground without triggering a complaint. If the first thing you received was a sheet of measurements, you would object. After weeks of small remarks, you have already started to wonder.

Comparative Devaluation makes the remarks cohere. Once an absent reference exists, every little comment has something to point at, and the failing is no longer someone's taste but a gap. The predecessor must remain unnamed, because a named person can be asked about, looked up, and found to be an ordinary human being. An unnamed one stays perfect.

Objectification then changes what you are in the room. Having been measured against a standard, you can be discussed as a thing that meets or misses it. This is the step that removes independent evaluation: if your body is the production's asset, your opinion about it is a mere complication.

Shame Installation is the conversion point. It happens when the audit continues inside you, and the people running it can stop without the pattern stopping. The initialled box matters because it makes you a party to the record.

Need Concealment closes the loop and supplies the deniability. The production never denied you food or care; you stopped asking. What weakens the sequence is naming the comparison out loud, asking for the criteria in writing, and a doctor who knows nothing about the show.

## Individual Level

[SPEAK] The person may begin to check themselves before every appearance, reading their body the way the monitor does. Over time, they may skip meals, eat in secret, or avoid mirrors and the monitor. They may stop attending medical appointments, or minimise symptoms to colleagues. They may defend the remarks to friends ("it is a visual job"). Some withdraw from partners, friends and family who say they look fine, because those voices no longer carry weight. Others become unusually grateful for any praise. No single sign proves anything, and a doctor, not a script, is the right place to understand what is happening.

## Institutional Echo

[SPEAK] Television, video and live-performance production run on measurements: costume fitting sheets, camera tests, continuity photographs, look-books [COMMON INDUSTRY PATTERN]. Those are normal. They can become the Body Audit when a document compares a person to an unnamed standard, when the notes are shared with people who do not need them, or when the performer's weight, shape or "line" becomes part of a renewal conversation without written criteria. Brand partnerships and wardrobe-sponsor arrangements may add commercial pressure, and a contract with image and approval clauses can turn a styling opinion into a condition of work [LEGAL CLAIM — JURISDICTION-SPECIFIC]. Whether a given department is doing careful costume work or running an audit turns on separable questions: are the criteria written, is the comparison named, is health kept out of the file, and can the performer say no?

## Civilizational Echo

[SPEAK] The concealing myth is that the camera adds weight and the screen is simply honest. It is a persuasive story, because cameras do flatten, and because the industry has always sold images. Around it sit the sayings that you have to suffer for the work, that looks are part of the job, and that anyone who objects is being precious. The value being exploited is the very ordinary wish to be accepted as you are. The public story is a bright, effortless presenter in a perfect jumpsuit. The private cost, when this pattern is present, is a person who cannot eat in front of her crew.

## Observable Indicators

1. Remarks about a performer's body, delivered as jokes, recur during fittings or camera tests.
2. Written notes compare the performer to an unnamed predecessor or "reference."
3. Fitting sheets include a comparison column without stated criteria.
4. The performer is discussed in the third person in meetings where they are present.
5. Measurements or weight-related notes are shared beyond those who need them.
6. A nickname for fitting day or camera tests normalises the scrutiny.
7. The performer is asked to initial or sign body-related notes weekly.
8. The performer begins skipping meals, eating privately, or declining food at shared tables.
9. Medical appointments are postponed and symptoms are minimised.
10. Praise is attached to weight change, and concern to health is dismissed as "being sensitive."

## Evidence To Preserve — Additions

- Camera-test notes and fitting sheets in every version, including the comparison column and dates.
- Any wording that refers to a predecessor or "reference," and your request, if any, for the criteria behind it.
- Dates, names and wording of body remarks, written in a private log soon after each fitting.
- Initialled or signed acknowledgments of body-related notes.
- Contract clauses on image, wardrobe, approval and fitness for the role.
- Records of any medical appointments postponed, and any accommodation requests.

## False-Positive Check — Specific

Costume and camera work genuinely involve measurement. A fitting sheet, a camera test with notes on how a fabric sits, a request for a different cut, or a note that a colour reads badly on screen is ordinary craft. A stylist who is blunt but equally blunt about everyone, who names the garment as the problem, and who stops when asked is not running an audit. Honest health concern from a colleague, offered once and kindly, is not negging.

The discriminators are criteria and direction. Craft talks about the garment, the light and the lens, in writing, and welcomes your view. The pattern talks about your body, compares you to someone you cannot see, and treats your discomfort as a mood.

Concern strengthens when: remarks cluster on the body rather than the garment; comparisons are written but their subject and criteria cannot be produced; the performer begins hiding food, symptoms or appointments.

Apply the Universal Benign Explanations and concern tests in the Sector Standards.

## Recognition Cue Cards

**SPOT IT — The Reference Test:**
Ask: "Who exactly is the reference, and what are the written criteria?"
Concern rises if: nobody can name her, the criteria are vague, and the notes keep citing her.

**SPOT IT — The Garment Test:**
Ask: "Is the note about the garment, the light and the lens, or about my body?"
Concern rises if: the notes keep describing you and almost never the clothes.

**SPOT IT — The Room Test:**
Ask: "Am I in the room when decisions about my body are made?"
Concern rises if: you are discussed as "she" while present, or not invited at all.

**SPOT IT — The Hiding Test:**
Ask: "Is there something I am avoiding eating, saying or booking because of what it would add to a sheet?"
Concern rises if: yes, and the avoidance has been going on for weeks.

## Chain Breakers

- **Ask for the criteria in writing.** "Could you send me what the reference means in terms of fit and lens? I'd like to work to it." A real standard can be written down; a phantom one cannot.
- **Move the conversation to the garment.** "Can we talk about what the jumpsuit is doing, rather than what I'm doing?"
- **Decline the initialling of body notes.** "I'm happy to sign off on the costume schedule. I'd rather not sign off on notes about my body."
- **Keep a private log** of remarks, dates and who was present. A page, written the same day.
- **Keep health out of the wardrobe loop.** See a doctor who has nothing to do with the production, and share only what you choose.
- **Keep one relationship that is not about the show.** A friend, a family member or a teacher who will tell you what they see.
- **Ask who benefits.** If the notes serve the garment, fine. If they serve a brand partner or a renewal conversation, ask where that is written.
- **If it is a minor, bring in the guardian or an independent adult** and the appropriate child-welfare route, not a confrontation with the department.

## Escalation Triggers — Specific

**LOW → MODERATE:** Comparisons to an unnamed reference appear in writing; remarks recur across several fittings; you are asked to sign body-related notes; you start skipping meals or appointments because of the notes.

**MODERATE → HIGH:** Weight, shape or "line" is linked to renewal, casting or pay; notes are shared beyond need-to-know; your requests for criteria are ignored; a union or guild representative, an independent entertainment attorney, the production's formal complaint channel or a workplace-conduct authority may be appropriate. Which routes exist is jurisdiction-specific. [LEGAL CLAIM — JURISDICTION-SPECIFIC]

**HIGH → URGENT:** Fainting, rapid unintended weight loss, restrictive eating, purging, or any thought of self-harm; any request involving a minor's body; any pressure to continue despite a medical warning. Seek medical and crisis support now, tell a trusted person, and do not negotiate or confront anyone first.

## Safer Response Language

- "Could you send me the criteria for the reference in writing, so I can work to it?"
- "I'd like to keep the fitting notes about the garment. My body isn't something I'll sign notes about."
- "Who is the reference, and is the note about her or about the costume?"
- "I'm happy to try a different cut. I'd like to hear what's wrong with the cut."
- "I'd like to be in the room when my fittings and camera notes are discussed."
- "I'm seeing my own doctor, and I'd prefer to keep that private."

## Reflection

If the standard you are failing was never shown to you, who built it, and who gains each time you try to meet it?
