# CAT07: Institutional & Systemic Composites — Entertainment & Music Industry

## Category Introduction

[SPEAK] Step back from the studio and look at the building. This category is not about a single manager, producer or executive. It is about how the structure of the business can do the work on its own: the recoupment waterfall, the cross-collateralized accounts, the royalty portal that shows you a total and nothing underneath, the approval chain with nobody at the top of it, the standard form that is the only form. Nobody in the chain has to want a bad outcome. Each person processes the next step, applies the clause, follows the template, and the artist ends up exactly where the structure leads.

Money, access and credentials are the levers. Who funds the tour, who owns the dashboard, who decides which sessions get booked, whose registration counts as the official one. The mission language is warm: development, partnership, supporting the artist's vision. The measured activity is often different. A metric slowly replaces the career it was supposed to describe. A person's standing depends on numbers they cannot inspect. A knowledgeable insider who understands the contract can use that knowledge as leverage over someone who does not. Staff turn over and the new head of the department says they weren't here for that. A paper trail built in the background becomes the reference point the first time anything goes wrong.

Because it is structural, it is often invisible to everyone inside it. An assistant is simply following the process. A business affairs lawyer is protecting the company, which is their job. An artist in the middle of it is told this is just how the industry works, and often the artist is right that it is how it works. Standard does not mean fair, and unusual does not mean sinister. [COMMON INDUSTRY PATTERN] Some structures are heavy because the risk is heavy. The question is whether anyone can explain, on paper, why it is built this way and who benefits.

The defense lives in questions that do not accuse anyone. Who benefits from this structure? Where is the policy, the formula, the underlying data behind this decision? What does the mission say, and what is actually funded, rewarded and counted? Ask in writing, ask a second time, ask someone independent to read the answer. A fair system can show its workings. A closed one tends to answer with the word standard.
