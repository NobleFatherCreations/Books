# C201: The Body Audit — Entertainment & Music Industry

## Safety Note (read first)

This script touches on body image, eating and physical health. If you are restricting food, feeling faint, losing weight you did not intend to lose, or having thoughts of harming yourself, please contact a doctor, a qualified eating-disorder or mental-health professional, or local crisis and emergency services. Do not wait for a better moment in the schedule. If the person affected is under eighteen, the concern also belongs with a parent or guardian outside the production and the appropriate child-welfare authority. Nothing here is medical advice.

## Cold Open

You're a music-show presenter, twenty-nine years old, and you have wanted this since you were a teenager introducing songs to your bedroom mirror. You earned it the slow way: regional radio, a web series, two years of guest slots where nobody remembered your name. When the network offered you the weekly show, the executive producer said, "We just want you to be you." You heard it as a promise. The wardrobe department is on the second floor, past the green room, and for the first month it feels like a gift. The stylist hums while she pins a hem and calls you "my girl."

The first remark is so small you almost miss it. "Ooh, that's brave, with your arms." She laughs, and you laugh, because you are not a person who can't take a joke. The next week it is, "We'll go a size up on camera, it only ever adds." Each one arrives with a smile and a pin in her mouth. You start holding your elbows close at fittings, and you could not explain why to anyone without sounding petty.

Then comes the camera test, a grey room with a tape mark on the floor and a monitor that shows you to yourself in a way no mirror ever has. Afterwards you are handed a sheet: bust, waist, hip, thigh, upper arm, each with a column headed Current and a column headed Reference. *Face carries weight on the long lens. Reference line cleaner through the midsection. Jumpsuit pulls at the hip, reference did not have this issue.* You ask who the reference is. The producer says, "Oh, just the look we had before. Don't worry about her." Nobody will tell you her name. She has already moved into your head and taken a chair.

In a planning meeting you hear yourself discussed in the third person while you are in the room. "Can she carry the jumpsuit by episode six?" "The partners want the look to sell." Someone says the show is a shop window for the clothing deal. Nobody is cruel. They are talking about a rail of clothes, and you are the rail. You keep your face pleasant and your hands flat on the table.

After that the sheet comes every fortnight, with a small box at the bottom marked *Noted*. "Just your initials there, lovely," the producer says, turning the pen toward you, "and then we can book your fittings." You initial it, because everybody initials things. The floor crew call fitting day "the audit." You've heard it so many times that you stop hearing the joke. One night at home you stand sideways at the mirror and check yourself against the columns, though nobody asked you to.

You begin to arrive at the camera test having skipped dinner. You stop taking the craft-table lunch because a runner once glanced at your plate and said, kindly, "Good for you." You carry a cereal bar in your bag like contraband and eat it in a toilet cubicle. For three weeks you have been dizzy during the long link segments, and you keep rescheduling a doctor's appointment, because a doctor might say a number, and then the number will be on a sheet. On Thursday the executive producer finds you in the corridor and tilts her head. "You seem quiet. Everything all right?" "Never better," you say, in your on-air voice, and the voice works perfectly.

When the monitor shows your face, you look away first, before anyone can. You no longer eat in front of anyone from the show, and you have stopped going to your friends' Sunday lunches because there is always a plate to explain. You used to introduce songs as if you loved them; now you rehearse your links watching your jaw instead of the words. The doctor's number is still in your phone, under the first appointment you cancelled.

## The Name

That's **The Body Audit**.

## Definition

The Body Audit is the sequence where small remarks about a performer's body, measured against a comparison nobody will show, turn the body into a standing deficiency and a work object, until shame sits where self-regard used to be and the person stops saying what they need. It uses Negging, Comparative Devaluation, Objectification, Shame Installation and Need Concealment to convert the wish to be accepted as you are into lost physical confidence and a reluctance to seek care. It is hard to detect because every remark is small, wardrobe and camera work really do involve measuring bodies, and the harm shows up as private behaviour: skipped meals, hidden symptoms, unmade appointments.

## Verbal Breakdown

The component tactics, in order:

1. **Negging** — The affectionate half-insults: "brave with your arms," "it only ever adds." This functions by lowering confidence in small increments, each wrapped in warmth so that objecting costs more than absorbing. Concern rises when the remarks repeat, cluster around the same body area, and always arrive when you are pinned, fitted or on a tape mark.

2. **Comparative Devaluation** — The unnamed predecessor, the "reference line," the look "we had before." This functions by setting you against a standard you cannot examine, challenge or even see, so the failing is real in your mind and unverifiable on paper. Concern rises when a comparison is written down but its subject, criteria and date cannot be produced.

3. **Objectification** — Talk about "the body" and "the look," third-person discussion with you present, the show as a shop window. This functions by relocating your value from your skill to your fit, so being a person with a view becomes an inconvenience to the look. Concern rises when decisions about your body are made in meetings you are not invited to.

4. **Shame Installation** — The fortnightly sheet, the initialled box, the nickname for fitting day. This functions by making the judgment routine and recorded, until you deliver it to yourself and no one else has to. Concern rises when you monitor your own body before anyone has spoken.

5. **Need Concealment** — Fainting spells explained away, appointments cancelled, food eaten in secret, "never better." This functions by teaching that needs are evidence against you, so the person removes themselves from the people who could help. Concern rises when someone hides a symptom or a meal because of what it will add to a sheet.

## Why This Order

**Negging** comes first because it needs no justification. A single remark is deniable, affectionate and almost free to deliver, and it softens the ground without triggering a complaint. If the first thing you received was a sheet of measurements, you would object. After weeks of small remarks, you have already started to wonder. It has to come first, because after 'brave, with your arms' the unnamed Reference column feels like proof of something you suspected; start with the Reference column instead and you would ask who she is.

> Picture a friend who says, "You're so brave to wear that," with a big smile. You laugh along, and later you quietly change your top. Plenty of stylists give honest notes on fit; what matters is whether the notes are about the clothes or about you. Flip it: if your friend compared you to someone you'd never met before any "so brave to wear that", you would ask who that was and keep wearing your top.

**Comparative Devaluation** makes the remarks cohere. Once an absent reference exists, every little comment has something to point at, and the failing is no longer someone's taste but a gap. The predecessor must remain unnamed, because a named person can be asked about, looked up, and found to be an ordinary human being. An unnamed one stays perfect. It has to come before 'Can she carry the jumpsuit?', because with an unnamed reference on file, talking about you in the third person sounds routine; the other way round, you would say you are in the room and answer.

> Imagine a new teacher who keeps saying "last year's class never had this problem." You never met last year's class, and you can't measure up to someone you can't see. Flip it: if the teacher talked about you as if you weren't there before any comparisons with last year, you would say you are here and answer yourself.

**Objectification** then changes what you are in the room. Having been measured against a standard, you can be discussed as a thing that meets or misses it. This is the step that removes independent evaluation: if your body is the production's asset, your opinion about it is a mere complication. It has to come before the box marked Noted, because after being discussed as a rail of clothes, initialling it feels like part of the job; the other way round, you would ask what Noted means before initialling.

> Think of sitting at a family dinner while relatives discuss your haircut as if you weren't there. After a while you stop feeling like a guest at the table and start feeling like the thing on it. Flip it: if relatives kept telling you "needs work" before ever discussing your haircut as if you weren't there, you would ask what exactly they meant.

**Shame Installation** is the conversion point. It happens when the audit continues inside you, and the people running it can stop without the pattern stopping. The initialled box matters because it makes you a party to the record. It has to come before you skip lunch and the doctor, because once you check yourself against the columns at home, a doctor's number feels like another sheet; the other way round, you would keep the appointment and eat normally.

> Picture signing a card at every visit that says "effort: needs work." Soon you stop thinking "I missed a week" and start thinking "I'm just bad at this." Flip it: if you had started hiding when you needed help before any "needs work" cards, you would see no reason to and ask for help at the next visit.

**Need Concealment** closes the loop and supplies the deniability. The production never denied you food or care; you stopped asking. It has to come last, because hiding the dizziness only follows the jokes, the Reference column, the meeting and the Noted box; earlier, you would tell the executive producer you feel dizzy and see the doctor that week.

> Imagine twisting your ankle before a big match and saying nothing so you won't be left out. Nobody stopped you getting help; you just never asked for it. Flip it: if hiding the twisted ankle had come before any feeling that you were just bad at this, you would have seen the risk and told the coach straight away.

What weakens the sequence is naming the comparison out loud, asking for the criteria in writing, and a doctor who knows nothing about the show.

## Individual Level

The person may begin to check themselves before every appearance, reading their body the way the monitor does. Over time, they may skip meals, eat in secret, or avoid mirrors and the monitor. They may stop attending medical appointments, or minimise symptoms to colleagues. They may defend the remarks to friends ("it is a visual job"). Some withdraw from partners, friends and family who say they look fine, because those voices no longer carry weight. Others become unusually grateful for any praise. No single sign proves anything, and a doctor, not a script, is the right place to understand what is happening.

## Institutional Echo

Television, video and live-performance production run on measurements: costume fitting sheets, camera tests, continuity photographs, look-books. Those are normal. They can become the Body Audit when a document compares a person to an unnamed standard, when the notes are shared with people who do not need them, or when the performer's weight, shape or "line" becomes part of a renewal conversation without written criteria. Brand partnerships and wardrobe-sponsor arrangements may add commercial pressure, and a contract with image and approval clauses can turn a styling opinion into a condition of work. Whether a given department is doing careful costume work or running an audit turns on separable questions: are the criteria written, is the comparison named, is health kept out of the file, and can the performer say no?

## Civilizational Echo

The concealing myth is that the camera adds weight and the screen is simply honest. It is a persuasive story, because cameras do flatten, and because the industry has always sold images. Around it sit the sayings that you have to suffer for the work, that looks are part of the job, and that anyone who objects is being precious. The value being exploited is the very ordinary wish to be accepted as you are. The public story is a bright, effortless presenter in a perfect jumpsuit. The private cost, when this pattern is present, is a person who cannot eat in front of her crew.

## Observable Indicators

1. Remarks about a performer's body, delivered as jokes, recur during fittings or camera tests.
2. Written notes compare the performer to an unnamed predecessor or "reference."
3. Fitting sheets include a comparison column without stated criteria.
4. The performer is discussed in the third person in meetings where they are present.
5. Measurements or weight-related notes are shared beyond those who need them.
6. A nickname for fitting day or camera tests normalises the scrutiny.
7. The performer is asked to initial or sign body-related notes weekly.
8. The performer begins skipping meals, eating privately, or declining food at shared tables.
9. Medical appointments are postponed and symptoms are minimised.
10. Praise is attached to weight change, and concern to health is dismissed as "being sensitive."

## Evidence To Preserve — Additions

- Camera-test notes and fitting sheets in every version, including the comparison column and dates.
- Any wording that refers to a predecessor or "reference," and your request, if any, for the criteria behind it.
- Dates, names and wording of body remarks, written in a private log soon after each fitting.
- Initialled or signed acknowledgments of body-related notes.
- Contract clauses on image, wardrobe, approval and fitness for the role.
- Records of any medical appointments postponed, and any accommodation requests.

## False-Positive Check — Specific

Costume and camera work genuinely involve measurement. A fitting sheet, a camera test with notes on how a fabric sits, a request for a different cut, or a note that a colour reads badly on screen is ordinary craft. A stylist who is blunt but equally blunt about everyone, who names the garment as the problem, and who stops when asked is not running an audit. Honest health concern from a colleague, offered once and kindly, is not negging.

The discriminators are criteria and direction. Craft talks about the garment, the light and the lens, in writing, and welcomes your view. The pattern talks about your body, compares you to someone you cannot see, and treats your discomfort as a mood.

Concern strengthens when: remarks cluster on the body rather than the garment; comparisons are written but their subject and criteria cannot be produced; the performer begins hiding food, symptoms or appointments.

Apply the Universal Benign Explanations and concern tests in the Sector Standards.

## Recognition Cue Cards

**SPOT IT — The Reference Test:**
Ask: "Who exactly is the reference, and what are the written criteria?"
Concern rises if: nobody can name her, the criteria are vague, and the notes keep citing her.

**SPOT IT — The Garment Test:**
Ask: "Is the note about the garment, the light and the lens, or about my body?"
Concern rises if: the notes keep describing you and almost never the clothes.

**SPOT IT — The Room Test:**
Ask: "Am I in the room when decisions about my body are made?"
Concern rises if: you are discussed as "she" while present, or not invited at all.

**SPOT IT — The Hiding Test:**
Ask: "Is there something I am avoiding eating, saying or booking because of what it would add to a sheet?"
Concern rises if: yes, and the avoidance has been going on for weeks.

## Chain Breakers

- **Ask for the criteria in writing.** "Could you send me what the reference means in terms of fit and lens? I'd like to work to it." A real standard can be written down; a phantom one cannot.
- **Move the conversation to the garment.** "Can we talk about what the jumpsuit is doing, rather than what I'm doing?"
- **Decline the initialling of body notes.** "I'm happy to sign off on the costume schedule. I'd rather not sign off on notes about my body."
- **Keep a private log** of remarks, dates and who was present. A page, written the same day.
- **Keep health out of the wardrobe loop.** See a doctor who has nothing to do with the production, and share only what you choose.
- **Keep one relationship that is not about the show.** A friend, a family member or a teacher who will tell you what they see.
- **Ask who benefits.** If the notes serve the garment, fine. If they serve a brand partner or a renewal conversation, ask where that is written.
- **If it is a minor, bring in the guardian or an independent adult** and the appropriate child-welfare route, not a confrontation with the department.

## Escalation Triggers — Specific

**LOW → MODERATE:** Comparisons to an unnamed reference appear in writing; remarks recur across several fittings; you are asked to sign body-related notes; you start skipping meals or appointments because of the notes.

**MODERATE → HIGH:** Weight, shape or "line" is linked to renewal, casting or pay; notes are shared beyond need-to-know; your requests for criteria are ignored; a union or guild representative, an independent entertainment attorney, the production's formal complaint channel or a workplace-conduct authority may be appropriate. Which routes exist is jurisdiction-specific.

**HIGH → URGENT:** Fainting, rapid unintended weight loss, restrictive eating, purging, or any thought of self-harm; any request involving a minor's body; any pressure to continue despite a medical warning. Seek medical and crisis support now, tell a trusted person, and do not negotiate or confront anyone first.

Tier definitions and sector bodies are in the Sector Standards.

## Safer Response Language

- "Could you send me the criteria for the reference in writing, so I can work to it?"
- "I'd like to keep the fitting notes about the garment. My body isn't something I'll sign notes about."
- "Who is the reference, and is the note about her or about the costume?"
- "I'm happy to try a different cut. I'd like to hear what's wrong with the cut."
- "I'd like to be in the room when my fittings and camera notes are discussed."
- "I'm seeing my own doctor, and I'd prefer to keep that private."
