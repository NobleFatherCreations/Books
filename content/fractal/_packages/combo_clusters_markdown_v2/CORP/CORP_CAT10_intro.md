# CAT10: Specialized Exploitation Composites — Corporate & Consulting

## Category Introduction

The earlier categories each follow a stage: the hook, the lock-in, the pressure, the exit. This one is different. These composites are built for a particular person in a particular situation, and they borrow whatever they need from the other nine. The first-time founder who has no peer group. The family office principal who built wealth alone. The early employee holding undocumented equity. The compliance officer who raised a concern. The consultant whose entire income runs through one anchor client. The population is specific, and so is the setting. The mechanism underneath is usually one you have already met.

In corporate and consulting life, these show up where the stakes and the dependence are both high. An exception that is granted to you "because of our relationship" and later cannot be repeated. A competitive grid of advisors, each told the others are not to be trusted. An ally whose public statements about fairness do not match the private terms. A manager who runs two different standards, one for the room and one for the paper. A leader whose personal charisma has become the governing document. A set of favors, introductions and gifts that quietly become a ledger. A wellness or resilience program that treats your exhaustion as a personal failing. The sector disguises all of this as mentorship, partnership, mission, loyalty, culture and care. Some of those are real, and a well-meaning leader can be clumsy without being predatory. Intent would require additional evidence, and a poor outcome can come from incompetence, overreach or changed circumstances.

Because these composites combine mechanisms, they are best recognized by asking who is being targeted and what that person depends on. Look for the same markers that run through the whole book: a gap between promise and paper, resistance to written terms, outside review discouraged, dependency that keeps growing, consequences for questions, and a quiet cost to leaving. For anyone raising a concern inside an organization, rules on retaliation and whistleblower protection vary by place and need qualified review. Each script that follows is an illustration of a sequence, not a report about any real person or firm.

For prevention, borrow from whichever category a given composite draws on: written terms and independent counsel from the early categories, relationships outside the hub from the social ones, plain questions about who benefits from the structural ones. Keep records lawfully, keep your options open, and do not confront anyone where threats, extortion, fraud, retaliation or any danger to your safety are in play. In those cases, go to a qualified professional or emergency services first. Nine composites follow. Read the one that matches your situation, and then read the others, because the mechanism is often hiding in one you did not expect.
