# CAT01: Capture & Attachment Composites — Corporate & Consulting

## Category Introduction

This category sits at the very beginning of the story. Before there is a signed engagement letter, before the retainer schedule, before anyone talks about the cap table, there is a first meeting that goes unusually well. An investor says your deck is the sharpest they have seen this quarter. A partner at a consulting firm says you are exactly the kind of leader they want to build a relationship with. A mutual friend makes an introduction and says, quietly, that this person does not take just anyone's calls. In the corporate world, capture rarely looks like capture. It looks like being recruited, backed, sponsored, mentored, or let into the room.

The people most exposed at this stage are not foolish. They are first-time founders with a runway clock running. They are solo operators with no peer group to sanity-check a term sheet. They are independent consultants hoping an anchor client turns into something steady, or family office principals who built their wealth alone and now sit across from polished advisors offering to take the weight off. What these situations share is a gap: the attention arrives fast, and the paper arrives slowly. Praise is specific and generous. Access is offered early. Promises are made in a call, a dinner, a walk after a board meeting. Equity will be papered once the cleanup is done. A seat in the round is guaranteed, as long as you decide soon.

The sector has its own respectable disguises for this. It calls it partnership, not vendor treatment. It calls it relationship capital, a warm introduction, a trusted advisor, a sponsor who believes in you. It tells you that sophisticated people do not need lawyers at every step, that a handshake matters more than the paper, and that asking for terms in writing is a sign of distrust. Much of this is also simply how good professional relationships begin, and strong mentorship, real sponsorship and fast-moving rounds do exist. Early warmth is not evidence of bad intent, and a delay in paperwork is often just a delay.

So this category is about noticing the shape, not accusing anyone. Watch for intensity that outruns the documents, for commitments that live only on calls, for outside advice described as unnecessary, and for a deadline that happens to leave no time for counsel. What you can observe is the sequence: the pace, the paper, and what happens when you ask. The protective moves are ordinary and calm. Slow the timeline. Get terms in writing before work, money or exclusivity begins. Ask who benefits from each step. Keep your own counsel, a peer, or a second reader close. When real money, equity or career is at stake, qualified professionals in your jurisdiction can tell you what the paper actually does.
