## Capture — how access and trust get established

*Borrowed credentials, the lineage that cannot be checked, recruitment of the vulnerable.*

### 1. “I TRAINED WITH A LINEAGE”
*The Unverifiable Credential / Borrowed Authority — clinically, credential fabrication / appeal to unfalsifiable lineage.*

Here's who this is. Speaks with total calm certainty about where the work came from. Names a teacher, a region, a tradition, a number of years. Everything he says is structurally impossible to check, and he chose it for that reason.

Picture this. He says he trained for nine years with a lineage holder in a place you would have to fly to, whose name he gives softly, as though saying it too loudly would be disrespectful. Nobody in this tent is going to fact-check that. Nobody in this tent could. And you notice, as he says it, that the reverence in his voice is doing all the work that a certificate would otherwise have to do.

> 🖼️ **[INSERT IMAGE HERE: `images/heal/tells-card.png`]** — hold this on screen with that scenario's tells typed in; you can shorten the next line to "Here's what to watch for" instead of narrating every tell if you want the runtime down.

Here are the signs.

- Credentials that cannot be verified by design — remote, oral, or a deceased teacher
- Reverence used to make questions feel rude
- No insurance, no professional body, no supervisor, no complaints route
- Scope that expands to whatever you need — trauma, grief, fertility, diagnosis
- Vague about the parts a trained person is precise about: contraindications, referral, limits
- Gets cooler toward anyone who asks a second question

Here's the mechanism underneath it. Every regulated healing profession is built on three things a false practitioner cannot supply: a checkable qualification, a body that can remove you, and a named limit to your scope. Unverifiable lineage is chosen precisely because it substitutes for all three at once — authority without accountability. It is the single most common structure in this scene's abuse cases, and the reason is not mysticism, it is architecture. The honest practitioners with real traditional training are usually the easiest people in the world to ask about it; they will tell you their teacher, their years, what they do not do, and who they refer to. Precision is the tell in both directions.

> 🖼️ **[INSERT IMAGE HERE: `images/heal/check-card.png`]** — hold this on screen with the check question and letter typed in; you can shorten the spoken line below to just naming the check instead of reading the full quote if you want the runtime down.

Here's your check. The E check: “Can I name this person's training, their limits, and who they answer to?” — Three blanks is not a lineage. It is a costume.

Here's the part that's hard to hear. Unfalsifiable Is The Feature. Pay attention to what the claim is built out of: every element is unfalsifiable on purpose. Far away, long ago, orally transmitted, teacher deceased, tradition that does not certify. A person inventing a credential does not pick a claim you could check, and the ones who pick unfalsifiable claims are not being humble about the ineffable — they are engineering a position where doubting them is a spiritual failing rather than due diligence. That inversion is the actual product, because it is what makes the later stuff survivable: once questioning him reads as your resistance, the room will help him. Ask the second question. His temperature when you do is more informative than the answer.

Here's what you do. Ask three plain questions in a friendly voice: who trained you, what do you not do, and who would I talk to if I had a problem with a session. Ask them in front of other people. Write down what he says.

Say this, out loud. “Love the sound of that — who did you train with, and what's outside your scope? And if I had a concern afterwards, who's the person I'd raise it with?”

Here's the truth underneath it. Real practitioners answer credential questions with relief. Only a costume is insulted by them.

## Condition — how the situation gets shaped once you're in it

*Consent inside altered states, scope creep, the session that keeps extending.*

### 2. “JUST RELAX INTO IT”
*Scope Creep On The Table / The Session That Keeps Extending — clinically, boundary erosion / progressive scope violation.*

Let's talk about who does this. Booked for one thing, doing a slightly different thing by minute twenty. Narrates it as responsiveness — the body asked for it, the energy moved there. Never quite announces the change, which is how you end up somewhere you did not agree to.

Here's how it plays out. You booked a shoulder session. Forty minutes in, his hands are on your ribs and then lower and he is saying something about where the grief is held, and you are running an internal calculation about whether objecting would make you the person who does not understand the work. He has not done anything you could name. He has moved four times, and each individual move was two inches.

> 🖼️ **[INSERT IMAGE HERE: `images/heal/tells-card.png`]** — hold this on screen with that scenario's tells typed in; you can shorten the next line to "Here's what to watch for" instead of narrating every tell if you want the runtime down.

Watch for these.

- Contact moves to new areas without being named beforehand
- Changes framed as the body's request rather than a practitioner's decision
- Draping, positioning, or clothing renegotiated mid-session
- Session runs long without being re-agreed
- Objecting is pre-framed as not understanding the modality
- Each step is small enough to be deniable; the sequence is not

Here's what's really going on. Progressive boundary erosion is the documented mechanism in the overwhelming majority of bodywork and therapeutic misconduct cases, and it works because a body on a table is passive, undressed, prone, and socially committed to trusting the person touching it. Each two-inch move is individually defensible, which is the design — the practitioner is not testing whether you consent, he is testing whether you will interrupt. That is why the professional standard in every legitimate manual therapy is spoken permission before each new region, every session, regardless of history. It removes the thing being exploited, which is your reluctance to be the one who makes it weird.

> 🖼️ **[INSERT IMAGE HERE: `images/heal/check-card.png`]** — hold this on screen with the check question and letter typed in; you can shorten the spoken line below to just naming the check instead of reading the full quote if you want the runtime down.

Ask yourself this. The H check: “Did they say what they were about to do, before they did it?” — Unannounced contact is the violation, whatever it turns out to be.

Here's what's underneath all of it. They Are Testing The Interrupt, Not The Consent. Understanding this changes what you watch for. He is not building toward a moment where you agree — he is establishing that you do not interrupt, because a person who did not object at two inches is statistically less likely to object at four, and he knows it. That is also why the recovery from this is so hard afterwards: you will interrogate your own silence rather than his sequence. Put it down. The obligation was never on the person lying face-down and half-dressed to narrate the boundary. It was on the trained professional to announce the next move, and he chose not to, repeatedly, which is the finding.

Here's the play. Interrupt early and cheaply, because the first interruption is the whole thing — it costs almost nothing at minute five and everything at minute forty. Sit up. Sitting up ends the physical frame, and you can be polite from upright.

Here are the words. “Stop there — I booked shoulders, I want to stay on shoulders. — I'm going to sit up for a minute.”

Remember this. You do not owe a practitioner the benefit of the doubt while they are touching you. That is exactly the wrong moment for generosity.

### 3. “THE CLIENT WHO BOOKS YOU EVERY DAY”
*When You Are The One Being Targeted — clinically, practitioner victimization / therapeutic frame exploitation.*

Here's who you're dealing with. Books repeatedly, discloses heavily and fast, and treats the session as a relationship rather than an appointment. Sometimes genuinely in distress. Sometimes using the one structure on site where a stranger will touch you alone in a tent for an hour.

Here's the scene. Third booking in two days. He has started arriving early and staying after, and the disclosures have moved from his shoulder to his marriage to the fact that you are the only person who has ever really understood him, and today he asked whether you would have dinner, and when you said no he apologized so thoroughly that you ended up reassuring him.

> 🖼️ **[INSERT IMAGE HERE: `images/heal/tells-card.png`]** — hold this on screen with that scenario's tells typed in; you can shorten the next line to "Here's what to watch for" instead of narrating every tell if you want the runtime down.

Here's what to watch for.

- Books far more often than the presenting issue warrants
- Rapid, heavy personal disclosure aimed at building intimacy
- Arrives early, stays late, wants to be in your space outside the session
- Gifts, or paying over the rate
- Tests small boundaries and apologizes elaborately when they hold
- You have started dreading the booking and have not told anyone

Here's what's actually happening. The therapeutic frame is unusually exploitable in this direction: a practitioner is alone, often in a soft-walled space, professionally committed to warmth and non-judgement, and financially motivated to keep a repeat client. All four of those work against you. This is a known pattern in massage, bodywork and complementary practice, and the festival version is worse because there is no reception, no colleague next door, no booking system, and no employer — just you, a tent and somebody who booked an hour of unobserved access.

> 🖼️ **[INSERT IMAGE HERE: `images/heal/check-card.png`]** — hold this on screen with the check question and letter typed in; you can shorten the spoken line below to just naming the check instead of reading the full quote if you want the runtime down.

Run the check. The L check: “Is this a client, or is somebody building a relationship inside an appointment?” — Frequency, disclosure and after-time are the three that tell you.

Here's the dark reality. Your Training Is Being Used Against You. Notice which of your professional instincts are working for him. Non-judgement stops you naming it. Holding space keeps you in the tent. Not wanting to shame a client stops you saying no plainly. Needing the booking stops you cancelling. Every one of those is a good quality and all four are load-bearing for him, which is why practitioners are so slow to act on this and why by the time they do it has usually escalated. Break it with structure rather than with a judgement about him: somebody else on site knows your schedule, the tent flap stays open, and the booking pattern is capped. You do not have to decide he is dangerous to stop being alone with him.

Here's your move. Cap repeat bookings and say the cap is policy. Tell one person your schedule and have them walk past at the end of each session. Keep the space semi-open. End the after-time at the door rather than inside, and write down what happened the same day.

Here's exactly what to say. “I keep it to one session a day per person so everyone gets a slot — book you in Sunday. — I'm going to wrap it there, I've got Mira coming in. [walking out with him]”

And here's the truth. Warmth is your professional tool. It is not an obligation to be alone with somebody who is using it.

## Control — how leverage is held and used

*Dual relationships, integration dependency, silencing through spiritual framing.*

### 4. “THE MEDICINE CHOSE THIS”
*Spiritual Framing As A Consent Override — clinically, ceremonial coercion / consent override in altered states.*

Here's who this is. Holds the container, poured the cup, and is the only person in the room who is not altered. Interprets what is happening for everyone, including for you, including about you. Is never wrong inside the frame he built.

Picture this. You are four hours into something and the room has stopped having edges. He sits down close and says the medicine has shown him that you and he are meant to work together more deeply, that your resistance is the thing the work is for, and that this is the part where people either open or run. You cannot currently construct a sentence. He knows that. That is the reason this conversation is happening now and not on Tuesday.

> 🖼️ **[INSERT IMAGE HERE: `images/heal/tells-card.png`]** — hold this on screen with that scenario's tells typed in; you can shorten the next line to "Here's what to watch for" instead of narrating every tell if you want the runtime down.

Here are the signs.

- The pitch arrives while you are altered and not before
- Your hesitation is renamed as resistance, fear, or ego
- He is the sole interpreter of what the experience means
- Pre-ceremony agreements about touch are revised mid-ceremony
- Nobody sober is in the room who is not him
- Leaving is framed as spiritually dangerous or as abandoning the work

Here's the mechanism underneath it. This is the single most important line in this guide: a consent given before an altered state cannot cover what happens inside it, and a consent given during one is not consent, because capacity is the thing the substance removed. Every serious ceremonial ethic in the world states it — no romantic or sexual contact with participants, no revision of agreements mid-state, a sober second person present. The reason is not squeamishness, it is that the facilitator holds total interpretive authority over someone who cannot currently evaluate interpretations. Any framework where your objection is proof the framework is working is not a spiritual technology. It is a closed loop, and closed loops are what this book calls a cage.

> 🖼️ **[INSERT IMAGE HERE: `images/heal/check-card.png`]** — hold this on screen with the check question and letter typed in; you can shorten the spoken line below to just naming the check instead of reading the full quote if you want the runtime down.

Here's your check. The A check: “Is this being asked of me now because now is when I cannot assess it?” — Timing is the tell. A real invitation survives Tuesday.

Here's the part that's hard to hear. The Loop Where Your No Is Evidence. The structure to name is the unfalsifiable loop: agreement proves the medicine is working, and refusal proves you have more work to do. Once that loop is running there is no answer you can give that exits it, which means the conversation was never a request. It is the same architecture as the unverifiable lineage on the first page of this guide and it is built by the same person for the same reason. This is also where the room turns — other participants, sincerely, will help, because they are inside the frame too and your resistance genuinely looks to them like fear. That is why the protection has to be structural and installed beforehand: a sober ally physically present, an agreement in writing, and someone outside the ceremony expecting to hear from you.

Here's what you do. Set it before you sit down, in writing, and give a copy to someone not in the room: no touch beyond what is named, no agreements revised during, and a sober person of your choosing present. If you are already in it — you are allowed to say nothing, stand up, and walk to the sober person. You do not have to win the argument.

Say this, out loud. “Before we start: nothing gets renegotiated once I've drunk. Anything you want to propose, propose it on Tuesday. — [in the moment] I'm not discussing this now. I'm going to sit with Nadia.”

Here's the truth underneath it. Anything that is only true while you are altered is not true. It is just unopposed.

### 5. “I'LL WALK YOU THROUGH INTEGRATION”
*The Integrator / Dependency After The Fact — clinically, dual relationship / post-session dependency.*

Let's talk about who does this. The same person who facilitated, now offering to be the one who helps you make sense of it — for a fee, or for closeness, or both. Often kind. Almost always the wrong person for the job.

Here's how it plays out. The ceremony was enormous and you are not okay and he is the only person who was there and understands. He offers weekly calls. Then he offers to have you stay. Then there is a retreat he thinks you should be at, and a role in his organization, and at no point was there a moment where you decided any of this — there was a moment where you needed help and he was standing there.

> 🖼️ **[INSERT IMAGE HERE: `images/heal/tells-card.png`]** — hold this on screen with that scenario's tells typed in; you can shorten the next line to "Here's what to watch for" instead of narrating every tell if you want the runtime down.

Watch for these.

- The facilitator is also the integrator, the landlord, the employer, or the partner
- Your understanding of your own experience routes entirely through him
- Cost escalates — money, time, proximity, loyalty
- He discourages outside therapy, framed as it not understanding the work
- Leaving the relationship would mean losing your sense of what happened to you
- Nobody independent has ever spoken to you about that ceremony

Here's what's really going on. Dual relationships are prohibited in every clinical code for one reason: each additional role removes an exit. When your facilitator is also your integrator, you cannot process a bad experience of him with anyone but him, and a person's interpretation of their own most vulnerable hours becomes something another person authors. Post-ceremonial suggestibility is real and elevated for a meaningful period afterwards, which makes that window the highest-leverage one that exists. An honest practitioner treats it as the most dangerous part of their work and refers out. That referral is the single clearest marker of a real one.

> 🖼️ **[INSERT IMAGE HERE: `images/heal/check-card.png`]** — hold this on screen with the check question and letter typed in; you can shorten the spoken line below to just naming the check instead of reading the full quote if you want the runtime down.

Ask yourself this. The L check: “How many roles does this person hold in my life?” — One is a relationship. Three is an enclosure.

Here's what's underneath all of it. Who Authors What Happened To You. The lasting damage here is not financial and it is not even always sexual — it is authorship. If the only person who can tell you what your ceremony meant is the person who ran it, then a harm that occurred inside it is uninterpretable to you without his help, and he will help. People come out of these arrangements years later describing the disorientation of realizing they had no independent memory of their own experience, only his account of it. The counter is boringly practical and it has to be arranged in advance, while you still think you will not need it: a separate integrator, booked before the ceremony, who has never met the facilitator.

Here's the play. Separate the roles before you go in — book your integration with someone unconnected, ideally a licensed therapist familiar with this territory. Keep one relationship outside the whole ecosystem. If you are already inside it, the first move is one outside conversation, not a confrontation.

Here are the words. “I've got integration booked with someone separate — I keep those roles apart on principle. Can you send me your notes for them?”

Remember this. Nobody should be both the person who took you in and the only person who can bring you back.

### 6. “WE CAN JUST TRADE”
*Barter, And The Deal That Stops Being A Deal — clinically, undefined exchange / boundary ambiguity in barter.*

Here's who you're dealing with. Everyone, at every festival, because the gift economy is real and beautiful and almost entirely undocumented — which means every trade has terms nobody said out loud.

Here's the scene. You will do a session and he will sort you out for the weekend. It is a fair trade and you both meant it kindly. Then it is unclear whether one session covered it or three, and he keeps appearing, and on Saturday when you say you are done for the day there is a silence, and you realize that neither of you ever said what the deal was and he has been keeping a different tally.

> 🖼️ **[INSERT IMAGE HERE: `images/heal/tells-card.png`]** — hold this on screen with that scenario's tells typed in; you can shorten the next line to "Here's what to watch for" instead of narrating every tell if you want the runtime down.

Here's what to watch for.

- A trade agreed in a sentence, with no scope and no end point
- Value on both sides assumed rather than stated
- The other party's side arrives first, creating an open obligation
- Being traded substances, which ties your work to a supply
- The deal is renegotiated afterwards, upward
- You are doing sessions you do not want to do and cannot say why

Here's what's actually happening. Barter is not the problem — vagueness is. An undefined trade creates exactly the open-ended obligation that the reciprocity pages elsewhere in this book describe, with the additional feature that professional work is now entangled with a personal relationship and nobody can point to the terms. Trading for substances is the version worth refusing outright: it makes your practice dependent on a supply and a supplier, it is the clearest route to a dual relationship, and it converts a professional service into something you can be leaned on for.

> 🖼️ **[INSERT IMAGE HERE: `images/heal/check-card.png`]** — hold this on screen with the check question and letter typed in; you can shorten the spoken line below to just naming the check instead of reading the full quote if you want the runtime down.

Run the check. The L check: “Does this trade have a stated scope and an end?” — If not, it is not a trade, it is an account somebody else is keeping.

Here's the dark reality. An Open Account Is A Hold. The damage is not the specific trade, it is the self-editing that starts once you believe you owe somebody something you cannot quantify. You do the extra session. You do not raise the thing that annoyed you. You keep the appointment you wanted to cancel. That is the same quiet accommodation the gift-with-a-ledger pages describe, and in a practitioner it is worse, because the currency you are paying in is unobserved physical access to your own time and body. Close the account explicitly, even at the cost of seeming transactional in a scene that prizes not being.

Here's your move. Say the terms out loud and keep them small and closed — one session for one named thing, done, settled. Never trade for substances. Write the trade in a message so it exists. If an account has already opened, close it deliberately rather than letting it run.

Here's exactly what to say. “Happy to trade — one session for the [x], and then we're square, yeah? Just so neither of us is keeping score. — I don't trade for anything you consume, but I'll do you a mate's rate.”

And here's the truth. A trade with no end point is not generosity, it is an open account. Say the number, even when it feels unspiritual.

## Tools — practical structures, scripts and kit

*The spoken consent brief, scope limits, referral and insurance discipline.*

### 7. “THE SPOKEN CONSENT BRIEF”
*The Ninety Seconds Before Any Hands — clinically, informed consent protocol / session framing.*

Here's who this is. For practitioners, and for anyone about to get on a table, into a container, or under someone's hands. Ninety seconds, said out loud, every session, including with the client you have seen forty times.

Picture this. The brief is the thing that makes the rest of this guide unnecessary. It is also the thing that gets skipped first, because it feels clinical and the work is supposed to feel sacred — and that feeling is precisely what every pattern in this guide has been exploiting. Said once, it converts an unspoken understanding into a shared agreement, and unspoken understandings are the medium predators swim in.

> 🖼️ **[INSERT IMAGE HERE: `images/heal/tells-card.png`]** — hold this on screen with that scenario's tells typed in; you can shorten the next line to "Here's what to watch for" instead of narrating every tell if you want the runtime down.

Here are the signs.

- Name the modality and what it actually involves, in plain words
- Name every area you will work on, and what stays covered
- Name what is outside your scope and who you refer to
- Say how long, and that any extension gets re-agreed out loud
- Say that stop ends it instantly, with no explanation owed and no cost
- Say who they can raise a concern with who is not you — and mean it

Here's the mechanism underneath it. Every documented mechanism in this guide — scope creep, unannounced contact, sessions that extend, agreements revised mid-state — fails against a brief that was given out loud beforehand. Not because a bad practitioner will not try, but because the client now has a stated baseline to notice the departure from, and noticing is the whole difficulty. This is the same logic as the pre-burn check in F.L.O.W. and the access plan in A.C.C.E.S.S.: front-load the decisions to a moment when everyone is clear-headed, so the moment of pressure only requires recognition rather than judgment.

> 🖼️ **[INSERT IMAGE HERE: `images/heal/check-card.png`]** — hold this on screen with the check question and letter typed in; you can shorten the spoken line below to just naming the check instead of reading the full quote if you want the runtime down.

Here's your check. The H check: “Was the brief given out loud this session — not implied, not last time?” — If it was skipped, that is the first departure, and it happened before anything else did.

Here's the part that's hard to hear. Who Objects To Ninety Seconds. There is diagnostic value in offering it. Honest practitioners are glad of the brief — it protects them as much as the client, and most will tell you it has saved them from a misunderstanding at some point. A practitioner who finds it cold, clinical, unnecessary between people who trust each other, or contrary to the flow of the work is telling you that they prefer the ambiguity, and ambiguity has exactly one beneficiary in a room where one person is dressed and standing. You are allowed to ask for it as a client. The answer you get is worth more than the ninety seconds.

Here's what you do. Practitioners: write yours, six lines, and say it every time until it is automatic. Clients: if nobody gives you one, ask — what will you be working on, what stays covered, how long, and who do I talk to if something feels off.

Say this, out loud. “Before we start: shoulders and upper back only, everything else stays covered, fifty minutes, and if I want to go anywhere else I'll ask you first. Stop ends it immediately, no explanation needed. And if anything sits badly afterwards, here's who you can talk to who isn't me.”

Here's the truth underneath it. Ninety seconds of plain speech removes most of what is in this guide. That is why the people in this guide skip it.

## Accountability — what you owe when the power is yours

*Being a practitioner people can safely report, and what you owe when it is you.*

### 8. “WHEN THE PRACTITIONER IS YOU”
*The Page For Your Own Practice — clinically, practitioner accountability / power differential awareness.*

Let's talk about who does this. You. Honest, well-trained, in this for the right reasons, and holding more power in every session than the frame of healing makes it easy to notice.

Here's how it plays out. A client tells you, three weeks later, that something in a session did not sit right. Your entire body wants to explain — what you actually did, what the modality is, what they may have misread. That impulse is normal, it is human, and it is the exact moment where good practitioners become bad ones. What you do in the next sixty seconds is your whole ethics, and it is not a feeling, it is a procedure.

> 🖼️ **[INSERT IMAGE HERE: `images/heal/tells-card.png`]** — hold this on screen with that scenario's tells typed in; you can shorten the next line to "Here's what to watch for" instead of narrating every tell if you want the runtime down.

Watch for these.

- You are the only one who could say what happened in that room
- Your client was undressed, prone, altered, or grieving — sometimes all four
- Attraction to a client has arisen and you have not told a supervisor
- You have extended a session, or a role, because it felt right in the moment
- You have no supervisor, no insurance, and no complaints route
- Your first instinct on hearing a concern is to explain rather than to hear

Here's what's really going on. The power differential in this work does not switch off because your intentions are good. You control the room, the interpretation, the duration, and the narrative afterwards, and your client is frequently in a state they cannot fully assess. That is why every regulated profession requires supervision, insurance, a scope statement, and a complaints route that does not run through you — not because practitioners are assumed to be predators, but because an unsupervised practitioner cannot self-detect drift. The false shaman this guide opens with relies on the entire field being unregulated. The way honest practitioners take that cover away is by visibly having the things he cannot have.

> 🖼️ **[INSERT IMAGE HERE: `images/heal/check-card.png`]** — hold this on screen with the check question and letter typed in; you can shorten the spoken line below to just naming the check instead of reading the full quote if you want the runtime down.

Ask yourself this. The L check: “If a client wanted to complain about me, could they — to someone who is not me?” — If not, you are running the same structure as the person you would never want to be.

Here's what's underneath all of it. Nobody Decides To Become That Person. Read the misconduct literature and the pattern is remarkably consistent: almost nobody sets out to do it. It goes one session long, then one region unannounced, then one dual relationship, then a rationalization that the work required it — and each step was taken by somebody who believed themselves to be a good practitioner, because they were, right up until they were not. Supervision exists because that drift is invisible from inside. If you are reading this and one specific session came to mind: that is not proof you are a bad practitioner, it is the ordinary early warning this whole page is for. Take it to a supervisor this week. The ones who never take anything to anyone are the ones this ends badly for.

Here's the play. Build what the false ones cannot fake: insurance, a named supervisor you actually use, a written scope with limits, a spoken consent brief every session, and a complaints route that bypasses you — published. If a concern comes to you, the first response is thank you for telling me, and the second is a referral to that route. Not an explanation.

Here are the words. “Thank you for telling me — that took something. I'm not going to defend myself at you. Here's the person you can take it to independently, and I'll cooperate with whatever they ask.”

Remember this. The practitioner people can safely complain about is the one worth going to. Be the one with a route that does not run through you.

## Emergency — what to do when it's already happening

*What to do when it is already happening.*

### 9. “SOMETHING BIGGER JUST OPENED UP”
*A Crisis Arrives On Your Table / Knowing Your Own Edge — clinically, scope of practice / crisis recognition and referral.*

Here's who you're dealing with. A client who came in for tension and is now in the middle of something much larger — a flashback, a panic attack, a disclosure of abuse, or a psychiatric emergency — in your tent, on your table, with you.

Here's the scene. Bodywork opened something and she is not in the room any more. She is shaking, breathing badly, somewhere in her own history, and telling you something she has clearly never said out loud. You are a massage therapist. You have ninety seconds to decide what you are and are not going to try to do, and every instinct you have is pulling you toward staying with it.

> 🖼️ **[INSERT IMAGE HERE: `images/heal/tells-card.png`]** — hold this on screen with that scenario's tells typed in; you can shorten the next line to "Here's what to watch for" instead of narrating every tell if you want the runtime down.

Here's what to watch for.

- Dissociation, flashback, or panic on the table
- A disclosure of abuse, self-harm or suicidal thinking
- Signs of a psychiatric emergency rather than emotional release
- You are considering doing something you were never trained to do
- The session has run long because stopping feels like abandonment
- You have no referral route and did not find one before the event

Here's what's actually happening. Bodywork and altered-state contexts surface trauma routinely, and the single most consequential skill a practitioner has is knowing where their own edge is. Attempting to process trauma without that training can deepen it, and the moment where it feels most compassionate to continue is the moment it is most likely to do harm. The competent response is small and unglamorous: stabilize, ground, do not excavate, and hand over to someone whose job it is. Every large festival has welfare, and most have psychedelic support services staffed by people trained for precisely this.

Run the check. The E check: “Am I still inside what I was trained to do?” — The honest answer arrives fast, and it is usually no, and no is the correct place to stop.

Here's the dark reality. Staying Is Not Always The Kind Thing. The mistake almost never looks like negligence — it looks like a caring practitioner who did not want to abandon somebody mid-crisis, and who therefore attempted trauma work they had no training for, in a tent, on a stranger, at a festival. That is how people are left more destabilized than they arrived, and it is committed by good practitioners with good intentions every season. Handing over is not abandonment, and it is worth saying out loud to the client that it is not, because they will experience it that way if you do not. Walk them there. Do not send them.

Here's your move. Stop the bodywork. Ground them — upright, feet on the floor, breathing, orient to the room and the day. Do not ask for more detail and do not interpret. Then walk them to welfare or the psychedelic support tent yourself, and debrief afterwards with your own person.

Here's exactly what to say. “I'm going to stop the massage. Feet on the floor, sit up with me, look at the doorway. You're at a festival, it's Saturday afternoon, you're safe. — This is bigger than what I'm trained for, and that's not me leaving you. I'm going to walk over to welfare with you and stay while you get someone who does this properly.”

And here's the truth. Knowing where your training stops is your training. Walking somebody to the right person is competence, not failure.
