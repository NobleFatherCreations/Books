# “WHAT GOOD LEADERSHIP SOUNDS LIKE”
*Using Your Platform for Actual Safety — clinically, platform responsibility / bystander activation.*

Here's who you're dealing with. If you have a stage, you have a microphone. If you have a microphone, you have an opportunity to make the space safer for everyone in it. This page is about using that opportunity.

Here's the scene. You're two songs into your set and you can feel the energy of the crowd — fully in it, totally open. You have their complete attention. A lot can happen in that crowd tonight. Some of it will be beautiful. Some of it won't be. You have an actual ability to influence which way it goes. The question is whether you choose to use it.

Watch for these signs.

- Stating consent expectations from the stage normalizes them in the crowd
- Pointing to Harm Reduction resources from the stage reduces the stigma of using them
- Calling out behavior you see from the stage carries enormous weight
- Creating moments of grounding and community check-in is part of what great performers do

Here's what's actually happening. Artists with stage platforms have a specific ability to shape the culture of a crowd that most people in that crowd don't have. Stating consent expectations, pointing to safety resources, calling out behavior you see — these are not political statements. They are basic community care that is within your power to provide. The artists who do this are often the ones whose shows feel different — where people feel safer to be fully present.

Run the check. The N check: “What does my platform allow me to contribute to the safety of this community right now?”

Here's the dark reality. From Stage — What To Actually Say. Simple, real, effective stage interventions: "Before we go any further — look around you. Everyone here deserves to have a great time. If you see something that doesn't look right, say something." "If you need support tonight, Harm Reduction is [location]. No judgment, no questions, just help." "Hands to yourself unless someone has specifically invited them — and if you're not sure, ask." These take 20 seconds. They change the energy of a room. They make the artists who say them more trustworthy, not less cool.

Here's your move. Use your platform. Even one sentence from the stage can change someone's night.

Here's exactly what to say. “[From stage]: "Before we get into this — look out for each other tonight. Harm Reduction is [location], no questions asked. Hands to yourself unless someone said yes. Now let's go."”

And here's the truth. Your platform is a privilege. Using it for community safety is one of the most powerful things you can do with it.
