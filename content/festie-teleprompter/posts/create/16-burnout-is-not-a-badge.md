# “BURNOUT IS NOT A BADGE”
*Over-Giving as a Control Dynamic — clinically, compassion fatigue / boundary erosion.*

Here's who you're dealing with. The pressure to create publicly, generously, and constantly — for the community, for the vibe, for the love of it — while ignoring the reality that your creative capacity is finite and the constant demands on it are not always coming from a neutral place.

Here's the scene. You came to the festival to create and you've made twelve pieces, done two free workshops, gifted four finished works, painted a wall for the camp, contributed to a collab you didn't really want to be part of, and helped three other artists with their setups. It's Sunday and you're empty. You feel proud and resentful simultaneously. You gave everything and you're not sure you gave it to the right people or the right places or even because you wanted to.

Watch for these signs.

- Requests for your creative labor accumulate until you can't find your own work in your schedule
- "For the community" is used to override your "I need to stop now"
- Your giving is received as a baseline expectation rather than a choice
- You feel proud and resentful simultaneously — the resentment is information

Here's what's actually happening. Giving is a value in festival culture and creative communities. The shadow side is that it can be systematically exploited — where one person's generosity is treated as an extractable resource rather than a chosen gift. When the word "no" starts to feel selfish inside a community that calls itself loving, that community is using your values against you. Resentment is the signal that giving has crossed from chosen to coerced.

Run the check. The E check: “Enough is enough — am I still giving from abundance or am I giving from depletion?”

Here's the dark reality. What Sovereign Creative Giving Looks Like. Sovereign giving is giving from fullness — you have something to give, you choose to give it, and the giving leaves you replenished rather than depleted. The test: do you feel genuinely good after giving, or do you feel relief that the request has been satisfied? Relief is not generosity. Relief is appeasement. You are allowed to say: "I'm not available for this right now." You are allowed to charge for your time. You are allowed to choose what you make and for whom. These are not failures of community. They are the conditions that make sustainable community possible.

Here's your move. Give from fullness. Stop when you're depleted. Resentment is information, not ingratitude.

Here's exactly what to say. “I'm not available for any more projects this weekend — I need to be protective of my own creative energy.”

And here's the truth. Your creative capacity is finite. Treating it like an unlimited public resource is not community. It's extraction.
