# “PAINT FOR FREE, GET FAMOUS”
*The Exposure Promoter — clinically, reciprocity trap / future faking.*

Here's who you're dealing with. The promoter, organizer, or influencer who wants your art for their event and offers exposure instead of payment. The opportunity sounds real. The exposure rarely materializes. The art is already on their wall.

Here's the scene. "We can't pay for the live painting but your work will be seen by 20,000 people this weekend and we'll tag you in everything — this could really launch you." You spend 16 hours creating something extraordinary on their main stage wall. The photos go everywhere — 40,000 likes. You get tagged in two of them. The painting stays on their wall. You leave with nothing except the work experience, which you already had.

Watch for these signs.

- Payment replaced with "exposure," "experience," or future opportunity — all unquantifiable
- The beneficiary of your labor is clearly defined; your benefit is vague and future-tense
- No written agreement about tagging, credit, reproduction rights, or ownership
- The ask comes under time pressure — event is this weekend, they need an answer now

Here's what's actually happening. Exposure is a real form of value in some contexts. It is also one of the most systematically used tools to extract free labor from artists. The test: would this person pay a photographer, sound engineer, or caterer in "exposure"? No — because those are recognized as labor. Live painting is also labor. The festival ecosystem has a long history of treating visual artists as ambient decoration. You are not decoration.

Run the check. The C check: “Is there a written agreement — or just a verbal promise that evaporates after the event?”

Here's the dark reality. The Live Painting Minimum Contract. Every engagement should have in writing: your fee or explicit agreement that this is donated (your choice, not their default), who owns the finished piece, photo/video rights and credit requirements, whether they can reproduce commercially, and what happens if they cancel. Volunteer Lawyers for the Arts (vlany.org) offers free legal help. A legitimate organizer will sign it. The ones who won't are telling you everything.

Here's your move. Know your rate before every conversation. Exposure is a supplement, not a replacement.

Here's exactly what to say. “I'd love to be involved — my rate for a live painting is [X]. What's your budget?”

And here's the truth. Your art is your labor. Your labor has a price. You get to know what it is.
