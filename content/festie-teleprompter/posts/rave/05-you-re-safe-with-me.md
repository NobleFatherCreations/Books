# “YOU'RE SAFE WITH ME”
*The Guardian Who Isn't / Exploiting Overwhelm — clinically, rescue hook / vulnerability targeting.*

Here's who you're dealing with. The person who appears exactly when you're most overwhelmed, most lost, most in need of an anchor. Positions themselves as your safe harbor. Uses the relief you feel at being found to establish proximity and trust that then gets leveraged.

Here's the scene. You got separated from your group Saturday night and your phone died. You're in the wrong part of the festival and you don't know how to get back. He appears — calm, oriented, kind. He walks you somewhere warm, gets you water, sits with you while your portable charger works. An hour later, when he suggests you stay in his camp rather than find your friends, the relief and gratitude are still running the show.

Watch for these signs.

- Appears at peak vulnerability: separated, lost, depleted, frightened
- Genuine help arrives first — establishing debt before asks are made
- Relief and gratitude are still active when the actual ask comes
- The suggested next step keeps you in their orbit rather than returning you to yours

Here's what's actually happening. The rescue hook targets the specific window when you're most resource-depleted and least capable of independent assessment. The help is often genuinely real — which makes the subsequent leverage feel confusing. Gratitude is a genuine human response to being helped. It is not a binding contract. You can thank someone sincerely for real help and still choose to go back to your own people afterward.

Run the check. The V check: “Does this person’s help return me to my own people and navigation — or keep me needing them for the next thing too?” — real help ends. It doesn’t renew itself.

Here's the dark reality. Dead Phone = Highest Risk Window. A dead phone at a festival is one of the most dangerous situations for a first-timer. Before you're ever in this situation: carry a portable charger and keep it topped up. Write your camp location and two friends' numbers in ink on your arm before each day. Know your camp by a physical landmark — not just GPS. Establish a meeting spot your whole group knows. "If we get separated, meet at the big wooden arch at midnight." These are not paranoid. They are just good festie practice.

Here's your move. Accept real help gratefully. Return to your own people as soon as you're able. Gratitude is not a contract.

Here's exactly what to say. “Thank you so much — that was genuinely kind. I'm going to try to find my camp now. Can you point me toward the Rangers?”

And here's the truth. Real help returns you to independence. Watch for that.
