# “BUYING AND SELLING AT YOUR FIRST FEST”
*Legal Risk, Undercovers, and How to Navigate Safely — clinically, legal risk awareness / undercover operations.*

Here's who you're dealing with. Transactions at festivals — tickets, substances, goods, services — carry real legal risk that first-timers often underestimate. Undercover officers work festivals and look exactly like attendees. This page is about navigating that with eyes open.

Here's the scene. He's got full kandi, wristbands up both arms, knows the lineup, vibing completely. He finds you and asks if you're looking for anything. He seems like just another attendee. He has the energy of someone who belongs here completely. Something about the sequence of the conversation — the way he guides it, the specificity of his questions — feels slightly scripted. You can't articulate why. But something is off.

Watch for these signs.

- They approach YOU rather than you finding them through organic conversation
- Unusual interest in quantities or whether you know "anyone else who might be interested"
- Wants to meet somewhere private or away from the crowd for any transaction
- Something about the interaction feels slightly rehearsed — you can't put your finger on it

Here's what's actually happening. Undercover officers at festivals are specifically trained to blend in and they're often very good at it. The tells are in the transaction pattern itself: they initiate, they guide toward isolation, they want specificity about quantities, they push pace. You are never obligated to complete any transaction. "Actually I'm good" ends it without incident. Never follow anyone to an isolated location for any reason.

Run the check. The A check: “Am I proceeding with this because I actually want to — or because saying no feels awkward?”

Here's the dark reality. If You're Approached By Police Or Detained. Know this before you arrive: (1) You have the right to remain silent — use it. "I'd like to speak with a lawyer" is the only thing you say. (2) You can ask if you're being detained or free to go — if free to go, go. (3) You can decline to consent to a search. (4) Do not physically resist any police interaction. (5) Write down badge numbers as soon as possible. Good Samaritan laws protect people seeking help for overdose in most US states — always call for medical help in an overdose situation.

Here's your move. "Actually I'm good" ends any transaction. Walk toward people. Never go somewhere isolated.

Here's exactly what to say. “"Actually I'm good" — then walk. No elaboration. No explanation.”

And here's the truth. No transaction at a festival is worth your freedom.
