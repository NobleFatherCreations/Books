# “IF THE CROWD GETS TOO TIGHT”
*Crowd Crush / What To Actually Do Inside One — clinically, compressive asphyxia / crowd density emergency.*

Here's who you're dealing with. Nobody. This is physics, and it is the most lethal thing that happens at music events, and almost nobody in a crowd knows the three things that keep them alive in one.

Here's the scene. You are forty rows back and the crowd has stopped feeling like people. You are being moved without walking. Your arms are pinned. There is a wave that goes through from behind and you feel your feet leave the ground briefly, and you cannot get your chest to expand fully on the in-breath.

Watch for these signs.

- You are being carried by the crowd rather than choosing your steps
- You cannot raise your arms or take a full breath
- Waves of pressure travelling through the crowd rather than individual movement
- You are shoulder-to-shoulder on all four sides with no gaps at all
- People near you have gone quiet
- You cannot see a gap, a barrier gate, or an edge

Here's what's actually happening. Crowd deaths are compressive asphyxia, not trampling: the pressure of bodies prevents the chest from expanding and a person suffocates upright, frequently while nobody around them realizes what is happening. It becomes dangerous above roughly five people per square metre and the window is short. The three things that matter are simple and completely counterintuitive, which is why they have to be known in advance rather than worked out at the time.

Run the check. The S check: “Can I raise my arms and take a full breath?” — The moment the answer is no, you are in a crush and you start moving out now, not later.

Here's the dark reality. The Three Things That Keep You Breathing. Memorize these now. One: get your arms up in a boxer's guard, hands in front of your chest, elbows out — this creates the few centimetres of space in front of your ribs that lets you keep breathing, and it is the single highest-value action. Two: do not push, do not fight the wave, and above all do not bend down for anything, including a phone or a person — going down in a dense crowd is how people do not come back up, and you cannot help anyone from the floor. Three: move diagonally and with the surges, not against them, using the moments of give to gain half a metre toward an edge; going against a crowd achieves nothing and exhausts you. And if somebody goes down near you, shout, and make a wall with the people around you rather than bending into the gap alone.

Here's your move. Arms up in a guard, protect your chest. Stay upright at all costs. Move diagonally toward an edge during the moments of slack. Shout if somebody goes down and form a wall with the people beside you. If you can, leave before it gets to this — density builds visibly for minutes beforehand.

Here's exactly what to say. “Arms up! — Somebody's down, make a wall! Don't bend, hold the space! — Back this way, move with it, not against it.”

And here's the truth. People suffocate standing up, and it takes minutes. Arms up, stay upright, move diagonally with the wave.
