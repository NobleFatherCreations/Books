# “GETTING HOME IS PART OF THE FESTIVAL”
*The Monday Drive / The Most Dangerous Part Of The Weekend — clinically, fatigue-impaired driving / residual impairment.*

Here's who you're dealing with. Everybody, on the Monday, in a car park of eleven thousand vehicles all being driven by people who have had four hours' sleep a night since Thursday.

Here's the scene. It is packed up, it is ten in the morning, and everyone is driving home. You feel alright. You had a big weekend, you slept badly in a tent for four nights, you may still have something in your system, and you are about to do three hours on a motorway with three exhausted friends asleep in the car.

Watch for these signs.

- Under five hours of sleep per night across the whole weekend
- Substances used within the last twenty-four hours — comedowns impair driving
- Still over the limit from the night before, which is very common
- Everybody in the car is asleep, so nobody is watching the driver
- A long motorway stretch in the early afternoon dip
- “I'll stop if I feel tired”, which is not how microsleeps work

Here's what's actually happening. The drive home is statistically the most dangerous part of a festival weekend and it gets no attention at all because the festival feels over. Three impairments stack: cumulative sleep debt from four nights in a tent, residual substances or a comedown, and in a large number of cases still being over the drink-drive limit the following morning. Fatigue impairment is comparable to alcohol impairment, and the microsleep is the specific killer — a few seconds of sleep that the driver does not know occurred.

Run the check. The E check: “How much have I actually slept since Thursday, and what is still in me?” — Answer honestly, out loud, to somebody else in the car.

Here's the dark reality. Sleep First, Then Drive. The safest thing available is completely free and almost nobody does it: sleep on site on the Monday morning and leave in the afternoon, or in the evening, or on Tuesday. Most events will let you, the car park empties, and you arrive alive. If you must go, make it a rule that the driver has slept and is the one nobody lets stay up on the Sunday — appoint them on Thursday, like a designated driver, and protect their sleep as a group. Swap every two hours, keep one passenger awake with the driver at all times, and stop the moment anybody misses an exit or cannot remember the last few miles, because that is what a microsleep feels like from the inside: nothing at all.

Here's your move. Appoint the Monday driver on Thursday and protect their sleep all weekend. Sleep before you drive, even if it costs you half a day. Swap drivers every two hours, keep one passenger awake, and stop at the first missed exit or lost mile.

Here's exactly what to say. “You're driving Monday, so you're getting a proper night on Sunday — we'll leave you to it. — I've slept about four hours a night since Thursday, I'm not driving till this afternoon.”

And here's the truth. You survived the festival. The car park on Monday is the part that has not finished with you yet.
