# “SIGNS OF A GENUINE MENTAL HEALTH CRISIS”
*When It Is More Than a Difficult Trip — clinically, mental health crisis / psychiatric emergency.*

Here's who you're dealing with. Festival environments can precipitate genuine mental health crises in people who may have underlying vulnerabilities — the combination of sleep deprivation, substances, emotional intensity, and social pressure can tip someone into a state that requires more than peer support.

Here's the scene. Your campmate has been quiet and withdrawn since Saturday morning. He came out once, said he was fine, went back in. It is Sunday and you have knocked twice and he said he was okay but something in his voice did not match the word. He has not eaten anything you know of. He made a comment last night about not being sure why he came. You are sitting with the question of whether to push harder to check on him. Push harder.

Watch for these signs.

- Withdrawal from the festival for an extended period when they came to engage
- Saying they are fine in a way that does not match their voice, energy, or behavior
- Comments that sound like they feel hopeless, purposeless, or like they should not have come
- Not eating, not sleeping, not coming out — for longer than exhaustion alone explains

Here's what's actually happening. Mental health crises at festivals are underreported and underidentified because the expectation is that everyone is having a good time and should be having a good time. The signs can be subtle and easy to attribute to tiredness or a difficult trip. If your gut says something is wrong with someone in your group — check on them again. Ask directly. "Are you actually okay — because I am asking for real." The question asked with genuine care and real eye contact is a different question than the passing "you okay?"

Run the check. The S check: “What is my gut actually telling me about this person — and am I acting on it?”

Here's the dark reality. Crisis Resources At Festivals. Zendo Project (zendoproject.org): difficult psychedelic experiences and mental health crisis — present at many major festivals. Festival medical tents: handle mental health crises as well as physical ones. 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline: call or text 988, 24/7, trained crisis counselors. Crisis Text Line: text HOME to 741741 — useful when a voice call feels too hard. If someone says anything that sounds like they are thinking about hurting themselves: take it seriously, stay with them, get help. "I am just going to make sure you are okay — come on, let us go find somewhere quiet together."

Here's your move. Knock again. Ask directly. Stay with them. Get help if needed. Do not normalize what your gut is flagging.

Here's exactly what to say. “I am asking for real — are you actually okay? Because I am not going anywhere until I know.”

And here's the truth. Checking on your people is not being dramatic. It is being a real friend.
