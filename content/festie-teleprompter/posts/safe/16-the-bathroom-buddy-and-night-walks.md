# “THE BATHROOM BUDDY AND NIGHT WALKS”
*Simple Habits That Significantly Reduce Risk — clinically, personal safety / environmental risk reduction.*

Here's who you're dealing with. Some of the simplest safety habits in festival culture are also the most consistently overlooked — especially as the weekend progresses and everyone gets more comfortable and more tired. These are the ones worth making non-negotiable.

Here's the scene. It is 3am Saturday. You need the bathroom and your campmate is finally asleep and your buddy is at a set you did not want to see. The walk to the bathroom is maybe four minutes through a part of the festival that is less busy at this hour. You almost go alone. The almost is the moment. The almost is where the simple habit matters.

Watch for these signs.

- Bathroom buddy rule: someone walks with you to the bathroom after dark, every time, no exceptions
- Walking each other back to tents after late sets — do not let altered friends navigate alone in the dark
- Phone charging as safety infrastructure — share chargers freely, a dead phone is a vulnerability
- The quiet check-in: "you good?" before splitting up is two seconds and it matters

Here's what's actually happening. Most adverse incidents at festivals happen in low-visibility, low-traffic situations in the later hours of the night — on walks between stages, in bathroom queues, in the paths between camping areas. The simple habit of not being alone in those moments is one of the most effective risk reductions available. It does not require elaborate systems. It just requires the group norm that you do not let people walk alone after dark.

Run the check. The E check: “Is anyone in our group alone right now in a situation where they should not be?”

Here's the dark reality. Night Navigation Safety Basics. After dark at festivals: bring a headlamp or phone torch — being able to see and be seen is basic safety. Stay on lit paths between stages when possible. If you are going somewhere unfamiliar or less trafficked, tell your buddy where you are going and when you expect to be back. If you feel uncomfortable on a walk — trust that. Change direction, find a busier path, head toward lights and sound. Ranger patrols concentrate on darker and less trafficked areas — if you see a Ranger and feel unsafe, walk with them or near them. You do not have to explain why.

Here's your move. Bathroom buddy rule. Walk each other back. Share your charger. Two seconds to check in before splitting up.

Here's exactly what to say. “I am going to the bathroom — come with me? I will walk you back after.”

And here's the truth. The simplest habits are the ones that matter most. Make them non-negotiable in your group.
