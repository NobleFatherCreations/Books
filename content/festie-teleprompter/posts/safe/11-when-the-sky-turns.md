# “WHEN THE SKY TURNS”
*Storms, Heat And Evacuation In A Field — clinically, severe weather response / mass evacuation.*

Here's who you're dealing with. The weather, at a site made of temporary metal structures, canvas, and eighty thousand people with nowhere indoors to go.

Here's the scene. The stage goes dark mid-set and a voice says something you cannot make out over the PA, and the crowd starts to move in a direction, and it is beginning to rain properly. Nobody around you knows whether this is a five-minute pause, a storm hold, or a full evacuation, and the field is already becoming a single large moving mass.

Watch for these signs.

- Stages held or dark with no clear announcement reaching you
- Lightning visible, or thunder inside thirty seconds of a flash
- Standing water, mud, or wind moving temporary structures
- Crowds converging on one route out of an arena
- Extreme heat with no shade and long queues for water
- You do not know where your site's shelter or muster points are

Here's what's actually happening. Weather is the likeliest cause of a mass evacuation at an outdoor event and every festival has a plan for it that attendees have almost never read. The two lethal versions are lightning — a field of temporary metal structures with no substantial buildings is close to the worst possible place — and the crowd movement that an evacuation itself produces, which is where the injuries actually happen. Heat is the slower one and it kills more people than storms do, usually through people who have been dancing in sun for hours without shade or salt.

Run the check. The F check: “Do I know where my shelter is and where my camp's muster point is?” — Find both on Thursday, because there is no time to find them on Saturday.

Here's the dark reality. Vehicles, Not Tents, And Move Early. Two specifics worth having in advance. In lightning, a tent is worse than useless — it offers no protection at all and its poles are the tallest thing in a flat field. A hard-topped vehicle is genuine shelter and so is a substantial permanent building; a gazebo, a marquee and a stage structure are not. Get away from metal, from the highest ground and from lone trees, and use the thirty-second rule: if thunder follows the flash within thirty seconds, the storm is close enough to be dangerous. And in any evacuation, move early and sideways — the injuries happen in the converging crush at the main exit, so the moment you think it might be an evacuation, start walking toward a secondary route before the announcement is confirmed.

Here's your move. Find shelter options and your camp's muster point on arrival. Move at the first indication rather than at confirmation, and use secondary exits. In a storm, get to a hard-topped vehicle or a permanent building, not a tent. In heat, shade and electrolytes on a schedule rather than when thirsty.

Here's exactly what to say. “That's lightning close — we're going to the car, not the tent. Now, before everyone else does. — Muster point's the water tower if we get split up.”

And here's the truth. A tent is not shelter from lightning, it is a pole in a field. Move early and sideways, because the crush is at the main exit.
