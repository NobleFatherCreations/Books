# “THE MORNING AFTER”
*Processing Difficult Experiences Without Shame — clinically, post-incident support / trauma-informed care.*

Here's who you're dealing with. Something happened this weekend. Or you watched something happen. Or you supported someone through something. Or you are sitting with something you cannot fully name yet. This page is for Sunday — the quiet one when everything gets real.

Here's the scene. It is Sunday afternoon and the festival is winding down and you are sitting with something heavy. You are not sure if you are being dramatic. You are not sure who to tell. You are exhausted and overwhelmed and every feeling is louder than normal. Here is what is true: you do not have to have it figured out to deserve support. You do not need a perfect story. You do not need certainty about what happened or what to call it. You just need to reach out.

Watch for these signs.

- You do not need a perfect memory or perfect certainty to deserve support — reach out anyway
- Harm Reduction tents are confidential and are not required to report to law enforcement
- RAINN hotline (1-800-656-4673) is 24/7 and supports all genders and all types of experiences
- Crisis Text Line: text HOME to 741741 if talking feels too hard right now

Here's what's actually happening. Post-incident processing is hard enough without the additional weight of deciding whether your experience is bad enough to matter. It is. Whatever level of severity it sits at — it matters. You do not have to be okay by the time you load the car. You do not have to have it figured out before you talk to someone. Give yourself the first 48-72 hours for basic recovery — sleep, food, low stimulation — before trying to process fully. Then talk to someone you trust.

Run the check. The E check: “What does my body actually need right now — not what does the situation require me to perform?” — you don’t have to be okay yet. Support exists, and you deserve it.

Here's the dark reality. The Week After The Festival. Post-festival blues are real — the crash from social intensity, sleep debt, and substances can feel significant and last several days. What helps: sleep, real food, gentle movement, low stimulation, reconnecting with people you trust. What makes it harder: immediately jumping back into intensity, isolating, making major decisions in the first week. If you are carrying something heavy from the weekend: give yourself 3-5 days before trying to process it fully. If the weight does not lift after a week or two — reach out to a therapist. Psychology Today (psychologytoday.com) has a therapist finder by specialty including trauma.

Here's your move. Eat. Sleep. Tell one trusted person. Reach out to resources if you need more. You deserve support.

Here's exactly what to say. “Something happened this weekend I am still processing. I do not need you to fix it — I just need you to hear it.”

And here's the truth. You do not have to be okay yet. Support exists. You deserve it.
