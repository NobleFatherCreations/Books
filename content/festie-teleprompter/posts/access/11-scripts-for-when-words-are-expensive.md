# “SCRIPTS FOR WHEN WORDS ARE EXPENSIVE”
*Pre-Written Sentences for Low-Capacity Moments — clinically, self-advocacy scripting / cognitive load reduction.*

Here's who you're dealing with. For anyone whose speech, processing, or patience goes first when things get hard — which by Sunday is most people, and for a lot of us is Thursday. Scripts are not weakness. They are the same reason pilots read checklists out loud.

Here's the scene. Somebody is standing too close, asking something that needs a real answer, and the part of your brain that builds sentences has gone. You know what you want. You cannot currently construct it. Every second of silence makes the next one harder, and the person in front of you is reading the silence as agreement.

Watch for these signs.

- You freeze or go flat when you most need to say no
- You say yes to end an interaction and regret it inside a minute
- Your speech gets harder exactly when the stakes get higher
- You need processing time and people fill it
- Written or typed communication is easier than spoken, and nobody offers it
- You have said “it's fine” about something that was not fine, this weekend

Here's what's actually happening. Under load, generating a novel sentence is expensive and retrieving a memorized one is cheap. That difference is the entire technique. Pre-written lines survive shutdown, overload, pain, and the specific pressure of an audience, because they do not require you to compose anything — you are reading, not writing. They also remove the part that usually costs the most, which is deciding how much to explain. The scripts below explain nothing. That is deliberate: explanation invites negotiation, and negotiation is the thing you do not have the resources for right now.

Run the check. The C check: “Do I have a sentence ready, or am I going to have to build one?” — Ready is the difference between a no and a yes you did not mean.

Here's the dark reality. Silence Gets Read As Yes. The part that does real damage is not the freeze itself — it is what other people do with it. A pause while you assemble a sentence gets read as consideration, then as agreement, and somebody who wants a yes will take it and move. Afterwards it is extremely hard to explain that you did not agree, you just could not answer in time, because from the outside those look identical and the other person will say honestly that you did not object. This is why non-speaking and intermittently-speaking people are disproportionately affected, and why the counter is not courage. It is having the sentence already built, so that answering costs nothing you do not currently have.

Here's your move. Put four of these in your phone's notes, pinned, in large text, so you can hold the screen up instead of speaking if that is where you are. Practise saying two of them out loud before you travel, so your mouth already knows the shape.

Here's exactly what to say. “I need a minute before I answer. — I'm not deciding anything tonight. — No, and I'm not going to explain it. — I need you to step back, please. — Can you type it? I'm not processing speech right now.”

And here's the truth. A sentence you prepared on Tuesday is still your sentence on Saturday. Borrowing from your own calm self is not cheating.
