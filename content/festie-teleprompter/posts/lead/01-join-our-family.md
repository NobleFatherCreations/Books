# “JOIN OUR FAMILY”
*Camp Recruitment and What It Signals — clinically, manufactured belonging / dependency creation.*

Here's who you're dealing with. Camps recruit new members every season. The pitch — community, infrastructure, belonging, access — is real when the camp is genuine and a trap when the camp is extractive. Understanding which kind of camp you are running starts with honest assessment.

Here's the scene. You recruited twelve new members this season. The pitch was warm, the infrastructure is real, and most of them are having a genuinely good experience. Three of them are doing significantly more camp labor than others and seem reluctant to raise concerns. One new member has been spending most of their time with a senior camp member in ways that look more like dependency than connection. You have not looked closely at these dynamics because camp is running well overall.

Watch for these signs.

- New members doing disproportionate labor compared to senior members without discussion
- New members who seem reluctant to raise concerns or push back on anything
- Dependency dynamics forming between new members and specific senior members
- Camp culture that treats questioning the leadership as disloyalty rather than healthy input

Here's what's actually happening. Camp leaders are often the last to see problematic dynamics because they are managing the whole operation and because the overall experience is positive. The patterns to watch for in your own camp are the same ones that signal predatory structures in other contexts: unequal contribution without discussion, reluctance to raise concerns, dependency dynamics, and cultures that treat questioning as betrayal. Your camp is only as healthy as what you are willing to look at.

Run the check. The L check: “What dynamics in my camp am I avoiding looking at because things are running well overall?”

Here's the dark reality. Camp Health Self-Assessment. Honest questions to ask yourself about your camp: (1) Can any member raise a concern with me without social cost? (2) Is labor distributed equitably and has that been discussed explicitly? (3) Are there specific senior members whose relationships with new members I should look at more closely? (4) Do members feel free to leave the camp during the festival without it affecting their standing? (5) Has anyone ever pushed back on a camp decision and how was that received? (6) If I am honest, do any members seem afraid of me or of consequences from me? Your answers to these questions tell you what kind of camp you are running.

Here's your move. Look honestly at the dynamics you have been avoiding. The health of your camp depends on what you are willing to see.

Here's exactly what to say. “"I want to do a camp culture check-in before the festival — I want to hear honestly from everyone about how things are going."”

And here's the truth. You cannot protect your camp from dynamics you are not willing to look at.
