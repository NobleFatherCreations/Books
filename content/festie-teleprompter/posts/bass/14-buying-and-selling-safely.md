# “BUYING AND SELLING SAFELY”
*Legal, Otherwise, and How to Spot Undercovers — clinically, legal risk awareness / undercover operations.*

Here's who you're dealing with. Transactions at festivals — substances, goods, services, tickets — carry real legal risk. Undercover officers work festivals and look exactly like attendees. This page is about navigating that reality with eyes open.

Here's the scene. He's in full kandi, wristbands up both arms, talking knowledgeably about the lineup, vibing completely with the crowd. He finds you and opens a conversation about what you're looking for. Everything about him reads as a fellow festie. He seems genuinely connected. He asks for your number. He says to meet him near the back of the camping area in twenty minutes. Something about the sequence feels slightly scripted.

Watch for these signs.

- They approach YOU to buy or sell — not the other way around
- Unusual interest in quantities, prices, or whether you know "anyone else who's looking"
- Wants to meet somewhere private or away from the crowd to transact
- Pushes for a quick decision or seems vaguely impatient with your hesitation

Here's what's actually happening. Undercover officers at festivals are specifically trained to blend in and are often very good at it. The behavioral tells are in the transaction pattern itself, not the appearance: they initiate, they guide you toward isolation, they want specificity about quantities, and they push pace. You are never obligated to complete any transaction. "Actually I'm good" ends it without incident. Never follow anyone to an isolated location for any transaction regardless of what's being offered.

Run the check. The A check: “Who has access to my decisions in this transaction — and do I know enough about this person to be doing this at all?” — if the answer is no, the deal isn’t worth what it’s about to cost you.

Here's the dark reality. If You're Approached By Police Or Arrested. Know this before the festival: (1) You have the right to remain silent — use it. "I'd like to speak with a lawyer" is the only thing you say. (2) You can ask if you're being detained or are free to go — if free to go, go. (3) Do not consent to a search — you can decline without it being suspicious. (4) Write down badge numbers and officer names as soon as possible. (5) Contact a lawyer or the National Lawyers Guild (nlg.org) which has observer programs at many festivals. Good Samaritan laws protect people seeking overdose help in most states — use them.

Here's your move. "Actually I'm good" ends any transaction. Walk toward people. Never go somewhere isolated for any deal.

Here's exactly what to say. “"Actually I'm good" — then walk. No elaboration. No explanation. Just go.”

And here's the truth. The festival is not worth your freedom. No transaction is worth your freedom.
