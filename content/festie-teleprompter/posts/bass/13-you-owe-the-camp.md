# “YOU OWE THE CAMP”
*Camp Obligation / Manufactured Debt to the Group — clinically, reciprocity trap / group coercion.*

Here's who you're dealing with. The camp structure where housing, infrastructure, and event access are provided in exchange for vague "contribution" that expands over time to include labor, money, silence about what happens inside the group.

Here's the scene. The camp was incredible — shade structure, kitchen, generator, solid location. "We just ask that everyone contributes to community." Week 1: set up shifts. Week 2: purchasing supplies. Week 3: managing other members. Month 3: you're running operations for the camp and still paying your entry fee and you're not sure exactly how you got here, but leaving now feels like abandoning something you've built.

Watch for these signs.

- Contribution expectations vague at start and expand with each event
- Access to camp infrastructure tied to your continued compliance
- "After everything this camp has given you" appears when you express. a limit
- Camp leadership is not accountable in the same ways regular members are

Here's what's actually happening. Camp debt works the same way gifter debt does — at a larger scale with a group enforcement mechanism. The contributions feel small individually but accumulate. The belonging feels real and the infrastructure is genuinely valuable. The tell is whether the arrangement is reciprocal and defined. Real community has terms both parties can articulate. When your contributions keep expanding and the camp's contributions stay stable — you're working, not belonging.

Run the check. The S check: “When did I last actually talk to people outside this camp — my own people?”

Here's the dark reality. Calculate Your Actual Camp Cost. Write this down: (1) Entry fees and membership costs, (2) Hours of your labor at your hourly rate, (3) Cash contributions to camp expenses, (4) Value of any goods or services you provided. Compare to: what this event would cost you as an independent attendee with a solo ticket and your own setup. If the camp costs you more in total than going independently — you are paying for the privilege of working for them. Name that clearly before committing to another season.

Here's your move. Define the terms before you commit. Expanding terms after commitment is pressure, not negotiation.

Here's exactly what to say. “I need to understand what's expected specifically before I commit to another season.”

And here's the truth. Community costs should be mutual, defined, and agreed to in advance. Everything else is just uncompensated labor.
