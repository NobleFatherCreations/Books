# “THIS ONE'S ABOUT YOU”
*The Page Every Man Needs To Read — clinically, behavioral accountability / consent education.*

Here's who you're dealing with. This is not an accusation. This is the page that makes you genuinely safer to be around — which is what most of you actually want. The festival world needs men who understand this.

Here's the scene. She's been dancing near you all night and the energy has felt reciprocal. You've talked a few times, she laughed, she touched your arm once. You're reading it as interest. You might be right. You might also be reading into it through a haze of MDMA and wishful thinking. Before you make a move: the way to know if someone wants what you're offering is to ask. Out loud. With words. "Can I kiss you?" takes two seconds and changes everything about what happens next.

Watch for these signs.

- Altered states mean consent needs to be MORE explicit, not less — not less
- Someone who is very high or dissociating cannot give consent — even if they seem willing
- Offering substances to someone you're attracted to is a power dynamic — be honest about that
- "She was into it" is not the same as "she said yes" — only one of these is consent

Here's what's actually happening. Festival environments amplify everything — including the ways men unconsciously push past ambiguity because the cost of being wrong feels low and the reward of being right feels high. Altered states, dark spaces, PLUR culture, and the "anything goes" vibe create conditions where consent gets murky fast. It doesn't have to. Two words — "is this okay?" — asked clearly and genuinely, change the entire dynamic.

Run the check. The B check: “What is my body — and hers — actually saying right now, separate from what I want the answer to be?” — ask out loud rather than guessing. The men who ask are the ones people want to be around.

Here's the dark reality. If Something Happened That Shouldn't Have. If you crossed a line — even accidentally, even while altered, even if you're not sure — the courageous thing is to acknowledge it. Not to yourself in private. To the person affected. "I'm not sure if what happened last night was okay for you — I want to check in" is a sentence that can change someone's entire recovery. RAINN has resources for people who have caused harm as well as those harmed (rainn.org). Accountability is not the end. It is the beginning of being someone people can actually trust.

Here's your move. Ask with words. Receive the answer with grace. The yes you get after asking clearly is the only one that counts.

Here's exactly what to say. “"Hey — I'm feeling a connection with you. Can I kiss you?" Then respect whatever comes next.”

And here's the truth. The men who ask are the ones people want to be around. Be that man.
