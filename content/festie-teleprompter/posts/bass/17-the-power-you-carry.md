# “THE POWER YOU CARRY”
*When You're the One With Access or Status — clinically, power differential awareness / personal accountability.*

Here's who you're dealing with. If you're a camp lead, a promoter, a performer, a veteran with a big network, or simply the one who controls the substances, the ride, or the access — you have power. This page is about what you do with it.

Here's the scene. You run a camp that people want to be part of. You have connections, good substances, a reputation. People come to you. People want things from you. Some of them are also in vulnerable positions — first-timers, people new to the scene, people who are deep in an altered state. Your position means your "yes" is more weighted and your "no" is harder for some people to hear. That's not bad. It's a responsibility.

Watch for these signs.

- People in your orbit may feel unable to say no to you — even when they want to
- Offering substances, access, or opportunity creates a power dynamic regardless of intent
- Your "just joking" or "just testing" reads differently to people with less power than you
- Silence from someone in your camp or crew is not consent — it may be fear

Here's what's actually happening. Power differentials exist whether you acknowledge them or not. Camp leads, veterans, performers, and access holders in festival culture have real leverage over people who want what they have. That leverage doesn't disappear because the environment is loving or because your intentions are good. The question is not whether you have power. The question is whether you're using it in ways that leave people genuinely free to choose.

Run the check. The B check: “Are the people in my orbit genuinely free to say no to me — and do they know that?”

Here's the dark reality. What Responsible Leadership Looks Like. In your camp, your crew, your circle: explicitly state that people are free to leave any situation, any time, no explanation needed, with no social cost. Model this by not reacting poorly when someone does leave. Don't make your substances, your connections, or your space feel conditional on compliance with your preferences. Check in with people who seem quiet or withdrawn rather than assuming they're fine. The test of good leadership is whether the people in your orbit are more themselves at the end of the weekend, not less.

Here's your move. Use your access to create freedom for people in your orbit, not dependency on you.

Here's exactly what to say. “[To your camp]: "Everyone here is free to leave any situation at any time, no questions asked. That includes leaving this camp."”

And here's the truth. The best leaders in this community are the ones people feel most free around. Build that.
