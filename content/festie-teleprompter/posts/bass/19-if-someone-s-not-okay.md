# “IF SOMEONE'S NOT OKAY”
*Mental Health & Crisis Support at Festivals — clinically, crisis intervention / peer support.*

Here's who you're dealing with. Sometimes the festival brings up things that were already there. Sometimes the substances take someone somewhere dark. Sometimes it's exhaustion and overwhelm and feeling completely alone in a crowd of thousands. This page is for knowing what to do in those moments.

Here's the scene. Your campmate has been in his tent since Saturday afternoon. He came out once, looked off, said he was fine, went back in. It's Sunday morning. You've knocked twice. He said he's okay but something in his voice sounds wrong. You don't want to be dramatic. You also know him and this isn't normal. The moment you're sitting with right now — do you go back and check on him again, or do you leave it?

Watch for these signs.

- Withdrawal from the festival when they came to engage — especially sudden
- Saying they're fine in a way that doesn't match their voice or energy
- Not eating, not sleeping, not coming out — for hours longer than exhaustion explains
- Saying things that sound like goodbye or that they shouldn't have come

Here's what's actually happening. Festival environments can precipitate real mental health crises — substance effects, sleep deprivation, emotional openness that surfaces difficult things, and the isolation that can exist inside a crowd. Men are specifically at risk of not receiving support because "I'm fine" is the expected answer and people accept it. If your gut says something is wrong, knock again. Ask directly. "Are you actually okay?" delivered with real eye contact is a different question than "you okay?"

Run the check. The B check: “What is my gut actually saying about this situation — and am I acting on it?”

Here's the dark reality. Crisis Resources At Festivals. Zendo Project (zendoproject.org) specializes in difficult psychedelic experiences and is present at many festivals — they can help with everything from bad trips to deeper crisis. Festival medical tents handle mental health crises — this is not just for physical injuries. Crisis Text Line: text HOME to 741741 — works where voice calls feel too hard. 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline: call or text 988, available 24/7. If someone is in immediate danger, get festival medical or call 911. Staying with someone in crisis until help arrives is the most important thing you can do.

Here's your move. Knock again. Ask directly. Stay with them. Get help if needed. That's the whole protocol.

Here's exactly what to say. “Hey — I'm asking for real. Are you actually okay? Because I'm not going anywhere until I know.”

And here's the truth. Checking on your people is not being dramatic. It's being a real friend.
