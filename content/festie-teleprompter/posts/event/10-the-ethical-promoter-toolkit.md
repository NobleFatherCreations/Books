# “THE ETHICAL PROMOTER TOOLKIT”
*Practical Standards for Honest Event Production — clinically, professional ethics / operational standards.*

Here's who you're dealing with. This page is the practical one. Specific practices and standards that distinguish events built on ethical foundations from ones built on the exploitation of artists, vendors, staff, and attendees.

Here's the scene. The promoters you respect — the ones whose names on a lineup or a market mean something — have all built reputations over time through the same things: they pay what they agreed. They represent their events accurately. They enforce their own standards regardless of who is involved. Their staff know what to expect and have somewhere to go with concerns. Their artists are glad to come back. None of this is complicated. All of it is a choice.

Watch for these signs.

- Written agreements with all artists, vendors, and staff before any money changes hands
- Accurate representation of attendance, placement, and production in all pitches and agreements
- Clear, functional reporting processes for safety concerns with named responsible parties
- Post-event financial reconciliation completed and communicated within agreed timelines

Here's what's actually happening. Ethical event production is not a higher bar than most promoters can reach. It is a set of consistent practices that most promoters know they should follow and some do not. The ones who do build reputations that compound over time — artists want to play their events, vendors want placement at their markets, staff want to work their shows. The ones who do not build reputations that also compound — and the circuit has a very long memory.

Run the check. The T check: “Would the artists, vendors, and staff who worked my last event describe it the way I just did?” — ethical production isn’t complicated. It’s a set of choices made consistently. Make them.

Here's the dark reality. The Ethical Promoter Checklist. Before every event: (1) All agreements in writing and signed before any performance or payment. (2) Attendance and placement representations accurate and documented. (3) All fees including conditionals listed in written agreements. (4) Staff and volunteer agreements specify hours, accommodation, meals, and reporting process. (5) Safety infrastructure plan in place and staffed. (6) Consent policy in writing with enforcement process named. After every event: (7) Financial reconciliation within agreed timeline. (8) Feedback collected from artists, vendors, and staff. (9) Post-event safety review including analysis of any incidents and their handling. (10) Changes made based on what the review reveals.

Here's your move. Run the checklist before and after every event. The reputation you build is the one that is accurate.

Here's exactly what to say. “Here is our event agreement. Here is our safety plan. Here is how to raise concerns. Here is when payment will arrive.”

And here's the truth. Ethical event production is not complicated. It is a set of choices you make consistently. Make them.
