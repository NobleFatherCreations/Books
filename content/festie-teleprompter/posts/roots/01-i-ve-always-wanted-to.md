# “I'VE ALWAYS WANTED TO”
*The Collector / Fetishization As An Approach — clinically, racial fetishization / objectification as opening move.*

Here's who you're dealing with. Intense, immediate, and full of enthusiasm about a version of you he assembled before you spoke. Compliments a category. Has a type and tells you so as though it were a gift he is handing over.

Here's the scene. He has been talking for four minutes and every single thing he has said has been about how you look, in language that keeps reaching for somewhere — a country, a continent, a word he clearly thinks is a compliment. He asks where you are really from. He tells you he has always wanted to be with someone like you. There is no point in this conversation where you have been a person with a name.

> 🖼️ **[INSERT IMAGE HERE: `images/roots/tells-card.png`]** — hold this on screen with this scenario's tells typed in; you can shorten the next line to "Here's what to watch for" instead of narrating every tell if you want this under 3 minutes.

Watch for these signs.

- Compliments a category rather than anything you chose, said, or made
- “Where are you really from”, and a guess afterwards
- Announces a preference for people like you as though it flatters you
- Reaches for your hair, your skin, or a photo within minutes
- Knows nothing about you and is nonetheless certain about you
- Gets cold fast when you are ordinary rather than the thing he pictured

Here's what's actually happening. Fetishization does the same work as any other idealization in this book: it replaces a person with a symbol, and symbols are not allowed boundaries. It is also unusually efficient as an opening move, because it arrives disguised as enthusiastic admiration, which makes declining it look like you are the one being difficult. The practical tell is not the subject matter, it is the direction of attention — someone interested in you asks questions and updates on the answers; someone interested in a category delivers conclusions and does not update at all, because your actual self is not the point and is in fact slightly in the way.

Run the check. The T check: “Has he asked me anything, or only told me things?” — Interest asks. Collecting announces.

Here's the dark reality. Being A Type Means Being Interchangeable. The thing worth understanding early is what fetishization does to the aftermath. Somebody attracted to a category rather than a person has, by definition, no particular attachment to you — which means the ordinary brakes that come with actually knowing someone are not installed. It also gives him a ready explanation for anything that goes wrong: a misunderstanding, a cultural thing, you took it the wrong way. And if you raise it afterwards in a scene that prides itself on openness, you will frequently be met with the suggestion that he was just really into you and you have made it about race. That pre-built defense is part of why this approach gets used. None of it means you have to litigate it. You can simply leave a conversation that has never once been about you.

Here's your move. Test it once, cheaply: give him a real detail about yourself that has nothing to do with how you look, and watch whether he picks it up or steers back. Then leave, without a verdict and without a debate — you are not obliged to prove it to him or to anyone standing nearby.

Here's exactly what to say. “I'm going to find my people — have a good one. — [if pressed] Not interested, mate. That's the whole answer.”

And here's the truth. Someone who wanted you would ask you something. You are allowed to walk away from an audition you never entered.
