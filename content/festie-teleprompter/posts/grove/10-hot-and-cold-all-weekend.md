# “HOT AND COLD ALL WEEKEND”
*Intermittent Reinforcement / The Addictive Cycle — clinically, intermittent reinforcement / trauma bonding.*

Here's who you're dealing with. The one who blows hot and cold on a loop — intense and present, then distant and cold, then back to magic. The unpredictability doesn't kill the attachment. It deepens it. Your nervous system starts organizing the whole weekend around getting back to the warm version.

Here's the scene. Friday night was electric — the most connected you've felt in years. Saturday morning he was barely there. Cold, distracted, barely looked at you. Saturday afternoon he reappeared like nothing happened — warm, funny, magnetic. You felt such relief to have him back that you didn't ask about the morning. By Sunday you realize you've spent the whole weekend trying to figure out how to keep him in the warm phase.

Watch for these signs.

- Intense warmth followed by withdrawal without explanation
- You feel relief — not just happiness — when he comes back around
- You're adjusting your behavior to manage his temperature
- The cold phases make the warm phases feel even more valuable than they are

Here's what's actually happening. Intermittent reinforcement is the most powerful conditioning mechanism in human psychology — it's why slot machines are more addictive than ones that pay out every time. Random reward creates compulsive seeking. When warmth is unpredictable, you start to crave it more than you would if it were consistent. The cold isn't distance — it's the tool. The warmth isn't the reward — it's the hook being set deeper. A weekend of this wires your nervous system in ways that outlast the festival.

Run the check. The G check: “Am I feeling relief when he's warm — or genuine happiness? Those are different things.”

Here's the dark reality. Trauma Bonding In 72 Hours. Festival timelines compress everything — including the bonding that normally takes months to develop. Three days of intermittent hot-cold cycles can create a real trauma bond. Signs you're in one: you feel anxious when he's not around, his approval feels more important than your own comfort, you're making excuses for behavior that would be unacceptable from a friend. This feeling is neurochemical and real — and it does not mean you're in love. It means you've been conditioned. That can be unwound. It starts with naming it.

Here's your move. Notice whether you feel relief or joy when he returns. Relief is not connection. Relief is a warning.

Here's exactly what to say. “[To yourself]: "I deserve warmth that doesn't come with cold attached to it."”

And here's the truth. Consistency is not boring. Consistency is the baseline. Unpredictability is not chemistry — it's a mechanism.
