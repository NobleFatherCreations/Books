# “YOU ARE SO BEAUTIFUL AND FREE”
*Fetishization Dressed as Acceptance — clinically, fetishization / objectification via identity.*

Here's who you're dealing with. The person — usually but not always straight and cis — who is drawn to you specifically because of your queerness, your gender expression, or your identity. The attention feels like acceptance. It is actually collection. You are an experience they want to have, not a person they want to know.

Here's the scene. "I have always been so drawn to queer energy — there is something so free and authentic about it." She says it looking right at you and it feels like being seen. By the next conversation she is asking increasingly personal questions about your body, your relationships, your experience of sexuality. The attention is intense and feels validating. Something underneath it feels like you are being studied rather than known.

> 🖼️ **[INSERT IMAGE HERE: `images/pride/tells-card.png`]** — hold this on screen with this scenario's tells typed in; you can shorten the next line to "Here's what to watch for" instead of narrating every tell if you want this under 3 minutes.

Watch for these signs.

- Their interest centers your identity category rather than you as a specific person
- Questions escalate toward the personal and sexual faster than feels comfortable
- They position their attraction to you as enlightened or progressive rather than just human
- You feel like you are representing queerness to them rather than being yourself

Here's what's actually happening. Fetishization uses the language of appreciation and attraction to reduce a person to their identity category. In festival contexts where queer expression is celebrated and visible, this can be hard to distinguish from genuine connection — especially when the attention feels validating after a lifetime of less accepting environments. The tell is the direction of interest: genuine connection is curious about you as a specific person. Fetishization is curious about what you represent.

Run the check. The P check: “Is this person interested in me specifically — or in what I represent to them?”

Here's the dark reality. When Straight Curiosity Becomes Predatory. Straight and cis people who are genuinely curious about queer experience can be wonderful allies and genuine friends. The line is consent and direction: you get to decide how much of your experience to share, on what timeline, with what level of intimacy, and for what purpose. Someone who pushes past your comfort with that sharing, who uses festival openness to access intimacy you have not offered, or who treats your queer experience as entertainment or education you owe them — is not being curious. They are taking. You are not obligated to educate anyone about your identity, body, or experience.

Here's your move. Receive genuine interest warmly. Notice when interest shifts from you as a person to you as a category. Name it or leave.

Here's exactly what to say. “I appreciate the curiosity — I am actually pretty private about that stuff. Tell me about you.”

And here's the truth. You are a person, not an experience. Anyone who cannot tell the difference does not deserve access to you.
