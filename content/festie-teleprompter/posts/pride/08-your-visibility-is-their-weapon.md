# “YOUR VISIBILITY IS THEIR WEAPON”
*Using Your Expression Against You — clinically, identity-based coercion / expression weaponization.*

Here's who you're dealing with. The use of your gender expression, your visible queerness, or your presence in queer spaces against you — as evidence that you "asked for" something, that your limits are inconsistent with your presentation, or that your expression means something you did not intend.

Here's the scene. "You were being so flirty and open all night — I thought you were into it." The argument is that your gender expression, your dancing, your presence in a queer space, or your openness earlier in the night means something about your consent to what happened. It does not. Your expression and your consent are entirely separate. Your visibility was not an invitation.

Watch for these signs.

- Your gender expression or visible queerness invoked as evidence of consent
- Your presence in a queer or sex-positive space used to argue your limits are inconsistent
- "You seemed open" used as a reason why your no was unexpected or unreasonable
- Your earlier behavior in the night used to argue that later limits should not apply

Here's what's actually happening. Expression is not consent. Presence in a space is not consent. Dancing is not consent. Being visibly queer is not consent. Being in a sex-positive or queer-coded space is not consent. Consent is a specific, ongoing, informed, freely given yes to a specific thing. Nothing about your expression, your identity, your presence, or your earlier behavior removes the requirement for that yes. Anyone who argues otherwise is not describing the culture — they are manipulating it.

Run the check. The P check: “My presence and expression are mine. They do not communicate consent for anything less than what I explicitly say yes to.” — presence is not permission. Only yes means yes.

Here's the dark reality. Expression Is Not Consent — Ever. This is not complicated: how you dress, how you dance, how you identify, where you are, and what you did earlier in the night — none of these things constitute consent for anything that happens later. Consent is specific, ongoing, and can be withdrawn at any time for any reason. If someone is arguing that your expression, presence, or past behavior means you consented to something — they are wrong. Here's your move. Your expression is yours. It does not communicate consent for anything you have not explicitly offered.

Here's exactly what to say. “My expression is not consent to anything I have not specifically agreed to. Those are completely separate.”

And here's the truth. Expression is not invitation. Presence is not permission. Your yes is the only thing that means yes.
