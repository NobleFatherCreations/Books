# “YOU ARE NOT QUEER ENOUGH”
*Community Gatekeeping / Identity Policing — clinically, community gatekeeping / shame-based compliance.*

Here's who you're dealing with. The policing of who belongs and how — based on how visibly queer you are, how you identify, who you sleep with, your gender expression, your politics, or your relationship to queer history. Used to shame people into compliance or push people out of spaces.

Here's the scene. "I do not know if this is really your scene — you seem kind of straight-passing." It is said not unkindly but it lands in a familiar tender place. You have spent your whole life negotiating your identity and its visibility and here it is again, even in a space that is supposed to be for you. By the end of the conversation you are performing queerness for their approval rather than living it on your own terms.

Watch for these signs.

- Your identity, expression, or history is used to question your right to be in a space
- A hierarchy of queerness is enforced — who is "really" queer vs insufficiently so
- You find yourself performing or performing more visibly to pass a legitimacy test
- Your own comfort with your identity is undermined by someone else's framework for it

Here's what's actually happening. Community gatekeeping in queer spaces reproduces the exact same dynamics of exclusion and shame that cause harm in straight spaces — just with different criteria. It is not protection of the community. It is the use of community membership as leverage over individuals. Your queerness is valid in whatever form it takes, at whatever level of visibility you choose, with whatever history you have. Nobody else's definition of queer enough applies to you.

Run the check. The I check: “Is my sense of my own identity secure — or am I letting someone else’s framework define it for me?” — nobody else’s checklist gets a vote.

Here's the dark reality. Your Identity Is Yours. You do not need to justify, explain, or prove your identity to anyone at a festival or anywhere else. Bisexual people belong in queer spaces. Trans people who pass belong in queer spaces. Queer people who are not visibly queer belong in queer spaces. People who are questioning belong in queer spaces. Your presence in a space is not contingent on performing your identity to anyone else's standard. If someone is using community membership as a tool to control or shame you — that is not community. That is gatekeeping. You do not need their approval.

Here's your move. Your identity is valid on your terms, at your level of visibility, in your own timeline. Full stop.

Here's exactly what to say. “I am good with who I am — this conversation is not something I need to have.”

And here's the truth. Queer enough is you, exactly as you are. Anyone else's definition is their limitation, not yours.
