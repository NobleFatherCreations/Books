# “WE NEED YOU FOR THIS”
*Recruited Under False Premises / Scope Creep in Your Role — clinically, manufactured dependency / bait and switch role.*

Here's who you're dealing with. The volunteer role that is described as one thing and becomes something significantly different once you have committed and are on site. Your transportation, accommodation, and schedule are now controlled by the event and walking away has real costs.

Here's the scene. The volunteer description said: two four-hour shifts managing crowd flow, accommodation provided, meals covered, full festival access. You arrive to find: you are actually needed for six to eight hours a day, the accommodation is a tent in a staff area with no amenities, meals are inconsistent, and your festival access is conditional on covering gaps in the schedule. The festival organizer is warm and grateful and every time you raise a concern she reminds you how much the event needs you.

Watch for these signs.

- The role as described and the role as experienced are materially different
- Schedule, accommodation, or compensation change after you have committed and are on site
- Your transportation home is tied to completing a schedule that keeps expanding
- "We need you" and "after everything we have set up for you" used when you raise concerns

Here's what's actually happening. Volunteer exploitation at festivals is widespread and normalized under the cover of community contribution and festival love. The bait-and-switch works because by the time you discover the real terms you are already there, your transportation is tied to the event, and walking away feels like abandoning people who need you. That feeling of obligation was manufactured by the structure of the arrangement. Your labor has value. Volunteer exploitation is still exploitation.

Run the check. The O check: “Is what I am being asked to do now what I agreed to do before I arrived?”

Here's the dark reality. Volunteer Agreement Basics Before You Commit. Before accepting any festival volunteer role, get in writing: exact hours and number of shifts, what accommodation is provided and its actual standard, meal coverage — exactly what and when, whether festival access is during non-shift hours only or all access, what happens if the schedule changes after you arrive, and how you get home if you need to leave before the event ends. A legitimate organization will have this in writing. One that resists writing it down is showing you their intentions for your labor.

Here's your move. Get your volunteer terms in writing before you commit. They are not asking too much. You are not asking too much.

Here's exactly what to say. “"I am excited to help — can you send me the written volunteer terms including schedule, accommodation, and meals before I confirm?"”

And here's the truth. Volunteer labor is still labor. It deserves clear terms and honest expectations.
