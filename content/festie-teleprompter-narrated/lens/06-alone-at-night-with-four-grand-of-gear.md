SCENARIO 6 — “Alone, At Night, With Four Grand Of Gear”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Here's a particular kind of person. Whoever has been watching the person with the obvious camera bag walk back to the media tent alone at three in the morning for the third night running, on the same route, at roughly the same time.

Picture it. You shot the headliner, you are carrying two bodies and three lenses, and the walk back to the car park goes through the dark bit behind the production compound because it is quicker. You do this every night. You have never thought about it, because you are thinking about the edit.

Here are the signs. The same route, the same time, alone, every night. Visible high-value equipment and no discreet bag. Working at the crowd's edges and in unlit areas by the nature of the job. Head in the camera — the one posture that removes your situational awareness. Nobody knows your route or when you are due back. Somebody has been unusually interested in what you are carrying.

Now here's what's really happening. Photographers occupy the highest-risk position at a festival and almost never think of themselves as at risk: they carry the most valuable and most portable items on site, they work alone at night by necessity, they are in the unlit margins rather than in the crowd, and the job itself requires them to spend long stretches with one eye in a viewfinder and no awareness of what is behind them. The equipment loss is the visible risk. Being followed to a vehicle in an unlit car park at three in the morning is the one that matters more.

So here's your check. I call it the E check. Ask: “Does anybody know my route and my ETA tonight?” — If the answer is no, and it is the same route as last night, that is two problems rather than one.

And here's what sits underneath all of it. Vary The Route, Tell Somebody The Time. The pattern is what does it. Somebody who wants your gear does not need to find you; they need to watch you once and then wait in the right place the following night, and a photographer is the most predictable person on a festival site — same stages, same pits, same exit, same hour. Two changes remove almost all of it, and neither costs anything: alter the route and the timing, and have a named person expecting you with a time. The gear is insured. Being alone in an unlit car park at three in the morning with somebody who has been planning it since Friday is not.

So here's your move. Vary your route and your times. Use an unbranded bag. Walk out with someone — another shooter, crew, security — and agree an ETA with a named person each night. Insure the kit properly and keep serial numbers and photographs.

Here are the words. “Walking out at half two — anyone going the same way? — [message] Heading to the car park now, back at the hotel by three, I'll text when I'm in.”

And here's the truth underneath it. You are the most predictable, most valuable and least accompanied person on this site. Change two of those three tonight.
