SCENARIO 15 — “Test Everything”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Here's someone you'll recognize. This is not a judgment page. It's a life page. Fentanyl is in the supply chain, pills are pressed to be indistinguishable, and testing is the single most effective thing you can do to protect yourself and your people.

Picture it. "Someone in our camp tested their stuff Saturday morning and it came back completely different than what they thought they had. We wouldn't have known." This happens at every major festival. The person who handed it to you may not have known either. Testing isn't about distrust — it's about knowing what's actually in what you're putting in your body. You wouldn't eat a random mushroom you found in the woods without identifying it first. Same exact energy.

Here are the signs. Fentanyl test strips: dissolve tiny amount in water, one line = fentanyl present, stop. Reagent kits (Marquis, Mecke, Simon's): color reactions tell you what's actually in it. Start with a small test dose and wait — especially anything pressed or from a new source. Never take something handed to you by someone you just met without testing it yourself first.

Now here's what's really happening. A lethal dose of fentanyl is invisible. People die at festivals every year from substances that looked, smelled, and tasted exactly like what they expected. Testing costs under $40 for a full kit from DanceSafe. Harm Reduction tents at most major events have strips free. Naloxone (Narcan) reverses opioid overdose — available at many pharmacies without prescription, small enough to carry. Know how to use it. Having it in your camp has saved lives.

So here's your check. I call it the G check. Ask: “What is my gut saying about this substance, this source, this situation — before I put it in my body?” — test it anyway, even when the gut says yes. Verification isn’t distrust of magic, it’s respect for your own body.

And here's what sits underneath all of it. If Someone Is In Trouble Right Now. Signs of overdose: unresponsive or barely responsive, slow or stopped breathing, blue lips or fingertips, gurgling sound. What to do: call festival medics immediately, administer Narcan if available (nasal spray — one puff per nostril, repeat in 2-3 min if no response), put them in recovery position on their side, stay with them. Good Samaritan laws in most US states protect you when seeking overdose help. Saving someone's life matters more than anything else.

So here's your move. Test your stuff. Every time. No exceptions. Thirty seconds can save your life.

Here are the words. “Hold on — let me test it first. I've got strips in my kit. Two minutes.”

And here's the truth underneath it. You can love the magic of this world AND verify what goes into your body. These are not opposites.
