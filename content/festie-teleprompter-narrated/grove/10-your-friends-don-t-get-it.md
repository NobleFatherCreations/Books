SCENARIO 10 — “Your Friends Don't Get It”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let me tell you about the person you'll meet here. The Gradual Drift Engineer. Doesn't drag you away dramatically — makes your world slowly contract around him until your own camp starts to feel like an interruption of the main event.

Here's how it plays out. It's Sunday morning and you realize you haven't seen your campmates since Friday lunch. Every time you mentioned them he had something better lined up. He called your best friend "a bit of a wet blanket." Now your phone is dead, you're deep in a part of the festival you don't know, his friends are your only social context, and finding your people would require asking him for help. When did that happen?

So here's what to watch for. Your actual friends get subtly characterized as negative or "not on your level". Better plans always materialize right when you're about to reconnect with your crew. You've been in his orbit for most of the festival without deciding to be there. Getting back to camp now requires his help — you've stopped navigating independently.

Now here's what's really going on. Isolation is the infrastructure of control. It doesn't need to be dramatic. A series of small redirections over 72 hours can leave you completely dependent on one person in unfamiliar territory with a dead phone. The criticism of your friends is the tell — it's not about them being bad for you. It's about making you distrust the people who know you, love you, and would notice if something was off. Connection that requires losing your existing connections is not connection. It's replacement.

So run this check. I call it the O check. Ask: “Can I find my camp right now without his help? If not — that is the situation.”

And here's the darker reality. Camp Safety Infrastructure — Build Before You Arrive. Before gates open: set a daily check-in time with at least one campmate ("if I haven't texted by 2pm, come find me"). Set a physical meeting spot everyone knows. Establish a code word meaning "come get me now, no questions." Make sure your camp lead knows your general plan each night. Write your camp location in pen on your arm. These feel like overkill until they're not. Build them before you need them.

So here's the play. Make reconnecting with your camp a daily non-negotiable ritual. Your people are your root system.

Here's exactly what to say. "I need to go do my daily check-in with my camp — it's our thing. I'll find you at the main stage tonight."

And here's the truth. A connection that requires your isolation to survive is not a connection. It's a cage with better lighting.
