SCENARIO 8 — “That's Not What Happened”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let's talk about a specific type. The Revisionist. What happened last night is now disputed this morning — calmly, certainly, like your memory is the confused one. Festival environment gives him extra ammunition: sleep deprivation, altered states, time distortion.

Here's the scene. You wake up feeling wrong. Something happened that didn't feel right and you're trying to piece it together. You bring it up. He's completely calm: "You were into it, babe. You said you wanted to come to the tent." You're not sure of the full timeline but you're sure of the feeling. He looks at you like you're confused. "I think you just need water. You always get like this coming down." Now you're wondering if he's right.

So watch for these. Your memory is disputed with calm authority — not anger, but certainty. Your emotional state is attributed to substances rather than reality. "You wanted it, you initiated" when you have no clear memory of consent. You leave more uncertain about your own experience than when you started.

Here's the mechanism underneath it. Gaslighting makes you the unreliable narrator of your own life. In a festival context it has bonus ammunition: substances, sleep deprivation, emotional openness, time distortion all genuinely affect recall — creating plausible deniability. But your body holds memory that your conscious mind loses. If your nervous system says something is wrong, that signal is real data — even when you can't reconstruct a perfect timeline. You do not need a perfect memory to know something happened.

So ask yourself this. I call it the G check. Ask: “My body is the first source of information. If it feels wrong — that's real.”

Now here's the part that's hard to hear. Spiking — It Happens And It's Not Rare. Drink and substance spiking at festivals is underreported and more common than most people openly discuss. Signs: dramatically more impaired than what you took should explain, losing chunks of time, feeling paralyzed while semi-conscious, waking up not knowing how you got somewhere. If this happened: tell a Harm Reduction worker or festival medic — not security first. RAINN (1-800-656-4673) has 24/7 support. Your experience is valid without a perfect memory.

So here's what you do. Trust your body's signal. You do not need a perfect memory or a witness to know something was wrong.

Here's what to say, out loud. “I'm not debating what happened. I know how I feel and I'm going to honor that.”

And remember this. Your body's memory outlasts the timeline. If it feels wrong, it is enough.
