SCENARIO 5 — “This Is Once In A Lifetime”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let's talk about a particular kind of person. The Opportunity Creator. Always has something exclusive about to disappear — a private ceremony, a backstage pass, a connection you'd be crazy to miss. The window is closing. You need to decide right now. You're already moving before you chose to.

Here's the scene. "I don't do this for just anyone but there's a private ceremony happening in twenty minutes — only eight people, real medicine holders, you'd have to come right now." He's already walking. You're following before you've decided to. Something in his certainty made the decision feel already made. You don't know where you're going. You haven't told anyone.

So watch for these. "Right now" / "only tonight" / "this won't happen again". Decision must happen before you can find your friends or think clearly. The opportunity only exists in his world, on his timeline. You feel excited AND vaguely railroaded simultaneously.

Here's the mechanism underneath it. Urgency is a discernment bypass. When something must happen RIGHT NOW, your prefrontal cortex — your judgment — literally cannot keep up. The pressure is the point. Real once-in-a-lifetime moments don't require you to abandon your people and your pace to access them. Anything that evaporates the moment you ask to slow down was never safe to begin with.

So ask yourself this. I call it the G check. Ask: “What is my gut saying — and do I have time to text someone where I'm going?”

Now here's the part that's hard to hear. The Private Ceremony Trap. "Private ceremony" in a location you don't know, with people you've just met, without telling anyone where you're going — is one of the highest-risk situations at any festival. Real ceremonies have known facilitators, registered locations, and don't require you to disappear immediately with a stranger. Before going anywhere private with anyone you just met: text your location to a friend. Two sentences. Takes 30 seconds. If he's impatient about those 30 seconds — that impatience is your answer.

So here's what you do. Stop. Breathe. Text someone where you're going. If it evaporates in five minutes — it wasn't real.

Here's what to say, out loud. “If it's real it can wait two minutes for me to text my camp where I'm going.”

And remember this. Anything that disappears the moment you slow down was never safe to begin with.
