SCENARIO 1 — “You're My Person”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let me tell you about a particular kind of person. Full send from minute one. Flowers in your hair before you know their last name. Telling strangers you're "festival family" fourteen hours in. Could be any gender. Charming as hell — and that's exactly what makes it work.

Here's how it plays out. You met him at the silent disco Friday night and by Saturday breakfast he's saved your spot, got you coffee, and told his whole camp you're "the one." He keeps saying "we" — we should catch that set, we should stay together tonight. By Sunday you haven't seen your actual people since gates opened. You feel chosen, warm, slightly dizzy. He wants to share his tent because "the connection is just too real, babe."

So here's what to watch for. "I've never felt this way" before he knows your last name. "We" language before you agreed to a "we". You feel electric and slightly like you can't catch your breath — that rush is chemistry, not character. You're suddenly part of his plans, his camp, his crew — you didn't choose that, it just happened.

Now here's what's really going on. Love bombing is overwhelming affection deployed fast — before you can actually clock who someone is. The festival bubble supercharges it: nervous system already wide open, time doing that weird thing, culture normalizing fast intimacy. He might not know he's running this pattern. Doesn't matter — the effect is the same. You feel bonded to someone you've known for one sunrise. Chemistry is real. It is not the same as character. Pace is protection.

So run this check. I call it the R check. Ask: “Is this moving faster than I chose?” — if the speed is his, not yours, that's your first signal.

And here's the darker reality. The Isolator. Some festival boyfriends are deliberately running a playbook. By Sunday you're in their camp, dependent on their phone charger, can't find your friends, and your own tent feels far away. This is not accidental. Isolation is the setup. Connection is the costume. If you're three days in and can't easily get back to your own people without asking him — that IS the situation.

So here's the play. Enjoy the spark. Let the pace be yours. A connection that's real will still be real after you check in with camp.

Here's exactly what to say. “I'm feeling this energy — I also need to flow freely this weekend. Let's vibe without locking anything down.”

And here's the truth. Real connection can survive a slower pace. Anyone who can't wait is applying pressure, not offering magic.
