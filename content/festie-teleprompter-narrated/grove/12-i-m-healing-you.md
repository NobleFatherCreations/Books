SCENARIO 12 — “I'm Healing You”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Here's a specific type. The Self-Appointed Healer. Uses therapeutic, somatic, or spiritual language to create a context where physical touch seems appropriate and expected — while skipping the part where you actually agree to it.

Picture it. "You have blocked energy in your hips — I can feel it from here. I do somatic release work." He's already reaching. The ceremony just ended. You're tender and wide open. Three people told you he's legit. He has a beautiful altar. You don't want to block your own healing. His hands are on your body before you said yes. You didn't say no either. But you also never said yes.

Here are the signs. Physical contact initiated under therapeutic framing without your explicit verbal yes. Your hesitation reframed as resistance to healing — not a valid limit. Timing targets peak vulnerability: post-ceremony, post-peak, tender moments. Credentials asserted verbally but impossible to verify in the moment.

Now here's what's really happening. Spiritual authority abuse weaponizes the healer role to bypass the consent conversation. It works because festival culture values healing (the frame seems legit), the therapeutic context makes touch feel appropriate, and questioning a healer feels like questioning healing itself. Real somatic practitioners and ceremony facilitators are trained to obtain clear, uncoerced consent before any physical contact. That standard exists to protect you. If someone skips it — the reason is not that they're too advanced for it.

So here's your check. I call it the G check. Ask: “What is my gut saying about this person — underneath the role they’re performing right now?” — if the answer doesn’t match the title, trust the gut over the role.

And here's what sits underneath all of it. How To Actually Vet A Practitioner. "Legitimate" does not mean "has a following," "has an altar," or "has people here who vouch for them." Real vetting means asking for their training lineage and how long they've practiced, asking what their consent protocol is before they work with anyone, and ideally talking to people who know them outside festival contexts. A real practitioner welcomes these questions. Anyone who responds to "what's your consent process?" with spiritual deflection is giving you your answer.

So here's your move. No practitioner — no matter how credentialed — initiates physical contact without your explicit verbal yes. That is the baseline.

Here are the words. “I appreciate the offer. I'm not available for bodywork right now.”

And here's the truth underneath it. Your healing belongs to you. Nobody gets to access your body in the name of your own expansion.
