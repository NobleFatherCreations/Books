SCENARIO 18 — “I'm Your Ride”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Here's the person you'll meet here. Whoever owns the vehicle, the ticket, or the only spare seat. Often perfectly nice for three days. Becomes something else at the exact moment your plans and his stop matching.

Picture it. He drove. It is four hours each way and you are ninety miles from the nearest station with no signal in the car park. On Saturday you want to stay with the people from the neighbouring camp and he says that's fine, a bit flatly, and then mentions that he was thinking of heading off early tomorrow, and you feel the whole weekend quietly reorganize itself around not annoying him.

Here are the signs. One person controls the only realistic way home. Departure time shifts in response to your choices rather than to logistics. Your ticket, wristband, or bag is in his vehicle. “I could just go without you” floated as a joke. You are editing what you ask for to keep the lift viable. No independent route exists and you have not checked whether one does.

Now here's what's really happening. Transport is the cleanest leverage at a festival because it is invisible — nobody has threatened anything, there is nothing to report, and the pressure is purely structural. It works the same way as medication or accommodation control: whoever holds the thing you cannot do without sets the terms of every other conversation. Most people who hold it never use it. The ones who do rarely say anything explicit, because they do not have to; you will do the arithmetic yourself and adjust, and that adjustment is the entire mechanism.

So here's your check. I call it the E check. Ask: “If I had to leave this festival in the next two hours without him, could I?” — If there is no answer, your exit is somebody else's property.

And here's what sits underneath all of it. The Car Park Is The Trap. The dangerous shape is not the weekend, it is the journey. A car with one other person in it, four hours of road, no signal, and no way out at seventy miles an hour is a genuinely enclosed space, and people who want an enclosed space know that. This is why the lift home is where a lot of these situations actually turn — the festival was populated and watched, and the drive is neither. Decide the return arrangements before you travel, tell somebody off-site who you are travelling with and when you are due back, and make it entirely normal for them to be expecting a message when you land.

So here's your move. Arrange a second way home before you go — a coach time, a rail replacement, a friend in the next county, sixty pounds set aside for a taxi to a station. Keep your ticket, ID, phone and keys on your body, not in his boot. Text your arrival to an off-site person both ways.

Here are the words. “I've got a coach booked back as a backup either way, so don't wait on me if you want to head off — go when you like.”

And here's the truth underneath it. An exit that depends on somebody else's mood is not an exit. Buy the backup ticket; it costs less than the weekend does.
