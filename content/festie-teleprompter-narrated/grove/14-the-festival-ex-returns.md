SCENARIO 14 — “The Festival Ex Returns”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let's talk about the person you'll meet here. The person from last year — or the year before — who hurt you, who you finally processed, who shows up at the same festival like the circuit is their hunting ground. They know your world. They know your people. They know exactly what to say.

Here's the scene. You saw him across the fire Saturday night and your whole body went electric — not in a good way. Then he appeared next to you with that smile. "I've been thinking about you. I think I was in a really different place last year. I've done a lot of work." He sounds different. He looks softer. And some part of you that spent six months healing wants desperately to believe that the work is real.

So watch for these. Reappears at the same festivals — he knows your circuit. Opens with a change narrative: "I've done the work," "I've grown a lot". Creates urgency: "I need to talk to you this weekend before you leave". The feelings that come back are old trauma activation, not new information.

Here's the mechanism underneath it. Hoovering is the return of a previous manipulator after a period of absence — timed for maximum impact. Festival reunions are particularly potent because the environment is already emotionally heightened, your nervous system associates the setting with connection, and seeing someone in a context where you once felt magic reactivates those neurochemical pathways faster than almost anything else. "I've done the work" is a sentence. Changed behavior is a pattern. You need months of data, not a weekend of charm.

So ask yourself this. I call it the V check. Ask: “Do I actually know who this person is now — or am I responding to who I hoped they were?”

Now here's the part that's hard to hear. The Rule Of Months Not Moments. Changed people change in their regular life — in how they treat people when nothing is on the line, when they're stressed, when they're not in a magical environment trying to win you back. The festival weekend is the worst possible place to assess whether someone has actually changed because everything is heightened, everyone is performing the best version of themselves, and the environment itself creates false intimacy. "I need to see this in your regular life over months, not this weekend" is a full and complete response to any change narrative.

So here's what you do. Let the encounter be pleasant. Do not let the encounter be a reopening. You already did that chapter.

Here's what to say, out loud. "I'm really glad you're doing the work. I'm also going to protect what I built after you."

And remember this. Your healing is not available for disruption every time they decide they're ready.
