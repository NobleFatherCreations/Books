SCENARIO 2 — “I Got You, Babe”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let's talk about the person you'll meet here. The generous one. Always has extra. Covers your wristband, feeds you, shares her stash, lends her charger. Feels like pure festie abundance — until you try to leave or say no.

Here's the scene. She covered your wristband at the gate when your card declined. "Don't stress, we're family." Over two days she shares her hammock, food, connects. Every time you try to wander toward your crew she gets that look — "After everything I've done for you?" By Sunday you're in her orbit, your new friends are her people first, and the idea of leaving feels like doing something wrong.

So watch for these. Gifts arrive before any actual friendship — fast, often in front of witnesses. Every generous act gets quietly logged and referenced when you try to have independence. You feel a vague obligation you didn't agree to and can't name. Attempts to spend time with your own people are met with hurt feelings.

Here's the mechanism underneath it. Reciprocity is one of the deepest human wiring patterns — when someone gives us something we feel compelled to give back. Festival gifting culture makes this extra potent because giving IS sacred here. The predatory version flips it: the gift is an investment with a return expected. When the "return" gets called in, saying no feels ungrateful. That feeling of owing something you never agreed to? That was engineered.

So ask yourself this. I call it the O check. Ask: “Am I still connected to my actual people — or has dependency on her replaced that?”

Now here's the part that's hard to hear. When The Gift Is The Substance. Most dangerous version: the person who provides your substances becomes the person you have to stay near. "I'll get you sorted" sounds generous until you've been in their camp for 18 hours, your phone is dead, and leaving means losing access. This is dependency grooming. Your consciousness should never be owned by someone who wants something from you. Test your stuff. Know your source. Independence is harm reduction.

So here's what you do. Receive kindness warmly. Leave freely. Gratitude is a feeling — not a transaction that purchases your loyalty.

Here's what to say, out loud. “That was genuinely kind — thank you. I'm gonna find my camp. Catch you at the main stage.”

And remember this. In real gifting culture the gift is complete the moment it leaves your hand. Anyone who picks it back up was never giving.
