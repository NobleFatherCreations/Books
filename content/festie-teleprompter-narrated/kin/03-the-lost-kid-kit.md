SCENARIO 3 — “The Lost-Kid Kit”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let's talk about a particular kind of person. For everyone in the family field, every year, including the veterans who have done twelve of these and especially the ones who think their kid is too sensible to get lost.

Here's the scene. The first ninety seconds of a missing child are the ones that matter, and they are ninety seconds in which you will be useless — heart rate through the roof, unable to remember what they were wearing, phone at four percent. Everything on this page exists so that a calm Thursday version of you has already done the thinking.

So watch for these. Wristband with your mobile number, written fresh each morning. A photo of each child taken every morning, in that day's actual clothes. A meeting point they can say back to you, chosen and physically visited. The phrase: find a person with a radio, or a stall, or a mum with kids. Ear defenders, sun hat, water — the boring three that prevent most of it. Your phone charged, and a second adult's number on the band too.

Here's the mechanism underneath it. Lost children at festivals are common and almost always resolve in minutes, and the reason they resolve is infrastructure: wristbands with numbers, a welfare tent that does this constantly, and staff who are trained for it. The daily photo is the single highest-value item and the one almost nobody does — a described child is hard to search for and a photographed child in today's clothes is easy. Teaching who to approach matters as much as teaching not to wander: children told only about stranger danger will avoid the very adults most likely to help them, so name the categories instead — radio, stall, parent with kids.

So ask yourself this. I call it the I check. Ask: “Wristband on, photo taken today, meeting point they can repeat?” — Three things, every morning, before anything else.

Now here's the part that's hard to hear. Ninety Seconds, And What To Do In Them. Have the protocol memorized because you will not be able to reason it out. One adult stays exactly where the child was last seen and does not move — children double back, and a moving parent and a moving child can search past each other for an hour. A second adult goes straight to welfare or the nearest radio and reports it immediately; there is no waiting period and nobody will think you are overreacting, because they do this several times a day. Show the photo. Give the wristband number so it can be called. Everyone else checks the last three places the child liked, not the whole site. Ninety seconds of the right actions beats twenty minutes of frantic ones.

So here's what you do. Do the three every morning — band, photo, meeting point — and walk the child to the welfare tent on Thursday so they have physically seen where it is. Agree the stay-put rule with the other adults now, before it is needed.

Here's what to say, out loud. “If you can't find me: stay where you are and find someone with a radio, or a stall, or a mum with kids. Show them your wristband. Tell me the plan back?”

And remember this. Ten minutes on Thursday buys you the only ninety seconds that will ever matter. Do the photo — it is the one everybody skips.
