SCENARIO 7 — “The Camp Safety Infrastructure”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Here's someone you'll recognize. This page is practical. Specific structures that make the difference between a camp that talks about safety and a camp that actually provides it.

Picture it. The briefing before gates open is the most important ten minutes of the festival. What you say in that briefing — and what structures you establish in it — shapes what members feel able to do and say for the entire weekend. The camps with the best safety records are not the ones with the most rules. They are the ones where the culture of safety is established before anything difficult has happened.

Here are the signs. Pre-festival briefing: consent expectations, reporting process, code words, daily check-in times. Designated safety contact who is not you — someone members can go to with concerns about camp leadership. Clear, simple reporting process that does not require members to confront the person they are concerned about. Post-festival debrief: what happened, what worked, what needs to change next season.

Now here's what's really happening. The structures you build before the festival are the ones that work during it. A reporting process established in a calm briefing is infinitely more useful than one improvised in a crisis. A designated safety contact who is not you means members have a path for concerns about your behavior as well as others. A code word system means members can signal for help without having to explain themselves in the moment. These are not elaborate systems — they are ten minutes of planning that change everything.

So here's your check. I call it the A check. Ask: “Have I built safety infrastructure into my camp that works even when I am the problem?”

And here's what sits underneath all of it. The Pre-Festival Camp Safety Briefing Template. Cover in your briefing: (1) Consent expectations — specific, not just "be cool." (2) Reporting process — how to raise a concern, who to go to, what happens next. (3) Code word — one word that means "I need help right now, come find me." (4) Daily check-in time — when and how. (5) Exit is free — anyone can leave the camp any time for any reason with no social cost. (6) What to do if something goes wrong and you are not sure who to tell. (7) Resources: Harm Reduction tent location, Ranger station location, medical tent location. Ten minutes. Do it before gates open. Every season.

So here's your move. Build the safety infrastructure before you need it. Run the briefing before gates open. Every season.

Here are the words. “Before gates open — ten minutes for our safety briefing. This is how we take care of each other this weekend.”

And here's the truth underneath it. The camps with the best safety records are the ones that built the infrastructure before anything went wrong.
