SCENARIO 10 — “Two Of My People, One Story Each”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Here's the person you'll meet here. You, holding a disclosure from one camp member about another, both of whom you like, in a structure with no process, no training and no separation of roles whatsoever.

Picture it. She told you what happened on Friday. He has been your friend for nine years. You are now the investigator, the judge, the friend of both parties and the person who has to keep this camp functioning for another two days, and you have no idea what you are doing.

Here are the signs. You are close to both parties and there is no independent person. Your first instinct is to get them in a room together. You are trying to establish what really happened. The camp has begun taking sides before anything is decided. No notes have been taken and it is already three days ago. The reporting person has asked you not to do anything.

Now here's what's really happening. Camp leads are handed reports with none of the structure that makes handling them possible, and the two most common instincts are both wrong. Mediation — getting both parties in a room — is actively harmful where there is any power imbalance or alleged harm, because it requires the person who was harmed to negotiate with the person who harmed them. And investigating, in the sense of establishing the truth, is not your role and you are not equipped for it; your role is immediate safety, accurate notes, and routing it to someone independent.

So here's your check. I call it the L check. Ask: “Am I trying to find out what really happened?” — That is not the job. Safety, notes, and handing it on is the job.

And here's what sits underneath all of it. What The Reporting Person Controls And What They Do Not. Two things have to be held together and it is uncomfortable. The reporting person controls their own disclosure — who they tell, whether they make a formal report, whether they involve police — and you do not override that. But you do control your camp, and if there is an ongoing safety risk you can act on that without their consent, as long as you do it in a way that does not identify them. Separating sleeping arrangements, changing a rota, or removing somebody can all be done as a camp decision rather than as a response to a specific report. And write the notes tonight, factual and dated, because in four months this will come back and memory is not evidence.

So here's your move. Ask what she wants and what she needs to be safe tonight, and do the safety part immediately. Take dated factual notes the same day. Separate them physically without announcing why. Route it to the event's safeguarding lead. Do not mediate, do not investigate, and do not tell the camp.

Here are the words. “Thank you for telling me. Two things: what do you need tonight to feel safe, and what do you want to happen next — that part's yours to decide. I'm going to move some sleeping arrangements either way, and I won't say why to anyone.”

And here's the truth underneath it. You are not an investigator and you are certainly not a mediator. You are safety tonight, notes today, and a handover tomorrow.
