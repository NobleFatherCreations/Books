SCENARIO 3 — “This Is How We Do Things Here”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let's talk about someone you'll recognize. Camp culture is built by what you reward, what you tolerate, and what you address. It is built more by your behavior in difficult moments than by your stated values in calm ones. This page is about the specific ways camps build cultures that hurt people.

Here's the scene. Your camp has a norm of not talking about what happens in camp outside camp. It started as a way to protect privacy and maintain a sense of internal safety. Over time it has also functioned as a way to prevent accountability — things that happen in camp stay in camp because "that's our culture." Someone who was harmed by a camp member felt they could not report it externally because of this norm. The norm that was meant to protect people is now protecting the person who harmed them.

So watch for these. Privacy norms that function as reporting suppression. Loyalty norms that equate questioning the camp with betrayal of the community. Contribution norms that create unpayable debt for new or less senior members. Norms that make it harder rather than easier to raise concerns or exit the camp.

Here's the mechanism underneath it. Every camp norm was created for a reason and serves a function. The question is whether the function it currently serves is the one it was created for — or whether it has evolved into a protection mechanism for the camp's reputation rather than its people. Privacy norms that prevent reporting, loyalty norms that suppress dissent, contribution norms that extract labor — all of these start somewhere reasonable and can drift somewhere harmful. Examining your norms honestly is camp leadership.

So ask yourself this. I call it the E check. Ask: “Do my camp’s norms make it easier or harder for members to raise concerns and seek help when something has gone wrong?” — the norms you build in calm times are the ones that show up in the hard ones.

Now here's the part that's hard to hear. Camp Norm Audit. Take each of your camp's major norms and ask: (1) What was this norm created for? (2) Is it currently serving that purpose? (3) Who does this norm protect when there is conflict? (4) Does this norm make it easier or harder for harmed people to seek help? Specific norms to examine: confidentiality norms, loyalty norms, contribution expectations, seniority hierarchies, and any norms around how conflict is handled. If your honest audit reveals that a norm is primarily protecting the camp's reputation or the comfort of senior members at the expense of accountability — it needs to change.

So here's what you do. Audit your camp norms honestly. Change the ones that protect the camp at the expense of its people.

Here's what to say, out loud. "I want us to look at our camp norms together and be honest about whether they are serving the people in this camp or protecting the camp itself."

And remember this. The norms you build in calm times define what happens in the hard ones.
