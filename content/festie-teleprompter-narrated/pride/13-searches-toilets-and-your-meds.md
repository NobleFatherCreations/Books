SCENARIO 13 — “Searches, Toilets And Your Meds”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let me tell you about the person you'll meet here. Not a predator — the infrastructure. The pat-down at the gate, the toilet block, the medical tent, and four days away from a bathroom cabinet.

Here's how it plays out. The security queue splits into two lines by gender and you are doing the calculation you always do. Then there is the question of the toilets for four days, and the fact that your HRT is in a bag that may be searched by someone who will say something, and the medical tent where you may at some point have to explain your body to a stranger with a clipboard while not at your best.

So here's what to watch for. Gendered search lines with no stated alternative. No gender-neutral toilets, or one block at the far end of the site. Medication in original packaging you would rather nobody read. No plan for a medical interaction where you have to disclose. Binding for multiple days in heat, which is a genuine physical risk. You have not checked whether this event has an LGBTQ+ or welfare lead.

Now here's what's really going on. Most of what makes a festival hard for trans attendees is friction rather than malice, and friction is plannable. Every large event has a welfare team and most now have a named inclusion or access lead; searches can almost always be done by an officer of your choosing or in a private area if you ask, and that request is routine for staff even when it feels enormous to make. The health items are the ones that actually hurt people: binding for four days in heat restricts breathing and causes injury, and interrupted HRT is a medical issue rather than a cosmetic one.

So run this check. I call it the D check. Ask: “Have I found this event's welfare or inclusion lead, and do I know where the gender-neutral facilities are?” — Ask at the gate, not on Saturday.

And here's the darker reality. Binding And Heat Is The Actual Danger. The genuinely dangerous item on this page is not a person. Binding for consecutive long days in summer heat, while dehydrated, while dancing, is how people end up with bruised ribs, restricted breathing and serious respiratory trouble, and the festival environment removes every natural break you would normally get. Eight hours maximum, never sleep in it, bring a spare and something looser for the hot part of the day, and take the rest breaks in the tent where nobody is watching. Carry your HRT in original labelled packaging with a copy of the prescription — it makes a search conversation short and factual, and it protects you if a bag goes missing.

So here's the play. Email the event before you travel and ask three things: gender-neutral facilities, search policy, and the welfare or inclusion lead's name. Bring HRT in labelled packaging with a prescription copy and a spare stash in a second bag. Plan binding hours like you would plan hydration.

Here's exactly what to say. “Could I have the search done by a female officer, or somewhere private? — Hi, who's the welfare or inclusion lead this weekend, and where are the gender-neutral toilets?”

And here's the truth. Most of this is logistics, and logistics can be arranged in advance. Your ribs cannot be un-bruised afterwards.
