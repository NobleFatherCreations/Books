SCENARIO 5 — “I Can't Reach Them”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let me tell you about a specific type. You, at home, watching an unanswered message go from normal to not normal, with no idea where the line is.

Here's how it plays out. They have not replied since Friday afternoon. It is now Saturday evening. You know the phone probably died, because everyone's phone dies, and you also cannot stop refreshing, and you have no idea whether calling the festival makes you a reasonable parent or a ridiculous one.

So here's what to watch for. Silence longer than they said it would be. Their last message was odd, short, or unlike them. You do not know who they are with or where they are camped. You have been calling repeatedly, which achieves nothing and floods the line. You are about to post publicly, which you should not do yet. You do not know the festival has a welfare team that does exactly this.

Now here's what's really going on. Silence at a festival is overwhelmingly a dead battery and no signal — mobile networks at large events are saturated, charging queues are hours long, and messages sent from a field frequently never arrive. That is the likeliest explanation and it is worth holding onto. It is also true that every large festival has a welfare team whose job includes locating people and passing on messages, they are contactable, and they will not think you are overreacting. Most families do not know that team exists, so they oscillate between doing nothing and calling the police.

So run this check. I call it the E check. Ask: “Have I contacted the festival's welfare team?” — That is the step between waiting and panicking, and almost nobody knows it exists.

And here's the darker reality. The Ladder, So You Are Not Choosing Between Nothing And Everything. Work it in order rather than jumping. One: message the friends they went with, which is why you asked for names before they left — this resolves it most of the time within an hour. Two: call the festival's information or welfare line, give the name, camp area and a description, and ask them to pass a message; they do this many times a day. Three: if there is a genuine reason for concern — a worrying last message, a known health condition, a specific threat — say so to welfare, because that changes what they do. Four: police, and there is no waiting period for a missing person, that is a myth. What not to do is call them forty times, because it drains the battery you are hoping stays alive, and do not post publicly early, because it is very hard to undo and they are almost certainly asleep in a tent.

So here's the play. Message their friends first. Then ring the event's welfare or information line and ask them to pass a message. Send one text, not thirty, saying you are fine and just want a thumbs-up when they see it. Escalate to police if there is a specific reason, without waiting.

Here's exactly what to say. “Hi — my daughter's at your event, camped in the yellow field, I've not heard from her since Friday and her phone's likely dead. Could someone pass her a message to text home? — [text] No panic at all, just send me a thumbs-up when you see this. Have a brilliant time.”

And here's the truth. It is almost always a dead battery. Ring welfare, send one message, and do not call forty times — you are draining the phone you are waiting on.
