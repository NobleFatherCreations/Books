SCENARIO 4 — “What This Place Actually Is”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Here's someone you'll recognize. For you, at the end, from people who have spent their lives in this world and have written an entire book about its dangers — which is exactly why this page is worth trusting.

Picture it. Everything else in this book is about what can go wrong, because that is what a safety guide is for. Read as a whole it gives a false impression, and it would be dishonest to hand you a hundred pages of predators and let you think that is the place your person is going to.

Here are the signs. Strangers walk each other to medical tents at three in the morning, constantly. People search fields all night for someone they have never met. There are volunteers whose entire role is sitting with frightened people. Someone will give your person water, sunscreen, a charger, a lift, for nothing. The vast majority of people come home tired, sunburnt and happy. They will talk about it for a year, and you will get bored of hearing about it.

Now here's what's really happening. The reason this scene is worth protecting is that it is genuinely unusual: a place where radical acceptance is the default, where people look after strangers as a matter of course, and where somebody can be more themselves for seventy-two hours than they manage anywhere else all year. The patterns in this book exist because predators seek out open-hearted places, and open-heartedness is the thing worth defending rather than the mistake. Your person is not naive for loving it. They are going somewhere that gives them something the default world does not, and the overwhelming likelihood is that they will come home with a sunburn and too many stories.

So here's your check. I call it the H check. Ask: “Am I treating this as a threat to survive, or as a thing they love?” — They can tell which one you think it is, and it determines everything.

And here's what sits underneath all of it. Why You Were Given This. Somebody handed you this guide. That was not an accident and it was not an accusation — it means they want you inside this part of their life rather than outside it, and they were worried enough about your reaction to give you a manual first. That is worth sitting with. The single most protective thing in this entire book is not a tactic or a check or a script; it is a young person who is certain, without having to think about it, that there is somebody at home who will pick up at four in the morning and not make it worse. You can be that by Friday. Nothing else on this page matters as much.

So here's your move. Text them on the Saturday and ask what the best set was. Do not ask if they are okay — ask what was good. Then, when they get back, let them tell you about it at length and be genuinely interested, because that is what keeps the channel open until the year they need it.

Here are the words. “What's been the best thing so far? — [when they're home] Right, tell me everything. Start with the music.”

And here's the truth underneath it. They are going somewhere that makes them more themselves. Be the person they can come home and tell.
