SCENARIO 15 — “Community Beyond Your Camp”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let's talk about a specific type. The festival community is only as safe as what each person does when they see something that does not involve their own crew. This page is about extending your care beyond your group — because the stranger you help might be someone else's buddy who needs exactly what you have to offer.

Here's the scene. You are at the water station and a woman comes up alone and asks if you can spare a phone charger for ten minutes. She says she got separated from her group and cannot reach anyone. She seems okay but uncertain. You have a portable charger. The ten minutes it takes to help her reconnect with her group might be the ten minutes that means her story has a good ending. This is what community care actually looks like — not grand gestures, small ones.

So watch for these. Share your phone charger freely — a charged phone is safety infrastructure for everyone. If someone seems lost or uncertain — ask if they need help finding something. If you see someone alone and very impaired — check in, do not assume someone else will. If you are leaving a set and see someone alone at the edge who looks uncomfortable — the distraction approach works on strangers too.

Here's the mechanism underneath it. Community safety at festivals is not the responsibility of staff and Rangers alone — it is a distributed responsibility across every person there. The small acts of community care — sharing a charger, asking if someone is okay, walking someone to the Harm Reduction tent, using the distraction approach for a stranger in a difficult situation — these are what make the difference between a festival where harm happens and goes unaddressed and one where the community catches things before they escalate.

So ask yourself this. I call it the E check. Ask: “Am I extending care beyond my own group — or only looking out for the people I know?”

Now here's the part that's hard to hear. What Festie Community Care Actually Looks Like. Real community care in practice: share your charger without being asked. Ask the person sitting alone if they are waiting for someone or need help. Walk someone to a resource if they seem unsure where to go. Give your extra water to someone who clearly needs it. Use the distraction approach for a stranger in a situation that looks off. Tell a Ranger what you saw. These are not heroic acts. They are the distributed infrastructure of a safe festival. When everyone does the small things the big things happen less often.

So here's what you do. Extend your care beyond your camp. The small acts of community care are the infrastructure of a safe festival.

Here's what to say, out loud. "Hey — do you need help finding something? I have a charger if your phone is dead."

And remember this. The festival is only as safe as what each person does when they see something. That includes you.
