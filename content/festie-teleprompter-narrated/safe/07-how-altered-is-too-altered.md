SCENARIO 7 — “How Altered Is Too Altered”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Here's a specific type. Being altered is a normal part of many festival experiences. Being so altered that you cannot take care of yourself, communicate clearly, or make safe decisions is a different situation — and the people around you are your safety net when you are in that state.

Picture it. Your friend is having a bigger night than she intended. She is not in medical trouble but she is very out of it — she cannot navigate independently, she is making decisions that she would not make with clearer judgment, and she is attracting attention from some people who seem very interested in being helpful in ways that feel off to you. This is the moment where the buddy system is not a suggestion. It is the thing that determines how her night ends.

Here are the signs. Cannot navigate or find their way without help — beyond just being unfamiliar with the layout. Communication is significantly impaired — not just slow but unable to form coherent sentences. Making decisions that are completely out of character — especially about people or locations. Others are clustering around them in ways that feel more predatory than supportive.

Now here's what's really happening. Severe impairment in a festival environment is a specific vulnerability state that predatory people specifically target. The person who cannot walk straight, cannot communicate clearly, and cannot make coherent decisions cannot consent to anything and cannot protect themselves from anyone. If your friend or someone near you is in this state — stay with them. Get them to a safe space. If they need medical attention get it without hesitation. Good Samaritan laws exist for exactly this situation.

So here's your check. I call it the E check. Ask: “Can this person take care of themselves right now — and if not, who is actually with them?”

And here's what sits underneath all of it. When To Go To Medical Or Harm Reduction. Go immediately: if they are unresponsive or semi-conscious, if their breathing is wrong, if their lips or fingertips are blue, if they had a seizure, if they have a high fever with no sweating. Go sooner rather than later: if they are significantly confused and getting more so, if they took something unknown and are reacting badly, if they are very distressed and not calming down, if you are worried and not sure why. The Harm Reduction tent is specifically for situations where you are not sure — they will help you assess. You will not get in trouble for bringing someone for care. You might regret not doing it.

So here's your move. Stay with them. Get them to a safe space. Go to Harm Reduction or medical if you are unsure. Do not leave them alone.

Here are the words. “I am staying with you — we are going to find somewhere quiet. Come on.”

And here's the truth underneath it. The buddy who stays is the reason the story has a good ending. Be that person.
