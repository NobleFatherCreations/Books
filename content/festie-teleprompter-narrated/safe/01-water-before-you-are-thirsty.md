SCENARIO 1 — “Water Before You Are Thirsty”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Here's the person you'll meet here. By the time you feel thirsty at a festival you are already behind. The combination of dancing, heat, sun exposure, and many substances means your hydration needs are significantly higher than your normal daily baseline — and your body's thirst signal is often suppressed or delayed.

Picture it. It is 2pm Saturday and it is hot. You have been dancing since 11am. You have had one bottle of water and three sips from someone else's hydration pack. You feel fine — energized actually, slightly euphoric. That feeling is not a reliable indicator of your hydration status. By the time the headache hits and you start feeling off your balance you are already significantly behind. The medical tent is about to get busy because you are not the only one.

Here are the signs. Drink water consistently throughout the day — at least 500ml per hour in heat and while dancing. Electrolytes matter as much as water — plain water alone can cause hyponatremia in high volumes. Signs of heat exhaustion: heavy sweating, cool pale skin, weakness, nausea, headache. Signs of heat stroke — EMERGENCY: hot red dry skin, no sweating, confusion, loss of consciousness.

Now here's what's really happening. Festivals combine multiple dehydration risk factors simultaneously: physical exertion from dancing, sun and heat exposure, alcohol and stimulant use (both increase fluid loss), and environments that make stopping to drink feel inconvenient. Heat exhaustion is common and treatable. Heat stroke is a medical emergency that kills people. The difference between them is speed of treatment. Know the signs. Act early. The medical tent is there specifically for this.

So here's your check. I call it the F check. Ask: “Do I know where the medical tent is — and have I actually had enough water in the last hour?”

And here's what sits underneath all of it. Heat Stroke Is A Medical Emergency. Heat stroke signs: hot, red, dry skin with no sweating. Confusion, slurred speech, loss of consciousness. Body temperature above 104F. This is life-threatening and requires immediate medical attention. What to do right now: call for medical help immediately, move the person to shade and cool them with water or ice packs to the neck, armpits, and groin, do not give fluids to someone who is confused or unconscious. Festival medical teams are trained for this. Get them immediately. Do not wait to see if it gets better.

So here's your move. Drink water now. Take an electrolyte packet. Find shade for ten minutes. Do it before you need to.

Here are the words. “Hey — when did you last have water? Come on, let us both drink something right now.”

And here's the truth underneath it. Every medical tent at every festival sees preventable heat illness every day. Do not be that story.
