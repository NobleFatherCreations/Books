SCENARIO 6 — “See Something — Do Something”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let's talk about someone you'll recognize. At some point this weekend you will see something that does not look right. A situation. A person. A dynamic. The bystander effect is real — in groups, individuals are less likely to act because everyone assumes someone else will. You are the someone else.

Here's the scene. You are walking between stages and you see a woman sitting with a man who seems agitated. Her body language is closed. He is leaning into her space and she is looking at the ground. You are not sure if it is anything. You almost keep walking because you do not want to assume, do not want to make it weird, do not want to be wrong. But something caught. This is the moment. This is the one. You have three options and they all take less than two minutes.

So watch for these. THE DISTRACTION: walk up like you know her — "Oh my god, there you are! I have been looking everywhere for you!". THE RESOURCE: find a Ranger or Harm Reduction worker, describe location and exactly what you saw. THE CHECK-IN: wait for a private moment and ask her directly — "Hey — are you okay?". You do NOT have to confront the person causing concern — that is not required and not always safe.

Here's the mechanism underneath it. The most effective bystander interventions are the subtle ones — the distraction approach changes the social dynamic without confrontation and gives the person an exit without escalation. You do not have to be certain something is wrong to intervene. You do not have to be a hero. You just have to be the person who showed up. The festival community is only as safe as what each individual does in the moment they notice something.

So ask yourself this. I call it the S check. Ask: “What did I actually see — and which of the three moves fits this situation?”

Now here's the part that's hard to hear. Reading A Situation Quickly. Signs a situation may need intervention: someone who seems significantly more impaired than their companions and is being directed or moved somewhere. Body language that is closed, small, or fearful in contrast to the other person's open or dominant posture. Someone who looks uncomfortable when you make eye contact with them and then looks away quickly. Someone being separated from their group by another person. A transaction that looks pressured or coerced. You do not need certainty to intervene with a distraction or to alert a Ranger. You just need a gut feeling. Trust it.

So here's what you do. Pick one of the three moves and do it. Doing something imperfectly is always better than doing nothing perfectly.

Here's what to say, out loud. “Oh my god, there you are! I have been looking everywhere — come on, everyone is at the stage.”

And remember this. You do not need to be certain. You need to be the person who showed up.
