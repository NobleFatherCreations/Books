SCENARIO 8 — “The Promoter's Got A Deal For You”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let's talk about a particular kind of person. The promoter or "connector" who offers access — backstage, bookings, exposure — in exchange for labor, money, or loyalty that goes one direction. Common target: male artists, DJs, and performers hungry for the next level.

Here's the scene. He can get you on a bigger stage. He knows the right people. He wants to manage you — no contract yet, "let's feel this out." Three events later you've promoted his parties for free, paid your own travel, given him 30% of your merch "until you're established," and performed at two events where you were never paid what was agreed. The bigger stage keeps being three months away.

So watch for these. Handshake deals with no contract — "we're family, we don't need paperwork". Money and labor flow toward him; opportunity stays perpetually in the future. Raises the stakes of questioning him — "after everything I've done for you". Isolates you from other industry contacts — "those people don't understand your vision".

Here's the mechanism underneath it. Access is currency in the music and festival world and predatory promoters know exactly how to use it. The pattern: create genuine-feeling opportunity, establish dependency before paperwork, extract labor while the opportunity stays always ahead. The "family" framing removes the business structure that would protect you. Contracts are not distrust — they're what makes relationships honest.

So ask yourself this. I call it the A check. Ask: “Who controls my access to the next level — and what is that actually costing me?”

Now here's the part that's hard to hear. Get It In Writing — Every Time. Every booking, management agreement, revenue share — in writing, signed, before you show up. A basic performance contract covers: fee, payment timeline, cancellation terms, what you're responsible for. Music attorneys offer one-time consultations. Volunteer Lawyers for the Arts offers free/low-cost help. A legitimate promoter will have no problem signing. The ones who resist paperwork are the ones the paperwork was designed to protect you from.

So here's what you do. Opportunity is real. Opportunity without a contract is a liability. Get it in writing or keep walking.

Here's what to say, out loud. “I'm interested — let's put the basics in writing so we're both protected. I can send you a simple agreement tonight.”

And remember this. Any promoter who won't sign a contract is telling you what the deal actually is.
