You didn't do anything wrong. But the narrative is already in circulation on the circuit and your next event inquiries have gone quiet. The people spreading it are the same people who weren't paying you.

Your professional contacts become suddenly distant without clear reason. The people spreading the narrative are the same people you had a dispute with.

The goal is to make the cost of pushing back higher than the cost of compliance. It often works. So here's your check. I call it the S check. Ask: “Have I documented this situation in writing so I have something to point to?”

And here's what sits underneath all of it. Documentation Is Your Defense. Before any dispute escalates: screenshot every agreement, every payment confirmation, every communication. If payments are owed: send a written demand via email with terms attached — this creates a legal paper trail. If reputation damage is happening: keep records of what's being said, where, and when. Defamation (false statements presented as fact that damage your reputation) is actionable. Most predatory promoters rely on targets being too embarrassed or too tired to document. Document anyway.

So here's your move. Build independent circuit relationships. Document everything. Your reputation is built over time — one smear campaign doesn't end it.

And here's the truth underneath it. Your reputation is yours. It was built by your actual work. One person's narrative doesn't own it.
