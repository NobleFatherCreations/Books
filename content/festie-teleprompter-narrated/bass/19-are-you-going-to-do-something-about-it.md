SCENARIO 19 — “Are You Going To Do Something About It”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let me tell you about a specific type. Someone who wants this. Sometimes drunk and stupid, sometimes practised and calm, occasionally a pair working together — one to provoke and one to film. Has picked you because you responded to the first line rather than to the fifth.

Here's how it plays out. He has been at it for ten minutes. The comments started about the queue and are now about the woman you came with, and there are people watching, and he is asking whether you are going to do something about it in a tone that is doing exactly what it is designed to do. Your hands have already made the decision. There are two phones up.

So here's what to watch for. Escalates in public and specifically wants an audience. Moves to insults about the people you are with, because that works. Closes distance while keeping his hands visible and low. Somebody nearby is filming and started before anything happened. He is calmer than the situation warrants. You are being asked a question whose only wrong answer is engaging.

Now here's what's really going on. The provocation is the technique and the fight is the product. It produces a clip, a reputation, a claim of self-defence, or occasionally a legal position — and in every version of it the person who throws first is the person who loses, legally and practically. At a festival the additional problem is the ground: a single punch on hard standing, near a barrier, in the dark, after a night of drinking, is how people acquire a conviction or a headline. The people who do this are good at it and have done it before, which is why it feels so specifically calibrated to you.

So run this check. I call it the S check. Ask: “Is this a threat, or a performance aimed at getting a reaction?” — A real threat closes on you. A performance keeps asking questions.

And here's the darker reality. One Punch Is The Whole Rest Of It. The arithmetic is unforgiving and worth knowing while you are calm: a single punch that lands can kill somebody who falls badly, and campaigns in several countries exist for precisely that reason. The likeliest outcomes in descending order are that you are removed and lose your ticket, that you are arrested, that somebody is seriously hurt, or that you are the one on the ground. What is on the other side of not engaging is you being mildly embarrassed in front of strangers you will never see again. Men lose careers and lives to a four-second decision made because leaving felt worse than staying, and it never does the next morning.

So here's the play. Turn your body side-on, keep your hands open and visible, say nothing that answers the question, and move toward people and light. Take the group with you. Report it to security with a description — that is the response that costs him something and costs you nothing.

Here's exactly what to say. “Nah, you're alright, mate. — [to your group, walking] We're going. Come on.”

And here's the truth. He is offering you the chance to lose everything in front of an audience. Declining an invitation is not losing a fight.
