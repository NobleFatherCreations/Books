SCENARIO 8 — “The Check's Coming”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let me tell you about a specific type. Getting paid what you were promised, on the timeline agreed, is the baseline. It is also one of the most commonly violated terms in festival and live performance. Your leverage decreases dramatically after you've performed.

Here's how it plays out. "The accounting is still getting sorted — it'll be in your account by end of month." End of month: "we had some unexpected costs with the event, we're going to need to adjust the final payment." Or: the merch sales from your table were handled by their team and the reconciliation is $800 short of what you counted. Or: your 50% deposit never arrived but your show is tomorrow and the flights are booked.

So here's what to watch for. Deposit due before show hasn't arrived and you're too committed to pull out. Final payment delayed past agreed date without prior communication. Merch or door split reconciliation doesn't match your own count. "Unexpected costs" appear after the show as reasons for reduced payment.

Now here's what's really going on. Cash flow manipulation benefits the promoter while your leverage decreases once you've performed. That's why deposits exist. Get them before you travel. Count your own merch. Send a payment reminder email with terms on Day 1 after the show. Paper trail everything. Most non-payers rely on artists being too tired or too non-confrontational to pursue it.

So run this check. I call it the D check. Ask: “Do I have documentation of everything agreed financially — so I have something to point to when the money doesn’t show up on time?” — you performed. You get paid what was agreed. This is completely enforceable.

And here's the darker reality. When They Don't Pay — Your Options. Step 1: written demand email with contract attached, specific payment deadline (7-14 days). Step 2: small claims court handles disputes up to $10,000-$25,000 depending on state — filing fee usually under $100. Step 3: music attorney demand letter ($150-300) frequently resolves it. Step 4: American Federation of Musicians (afm.org) has a Claims & Collections department. Step 5: publicizing non-payment in musician communities (Indie on the Move, local music forums) is legal and effective. Document everything before going public.

So here's the play. 50% deposit before you travel. Count your own merch. Send payment follow-up in writing the morning after.

Here's exactly what to say. “Per our agreement — the remaining balance of [X] is due within 7 days of the show. Please confirm receipt of this.”

And here's the truth. You performed. You get paid what was agreed. This is completely enforceable.
