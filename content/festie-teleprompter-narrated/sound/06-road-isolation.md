SCENARIO 6 — “Road Isolation”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let's talk about the person you'll meet here. Touring creates a specific kind of isolation — from your home community, your support network, and the people who knew you before the music. That isolation makes you more dependent on the people immediately around you, which creates leverage for anyone on tour with you who wants to exploit it.

Here's the scene. You're four weeks into a run and you haven't had a real conversation with anyone from home in two weeks. The tour manager controls the schedule, the transport, the accommodation, and by extension a lot of how your days feel. The sound engineer has been making comments about your decisions. Your bandmate has a habit that's starting to affect the shows and nobody will address it. You feel completely dependent on the very people who are causing friction.

So watch for these. Your home support network feels increasingly remote and unreachable. One person on tour controls multiple aspects of your daily logistics and wellbeing. Decisions about your career are being made without your input during the run. You're too exhausted and too far from home to push back on anything.

Here's the mechanism underneath it. Road isolation is structural — touring literally removes you from your support network and puts you in a temporary closed system where the people with you have enormous power over your daily experience. This is not inherently predatory but it creates conditions that are easily exploited. Maintaining active connection to your home network, having at least one person on tour who is genuinely in your corner, and preserving your own decision-making authority are the protections.

So ask yourself this. I call it the U check. Ask: “When did I last have a real conversation with someone who knew me before the music?”

Now here's the part that's hard to hear. Maintaining Connection On The Road. Practical steps for staying connected during extended tours: (1) Weekly scheduled call with your most trusted person at home — protected in the schedule. (2) At least one person on tour who is genuinely your person, not the promoter's or label's. (3) Keep your own record of all agreements, all financial terms, all incidents — don't rely on tour management's records as your only documentation. (4) If something is happening on tour that feels wrong: name it to a trusted person at home who can help you think through it with outside perspective.

So here's what you do. Maintain active connection to your home network throughout the tour. Schedule it.

Here's what to say, out loud. [To your most trusted person at home]: "Can we do a weekly call on Sundays for the duration of this run? I need someone external to check in with."

And remember this. The road is a closed system. Keep one door open to the outside.
