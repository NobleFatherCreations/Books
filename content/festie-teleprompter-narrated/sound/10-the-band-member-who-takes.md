SCENARIO 10 — “The Band Member Who Takes”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Here's the person you'll meet here. The financial and power dynamics within a band or creative partnership are one of the most commonly neglected protection areas for musicians. Exploitation by a bandmate or creative partner is just as real as exploitation by a promoter — and harder to name because the relationship is closer.

Picture it. You write most of the songs. You book most of the shows. You handle most of the promotion. The split has always been equal. You never thought to question it — you're a band, you're family. Then a manager offers you a solo project and you realize that without you the band doesn't function, and with the band you're sharing everything you generate equally with people who generate a fraction of what you do.

Here are the signs. Contributions to the band's output and income are significantly unequal but compensation isn't. One member controls band finances, social media, or industry relationships. Leaving the band feels like losing ownership of work you created. Creative credit doesn't reflect actual creative contribution.

Now here's what's really happening. Band agreements are one of the most neglected legal documents in independent music. Who owns the name? Who owns the masters? What's the songwriting split? What happens when someone leaves? Who controls social media? Without answers in writing, whoever controls the logistics controls the band — and the person who does the most work often has the least formal protection. This isn't just about money. It's about who owns your creative legacy.

So here's your check. I call it the O check. Ask: “Do I know what I actually own in this creative partnership — in writing?”

And here's what sits underneath all of it. Band Agreement Basics. A band agreement should address: (1) who owns the band name and what happens to it if someone leaves. (2) Songwriting and publishing splits — and whether they're per-song or blanket. (3) Who owns the master recordings. (4) Revenue split for touring vs recording vs publishing vs merch. (5) Decision-making process — majority vote, unanimous, or designated authority. (6) Exit terms — what a departing member takes with them. Volunteer Lawyers for the Arts has band agreement templates. A music attorney review costs a fraction of what disputes cost later.

So here's your move. Get your band agreement in writing before you have a dispute about what you each own.

Here are the words. “I think we should get our band agreement written down so we're all protected — I can get a template and we can go through it together.”

And here's the truth underneath it. The band feels like family until there's money involved. The paperwork is how you protect the family.
