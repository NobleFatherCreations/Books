SCENARIO 16 — “The Ringing Stops Eventually”

Here's a specific type. The job itself. Monitors, the pit, load-in, the bus, three hundred nights a year, and a culture where asking for in-ears or wearing plugs still occasionally reads as not being committed.

Picture it. The ringing after a show used to last an hour and now it lasts until the afternoon. You have started asking people to repeat themselves in bars. You mix your own tracks slightly brighter than you used to, which is your ears telling you something you are choosing to hear as a preference.

Here are the signs. Tinnitus that persists longer after each run. Struggling with speech in noisy rooms. Turning monitors up across a tour rather than down. No custom moulded plugs, or a pair you have stopped using. Ear fatigue changing the mixing decisions you make. The idea that plugs would ruin the experience, which is the belief that costs the most.

Now here's what's really happening. Noise-induced hearing loss and tinnitus are the defining occupational injuries of this trade, they are cumulative, and they are permanent — there is no treatment that restores what is gone. The specific cruelty for musicians is that the damaged faculty is the instrument: the loss is not merely a disability, it degrades your ability to do the work, and it does so gradually enough that you will adjust to it rather than notice it. Custom moulded musicians' plugs attenuate evenly rather than muffling, which is the objection most people have and the one that is not true.

So here's your check. I call it the D check. Ask: “How long does the ringing last now compared with a year ago?” — Write it down. Trend is the measurement that matters.

And here's what sits underneath all of it. It Does Not Come Back. There is no recovery arc here and that is the whole point of the page. Hair cells do not regenerate, tinnitus that becomes permanent is permanent, and the endpoint for a significant number of people in this industry is a career ended by an injury that was completely preventable with a piece of equipment costing less than a single flight case. It is also the injury least likely to be taken seriously in the moment, because it does not hurt and there is nothing to see. Get a baseline audiogram now, get moulded plugs, use them at load-in and in the pit as well as on stage, and treat the trend as data rather than as ageing.

So here's your move. Get custom moulded musicians' plugs with interchangeable filters, and a baseline audiogram you repeat annually. Wear protection at load-in, soundcheck and in the crowd, not just on stage. Push for in-ears in your rider. Give your ears two quiet days after a run.

Here are the words. “In-ears are on my rider and they're not a nice-to-have — I'm protecting the thing I work with. — I've got moulded plugs in, I can hear you fine, they're flat.”

And here's the truth underneath it. Your ears are the instrument and they do not heal. The plugs cost less than one flight case.
