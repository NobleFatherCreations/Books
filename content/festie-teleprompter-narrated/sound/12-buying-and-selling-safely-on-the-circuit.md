SCENARIO 12 — “Buying And Selling Safely On The Circuit”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let's talk about a specific type. Musicians and touring artists face specific legal risks at festivals around buying, selling, and the substance culture of the touring life. This page is practical harm reduction for people who live on the circuit.

Here's the scene. After the show, in the green room, someone offers you something. You're still running on the set, the green room feels safe and private, and the person seems completely trustworthy — they've been on tour with you for weeks. The substances have been tested this run, you know what you're getting. Two hours later you're in a situation that's more complicated than the green room felt.

So watch for these. Green room transactions feel safer because they're "backstage" — they're not legally safer. Someone you trust on tour can still put you in a position of legal exposure. Substances offered in the context of a close working relationship still carry risk. The touring lifestyle normalizes substance use in ways that can gradually erode risk-assessment.

Here's the mechanism underneath it. Artists on tour are subject to the same drug laws as everyone else regardless of backstage location. Green room status does not create legal protection. The touring lifestyle creates normalization of substance use that can gradually erode your assessment of what's risky. Know what you're taking (test it), Know your legal exposure, and be aware that the people around you on tour have their own legal exposure to consider — which may or may not align with your interests.

So ask yourself this. I call it the U check. Ask: “Do I understand who is actually in this space and what the legal implications of this transaction actually are?” — backstage is not a legal gray zone just because it feels like one.

Now here's the part that's hard to hear. If You're Detained At A Festival Or Venue. Know this before you need it: (1) You have the right to remain silent — use it. "I'd like to speak with a lawyer." (2) Your manager or label does not automatically have the right to make legal decisions for you — that's your call. (3) Your tour rider and performance contract are your documents — you have the right to a copy of any contract you've signed. (4) Know the National Lawyers Guild (nlg.org) — they have observer programs at many major events. (5) Good Samaritan laws protect people seeking overdose help in most states — always call for medical help in an overdose situation.

So here's what you do. Know your legal exposure before any situation requires you to think about it.

Here's what to say, out loud. “I'd like to speak with a lawyer before I answer any questions.”

And remember this. The backstage is not a legal gray zone. Know your rights.
