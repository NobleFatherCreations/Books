SCENARIO 2 — “Let Me Manage You”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let me tell you about the person you'll meet here. The manager who positions themselves between you and every other industry relationship. "Let me handle this" becomes "let me control this" becomes "I am the only person who understands your vision." By the time you see it, most of your independent contacts are gone.

Here's how it plays out. She approached you after a set that went off. Clearly connected. "I think you're three months away from something major and I want to be the person who gets you there." No management contract yet. A year later: she takes 25% of everything including merch you handle yourself, discourages direct relationships with bookers ("let me handle it, it's cleaner"), and when you question any of it the conversation turns to everything she's done for you.

So here's what to watch for. Discourages direct relationships with bookers, labels, promoters — "let me handle it". Commission structure expands beyond agreed terms without renegotiation. Questions about finances or terms received as betrayal or ingratitude. Industry contacts you had before have been quietly replaced by her relationships.

Now here's what's really going on. Predatory management isolates artists from their industry network under the cover of professionalism. When your entire industry network runs through one person, that person has complete leverage. Standard management commission is 15-20% of gross income from work THEY source — not your merch, not your pre-existing relationships. Know that standard before any conversation.

So run this check. I call it the N check. Ask: “Can I talk directly to my booker or label — or does everything go through them first?”

And here's the darker reality. Management Contract Basics. A legitimate management contract specifies: commission rate and what it applies to, term length (1–2 years standard — not open-ended), sunset clause (they earn commission on deals they originated after the contract ends, for a defined period), and termination conditions for both parties. No management contract should give them power of attorney or control over your bank accounts. California Talent Agencies Act and similar state laws regulate management. Music attorney consultation before signing — non-negotiable.

So here's the play. Maintain direct relationships with at least 3 industry contacts independently of any manager. Always.

Here's exactly what to say. "I appreciate you handling things — I also keep direct relationships with my bookers. That's just how I work."

And here's the truth. A manager who needs to be your only industry contact needs to be your former manager.
