SCENARIO 4 — “After Party At Mine”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Here's a specific type. The post-show window is one of the highest-risk periods for performing artists. Adrenaline crash, endorphin flood, crowds wanting proximity — and a specific category of person who knows exactly how to exploit that window.

Picture it. You just came off a set that felt transcendent. Everyone backstage is warm and electric. Someone from the crowd has somehow gotten backstage — through who, you're not sure. They're beautiful and clearly want to be near you and the attention feels like an extension of the performance high. Two hours later, in a much quieter context with a much clearer head, you're trying to reconstruct how the decisions of the last two hours happened.

Here are the signs. People in backstage spaces you don't know how they got there. The post-show adrenaline state is specifically targeted — decisions here are impaired. Someone who zeroes in on you immediately post-performance before you've landed. An entourage that appears after every show and gradually never leaves.

Now here's what's really happening. The post-performance state is a genuine altered state — high adrenaline, endorphin flood, identity in flux between performer-self and regular-self. Predatory fans and industry people know this window intimately and specifically target it. As the performer you're also in the position of power in that space — which means your responsibility is higher, not lower. This page is simultaneously about protecting yourself AND about not using the post-show window to cover your own behavior.

So here's your check. I call it the U check. Ask: “Who is actually in this space — do I know how they got here and what they want?”

And here's what sits underneath all of it. Green Room Protocol — Yours To Set. As the artist, you set the terms of your backstage space. Legitimate protocols: explicit guest list with named individuals only (not "plus whoever"), a trusted person who manages access, a minimum 30-minute closed cooldown after you come off before socializing, and a clear policy communicated to promoters before the show about who can be back there. Your rider can include backstage access requirements. Use it.

So here's your move. Land first. Decompress with your core people. Then decide who you want in your space from a clearer head.

Here are the words. [To your team before the show]: "30-minute closed cooldown after we come off — then we open the room. Can you hold that?"

And here's the truth underneath it. The post-show high is real and beautiful. It is also the window people have been waiting all night to exploit.
