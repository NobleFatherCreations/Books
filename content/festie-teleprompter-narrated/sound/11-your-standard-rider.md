SCENARIO 11 — “Your Standard Rider”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let me tell you about someone you'll recognize. Your rider is not just technical requirements. It is the document that defines your professional terms, protects your rights, and sets the expectations for every engagement before you arrive. Most musicians don't use it fully. This page changes that.

Here's how it plays out. You've been showing up to gigs and figuring things out on arrival — the recording situation, the merch arrangement, the accommodation, the payment timing. Each thing individually seems manageable to negotiate in person. Collectively, negotiating all of these while you're also trying to prepare for a performance means you're always behind and always slightly at a disadvantage. Your rider is supposed to solve this before you arrive.

So here's what to watch for. Technical requirements: sound system specs, monitoring, load-in time, soundcheck time. Financial terms: fee, payment method and timing, deposit requirements and deadline. Recording and broadcast: explicit statement that Artist retains all recording rights. Accommodation and hospitality: what's provided, what's not, quality standards.

Now here's what's really going on. A comprehensive rider serves two functions: it communicates your professional requirements AND it creates a paper trail of what was agreed. When things don't match the rider at the venue, you have documentation of what was promised. When recording happens without your agreement, you have documentation of what your rights are. The rider is not a diva document — it's a professional standard that protects both parties.

So run this check. I call it the S check. Ask: “Does my rider include recording rights language, payment terms, and all my technical requirements, spelled out in writing?” — the rider is not arrogance. It’s professionalism, and it protects both sides of the booking.

And here's the darker reality. What A Complete Rider Covers. Technical rider: stage plot, input list, monitoring requirements, backline requirements, load-in/soundcheck/show times. Business rider: fee and payment terms, deposit amount and deadline, cancellation policy, accommodation and transportation specs, press photo and bio requirements. Rights rider: recording rights clause (you retain all), broadcast rights, how your name can be used in promotion. Hospitality rider: green room requirements, food and beverage, guest list. Template riders are available from AFM (afm.org) and from music attorney blogs. Customize and use yours consistently.

So here's the play. Send your rider with every booking confirmation. Update it annually. Use it as your baseline.

Here's exactly what to say. “I'll send my rider along with the signed contract — let me know if anything needs discussion before the show.”

And here's the truth. The rider is not arrogance. It is professionalism. It protects you and it protects the promoter.
