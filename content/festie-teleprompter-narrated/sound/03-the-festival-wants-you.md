SCENARIO 3 — “The Festival Wants You”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let's talk about someone you'll recognize. The booking that changes significantly between offer and show — stage size, set time, payment, production support — revealed after you've committed flights, accommodation, and your promotional energy to announcing the show.

Here's the scene. "We're really excited to have you — you'll be on the Garden Stage, Saturday evening set, solid slot." You announce it, post it, your fans make plans. Two weeks before the show: "there have been some lineup adjustments — we've moved you to the Morning Stage, Sunday 11am." The ticket buyers for your slot have already made travel plans. Pulling out now costs you more than accepting the downgrade.

So watch for these. Details confirmed verbally don't match the written confirmation when it arrives. "Adjustments" happen after you've publicly announced and promoted the show. Changes arrive late enough that backing out carries significant cost. Each change is framed as minor — the cumulative impact is significant.

Here's the mechanism underneath it. Bait and switch bookings work by getting your public commitment before you have the details in writing. Once you've announced a show to your audience, backing out has real social and professional cost — which the promoter counts on. The protection is simple: nothing goes on your social media until you have the booking confirmation in writing, with all material details (stage, set time, date, fee) confirmed. No exceptions.

So ask yourself this. I call it the S check. Ask: “Have I confirmed every material detail of this booking in writing before I announce it to my own audience?” — once you’ve announced it, the leverage in the negotiation is gone, and it’s gone in their favor.

Now here's the part that's hard to hear. Announce Only After Written Confirmation. The sequence for any festival booking: (1) Verbal offer received. (2) Written confirmation requested — stage, set time, date, fee, production details. (3) Contract signed. (4) Deposit received. (5) Announcement made. Skipping any of these steps removes your leverage. Promoters who push you to announce before paperwork is signed are using your audience as collateral. Your fans are excited to see you — that excitement belongs to you, not to a promoter who hasn't put anything in writing yet.

So here's what you do. Nothing gets announced until you have written confirmation of every material detail.

Here's what to say, out loud. “I'd love to announce this — can you send me the written confirmation with stage, set time, and fee first?”

And remember this. Your audience's excitement is yours. Don't let it become someone else's leverage.
