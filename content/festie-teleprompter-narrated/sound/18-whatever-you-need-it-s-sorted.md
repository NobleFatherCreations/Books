SCENARIO 18 — “Whatever You Need, It's Sorted”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let's talk about the person you'll meet here. The promoter, the tour manager, or the local fixer whose hospitality is unusually comprehensive and who never seems to want paying for that part of it.

Here's the scene. The rider is fine and there is something else on the table that was not on it, and he says it is sorted, and this happens at every one of his shows. By the fourth booking you are aware that you look forward to his shows for a reason that is not the audience, and that the conversation about the fee has become a conversation you are less inclined to have.

So watch for these. Substances provided as hospitality, routinely and unasked. Never itemized, never charged, never discussed. Availability increases around contract or payment conversations. Supply is tied to continuing to work with this person specifically. Your consumption has escalated in a way that tracks the touring schedule. You have stopped pushing on money and cannot say exactly when that started.

Here's the mechanism underneath it. Supply is one of the oldest forms of leverage in this industry and it works without a single explicit exchange being proposed. It creates reciprocal debt, it degrades judgement during exactly the conversations where your judgement matters commercially, and it establishes a dependency that is tied to one relationship. It also builds an accumulating hold: somebody who has supplied you for two years knows things about your consumption that they can allude to, and that hold does not require any intention at the time to be usable later.

So ask yourself this. I call it the N check. Ask: “Would I push as hard on this contract if the hospitality stopped?” — If the answer is yes, the hospitality is doing business.

Now here's the part that's hard to hear. The Supply Is A Record Of You. The part that arrives years later is the knowledge. A person who has supplied you routinely has a detailed, private account of your use, and at the point where you want to leave, renegotiate, or raise something about their conduct, that account exists and both of you know it. Nothing has to be said explicitly; the industry is full of artists who simply found it easier not to push. Keep your own supply if you use, decline provided substances as a flat policy rather than a judgement, and keep the money conversation in writing and in daylight where the hospitality cannot reach it.

So here's what you do. Make it a standing policy and say it early so it is not about anyone: your rider is your rider, and you do not take anything else from promoters. Do contract and money conversations by email, sober, before the show rather than after it.

Here's what to say, out loud. “Appreciate it, I keep it to what's on the rider — blanket rule for me, nothing personal. — Let's do the fee by email in the morning rather than tonight.”

And remember this. Hospitality that is never itemized is not hospitality. It is an account being opened in your name.
