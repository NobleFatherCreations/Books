SCENARIO 17 — “When You Have To Put Hands On Someone”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let me tell you about a particular kind of person. Every volunteer who has ever had to stop somebody walking into a road, hold a stretcher, move somebody who is unconscious, or get between two people who are fighting — with a two-hour briefing and no qualification whatsoever.

Here's how it plays out. He is unconscious and needs moving out of the crowd, or he is trying to climb something, or two people are going at it and you are the nearest person in hi-vis and everybody has turned to look at you. You have never been trained in any of this and you are about to do it anyway because nobody else is going to.

So here's what to watch for. No restraint or manual-handling training, and no licence. You are the nearest hi-vis and that has become an instruction. Someone unconscious being moved by well-meaning bystanders. Face-down or compressed positioning, which is the lethal one. No radio call made before hands went on. Nobody has told you what you are and are not permitted to do.

Now here's what's really going on. Untrained physical intervention is the highest-consequence thing an unqualified volunteer can do, and it happens constantly because the hi-vis creates an expectation the training never met. Two specifics are worth knowing above everything else. Positional asphyxia — holding or leaving someone face-down or folded, especially if they are intoxicated, large, or struggling — kills people, and it is the single most documented cause of restraint-related death. And an unconscious person who is breathing goes into the recovery position on their side, not face-up and not face-down, because the airway is the whole problem.

So run this check. I call it the H check. Ask: “Am I trained for what I am about to do?” — If not, the intervention is a radio call and a presence, not a hold.

And here's the darker reality. Face Down Is The One That Kills. If you remember one line from this guide, make it this. Never hold or leave anybody face-down, and never fold somebody forward while restraining them, and never sit or kneel on a back or a chest. Positional asphyxia gives almost no warning: the person stops struggling, and everyone present reads that as calming down when it is the opposite. If someone is unconscious and breathing, they go on their side. If they are struggling, you get a trained responder and you keep space rather than keeping hold. Your job in every one of these is to call it in fast, keep the area clear, and stay — presence and a radio have saved far more people at festivals than grip ever has.

So here's the play. Radio first, always, before hands. Use presence and space rather than contact. If someone is unconscious and breathing, recovery position and stay with them. Never face-down, never weight on a torso. Ask your supervisor on day one exactly what you are authorized to do physically, and get the answer.

Here's exactly what to say. “Control, I need a medic and security at the north bar now, one male unconscious but breathing, I'm with him. — [to bystanders] Give us room please, don't move him, I've got help coming.”

And here's the truth. A radio and your presence are the intervention. Hands are the part you were never trained for and the part that kills people.
