SCENARIO 14 — “Staff Culture That Protects People”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let me tell you about the person you'll meet here. The culture of a festival's staff team is one of the most important determinants of how safe the event actually is for everyone. This page is about what healthy staff culture looks like — and how to build it from wherever you are in the organizational structure.

Here's how it plays out. The briefing before gates open sets the tone. The lead says: "Our job this weekend is to hold the space so everyone can have the experience they came for. That means we take every concern seriously, we report what we see, we take care of each other so we can take care of everyone else, and we never dismiss a gut feeling. If something feels off — name it to your team. There are no bad reports. There are only missed patterns." That briefing changes how the whole weekend goes.

So here's what to watch for. Leadership explicitly states that all concerns will be taken seriously — not managed down. Staff are encouraged to name gut feelings and concerns without requiring certainty. Debrief and staff wellbeing are treated as operational priorities not afterthoughts. Accountability runs in all directions — leadership is held to the same standards as front-line staff.

Now here's what's really going on. Healthy staff culture is built from the first briefing and maintained through how leadership responds to the first concern that is raised. Organizations where leadership takes concerns seriously, supports staff wellbeing, and holds itself to the same standards it holds front-line workers create genuinely safer events. Organizations that suppress concerns, burn out volunteers, and deflect accountability downward create events where harm is more likely and less likely to be addressed.

So run this check. I call it the O check. Ask: “Does the culture of this staff team make it easy or hard to raise concerns?” — that tells you a great deal about how safe this event actually is.

And here's the darker reality. How To Build Healthy Staff Culture From Your Position. Regardless of where you sit in the organization: (1) Model taking gut feelings seriously by naming yours out loud. "Something about that felt off to me — I am going to document it." (2) Celebrate colleagues who raise concerns rather than treating them as troublemakers. (3) Debrief after difficult shifts even when leadership does not require it. (4) Give honest post-event feedback to the organization about what worked and what did not. (5) If you are in leadership: the first time a concern is dismissed sets the culture for every concern that follows. Take them all seriously.

So here's the play. Build the culture you want to work in. It starts with how you respond to the first concern you hear.

Here's exactly what to say. “I want to hear about that — tell me what you saw and let us figure out together what to do with it.”

And here's the truth. The safest events are the ones with staff teams who look out for each other as fiercely as they look out for attendees.
