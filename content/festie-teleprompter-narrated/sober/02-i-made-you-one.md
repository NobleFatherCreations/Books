SCENARIO 2 — “I Made You One”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let's talk about someone you'll recognize. Anyone holding a cup you did not watch being filled. Usually a friend being kind. Occasionally not — and the second category is why the rule has to apply to the first, because you cannot tell them apart by looking.

Here's the scene. Someone hands you a lemonade. They are being nice, they know you do not drink, they went and got it specially. Refusing it feels ungrateful and slightly insulting and like you are accusing a friend of something. It also costs nothing, and the alternative — a drink you cannot vouch for, in a body with no tolerance, in front of people who will later say you seemed fine — costs a great deal.

So watch for these. A drink arriving already poured, that you did not see made. Insistence when you set it down or ask for a sealed one. Attention to whether and how much you are drinking it. “It's only soft” as an argument for skipping the rule. Someone treating the rule as an accusation rather than a policy. Any drink left unattended, including yours, including for a minute.

Here's the mechanism underneath it. The most common drink adulterant is alcohol added to something someone believes is soft, which matters enormously here: for a sober or in-recovery person that is not a lesser version of spiking, it is the specific harm. Zero tolerance means a small dose lands hard, and the aftermath is not only physical — it can register internally as a relapse, with all the shame that carries, even though nothing about it was chosen. The rule is the same one this whole book gives everyone: you pour it, you watch it poured, or you carry it sealed. The difference is that for you the failure mode includes losing your sense of your own recovery.

So ask yourself this. I call it the O check. Ask: “Did I watch this being made, or open it myself?” — If not, it is not a drink, it is an unknown.

Now here's the part that's hard to hear. When It Registers As A Relapse. Here is the part worth having read in advance, because it matters in the hour it happens: if you are dosed without knowing, that is not a relapse. It is an assault. Your body may not distinguish, your recovery community's language may not distinguish, and at four in the morning you will absolutely not distinguish — which is exactly why a person who wanted you compliant and quiet would choose this method. The shame does the silencing for them. Decide now, while it is hypothetical: if it happens, the first call is to somebody in your recovery network and the second is to medical, and neither of those calls is a confession. Say it out loud once so the sober version of you has already made the ruling.

So here's what you do. Carry your own, sealed, and open it yourself. Decline anything handed to you already poured, cheerfully and as a blanket policy rather than as a judgment of the person offering. If you set a drink down, it is finished.

Here's what to say, out loud. “Ah, cheers — I've got a weird rule where I only drink stuff I've opened myself, nothing personal. I'll grab one with you though.”

And remember this. Being dosed is not drinking. Decide that now, in daylight, so you are not deciding it at four in the morning.
