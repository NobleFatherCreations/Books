SCENARIO 1 — “One Won't Hurt You”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let me tell you about the person you'll meet here. Usually not malicious. Frequently drunk. Genuinely believes he is being welcoming, and is also, underneath it, uncomfortable that somebody nearby is not doing what he is doing. Persistent in a way he would not be about anything else.

Here's how it plays out. Fourth time tonight. He is holding it out again, doing the thing where he waggles it slightly, saying he is not trying to pressure you but it is a festival, and asking — warmly, in front of five people — what actually happened, like your sobriety is a story he is owed and the story will settle whether your no is reasonable.

So here's what to watch for. Asks more than once after a clear answer. Wants the reason, and frames your reason as the thing that would justify the no. Does it in front of an audience, so declining has a social cost. Reframes your boundary as a mood you could be talked out of. “Just this once”, “it's a festival”, “you're no fun like this” Gets cold or wounded when the fourth no lands.

Now here's what's really going on. The pressure is usually about him rather than about you — a person drinking heavily next to someone who is not is being handed an unwelcome mirror, and the fastest way to put the mirror down is to get you to join. That does not make it harmless. Repeated asking is how boundaries get worn rather than broken, and the reason-demand is the actual move: once you are explaining, you are in a negotiation, and negotiations have outcomes. You are not in a negotiation. The most effective response is also the most boring one, which is the same three words, at the same volume, indefinitely, with no new information added.

So run this check. I call it the S check. Ask: “Am I explaining?” — The moment you start supplying reasons, you have accepted that your no needs approving. It does not.

And here's the darker reality. Some Of Them Know Exactly What You Are. Most of the people doing this are just drunk. A few are not, and the difference shows in what they do with the information once you give it. Someone who learns you are in recovery and keeps pushing is not being socially clumsy — they have been told there is a specific cost to you and have decided it is worth it, and a person who wants somebody impaired has just found the one person here whose impairment would come with built-in silence. This is why the reason is worth keeping to yourself in a crowd. Not shame — information discipline. Your recovery is not a secret; it is also not something to hand to a stranger who has already ignored three noes.

So here's the play. Repeat the same three words with no escalation and no new content, and move your body — turn a shoulder, go get water, join a different conversation. Broken-record plus physical exit ends it faster than any argument, because there is nothing to push against.

Here's exactly what to say. “I'm good. — I'm good. — I'm good, mate. Anyway, has anyone seen Priya?”

And here's the truth. Your no does not improve with an explanation attached. “I'm good” is already the strongest version of it.
