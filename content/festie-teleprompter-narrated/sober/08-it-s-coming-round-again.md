Let's talk about a particular kind of person. The circle itself. The joint, the bottle, the balloon, the vape — a physical object moving clockwise, arriving at you every few minutes, requiring a small public decision each time, for six hours.

Here's the scene. It is not one refusal, it is the fortieth, and by the fortieth you have stopped saying anything and started doing the small hand movement, and somebody has noticed and said something kind about it which made it worse, and you are now thinking about leaving a fire you were enjoying because the geometry of the circle has made your sobriety the most visible thing about you all night.

So watch for these. The object physically arrives at you on a loop. Each pass is a small public moment you have to handle. Somebody kindly announces your sobriety on your behalf. The circle re-forms around you in a way that makes leaving obvious. You are exhausted by evening and cannot say why. You have started avoiding fires you actually like.

Here's the mechanism underneath it. Refusal fatigue is real and underrated: the cost of any single no is negligible and the cost of forty is significant, particularly when each one happens in front of people and requires a micro-decision about tone. It is also the mechanism by which sober people quietly stop going to the parts of a festival they enjoy most, which produces exactly the isolation that everything else in this guide warns about. The circle is not doing anything to you. The geometry is doing it, and geometry can be changed.

So ask yourself this. I call it the B check. Ask: “Am I avoiding somewhere I like because of the effort of being there?” — That is the cost showing up, and it is fixable without leaving.

Now here's the part that's hard to hear. Do Not Let It Push You Out. The outcome to prevent is not any single awkward pass, it is the slow withdrawal — skipping the fire, skipping the camp, spending the weekend at the edges, and ending up alone at a festival, which is the precondition for every pattern in this guide. That withdrawal is invisible while it happens because each individual decision is reasonable. If you find yourself three nights in and mostly on your own, that is not you being antisocial; it is forty small refusals adding up. Fix the mechanics rather than removing yourself: sit at the end of the arc, hold your own cup, and let one friend know to pass it over you.

So here's what you do. Change the geometry — sit at the end of the rotation rather than mid-circle, keep both hands busy with your own drink, and ask one friend to skip you so the object never arrives. Pre-empt once, lightly, at the start of the night, so nobody announces it for you later.

Here's what to say, out loud. “Pass it over me all night, saves us both forty conversations. — [holding up your cup] I'm sorted, cheers.”

And remember this. Forty small refusals cost more than one big one. Move your chair rather than leaving the fire.
