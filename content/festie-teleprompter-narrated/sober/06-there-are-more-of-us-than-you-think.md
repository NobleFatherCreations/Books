SCENARIO 6 — “There Are More Of Us Than You Think”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Here's someone you'll recognize. The Wharf Rats with the yellow balloons. The Phellowship. The sober camp with the coffee urn. The Zendo volunteers. The ones who have been coming for thirty years and have not had a drink for twenty-five of them.

Picture it. You assume you are the only one. You are not remotely the only one — there is a meeting on this site, probably daily, probably signposted with a yellow balloon or a hand-lettered board, and the people at it have been navigating exactly this weekend for longer than you have been alive. Most of them found it the same way, which is late, having assumed the same thing you are assuming.

Here are the signs. Yellow balloons at the back of the crowd — that is the Wharf Rats, and it is an invitation. A meeting board, a sober camp, a coffee urn where a bar would be. Harm reduction volunteers, a lot of whom are sober themselves. People who clock your soda and say nothing, which is the good sign. Real: nobody asks for your story as the price of entry. Real: you can go, say nothing, drink the coffee, and leave.

Now here's what's really happening. Sober infrastructure inside festival culture is decades old and largely invisible unless you are looking for it — the Wharf Rats go back to the early eighties, the Phellowship not much later, and most large festivals now have something. The reason it matters more than any individual tactic in this guide is that isolation is the common factor underneath every pattern here: the convincer works on someone with no one to turn to, the assigned care role works on someone who does not know other sober people are around, and the engineered slip works on someone cut off from their network. Finding these people on Thursday removes the precondition for all three at once.

So here's your check. I call it the B check. Ask: “Do I know where the sober meeting is on this site — today, not hypothetically?” — Find it before you need it. That is the whole page.

And here's what sits underneath all of it. Isolation Is The Shared Precondition. Go back through this guide and the same thing is underneath every page. The convincer is only effective on somebody standing alone. The care role only becomes total when nobody else here is sober. The slip becomes leverage only when one person is the only one who knows. Every single mechanism requires you to be the exception in the room — which is exactly the belief you arrived with, and exactly the belief that a hand-lettered board at the back of the field disproves in about ninety seconds. Find them Thursday. Not because you are fragile, but because being findable is what makes the rest of this book mostly theoretical for you.

So here's your move. Locate the meeting, the sober camp, or the balloons in your first few hours on site, and go once when things are fine. Tell two people in your group you are sober so it is ordinary rather than a revelation at a bad moment. Then have the weekend you came for.

Here are the words. “Any sober meeting on site this year? — Just so you know, I don't drink, it's not a whole thing, I'm just easier to find at the coffee than the bar.”

And here's the truth underneath it. You are not the only sober person at this festival. You are the only one who has not found the others yet, and that takes about ten minutes.
