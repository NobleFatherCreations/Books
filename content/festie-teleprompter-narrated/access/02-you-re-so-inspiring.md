SCENARIO 2 — “You're So Inspiring”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let me tell you about a particular kind of person. Approaches with enormous warmth and a compliment about your existence rather than anything you did. Talks about your courage within ninety seconds of meeting you. Wants to tell you what seeing you here means to them, at length, and wants you to receive it.

Here's how it plays out. You are in the line for water. A woman you have never met takes your hand — takes it, does not ask — and tells you that seeing you out here has genuinely changed her whole weekend, that she was complaining about the walk and then she saw you, and she just had to say it. She is crying a little. Three people are watching. You have about four seconds to decide whether to be gracious about a thing you did not agree to.

So here's what to watch for. Compliments your existence rather than anything you chose or made. Touches you, your chair, or your service animal while saying it. Uses words like brave, courage, inspiring about you getting through a normal day. Wants a photograph, sometimes before asking your name. Positions your presence as being about their emotional experience. Reacts badly to a flat “thanks” — the gratitude is what was being purchased.

Now here's what's really going on. Inspiration framing converts a person into a symbol, and symbols do not get to have boundaries. Once you are a source of someone's uplift, declining anything reads as cruelty — you are refusing to be the good thing that happened to them today. It is usually not predatory. It is usually a person feeling something clumsily out loud. But it does exactly the same work as a deliberate opening move: it establishes touch without consent, creates an audience that expects grace from you, and installs a debt in a stranger's favor inside two minutes. People who are running something deliberate use it because it works, and because the follow-up — I just feel a connection with you — is already half-built.

So run this check. I call it the A check. Ask: “Is this about something I did, or about what my existence is doing for them?” — Warmth about your work is a compliment. Warmth about your existence is a job you did not apply for.

And here's the darker reality. The Audience Is The Trap. The spectators are not incidental — they are the pressure. A stranger who wanted a private moment would not do this in a queue. Doing it in front of people means any “please let go of my hand” has a cost, and both of you know it. This is the same architecture as the public grand gesture that makes a no expensive, and it is the reason the follow-up ask so often arrives about ninety seconds later, while the audience is still there and still approving. You are allowed to be the one who breaks the scene. The discomfort belongs to the person who started it.

So here's the play. Answer the content, not the frame, and keep your hands to yourself first: pull back, say something flat and unmemorable, redirect to a task. Flatness is the tool. A symbol cannot survive a boring answer.

Here's exactly what to say. “Ah, thanks. I'm just here for the music, same as you. — Hey, could you pass me that?”

And here's the truth. You did not come here to be somebody's feeling. Being ordinary in public is a thing you are allowed to insist on.
