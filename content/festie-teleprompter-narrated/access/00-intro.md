This guide is the one called A.C.C.E.S.S. — the Disabled & Neurodivergent Edition. And it exists for a specific reason. The scene says everyone belongs. This guide is for making that true for you — and for spotting the person who uses your access needs as a door.

So let me walk you through what A.C.C.E.S.S. stands for, because these 6 ideas are the frame that everything else in this guide runs on.

The letter A stands for “Access Is Not A Favor.” Your accommodations are not gifts. Anyone who frames them as generosity has set up a debt you never agreed to — and a thing they can withdraw.

The first C stands for “Control Of Your Equipment Is Yours.” Your chair, your cane, your meds, your hearing aids, your AAC device. Nobody moves, holds, or “looks after” them without you saying so, every time.

The last C stands for “Capacity Changes And That Is Data.” A yes at hour two is not a yes at hour twenty. Fatigue, pain, and sensory load all move the line. Somebody tracking when you are most depleted is not being attentive.

The letter E stands for “Exit Requires A Route.” An exit you cannot physically use is not an exit. Know how you leave — terrain, distance, lighting, who pushes — before you agree to be anywhere.

The first S stands for “Support People Are Chosen.” Whoever helps you should be someone you picked, who can be replaced without your weekend collapsing. One irreplaceable helper is a single point of failure.

The last S stands for “Sensory Overload Can Be Aimed.” Sometimes the wall of sound and light is just the festival. Sometimes it is where you were walked on purpose, because you decide worse there and you both know it.

That's the framework. Access. Control. Capacity. Exit. Support. Sensory. 6 ideas, and they hold this whole thing together.
