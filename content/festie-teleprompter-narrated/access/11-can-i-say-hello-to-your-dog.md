SCENARIO 11 — “Can I Say Hello To Your Dog”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let me tell you about the person you'll meet here. Mostly delighted, drunk, well-meaning people, arriving at a rate of roughly one every four minutes all weekend. Occasionally somebody who has worked out that distracting the dog is a way to get at you.

Here's how it plays out. She is in her vest and she is working and a man crouches down in front of her going hey, hey gorgeous, clicking his fingers, and behind him two other people are also crouching, and one of them is offering her a chip. Your dog's whole job is to notice things about you and she has just spent forty seconds noticing him instead.

So here's what to watch for. Approaching, touching, feeding or calling a dog in a working vest. Continuing after being told she is working. Treating your no as unfriendliness rather than as a medical instruction. Using the dog as an opening to keep talking to you. Letting their own off-lead dog approach yours. Photographing her, or you, without asking.

Now here's what's really going on. A distracted assistance dog is a disabled piece of equipment. An alert dog that misses a signal, a guide dog that loses the line, a mobility dog that breaks position — these are functional failures with real consequences, and a festival supplies distraction at a density found almost nowhere else. Feeding is the most dangerous version because it can make a working animal ill for the rest of the weekend, which removes your support entirely. The legal position in most places is clear that interference is not permitted; the practical position is that fifty people a day will do it anyway.

So run this check. I call it the C check. Ask: “Is my equipment currently able to do its job?” — A dog is equipment while the vest is on, and this is the one piece of equipment strangers will touch without asking.

And here's the darker reality. The Dog As An Approach. There is a version of this that is not about the dog at all. An assistance animal is a guaranteed conversation starter with a person who cannot easily leave, and somebody who wants sustained access to you has an unlimited, socially approved reason to keep returning — asking after her, bringing her water, wanting a photo. It is the inspiration approach from earlier in this guide with a better cover story, and it is harder to refuse because refusing looks like you are being precious about a dog everybody can see is lovely. Watch for the person who comes back. Once is a stranger; the fourth time is a pattern.

So here's the play. Put a bright DO NOT DISTRACT patch on the vest so the sentence is pre-said, and use a flat one-liner without a smile so it does not open a conversation. Keep her water and her food entirely under your control, and do not accept treats from anyone.

Here's exactly what to say. “She's working, please don't — and please don't feed her, it makes her ill. — [flat, turning away] Thanks.”

And here's the truth. She is not a pet this weekend. She is the reason you can be here, and a stranger's forty seconds of fun costs you her attention.
