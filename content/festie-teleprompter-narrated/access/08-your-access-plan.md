SCENARIO 8 — “Your Access Plan”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let me tell you about someone you'll recognize. This page is the boring one and it is the one that works. Everything here is done before you arrive, in a calm room, by a version of you who is not tired, not overloaded, and not being asked anything by anyone.

Here's how it plays out. It is the Tuesday before. You have fifteen minutes and a notes app. Everything that will go wrong on Saturday night is currently a thing you can fix in one sentence, and on Saturday night it will not be, because the whole point of Saturday night is that you will not have the capacity to invent a plan from nothing.

So here's what to watch for. Terrain and distance: where you sleep, where the accessible toilets are, how far, what surface. The two-person rota: who does what, and who is the backup for each. Meds on your body, spare elsewhere, photo of the label and dose on your phone. The quiet places, located on the map before you need one. The accessibility coordinator's name and contact, obtained on arrival. One off-site person who expects a check-in and will notice if it does not come.

Now here's what's really going on. Everything in this guide is easier to counter in advance than in the moment, because every mechanism in it works by targeting a depleted version of you. Pre-commitment is the general answer: decisions made Tuesday hold on Saturday in a way that Saturday's decisions do not. This is also the part where you get to stop treating your access needs as a negotiation. Written down as logistics — surfaces, distances, timings, names — they stop being a thing you justify to people and become a thing you operate. That shift in framing is doing as much work as any individual item on the list.

So run this check. I call it the E check. Ask: “Can I answer, right now, how I physically leave from where I will be tonight?” — If not, that is the fifteen minutes.

And here's the darker reality. The Plan Is For The Version Of You Who Cannot Make One. Here is the reason this page is not optional filler. Every mechanism in this guide — the helper who consolidates, the quiet place that is private, the ask at the bottom of an overload — works by arriving when your capacity is lowest. None of them work on Tuesday. That asymmetry is the whole game, and the plan is the only move that plays on your side of it, because it lets a rested version of you make the decisions and hands them to a depleted version who only has to follow. People skip this page because nothing has gone wrong yet. That is precisely when it costs fifteen minutes instead of a weekend.

So here's the play. Write the six lines above in a note titled with the festival's name. Share it with your two people before you travel. Photograph your meds. Get the coordinator's contact at the gate, not on Sunday.

Here's exactly what to say. “Here's my access plan for the weekend — two minutes to read. Tell me if you can't cover the bit I've put you down for, that's completely fine, I'd just rather know now.”

And here's the truth. The plan is not because you expect something to go wrong. The plan is so that a tired version of you does not have to invent one.
