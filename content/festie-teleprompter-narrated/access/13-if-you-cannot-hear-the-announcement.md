SCENARIO 13 — “If You Cannot Hear The Announcement”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Here's a specific type. For Deaf and hard-of-hearing attendees, and for anyone whose hearing aids or implants have to survive four days of dust, rain and a hundred and ten decibels.

Picture it. Every piece of safety information at a festival is delivered by tannoy: the weather warning, the stage closure, the evacuation route, the missing child, the thing that has just happened at the other end of the site. All of it is audio, all of it is over a distorted PA, and none of it reaches you. You will find out what happened when the crowd starts moving.

Here are the signs. Emergency and safety announcements are audio-only with no visual equivalent. No interpreter listed, and no text alert or app notification option. Staff default to talking louder rather than to writing it down. Your aids or processor have no dry storage, spare batteries, or backup. Nobody in your group has agreed a visual signal for we are leaving. You have not told welfare that you will not hear a tannoy.

Now here's what's really happening. Deaf attendees are systematically excluded from the emergency information layer of an event, and that is a safety issue rather than an inclusion one — evacuation instructions, severe weather warnings and stage cancellations are all broadcast in the one modality you cannot receive. Most events will provide interpreters, text alerts or a welfare contact if asked in advance, and almost none provide them by default. The equipment problem is separate and severe: rain, dust and a dead battery are all far more likely here than at home, and a processor lost at a festival is a five-figure object in a field.

So here's your check. I call it the A check. Ask: “How will I find out if something goes wrong on this site?” — If the only answer is the tannoy, you have no route to emergency information.

And here's what sits underneath all of it. Evacuations Are Announced Out Loud. The scenario that matters is the rare one. A crowd evacuation, a severe weather call, a stage closure that redirects forty thousand people — these are announced by PA, once, urgently, and then everyone around you starts moving in a direction you do not understand for a reason you have not been told. Moving in a dense crowd without knowing why is how people get hurt. Arrange the alternative in advance, in writing: ask the event for text alerts, register with welfare so they know to find you, and agree a physical signal with your group that means leave now, follow me, no questions.

So here's your move. Email the event before you travel about interpreters and text alerts. Register at welfare on arrival so somebody knows you will not hear a tannoy. Bring a dry box, spare batteries and a backup, and agree a visual emergency signal with your group.

Here are the words. “I'm Deaf and I won't hear announcements — can you register that at welfare and tell me how safety info gets out here? — [to your group] If we need to go, this signal. Don't explain, just do it and I'll follow.”

And here's the truth underneath it. The safety information at a festival is audio-only. Arrange your own channel before you need it, because there will be no time afterwards.
