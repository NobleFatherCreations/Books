SCENARIO 12 — “Digital Protection For Artists”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let's talk about someone you'll recognize. Your digital presence as an artist at festivals creates specific vulnerabilities: your work being appropriated without credit, your images being used without consent, and your online presence being used to locate or contact you in ways you didn't invite.

Here's the scene. Someone has been using your work without credit for three months. You've found it on their Etsy, their promotional materials, their social media. You've found your face — from a photo someone else took at a festival — being used in a "free creative" stock image collection. You've received DMs from a man who clearly knows your festival schedule, your booth location, and details about your process that feel like surveillance. Each thing is individually manageable. Together they paint a picture.

So watch for these. Your work appearing online without credit or permission in commercial contexts. Photographs of you from festivals used in contexts you didn't authorize. Someone who seems to know your festival schedule, locations, and movements in detail. DMs that reference specific details about your process or whereabouts that weren't publicly shared.

Here's the mechanism underneath it. Digital exploitation of artists operates on multiple simultaneous tracks: IP theft (your work), image rights violations (your likeness), and stalking behavior (your location and schedule). Each track requires different tools. All of them require documentation. Screenshot everything that might be relevant before you report it — platforms remove content on reports and you lose your evidence if you don't capture it first.

So ask yourself this. I call it the A check. Ask: “Have I documented this situation before reporting it — do I have screenshots and dates?”

Now here's the part that's hard to hear. Digital Safety Basics For Festival Artists. (1) Watermark your digital work — your name and website embedded in the image itself, not just the caption. (2) Use a dedicated email for professional inquiries — not your personal email. (3) Don't share your festival schedule publicly beyond the event you're at. (4) If you feel someone is tracking your location or movements: document it, tell a trusted person, contact festival security, and if it escalates contact local law enforcement (Stalking Prevention Awareness and Resource Center: stalkingawareness.org). (5) Your festival address, vehicle, and home location are private. Treat them that way.

So here's what you do. Watermark everything. Document anything concerning. Report before you think you should.

Here's what to say, out loud. [To festival security]: "Someone has been tracking my location and movements across events. I'd like to make a report."

And remember this. Your online presence is an extension of your work. Protect it with the same intentionality you bring to your art.
