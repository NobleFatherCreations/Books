SCENARIO 14 — “We Ran Your Stuff Through A Model”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let me tell you about a particular kind of person. A promoter, a brand, or a former client who has discovered they can get something that looks like your work without booking you — and who genuinely does not understand why you would object, because no file of yours was copied.

Here's how it plays out. The poster for this year's event is unmistakably yours. The palette, the linework, the way you do figures. You did not make it. When you ask, they say they made it in-house, and then, cheerfully, that they used your last three pieces as references, and they say it as though that were a compliment rather than a description of what they did.

So here's what to watch for. Work that reproduces your recognizable style without your involvement. “In the style of”, or your name used as a prompt reference. A former client who stopped booking you and whose visuals did not change. Your live-painting footage or process shots used as training references. A contract clause licensing your work for “any purpose including machine learning” They are baffled by your objection and cite that nothing was copied.

Now here's what's really going on. Style is not protected the way a specific work is, which makes this legally murky and commercially devastating — a festival that can generate something close enough to your look does not need to book you, and the thing that made you employable becomes the thing that replaces you. The practical defence is therefore contractual and evidential rather than legal after the fact: what you license, to whom, for how long, and whether machine learning uses are excluded. Most artists have never read that clause and most event contracts now contain one.

So run this check. I call it the A check. Ask: “What exactly did I license, and does it exclude training and generation?” — If you have never checked, assume it does not.

And here's the darker reality. The Clause You Signed In A Queue. Look at the media and artist agreements you have already signed, because the grabbing happens there rather than later. Perpetual, worldwide, sublicensable, all media now known or hereafter devised, for any purpose — that language predates all of this and covers it completely, and the artists most exposed are the ones who signed it years ago and never looked again. Add an exclusion in writing to every future agreement, register the significant pieces where registration exists in your country, and document your process with dated footage. Provenance is what you will have instead of a remedy.

So here's the play. Add an explicit exclusion to every contract — no training, no generative reuse, no style derivation — and get it in the email even when you cannot get it in the document. Watermark and register key work. Keep dated process footage. Raise it once, in writing, with the actual decision-maker rather than publicly.

Here's exactly what to say. “My licence excludes any machine-learning or generative use, including style references — can you confirm that in writing before I invoice? — That poster is derived from my work. I'd like to talk about a fee or its removal.”

And here's the truth. They did not copy a file, they copied the decade. Put the exclusion in the contract, because there is no remedy afterwards.
