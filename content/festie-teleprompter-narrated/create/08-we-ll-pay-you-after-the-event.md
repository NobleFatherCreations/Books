SCENARIO 8 — “We'll Pay You After The Event”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let me tell you about someone you'll recognize. Getting paid what you were promised, on the timeline agreed, is the baseline. It is also one of the most commonly violated terms for creative workers at festivals. The variations are endless. The pattern is always the same.

Here's how it plays out. "The accounting is still getting sorted — payment will be in your account by end of month." End of month: "we had some unexpected costs, we're going to need to adjust the final payment." Or: you delivered the commission, the client went quiet, three weeks later you're following up for the third time. Or: the event was three months ago and the check that was "in the mail" has never arrived.

So here's what to watch for. Payment delayed past agreed date without prior communication. "Unexpected costs" or "accounting issues" appear after the work is delivered. Communication becomes vague or slow after the work is delivered. Each follow-up produces a new reason and a new timeline.

Now here's what's really going on. Cash flow manipulation is a feature, not a bug, of predatory event economics. Your leverage decreases dramatically once you've delivered the work — which is why deposits exist. Get 50% before you start. Invoice immediately on delivery. Send written payment requests with your contract attached. Paper trail everything. Most non-payers rely on artists being too tired, too embarrassed, or too non-confrontational to pursue it. Pursue it.

So run this check. I call it the C check. Ask: “Do I have a signed agreement with payment terms — so I have something to enforce?”

And here's the darker reality. When They Don't Pay — Your Options. Step 1: written demand email with contract attached, specific payment deadline (7-14 days). Step 2: small claims court handles disputes up to $10,000-$25,000 depending on state — filing fee usually under $100, you represent yourself. Step 3: for larger amounts, one attorney demand letter (often $150-300) frequently resolves it. Step 4: publicizing non-payment in artist and vendor communities is legal and effective — many predatory organizers rely on silence. Document everything before going public.

So here's the play. 50% deposit before you start. Invoice immediately on delivery. Paper trail everything.

Here's exactly what to say. “Per our agreement, the remaining balance of [X] is due within 7 days. Please confirm receipt of this.”

And here's the truth. You created the work. You get paid what was agreed. This is not complicated and it is completely enforceable.
