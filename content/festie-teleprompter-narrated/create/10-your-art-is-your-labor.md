SCENARIO 10 — “Your Art Is Your Labor”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Here's a particular kind of person. This is the practical page. Rates, rights, contracts, credit. The unglamorous infrastructure that means you can keep making art without being slowly drained by every festival you attend.

Picture it. The artists who thrive on the festival circuit long-term are not the ones who work hardest. They're the ones who figured out their value early, put it in writing consistently, and stopped apologizing for having a professional framework inside a culture that frames everything as family and love. You can love this community AND operate professionally within it.

Here are the signs. Know your day rate and piece rate before any conversation — not during it. Every engagement in writing: fee, deliverables, credit requirements, ownership, cancellation. Photograph your own work before, during, and after — your documentation is your record. Watermark your digital images before sharing widely online.

Now here's what's really happening. Festival culture's "we're all family" framing is beautiful and also systematically used to remove the professional frameworks that protect artists. Family doesn't charge family. Family doesn't need contracts. Family gives freely. This creates the conditions for systematic exploitation of your labor. You can operate with full love for the community AND maintain professional clarity about how your work is valued. In fact, artists who know their worth make the community stronger by modeling that art has value.

So here's your check. I call it the R check. Ask: “Is my rate for this engagement something I decided — or something that got decided around me?” — a rate you didn’t set is a rate someone else set, on purpose, in their favor.

And here's what sits underneath all of it. Resources For Artists. Volunteer Lawyers for the Arts (vlany.org): free/low-cost legal help on contracts, copyright, and disputes. US Copyright Office (copyright.gov): automatic copyright on creation, registration ($65) enables enforcement. Graphic Artists Guild Handbook — Pricing and Ethical Standards: industry reference for rates. CERF+ (cerfplus.org): emergency assistance for working craft artists facing crisis. Artists' legal clinics exist in most major cities — search "volunteer lawyers for the arts [your city]."

So here's your move. Set your rate. Put it in writing. Say it out loud without apologizing. Repeat.

Here are the words. “My rate for live painting is [X] for the weekend. Does that work with your budget?”

And here's the truth underneath it. Knowing your worth and saying it out loud is not ego. It is what keeps you in the game.
