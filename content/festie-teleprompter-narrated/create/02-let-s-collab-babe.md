SCENARIO 2 — “Let's Collab, Babe”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let me tell you about a particular kind of person. The person who wants to "collaborate" — which in practice means they contribute a concept or their presence while you contribute the actual skill, time, and materials. Credit gets split equally. Labor doesn't.

Here's how it plays out. "I have this incredible vision — I can see it so clearly — I just need someone with your skills to bring it to life. We'd be equal partners." You spend the next day executing their vague concept with your specific technical ability, your materials, your time. The finished piece gets posted with both your names. Their 8,000 followers see it first. The caption says "our piece." When you mention the execution was entirely yours, the vibe goes cold.

So here's what to watch for. They bring concept or personality; you bring skill, time, materials, and execution. Credit proposed as 50/50 before any conversation about actual labor split. Pushback about contribution equity received as you being difficult or insecure. Their platform/following positioned as their "equal contribution".

Now here's what's really going on. Platform is not the same as labor. A concept is not the same as execution. In the art world these distinctions matter enormously for credit, compensation, and professional record. The collab frame is used to extract skilled labor under the cover of partnership. A genuine collaboration is one where both parties' contributions are named honestly and the credit reflects actual work.

So run this check. I call it the A check. Ask: “Does my name appear clearly on this work in a way that reflects my actual contribution?”

And here's the darker reality. Photo And Video Credit — Non-Negotiable. Every time your work appears online, your name should appear in the caption — not just a tag that can be removed, a written credit. Before any event or collab, establish in writing: (1) all posts featuring your work credit you by name in the caption, (2) you receive advance notice before your work appears in any commercial context, (3) you retain rights to your own images of the work. Screenshot every post featuring your work.

So here's the play. Define the contribution split before you start creating. If they can't have that conversation — that's your answer.

Here's exactly what to say. “I'm into the idea — let's talk about what each of us is actually bringing so we're clear on credit before we start.”

And here's the truth. Equal credit requires equal contribution. Concepts and execution are not the same thing.
