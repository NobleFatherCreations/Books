SCENARIO 4 — “I Just Love Watching You Create”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Here's someone you'll recognize. The person who camps near your workspace all weekend under the banner of admiration. Creating in public is inherently intimate — your focus makes you less boundaried — and predatory hovering exploits all of that.

Picture it. He's been near your painting station since you set up. Brings you water. Makes comments about your process that show he's genuinely paying attention. It feels nice to be witnessed. By Sunday he feels like a fixture of your weekend — he knows your whole process, has opinions about your choices, offered to help break down. When he starts steering the direction of the piece and you redirect, he takes it personally. You feel like you owe him something.

Here are the signs. Presence near your workspace extends across the whole weekend without invitation. Witness role gradually becomes advisory role — opinions about your work appear uninvited. Small acts of service (water, help) build invisible debt over time. Redirecting their input or asking for space is received as rejection.

Now here's what's really happening. Creating in public is an intimate act — you're in a focused, slightly vulnerable state. This is a window that predatory observers specifically exploit. Real appreciation respects the artist's process and follows their lead about interaction. Hovering that gradually expands into advisory territory, then into physical presence in your workspace, then into emotional debt — is not admiration. It's occupation disguised as appreciation.

So here's your check. I call it the T check. Ask: “Who is actually in my creative space right now — and did I invite them there?”

And here's what sits underneath all of it. Substances And Creative Work — The Vulnerability Window. Many live painters work with substances as part of their creative practice — completely valid. Your altered state while working is a specific vulnerability window. You're focused on the work, your social threat-detection is lower, and your boundaries are softer. People who offer substances to you while you're creating are not always doing it neutrally. Know what you're taking, test it before you're in the flow state, and have a trusted person in your orbit who is less altered than you while you work.

So here's your move. Name your workspace boundaries clearly and early. "I go pretty internal while I work" is a complete sentence.

Here are the words. “I really appreciate the support — I need to be heads-down for a while. I'll find you when I come up for air.”

And here's the truth underneath it. Admiration that respects your process from a distance is real. Admiration that requires access to your workspace is not admiration.
