SCENARIO 3 — “I Know People”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let's talk about the person you'll meet here. The "connector" — not quite a manager, not quite a promoter — who positions themselves as the key to your next level of exposure and begins expecting ongoing commission on work they introduce you to, then on work they didn't introduce you to.

Here's the scene. "I'm going to connect you with everyone this weekend — I know all the right people." He does introduce you to two legitimate leads. He also begins referring to himself as your "rep" in conversations with those leads and starts mentioning a 20% "arrangement" that you never agreed to. By the time you realize what's happening, the leads think he's already your manager.

So watch for these. Introduces themselves as your "rep" or "manager" in conversations without your agreement. Commission expectations appear after introductions, not before — retroactively. Scope of what they expect commission on keeps expanding. You're now in a complicated position with leads who believe a deal exists.

Here's the mechanism underneath it. The parasitic middleman move works by creating facts on the ground before you've had a chance to define the relationship. By the time you understand what they're claiming, they're already representing you to the people who matter to you professionally. Clear, early conversation about what any assistance means in terms of compensation is the only protection. If someone introduces themselves as your representative without your agreement — correct it immediately and directly.

So ask yourself this. I call it the C check. Ask: “Have I agreed to any representation arrangement — or is someone claiming one that was never actually agreed?” — a representative you never hired is telling you exactly who they are.

Now here's the part that's hard to hear. Correcting False Representation. If someone is representing themselves as your manager or rep without your agreement: (1) Contact the leads directly and privately: "I want to clarify — [name] is not my manager/rep. I'm handling my own bookings." (2) Tell the person directly: "I haven't agreed to any representation arrangement and I need you to stop representing yourself that way." (3) Document this conversation in writing immediately after. False representation can create enforceable implied contracts in some jurisdictions — address it fast.

So here's what you do. Define every assistance relationship before it becomes a representation relationship.

Here's what to say, out loud. "I appreciate the intros — I want to be clear that I'm handling my own arrangements. Any intros are just that — intros, not representation."

And remember this. Anyone who represents themselves as your representative before you've agreed to that is telling you who they are.
