SCENARIO 12 — “Buying And Selling Safely”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let's talk about the person you'll meet here. Transactions at festivals — substances, goods, services, tickets — carry real legal risk that vendors often underestimate because the festival environment feels safe and familiar. Undercover officers work festivals and look exactly like attendees.

Here's the scene. He has been browsing your booth for twenty minutes asking thoughtful questions about your process. The conversation shifts — he mentions he knows people who might be interested in other things you might have access to, winks included. It feels casual. It feels like a fellow festie making a sideways inquiry. Something about the specificity of the questions feels slightly rehearsed. You cannot put your finger on it. But something is off.

So watch for these. They approach YOU with an inquiry that moves from your products to something else entirely. Unusual specificity about quantities, prices, or sourcing that goes beyond normal customer curiosity. Wants to move the conversation somewhere more private or away from your booth. Something about the interaction feels slightly scripted — you cannot articulate why but it is there.

Here's the mechanism underneath it. Undercover officers at festivals are specifically trained to blend in and they are often very good at it. As a vendor with a public booth you are particularly visible and accessible. The behavioral tells are in the conversation pattern itself: they redirect from legitimate commerce to other things, they guide toward isolation, and they push for specificity. You are never obligated to complete any conversation or transaction. "I am just focused on my product today" ends it cleanly.

So ask yourself this. I call it the A check. Ask: “Am I proceeding with this conversation because I actually want to — or because saying no feels riskier than it is?” — no transaction at a festival is worth your freedom, and no conversation is worth your business.

Now here's the part that's hard to hear. If You Are Approached By Police Or Detained. Know this before you need it: (1) You have the right to remain silent — use it. "I would like to speak with a lawyer" is the only thing you say. (2) You can ask if you are being detained or free to go — if free to go, go. (3) You can decline to consent to a search. (4) Do not physically resist any police interaction. (5) Write down badge numbers and details as soon as possible. (6) Contact a lawyer or the National Lawyers Guild (nlg.org) which has observer programs at many major festivals. Good Samaritan laws protect people seeking overdose help in most US states.

So here's what you do. "I am just focused on my product today" ends any sideways conversation. Clean and done.

Here's what to say, out loud. “I am just focused on my booth today — thanks for stopping by.”

And remember this. No transaction at a festival is worth your freedom. No conversation is worth your business.
