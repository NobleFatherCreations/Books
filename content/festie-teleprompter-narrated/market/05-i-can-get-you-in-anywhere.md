SCENARIO 5 — “I Can Get You In Anywhere”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let me tell you about someone you'll recognize. The promoter, camp lead, or circuit organizer who becomes romantically involved with a vendor and uses that relationship to control business access. Starts as genuine connection. Becomes leverage when the relationship gets complicated.

Here's how it plays out. You met him at the winter market and the connection was real. By spring he was helping you get into better events and your bookings genuinely improved. By summer you are in a relationship that has become complicated and painful — and every conversation about ending it carries an unspoken freight of what happens to your bookings and circuit relationships when you do. The personal and professional are completely entangled and you have less power in both.

So here's what to watch for. Romantic relationship develops with someone who controls your business access. Professional benefits and relationship status feel tied — both rise and fall together. Contemplating ending the relationship includes calculating professional costs. They reference your bookings or placement during relationship conflicts.

Now here's what's really going on. Romantic entanglement with access holders is a specific and common vendor vulnerability because the festival circuit is small and relationship-driven. The problem is not the relationship. The problem is when the relationship becomes the mechanism of professional control. If you cannot evaluate your relationship independently of what it costs your business — the entanglement has already become control. Your professional access should never live inside a romantic relationship.

So run this check. I call it the A check. Ask: “Does my business access depend on this relationship — and what does that mean for me the day this relationship ends?” — access that depends on one relationship was never really yours.

And here's the darker reality. Building Access Independent Of Any One Person. Your vendor circuit access should run through multiple independent relationships. Practical steps: join at least two vendor Facebook groups and participate so people know you independently. Apply to events directly rather than through an intermediary whenever possible. Build relationships with multiple promoters independently of any romantic partner. Document your own sales history and reviews so your reputation is yours — not borrowed from someone else's endorsement. The goal is that your access to the circuit survives any single relationship ending.

So here's the play. Keep your professional relationships independent of your romantic ones. Build your own circuit reputation.

Here's exactly what to say. “I am going to apply to this one directly — I want to build my own relationship with the organizer.”

And here's the truth. Your business access is yours. It should never live inside someone else's hands.
