SCENARIO 7 — “She Is Not Really Handmade”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Here's a particular kind of person. The fellow vendor who uses the tight-knit circuit to spread reputation damage — questioning your craft authenticity, poaching your customers, or running whisper campaigns to promoters about why they should not book you.

Picture it. Sales started dropping at your third event of the season without obvious reason. A promoter friend tells you she has been hearing your work is not really handmade — she does not know where it is coming from but multiple people have mentioned it. You trace it back to a vendor two spots over who you have been outselling for three seasons. Nothing being said is true. But it is already in circulation and you do not know how to correct something you are not supposed to know is being said.

Here are the signs. Sales drop or bookings thin without clear cause — investigate the social layer. Promoters who previously booked you easily are now vague or unresponsive. Other vendors seem to have information about your business you did not share. A specific competitor directly benefits from the narrative circulating about you.

Now here's what's really happening. The festival vendor circuit is small, relationship-driven, and operates heavily on reputation. Smear campaigns are highly effective because the market is interconnected — a few well-placed doubts can close doors quietly without you knowing they were closing. The most effective counter is documented proof: photos of your process, behind-the-scenes content, certificates of authenticity for materials, and direct relationships with promoters that are independent of any one person.

So here's your check. I call it the K check. Ask: “Do I have documentation of my process and materials that speaks for itself?”

And here's what sits underneath all of it. Document Your Process — This Is Your Defense. Build a documentation library: time-lapse or process videos of your making, raw material photos with supplier tags, works-in-progress shots, and studio photos. Post these regularly — not just finished pieces. This creates an independent timestamped record that no smear campaign can erase. When you hear something is being said: address it directly with your promoter contacts with evidence. "I heard there is a question about my process — I wanted to share my documentation directly with you." Confident and documented beats defensive every time.

So here's your move. Let your process documentation be louder than anyone's whisper campaign.

Here are the words. “I make everything myself and I have the receipts — happy to share my process documentation if that would be helpful.”

And here's the truth underneath it. You cannot control what people say. You can make the truth undeniable.
