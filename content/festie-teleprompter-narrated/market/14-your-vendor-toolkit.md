SCENARIO 14 — “Your Vendor Toolkit”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let me tell you about a specific type. This is the reference page. The documents, resources, and practices that mean you are protected before any problem starts — not scrambling to protect yourself after it already has.

Here's how it plays out. The vendors who have long and sustainable careers on the festival circuit are not the ones who never get taken advantage of. They are the ones who built the infrastructure early, put things in writing consistently, and stopped apologizing for having professional standards inside a culture that frames everything as love and vibes. You can love this community AND operate professionally within it. These are not opposites.

So here's what to watch for. Standard vendor agreement — one page, covers placement, fees, refund policy, and exclusivity. Standard consignment agreement — covers inventory list, split, payment schedule, and returns. Invoice template with payment terms, late fees, and your legal remedies clearly stated. Process documentation library — timestamped photos and videos of your making process.

Now here's what's really going on. Professional infrastructure is not about distrust — it is about making every relationship honest from the start. Vendors with clear written terms attract the promoters and buyers who are comfortable with clear written terms, which turns out to be exactly the promoters and buyers you want to work with. The ones who resist paperwork are showing you their payment intentions. Let paperwork be the filter. It saves enormous amounts of time and energy.

So run this check. I call it the R check. Ask: “Do I have the documents I need to protect myself before I need them — not after?”

And here's the darker reality. Vendor Legal And Business Resources. Volunteer Lawyers for the Arts (vlany.org): free and low-cost legal help for artists and makers on contracts, copyright, and business disputes. US Copyright Office (copyright.gov): automatic copyright on creation, registration $65 enables enforcement. SCORE (score.org): free business mentoring from experienced business owners — very useful for pricing and business structure. Small Business Development Centers (sba.gov/sbdc): free consulting on business plans, finances, and legal structure. Indie Craft Experience and similar vendor communities: peer knowledge about specific promoters and events.

So here's the play. Build the documents before you need them. They are the filter that attracts the right partners.

Here's exactly what to say. “Here is my standard vendor agreement — I use this for all my events so we are both protected.”

And here's the truth. Professional standards do not push the right people away. They push the wrong people away. That is the point.
