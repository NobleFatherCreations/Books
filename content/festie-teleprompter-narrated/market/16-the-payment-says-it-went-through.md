SCENARIO 16 — “The Payment Says It Went Through”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Here's the person you'll meet here. Somebody who has worked out that a vendor at hour nine, in bad light, with a queue forming and no signal, cannot verify anything and will not hold up the line to try.

Picture it. He shows you the screen. It says sent, with your name on it and a tick. It is half past eleven, the signal has been dropping all day, your banking app will not load, and there are four people waiting behind him holding things they want to buy. You hand over the piece.

Here are the signs. A screenshot of a transfer rather than money in your account. Payment claimed at the exact moment your signal is worst. A card that declines and is retried on a different reader. Large notes for small items, late at night, in poor light. Hurrying you, with a queue behind, and a reason it must be now. Refunds requested in cash for a card transaction.

Now here's what's really happening. Every one of these relies on the same three conditions a festival supplies reliably: bad connectivity, bad light and social pressure from a queue. A transfer screenshot proves nothing and is trivial to fake; a pending or unsettled card payment is not a payment; and counterfeit notes at a market stall late at night are an old trade that persists because nobody wants to be the vendor who holds up the line over a twenty. The cash-refund version is the cleanest fraud of the lot, and the answer to it is that a refund goes back the way the money came, always.

So here's your check. I call it the M check. Ask: “Has the money arrived in my account, or have I been shown a picture of money?” — Those are not the same event.

And here's what sits underneath all of it. The Queue Is The Weapon. Notice what is actually being used against you, because it is not technology. It is your reluctance to make four other paying customers wait while you check. That pressure is manufactured, it is often manufactured deliberately, and it is the reason this works at eleven at night and never at eleven in the morning. Decide the policy in advance so there is no decision to make in the moment: no goods leave the stall until the money is confirmed in your account or the terminal says approved, and if the signal is down you take cash or you take a name and hold the item. Losing one sale is not a cost. Losing four pieces across a weekend is.

So here's your move. Set the rule before the event and put it on a small card on the stall. Keep a UV pen and check large notes. Never refund cash on a card payment. Take a deposit and hold the item if you cannot verify. Reconcile takings nightly rather than on Monday.

Here are the words. “I'll hold it for you — I just can't let it go until it lands my end, signal's awful. Two minutes and it'll come through. — Refunds go back to the card, not in cash, sorry.”

And here's the truth underneath it. A screenshot is not a payment. The queue behind him is the pressure, and it is doing exactly what it was arranged to do.
