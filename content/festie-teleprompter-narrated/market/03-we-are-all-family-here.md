SCENARIO 3 — “We Are All Family Here”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let's talk about a particular kind of person. The camp or collective that offers housing, infrastructure, or event access in exchange for vague contribution — which expands over time to include gifting your inventory, free labor, and staying quiet about how the arrangement works.

Here's the scene. The camp offered you a booth placement and a campsite. "We just ask that everyone contributes to the community." By the second event you have gifted $400 of inventory, worked 12 hours of camp setup and breakdown, and contributed $200 cash to camp costs. You are making less per event than you were before you joined the family. When you raise it, the response is a hurt look and a reminder of everything they have done for you.

So watch for these. Contribution expectations are vague at start and expand with each event. Gifting your inventory positioned as community participation not a cost to your business. Access to placement tied to your continued compliance with camp demands. Raising the financial impact of the arrangement is received as not being a good community fit.

Here's the mechanism underneath it. Camp-based vendor arrangements can be genuinely mutually beneficial and they can be a systematic way to extract inventory, labor, and compliance from traveling vendors who need the infrastructure. The tell is always whether terms are defined and mutual. Real community has reciprocity both parties can articulate. When your contributions keep expanding and the camp contributions stay stable — that is feudalism with better branding.

So ask yourself this. I call it the E check. Ask: “What would it actually cost me to leave this arrangement — and is that cost getting bigger the longer I stay quiet about it?” — an undefined cost that grows with your silence is not community. It’s a bill you didn’t agree to.

Now here's the part that's hard to hear. Calculate Your Actual Camp Arrangement Cost. Write this down: (1) Vendor fee or equivalent you are paying. (2) Value of inventory gifted at your wholesale cost. (3) Value of your labor hours at your day rate. (4) Cash contributions to camp costs. Now compare to what a solo vendor spot at the same event would cost and what your actual net sales were. If the camp arrangement costs more than going independently — you are paying for the privilege of working for them. Name that clearly before committing to another season.

So here's what you do. Calculate every camp arrangement in actual dollars before you commit to another event.

Here's what to say, out loud. “I want to understand what the arrangement looks like financially before I commit — can we talk through what is expected specifically?”

And remember this. Community costs should be defined, mutual, and agreed to in advance. Anything else is uncompensated labor.
