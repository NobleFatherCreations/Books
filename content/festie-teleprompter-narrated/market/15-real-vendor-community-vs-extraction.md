SCENARIO 15 — “Real Vendor Community Vs Extraction”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let's talk about a particular kind of person. The vendor circuit at its best is one of the most genuinely supportive professional communities that exists — people who share leads, warn each other about bad promoters, help each other during breakdown, and celebrate each other's success. This page is about building that and protecting it from the extractive version.

Here's the scene. The vendor two booths over comes by mid-morning on Saturday: "Hey I noticed you are running low on bags — I have extras, take some." No reciprocity mentioned. No ledger. Later she tells you she heard a rumor about next year's event moving to a smaller venue — thought you should know before you committed your spot fee. Later still she helps you carry your tent poles to the car. None of it was transactional. All of it was real.

So watch for these. Real vendor community: sharing information freely including about bad promoters and difficult buyers. Real vendor community: helping during load-in and breakdown without keeping score. Real vendor community: celebrating each other's success including when a neighboring booth has a great day. Real vendor community: honest feedback about your work and business when you ask for it.

Here's the mechanism underneath it. Real vendor solidarity is built through repeated small interactions where each person is free to give or not give without social cost either way. It looks like sharing information, helping during the hard moments of setup and breakdown, warning each other about problems on the circuit, and genuinely celebrating when someone in the community thrives. It does not look like keeping score, invoking community as leverage in commercial transactions, or using shared history as a reason you owe someone something.

So ask yourself this. I call it the K check. Ask: “Is this interaction genuinely mutual — or is community being invoked to extract something one direction only?” — real community runs both ways. Extraction only runs one.

Now here's the part that's hard to hear. How To Build Your Real Vendor Network. Real vendor community is built through: sharing information generously including about bad promoters — this is the most valuable thing you can do for each other. Participating in vendor Facebook groups not just to ask questions but to answer them. Introducing yourself to neighboring vendors at every event with genuine curiosity about their work. Helping during breakdown even when you are exhausted. Giving honest referrals and honest warnings. The vendors who build the longest and most sustainable careers are almost always the ones who invest most genuinely in the community around them.

So here's what you do. Invest in real vendor community. It is the best business development and the best protection at the same time.

Here's what to say, out loud. “I heard something about that promoter you should know before you commit — let me tell you what happened to me.”

And remember this. The real vendor community is out there and it is extraordinary. Protect yourself from the extractive version so you have the energy to find it.
