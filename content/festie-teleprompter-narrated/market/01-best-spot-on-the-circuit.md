SCENARIO 1 — “Best Spot On The Circuit”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Here's someone you'll recognize. The event promoter who controls your placement, electricity, water, foot traffic, and your access to the event. Starts with a perfect pitch. Delivers something different. Uses your sunk costs to keep you from pushing back.

Picture it. The pitch was perfect: high-foot-traffic spot near the main stage, 2,000 attendees, electricity included. You paid the $800 vendor fee two months out. You arrive to find your spot is behind the food trucks, electricity needs an additional $150 hookup fee nobody mentioned, and the 2,000 attendee number was from three years ago. When you raise it he reminds you with a smile that the vendor fee is non-refundable.

Here are the signs. Placement promises made verbally — not written into the vendor agreement. Additional fees appear after you have committed and payment is non-refundable. Attendance or traffic numbers are from a different year or simply made up. Complaints are met with the reminder that you have already paid.

Now here's what's really happening. Vendor fee manipulation relies on the non-refundable deposit to eliminate your leverage once you have committed. The bait-and-switch is a known and widespread practice on the festival vendor circuit. Your only real protection is what is written down before you pay. If it is not in the vendor agreement it did not happen and you have no recourse. Research every promoter in Facebook vendor groups and Indie Craft Experience forums before you commit. The circuit has a long memory for bad promoters.

So here's your check. I call it the M check. Ask: “Is the placement, the attendance number, and every fee in writing — or just verbally promised by someone with every reason to over-promise?” — a great pitch that isn’t in writing is just a story.

And here's what sits underneath all of it. Vendor Agreement Must-Haves. Before paying any vendor fee, the written agreement must include: exact booth location or tier with a map, all fees fully itemized including electricity and water hookups, minimum attendance guarantee or clear as-is disclaimer, refund policy under specific conditions including event cancellation, your sales category and exclusivity protections, and setup and breakdown time windows. Research every promoter on Facebook vendor groups before committing.

So here's your move. If it is not in the written agreement it does not exist. Pay only what the paper says.
