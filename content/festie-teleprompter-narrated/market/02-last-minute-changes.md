SCENARIO 2 — “Last Minute Changes”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let me tell you about a specific type. The promoter who confirms your spot, collects your fee, then changes material terms after you are too invested to walk away without serious loss. Each change seems small. The cumulative impact is significant.

Here's how it plays out. You confirmed spot 14 near the main entrance with electrical hookup in writing two months ago. You arrive to find spot 14 has been given to a premium sponsor and you are now in spot 47 near the back fence with no electricity. When you protest: "We sent an email about changes last week" — to an address you had not checked. The show opens in four hours and your inventory is on the truck.

So here's what to watch for. Material terms change after full payment and you are completely committed. Changes communicated through easily missable channels at the last minute. Your leverage is zero because your inventory is already there. Each individual change is presented as minor or outside their control.

Now here's what's really going on. Last-minute term changes exploit the sunk cost of your presence. You have traveled, brought inventory, and made plans that are hard to reverse. The cost of walking away feels higher than accepting the change — and that is exactly the calculation the promoter is banking on. Documenting the original agreement in writing and responding to every change in writing creates a paper trail that supports a refund claim or small claims filing after the event.

So run this check. I call it the R check. Ask: “Do I have written documentation of what was originally agreed so I can actually point to it when the terms shift?” — the paper trail you build before the event is the only thing that protects you at it.

And here's the darker reality. When They Change Your Terms After Arrival. (1) Document immediately — photograph your contracted spot vs where they have put you. (2) Send a written objection via email before the event opens: "This placement does not match our agreement. I am proceeding under protest and reserving the right to seek a refund." (3) After the event: small claims court handles disputes up to $25,000 depending on state, filing fee usually under $100, you represent yourself. Your written agreement is your evidence. Most promoters will settle rather than face a filing.

So here's the play. Document the original agreement. Document every change. Respond to all changes in writing immediately.

Here's exactly what to say. “This placement does not match our agreement. I am setting up under protest and will follow up in writing.”

And here's the truth. The paper trail you build before the event is the only thing that protects you at the event.
