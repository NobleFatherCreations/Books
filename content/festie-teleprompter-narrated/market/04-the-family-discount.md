SCENARIO 4 — “The Family Discount”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Here's the person you'll meet here. The pressure from fellow vendors — framed as community solidarity — to give discounts, free products, or special arrangements. Real community exists. So does community being used as cover for extracting commercial value from you.

Picture it. The vendor two booths over has been friendly all weekend and helped you with a few things. Now she wants $200 of your inventory for $80 "because we take care of each other here." The festie culture frames this as normal generosity. But $120 of lost margin on a slow sales day is not a gift you can afford. When you hesitate she mentions how she helped you with the tent stakes yesterday morning.

Here are the signs. The "we are family" frame used specifically in commercial transactions. Reciprocity invoked one-sidedly — their help earlier, your discount now. Declining is framed as being un-community or not understanding how the circuit works. The pressure arrives when you are already depleted or in a vulnerable moment.

Now here's what's really happening. Festival vendor culture has genuine traditions of mutual aid. The predatory version uses that cultural norm to extract commercial value — discounts, free inventory, special pricing — framed as solidarity. Real community support does not require you to absorb losses to prove you belong. A discount is a business decision. So is not giving one. You are allowed to make that decision based on your actual financial situation.

So here's your check. I call it the M check. Ask: “Is this exchange actually mutual — or am I absorbing a loss to avoid a social cost?”

And here's what sits underneath all of it. How To Decline Gracefully And Firmly. You do not need a lengthy explanation to decline a discount request. Simple responses that work: "I keep my pricing consistent across the weekend — I cannot do discounts." "My margins are pretty tight this event — I appreciate you asking though." "I am not in a position to do discounts right now." You do not owe anyone your cost breakdown or a justification for your pricing. A fellow vendor who responds to a firm but kind no with guilt or withdrawal was not being community with you.

So here's your move. Your pricing is your pricing. You are allowed to hold it regardless of social pressure.

Here are the words. “I keep my pricing consistent all weekend — I am not able to do discounts. I appreciate you though.”

And here's the truth underneath it. Your margin is your livelihood. Protecting it is not being anti-community. It is being a sustainable business.
