SCENARIO 6 — “Your Traveling Partner”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let's talk about a specific type. The traveling vendor partner who controls the shared vehicle, the shared float, or the shared logistics — and uses that control to manufacture compliance, silence about problems, or tolerance of exploitation.

Here's the scene. You share a van and travel costs with her. It made the circuit financially viable for the first time. Six months in you realize: she sets the travel schedule without your input, she manages the shared float with accounting you never see, and she decides which events you attend based on criteria that seem to benefit her inventory over yours. The van is hers. Leaving means losing the whole circuit arrangement.

So watch for these. One partner controls transportation and the other is fully dependent on it for the circuit. Financial decisions — shared float, joint purchases, event fees — made unilaterally. Event selection appears to systematically favor one partner's inventory or relationships. Raising concerns triggers the implicit threat of losing the arrangement entirely.

Here's the mechanism underneath it. Travel partnerships on the vendor circuit are economically valuable and structurally vulnerable to exploitation when one party holds more resource control. The vehicle, the float, the event relationships — each creates leverage. Without a written partnership agreement defining decision-making, financial access, and exit terms, the person with more physical control has more power regardless of what was verbally agreed at the start.

So ask yourself this. I call it the A check. Ask: “Do I have independent access to the money and transportation in this arrangement?”

Now here's the part that's hard to hear. Traveling Vendor Partnership Agreement Basics. Every traveling vendor partnership needs in writing: who owns the vehicle and what the other partner's rights are, how travel costs are split and documented, how event selection decisions are made and by whom, how the shared float is managed and who has access to records, how revenue and costs are reconciled after each event, and exit terms — what happens if one partner wants to stop. This is not distrust. This is the document that makes the partnership sustainable. A one-page agreement prevents years of resentment.

So here's what you do. Never enter a traveling vendor partnership without a written agreement covering money, transport, and exit.

Here's what to say, out loud. “I am really excited to do this together — let us get the basic terms in writing so we are both protected.”

And remember this. The partnership that starts as family needs the same paperwork as a business partnership. Because it is both.
