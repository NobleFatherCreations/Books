SCENARIO 9 — “Cash And Carry”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let's talk about someone you'll recognize. Traveling vendors are a specific target: you arrive with known valuable inventory, leave with known cash, are often solo, and festival environments give people multiple days to observe your patterns, schedule, and vehicle.

Here's the scene. Sunday breakdown. Strong weekend — the cash box is full. You are loading your van alone in the dim hour before dawn, moving merchandise in multiple trips. The spot next to you is empty. You have been so focused on the physical work of loading that you have completely stopped being aware of who is around. This is the window. It is the one experienced thieves specifically wait for.

So watch for these. Loading and unloading solo in low-traffic hours with visible valuable inventory. Cash box visible during transactions and during breakdown. Same parking location over multiple days — your vehicle and patterns become known. Arrival time, departure time, and cash handling routine all become observable.

Here's the mechanism underneath it. Festival vendor robbery targets specific patterns: the breakdown window when you are tired and often solo, visible cash after a strong sales day, and known vehicle location. It is not always strangers — sometimes it is other vendors or people with festival access who have been watching your patterns all weekend. Cash management, breakdown protocol, and travel companions are not paranoia. They are the professional infrastructure of the traveling vendor life.

So ask yourself this. I call it the T check. Ask: “Am I traveling and breaking down in a way that minimizes my visibility as a target?”

Now here's the part that's hard to hear. Traveling Vendor Physical Safety Protocol. Cash management: bank deposits same-day when possible, never keep full weekend cash in the vehicle overnight, use a money belt or hidden pouch during breakdown. Vehicle: do not leave inventory visible in a parked vehicle overnight, park in well-lit areas, share your spot location with someone not at the event. Breakdown: break down with at least one other vendor present whenever possible, load valuables last when you are ready to drive immediately. Travel: share your route and ETA with someone. Check in when you arrive.

So here's what you do. Never break down solo with a full cash box. Build the protocol and use it every time.

Here's what to say, out loud. “Hey — want to watch each other's spaces during breakdown? I will help you load if you help me.”

And remember this. The most experienced vendors on the circuit are also the most careful ones. That is not a coincidence.
