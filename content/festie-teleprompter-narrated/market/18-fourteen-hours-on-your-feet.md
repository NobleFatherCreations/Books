SCENARIO 18 — “Fourteen Hours On Your Feet”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let's talk about a specific type. The schedule. Four days, fourteen-hour trading days, a build at either end, no chair because standing sells better, and no break because a break is a closed stall.

Here's the scene. Day three. You have not sat down since seven, you have eaten one thing, you cannot leave because there is nobody to cover you, and you have started making decisions — about pricing, about a wholesale enquiry, about whether that transfer really went through — with a brain that has been standing up in the sun since dawn.

So watch for these. No cover, so no breaks, so no meals and no toilet trips. Standing all day on hard ground with no anti-fatigue matting. Dehydration because drinking means needing the toilet you cannot go to. Business decisions being made at hour twelve. Lifting the whole build alone, twice per event. Recovery time between events that is shorter each season.

Here's the mechanism underneath it. Vendor fatigue is an economic problem before it is a health one. Every transaction on this page's neighbours — the fake transfer, the renegotiated deal, the wholesale offer that is worse than it sounds, the pressure to drop a price — arrives at the end of a long day and lands on a depleted person, and depleted people concede. The physical toll is real and cumulative too, but the immediate cost is commercial: you are making your most consequential decisions in your worst condition, by design, several times a weekend.

So ask yourself this. I call it the E check. Ask: “Am I about to make a real decision at hour twelve?” — Defer it to the morning. Everything that is genuinely good will still be there.

Now here's the part that's hard to hear. They Come At You At Closing Time. Notice the timing of the difficult conversations across a weekend. The wholesale enquiry, the request for a discount, the organizer wanting to change your pitch, the payment that cannot quite be verified — these cluster late, and not entirely by accident. Some of it is simply when people wander past, and some of it is people who have learned that a vendor at closing on day three will agree to things they would refuse on Friday morning. So make the policy, not the judgement: no deal over a set value, no contract, no wholesale terms and no price change is agreed after a certain hour. You will never regret the sentence, and the good ones come back in the morning.

So here's what you do. Rota real breaks with a neighbour — mutual stall cover is the most valuable trade on any circuit. Anti-fatigue mat, a stool, water on a schedule. Set a cut-off hour after which no significant decision is made, and say so cheerfully.

Here's what to say, out loud. “Can we cover each other? Twenty minutes each, twice a day — I'll do yours first. — That sounds great and I don't do deals after seven — come back at ten tomorrow and we'll sort it properly.”

And remember this. Your worst decisions happen at hour twelve, and the people asking know it. Defer anything that matters to the morning.
