SCENARIO 2 — “Take It, It's Yours”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let's talk about the person you'll meet here. Generous, well-equipped, and quick to notice what you cannot afford. Hands you a set of pro-grade props, a rig, a costume, a fire kit. Does not want anything for it, says so repeatedly, and mentions its value twice.

Here's the scene. Your poi are entry-level and everyone knows it. He has a spare set of the ones you have been saving for — seven hundred dollars of them — and he wants you to have them, no, really, take them, he has three sets. Two weeks later there is an afterparty you do not want to go to and the sentence in your head, unprompted, is that he gave you the poi.

So watch for these. The gift is expensive enough to be uncomfortable and given fast. Refusing is treated as an insult rather than a choice. Its value gets mentioned more than once, casually. Given in front of other people, so the generosity is witnessed. Followed within weeks by an ask that is hard to decline. Taking it back is floated, lightly, when you say no to something.

Here's the mechanism underneath it. Reciprocity is the strongest lever in the book — a received gift creates a felt obligation that operates below deliberation, and it does not care whether you asked for the gift or wanted it. Gift economy culture supercharges it, because the frame says a gift is pure, which removes your permission to be suspicious of one. Expensive gear is a particularly efficient version: it is high value, it is useful daily, it visibly improves your practice, and every time you use it you renew the debt. Most gifts in this scene are exactly what they look like. The tell is not generosity. It is generosity that arrives before the relationship has any other content.

So ask yourself this. I call it the O check. Ask: “Would I be comfortable saying no to this person tomorrow?” — If the gear makes that harder, the gear is doing something other than spinning.

Now here's the part that's hard to hear. The Ledger Nobody Admits Exists. The damage is not the moment the ask arrives — it is the weeks before, when you quietly start being easier to be around. You stop raising the thing that bothered you. You go to the thing you did not want to go to. Nobody has threatened anything and there is nothing to report, and if you told a friend they would say he sounds nice. That self-editing in advance of a demand that has not been made is the whole mechanism, and it is the most reliably reported experience of people who later realized they had been managed. If you want to test it cheaply: decline something small this week and watch whether the temperature of the friendship changes.

So here's what you do. Price the gift and settle it — pay something, trade something, or return it. If none of that is possible, say out loud and early what the gift does not buy, which converts an unspoken ledger into a stated one and removes its power.

Here's what to say, out loud. “These are amazing and I'm not comfortable with a gift this size — let me pay you half, or I'll take them as a loan for the season and get them back to you.”

And remember this. A real gift survives you saying no to the giver a week later. Test it early, while the test is cheap.
