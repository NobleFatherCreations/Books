SCENARIO 1 — “I'll Teach You The Real Stuff”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let me tell you about a particular kind of person. Genuinely good, visibly senior, and the reason half this circle can do what they do. Teaches selectively. Makes it clear that being taught is a thing you are chosen for, and that the choosing is ongoing.

Here's how it plays out. He has been doing this for fifteen years and the thing he does with a staff is the reason you started. He offers to work with you properly — not in the jam, at his camp, after, when it's quiet and there's space. The first session is the best hour of instruction you have ever had. The second session he corrects your stance with his hands and holds the correction slightly longer than the correction needs.

So here's what to watch for. Teaching happens privately and late rather than in open practice. Access to instruction is framed as being chosen, and is reviewable. Physical correction escalates in duration or location over sessions. Discourages you from training with others — they'll teach you bad habits. Mentions what he could do for your booking or your profile. The lesson quality is real, which is what makes leaving expensive.

Now here's what's really going on. Skill is the currency in this scene and a person who controls access to it controls a great deal. The mechanism is the same as every other access trap in this book, but it has an unusual protection: the teaching is genuinely valuable, so the cost of walking away is real and everybody can see it. That asymmetry is what gets exploited. It is also why the correction-by-touch escalation works — in a discipline where hands-on adjustment is legitimate and constant, the line between instruction and testing is exactly where a person can operate while retaining deniability, and where you will second-guess yourself for weeks.

So run this check. I call it the O check. Ask: “Would this lesson be identical if it happened at 2pm in the open jam?” — If the answer is no, the privacy is doing something the teaching does not need.

And here's the darker reality. The Adjustment That Tests You. The escalation is deliberately gradual and it is designed to be unreportable. A hand on the hip is technique. The same hand, a beat longer, is a test — and if you do not react, the next one is longer. Every individual step is defensible and the whole staircase is not, which is precisely why it is built in steps. Dance, martial arts, aerial, and acro have all documented this shape, and it works the same way here. The counter is not deciding where the line is in the moment, when you are mid-correction and unsure. It is deciding beforehand that instruction happens where people can see it, and treating any pressure to move it somewhere quieter as the finding rather than as a scheduling preference.

So here's the play. Train in the open. Take instruction from at least two people so no single person is your route to progress. If a session has already moved somewhere private, bring someone — “my friend wants to watch” is unarguable and tells you a great deal by how it lands.

Here's exactly what to say. “I learn better in the jam — more eyes, more feedback. Can we do it there? I'll bring Kai, they've been wanting to work on the same thing.”

And here's the truth. A teacher worth having will teach you in daylight with people watching. The ones who need the room quiet are not protecting your focus.
