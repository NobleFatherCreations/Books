SCENARIO 7 — “The Drive Home”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let's talk about a specific type. You, on the Monday, after a three-week build and a two-day strike, with a van full of kit and four hours of motorway between you and a bed.

Here's the scene. Everything is loaded. The site is a field again. You have slept eleven hours in the last three days and you are going to drive home now because that is what everybody does and because the alternative is another night in a field. You will be fine for the first hour. The one that gets people is the third.

So watch for these. Driving at the end of a long build or a full strike. Fewer than five hours' sleep in each of the preceding nights. Driving in the window your body expects to be asleep. Loaded van, unfamiliar route, motorway. The whole crew leaves at once, so nobody is checking on anybody. “I'll stop if I get tired”, which is the belief that does the damage.

Here's the mechanism underneath it. Fatigue is a leading cause of fatal road collisions and the risk profile matches this exact moment: sustained sleep debt, monotonous roads, and driving at a time of day when the body is trying to shut down. The specific danger is the microsleep — a few seconds of sleep with the eyes open, which the driver does not experience as having happened, which is why “I'll know when I'm too tired” is false. At seventy miles an hour, four seconds is a hundred and twenty metres of unattended vehicle.

So ask yourself this. I call it the D check. Ask: “How many hours have I slept in the last three nights?” — If it is under fifteen, you are not driving tonight. That is arithmetic, not opinion.

Now here's the part that's hard to hear. Crews Lose People On The Monday. This is the one that actually kills build crew, and it happens after the dangerous work is finished, which is exactly why nobody plans for it. The whole site has spent weeks being careful about heights and plant and blades, and then everybody gets in a vehicle in the worst physiological state of the entire job and drives home alone. Budget the extra night the way you budget fuel — sleep on site and leave Tuesday morning, or put the money for a room in the quote. If you are running the build, build it into the schedule and say out loud that nobody drives on Monday, because a crew will not do it individually if the culture expects the Monday drive.

So here's what you do. Sleep before you drive — one more night on site is cheaper than any alternative. Share the driving and stop every two hours regardless of how you feel. Caffeine buys twenty minutes, not four hours. Text somebody when you arrive so a missing message means something.

Here's what to say, out loud. “I'm not driving tonight, I've had about four hours a night all week — I'll go first thing. — Crew rule this year: nobody drives on the Monday. Put the extra night in the budget.”

And remember this. The build did not hurt you. The drive home is the part that kills people, and it happens after you think the job is over.
