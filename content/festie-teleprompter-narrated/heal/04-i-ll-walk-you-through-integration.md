SCENARIO 4 — “I'll Walk You Through Integration”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Here's someone you'll recognize. The same person who facilitated, now offering to be the one who helps you make sense of it — for a fee, or for closeness, or both. Often kind. Almost always the wrong person for the job.

Picture it. The ceremony was enormous and you are not okay and he is the only person who was there and understands. He offers weekly calls. Then he offers to have you stay. Then there is a retreat he thinks you should be at, and a role in his organization, and at no point was there a moment where you decided any of this — there was a moment where you needed help and he was standing there.

Here are the signs. The facilitator is also the integrator, the landlord, the employer, or the partner. Your understanding of your own experience routes entirely through him. Cost escalates — money, time, proximity, loyalty. He discourages outside therapy, framed as it not understanding the work. Leaving the relationship would mean losing your sense of what happened to you. Nobody independent has ever spoken to you about that ceremony.

Now here's what's really happening. Dual relationships are prohibited in every clinical code for one reason: each additional role removes an exit. When your facilitator is also your integrator, you cannot process a bad experience of him with anyone but him, and a person's interpretation of their own most vulnerable hours becomes something another person authors. Post-ceremonial suggestibility is real and elevated for a meaningful period afterwards, which makes that window the highest-leverage one that exists. An honest practitioner treats it as the most dangerous part of their work and refers out. That referral is the single clearest marker of a real one.

So here's your check. I call it the L check. Ask: “How many roles does this person hold in my life?” — One is a relationship. Three is an enclosure.

And here's what sits underneath all of it. Who Authors What Happened To You. The lasting damage here is not financial and it is not even always sexual — it is authorship. If the only person who can tell you what your ceremony meant is the person who ran it, then a harm that occurred inside it is uninterpretable to you without his help, and he will help. People come out of these arrangements years later describing the disorientation of realizing they had no independent memory of their own experience, only his account of it. The counter is boringly practical and it has to be arranged in advance, while you still think you will not need it: a separate integrator, booked before the ceremony, who has never met the facilitator.

So here's your move. Separate the roles before you go in — book your integration with someone unconnected, ideally a licensed therapist familiar with this territory. Keep one relationship outside the whole ecosystem. If you are already inside it, the first move is one outside conversation, not a confrontation.

Here are the words. “I've got integration booked with someone separate — I keep those roles apart on principle. Can you send me your notes for them?”

And here's the truth underneath it. Nobody should be both the person who took you in and the only person who can bring you back.
