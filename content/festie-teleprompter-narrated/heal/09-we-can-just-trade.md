SCENARIO 9 — “We Can Just Trade”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let's talk about a specific type. Everyone, at every festival, because the gift economy is real and beautiful and almost entirely undocumented — which means every trade has terms nobody said out loud.

Here's the scene. You will do a session and he will sort you out for the weekend. It is a fair trade and you both meant it kindly. Then it is unclear whether one session covered it or three, and he keeps appearing, and on Saturday when you say you are done for the day there is a silence, and you realize that neither of you ever said what the deal was and he has been keeping a different tally.

So watch for these. A trade agreed in a sentence, with no scope and no end point. Value on both sides assumed rather than stated. The other party's side arrives first, creating an open obligation. Being traded substances, which ties your work to a supply. The deal is renegotiated afterwards, upward. You are doing sessions you do not want to do and cannot say why.

Here's the mechanism underneath it. Barter is not the problem — vagueness is. An undefined trade creates exactly the open-ended obligation that the reciprocity pages elsewhere in this book describe, with the additional feature that professional work is now entangled with a personal relationship and nobody can point to the terms. Trading for substances is the version worth refusing outright: it makes your practice dependent on a supply and a supplier, it is the clearest route to a dual relationship, and it converts a professional service into something you can be leaned on for.

So ask yourself this. I call it the L check. Ask: “Does this trade have a stated scope and an end?” — If not, it is not a trade, it is an account somebody else is keeping.

Now here's the part that's hard to hear. An Open Account Is A Hold. The damage is not the specific trade, it is the self-editing that starts once you believe you owe somebody something you cannot quantify. You do the extra session. You do not raise the thing that annoyed you. You keep the appointment you wanted to cancel. That is the same quiet accommodation the gift-with-a-ledger pages describe, and in a practitioner it is worse, because the currency you are paying in is unobserved physical access to your own time and body. Close the account explicitly, even at the cost of seeming transactional in a scene that prizes not being.

So here's what you do. Say the terms out loud and keep them small and closed — one session for one named thing, done, settled. Never trade for substances. Write the trade in a message so it exists. If an account has already opened, close it deliberately rather than letting it run.

Here's what to say, out loud. “Happy to trade — one session for the [x], and then we're square, yeah? Just so neither of us is keeping score. — I don't trade for anything you consume, but I'll do you a mate's rate.”

And remember this. A trade with no end point is not generosity, it is an open account. Say the number, even when it feels unspiritual.
