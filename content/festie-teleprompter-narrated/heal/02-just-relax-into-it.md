SCENARIO 2 — “Just Relax Into It”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let me tell you about a particular kind of person. Booked for one thing, doing a slightly different thing by minute twenty. Narrates it as responsiveness — the body asked for it, the energy moved there. Never quite announces the change, which is how you end up somewhere you did not agree to.

Here's how it plays out. You booked a shoulder session. Forty minutes in, his hands are on your ribs and then lower and he is saying something about where the grief is held, and you are running an internal calculation about whether objecting would make you the person who does not understand the work. He has not done anything you could name. He has moved four times, and each individual move was two inches.

So here's what to watch for. Contact moves to new areas without being named beforehand. Changes framed as the body's request rather than a practitioner's decision. Draping, positioning, or clothing renegotiated mid-session. Session runs long without being re-agreed. Objecting is pre-framed as not understanding the modality. Each step is small enough to be deniable; the sequence is not.

Now here's what's really going on. Progressive boundary erosion is the documented mechanism in the overwhelming majority of bodywork and therapeutic misconduct cases, and it works because a body on a table is passive, undressed, prone, and socially committed to trusting the person touching it. Each two-inch move is individually defensible, which is the design — the practitioner is not testing whether you consent, he is testing whether you will interrupt. That is why the professional standard in every legitimate manual therapy is spoken permission before each new region, every session, regardless of history. It removes the thing being exploited, which is your reluctance to be the one who makes it weird.

So run this check. I call it the H check. Ask: “Did they say what they were about to do, before they did it?” — Unannounced contact is the violation, whatever it turns out to be.

And here's the darker reality. They Are Testing The Interrupt, Not The Consent. Understanding this changes what you watch for. He is not building toward a moment where you agree — he is establishing that you do not interrupt, because a person who did not object at two inches is statistically less likely to object at four, and he knows it. That is also why the recovery from this is so hard afterwards: you will interrogate your own silence rather than his sequence. Put it down. The obligation was never on the person lying face-down and half-dressed to narrate the boundary. It was on the trained professional to announce the next move, and he chose not to, repeatedly, which is the finding.

So here's the play. Interrupt early and cheaply, because the first interruption is the whole thing — it costs almost nothing at minute five and everything at minute forty. Sit up. Sitting up ends the physical frame, and you can be polite from upright.

Here's exactly what to say. “Stop there — I booked shoulders, I want to stay on shoulders. — I'm going to sit up for a minute.”

And here's the truth. You do not owe a practitioner the benefit of the doubt while they are touching you. That is exactly the wrong moment for generosity.
