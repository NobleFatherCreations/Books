SCENARIO 9 — “Let Me Show You The Real Festival”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let me tell you about a particular kind of person. The veteran who dangles exclusive access — the private camp, the backstage, the "real" experience that regular attendees don't get — as a hook for keeping you in their orbit and dependent on their gatekeeping.

Here's how it plays out. "The main stages are for tourists. I'll show you the real festival — the private camps, the underground sets, the people who actually run this thing." By Day 2 you're spending all your time in his exclusive world and it IS more interesting. But you're also completely dependent on him for access to it, you've barely seen the artists you bought tickets to see, and your friends from home feel like they're at a different festival.

So here's what to watch for. The "real" festival is always exclusive and accessible only through them. Your original plans, interests, and friends become positioned as the lesser experience. Access to the exclusive world requires continued closeness and compliance. Independence is implicitly framed as missing out or being too naive to know better.

Now here's what's really going on. Access bait works by making your existing festival experience feel inadequate while positioning the gatekeeper as the key to something better. Your original excitement about the festival — the artists you love, the things you planned — gets subtly devalued. This is manufactured inadequacy. The festival you bought a ticket to is real and full and complete. Anyone who makes you feel like you're missing it by not being in their exclusive world wants something from you.

So run this check. I call it the R check. Ask: “Is this person showing me the festival or using the festival to show me I need them?”

And here's the darker reality. The Festival You Bought A Ticket To Is Complete. First-timers sometimes feel that the "real" experience is happening somewhere they're not. This is manufactured FOMO and it's extremely common. The sets you love, the art installations, the community stages, the random 3am campfire conversations — these are all real. The exclusive private camp is also real but it is not more real. Anyone who makes you feel like you're missing out unless you're with them is using FOMO as a control mechanism. You bought a ticket to a complete festival.

So here's the play. Explore the whole festival through your own curiosity, not through someone else's selective access.

Here's exactly what to say. “Thanks for the tour — I'm going to go catch the set I've been looking forward to. I'll find you later.”

And here's the truth. The festival you bought a ticket to is complete. You don't need a guide to access your own experience.
