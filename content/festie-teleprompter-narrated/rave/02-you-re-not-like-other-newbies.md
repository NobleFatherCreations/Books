SCENARIO 2 — “You're Not Like Other Newbies”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Here's the person you'll meet here. The one who singles you out as unusually ready, perceptive, or special for a first-timer. The compliment is real and lands hard. It also separates you from other first-timers — your most natural peer group — and attaches your self-image to their approval.

Picture it. "Most people who come for the first time don't get it like you do. You're different." She says it looking right at you and it feels completely true. By the next day you're slightly embarrassed to hang out with the other first-timers because you've been coded as more evolved. You're spending all your time with her crowd — people you've known for 36 hours — and your actual friends are somewhere on the other side of the festival.

Here are the signs. "You're not like other first-timers" — your natural peer group gets gently dismissed. The compliment attaches your self-worth to their assessment. You start to distance yourself from your actual friends to match the identity being offered. Your status in their world depends on staying close to them.

Now here's what's really happening. The pedestal ploy separates you from your natural support network by making that network feel beneath the version of yourself they're reflecting back. Other first-timers are your most reliable safety network this weekend. Anyone who positions themselves between you and that solidarity, however flatteringly, is removing your protection.

So here's your check. I call it the E check. Ask: “This person is still a stranger. Warmth and flattery are not the same thing as safety.”

And here's what sits underneath all of it. Your First-Timer Peer Group Is Your Greatest Asset. Other first-timers are your most honest mirrors this weekend — people also navigating the overwhelm, also learning the culture, also figuring it out. The veteran who makes you feel above that is separating you from your strongest source of solidarity. First-timer meetups exist at many festivals — seek them out on Day 1. You don't have to figure this out alone AND you don't have to figure it out through someone who wants something from you.

So here's your move. Receive the compliment. Stay with your people. Specialness that requires isolation isn't specialness.

Here are the words. “That means a lot — I'm gonna go check in with the people I came with. See you at the set tonight.”

And here's the truth underneath it. The people who came with you are your actual family this weekend. Don't let flattery move you away from them.
