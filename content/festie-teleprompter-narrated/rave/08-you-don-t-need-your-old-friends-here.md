SCENARIO 8 — “You Don't Need Your Old Friends Here”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Here's a specific type. The gradual replacement of your actual people with their world. Your old friends become characterized as not on your level, not ready for this, not able to understand. Their crew becomes your crew. Their values become your values.

Picture it. By Day 3 you've barely seen the people you drove up with. Every time you've mentioned them this weekend the veteran has had something slightly dismissive to say — "they seem pretty default world honestly," "they're not really engaging with the deeper experience." His people feel more real now. When your original friends text asking where you are, you feel vaguely embarrassed of them. That shift happened fast.

Here are the signs. Your existing friends characterized as less evolved, less open, less real. Their world and people become your primary social context over 48-72 hours. Your pre-festival identity starts to feel naive or lesser than your new festival self. Returning to your original group feels like a step backward.

Now here's what's really happening. Social network replacement feels like growth from the inside. The tell is direction: genuine growth expands your world — you come back from a transformative experience and eventually bring your existing people along. Predatory replacement contracts your world — your existing people get smaller and your connection to this new person gets bigger. Your friends who drove up with you know your full self. They are not the problem.

So here's your check. I call it the E check. Ask: “My original people are still strangers to this person. My original people actually know me.”

And here's what sits underneath all of it. Transformation Is Real — And It's Not Isolation. Festivals genuinely change people. Real transformation happens here. The difference between real transformation and predatory social replacement: real transformation makes you want to bring your people along on the journey, even if they're not ready yet. Predatory replacement makes your people feel like obstacles to your evolution. If you come home from your first festival and your existing friendships feel like a problem — not a growth edge, but a problem — talk to someone outside the festival context about what happened.

So here's your move. Let the experience expand you. Don't let anyone use the expansion to replace your foundation.

Here are the words. “I need to go spend some time with the people I came with — I'll find you tomorrow.”

And here's the truth underneath it. Real growth makes your existing world bigger. It doesn't require deleting it.
