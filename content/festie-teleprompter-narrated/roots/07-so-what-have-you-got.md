SCENARIO 7 — “So What Have You Got”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let me tell you about a particular kind of person. Strangers, all weekend, who have decided what you are doing here before speaking to you. Sometimes it is a drinks order. Sometimes it is a bin bag handed to you. Sometimes it is somebody asking, very directly, what you are holding.

Here's how it plays out. You are standing near the edge of a crowd, doing nothing, and a man comes up close and asks quietly what you have got. Then later somebody hands you an empty cup as though you were staff. Then somebody asks where the toilets are in the tone people use with stewards, and you are wearing exactly what everybody else is wearing.

So here's what to watch for. Approached about substances by strangers who have no reason to. Mistaken for staff, security or crew while in ordinary clothes. Spoken to in an instructional register by people who want service. Security attention that does not match what you are doing. It happens repeatedly, to you and not to the people you came with. Correcting it makes the other person embarrassed, and then annoyed.

Now here's what's really going on. Each instance is small and the pattern is not, and there is a specific risk hiding inside the most common version. Being repeatedly approached about substances by strangers puts you in conversations about drugs, in public, that you did not initiate and did not want — and undercover officers work festivals. A person who is approached fifteen times a weekend has fifteen chances to be observed having exactly the wrong conversation, and the observation does not come with the context that you were approached rather than approaching.

So run this check. I call it the R check. Ask: “Am I in a conversation I did not start, about something I am not doing?” — End it flatly and physically, not with an explanation.

And here's the darker reality. The Conversation You Did Not Start. This is where the misidentification stops being an indignity and becomes a legal exposure. The differential-enforcement problem from the security page applies directly: if a conversation about substances goes wrong, whose account gets believed is not a neutral question, and being the person who was standing there is a worse position than being the person who walked off. So the move is not wit and it is not a lecture about assumptions. It is ending the exchange in under three seconds and physically relocating, every time, no matter how tedious that becomes by Sunday — and having witnesses who can say you walked away.

So here's the play. Two flat words and a step away. Do not engage, do not joke, do not correct at length. Stay near the people you came with, because the approaches drop off sharply when you are visibly in a group.

Here's exactly what to say. “Nothing, mate. — [stepping away] Not staff, sorry. — [to your group] That's the fourth one today, I'm sticking with you lot.”

And here's the truth. You did not start that conversation and you do not owe it a reply. Walking off is the whole technique.
