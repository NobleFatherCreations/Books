SCENARIO 8 — “If Your Status Is The Risk”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let's talk about the person you'll meet here. For anyone whose worst-case outcome from any encounter with authority is not a fine or a night in a cell but a removal from the country, a visa consequence, or a family separation.

Here's the scene. Everything else in this book says get help, tell security, make a report. For you, every one of those sentences has a second half nobody wrote down, and you have been quietly translating the whole guide as you read it. This page does the translating out loud.

So watch for these. Any police contact carries a consequence unrelated to what happened. You avoid medical and welfare because you are unsure who they report to. You do not carry ID, or you carry documents you cannot afford to lose. Someone knows your status and has mentioned it more than once. A dispute would end with you being asked questions the other party will not be asked. No one at home knows exactly where you are this weekend.

Here's the mechanism underneath it. A safety guide that assumes a neutral escalation route is giving some readers a plan that raises their risk. Medical and welfare at festivals are generally not immigration functions and treating people is their job, but the calculation is legitimately different, and pretending otherwise is not kindness. The practical answer is the same as the escalation-ladder page, with more rungs: welfare, harm reduction, camp and community leads, artist liaison — all of which resolve most situations and none of which is a police interaction.

So ask yourself this. I call it the O check. Ask: “What is my worst-case outcome from this escalation — not the average person's?” — Plan from your answer, not from the generic one.

Now here's the part that's hard to hear. Status As Leverage. The thing to watch for is somebody who knows and mentions it. Status is among the most effective coercive levers that exists — it needs no threat, it silences reporting completely, and it is why people in precarious immigration positions are targeted deliberately for unpaid work, for sexual coercion, and for keeping quiet about both. If somebody has brought up your status in a conversation that was about something else, that is not conversation, and you are not the first person they have done it to. Get advice from an immigration charity rather than from anyone in the situation, and get it before you need it.

So here's what you do. Know the non-police rungs by name before you need them. Carry a copy rather than an original document, and leave the original somewhere safe. Have one person off-site who knows where you are and who to call. Find an immigration advice line now and save the number, while nothing is happening.

Here's what to say, out loud. “I'd rather handle this with welfare than with anyone else — can you get them? — [if your status is raised] We're not talking about that. What's actually the issue here?”

And remember this. The standard advice was written for somebody whose worst case is a fine. Build the version whose worst case is yours.
