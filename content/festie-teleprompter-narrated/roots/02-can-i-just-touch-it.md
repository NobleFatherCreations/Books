SCENARIO 2 — “Can I Just Touch It”
🎬 TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Let's talk about a specific type. Frequently a stranger, frequently delighted, frequently already reaching while asking — which means the question was decorative. Often drunk, often a woman, often genuinely baffled that this lands badly.

Here's the scene. Her hand is already in your hair. She is saying it is amazing and asking how you get it like that and whether it is all yours, and three people are watching and smiling, and the entire social weight of this moment is arranged so that the rude person in it would be you. You have about two seconds and the good options all cost something.

So watch for these. Asks while already reaching, or does not ask at all. Treats your hair, skin, or features as a topic for public discussion. Does it in front of people, so objecting has an audience cost. “Is it real”, “how do you get it like that”, “I've never touched” Wants a photo with you rather than of the moment. Reacts to a no as though you have been unkind to them.

Here's the mechanism underneath it. Unsolicited touching is a boundary violation regardless of intent, and the specific version aimed at Black women's hair is documented well enough to have prompted its own body of workplace and school policy. The mechanism at a festival is the environment: everyone is close, warm, uninhibited and touching each other constantly, which supplies a ready-made explanation for the contact and makes objecting feel like importing outside-world rules into a space that is supposed to be free of them. That framing is the problem. A culture of physical affection is consent-based or it is not a culture of affection, it is just a culture where some people get touched.

So ask yourself this. I call it the O check. Ask: “Did the question stop the hand?” — If the hand kept moving, it was not a question, and you owe it nothing.

Now here's the part that's hard to hear. The Cost Is The Accumulation. Any one of these is small. The reason it belongs in a safety guide is what a weekend of them does: by Sunday you have absorbed forty small interactions in which your body was treated as a shared object and you were required to be gracious about each one, and that steady grinding down of your standing to say no is precisely the ground condition every predator in this book is looking for. Someone who has watched you accept unwanted contact all weekend, smiling, has learned something about you that they should not have been allowed to learn. Practising the flat no on the small stuff is not pettiness. It is keeping the muscle available for the night you need it.

So here's what you do. Block with your body rather than your words — step back and put a hand up before you speak. Then a flat, short sentence with no apology and no explanation, and move on immediately so there is no space for a discussion.

Here's what to say, out loud. “No — don't touch my hair. — [flat, no smile, then turn away] Anyway. Have you seen where the water point is?”

And remember this. You are not a texture. A space that needs you to be gracious about being handled is not actually free for you.
