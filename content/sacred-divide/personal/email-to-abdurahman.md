### What The Sacred Divide actually is, structurally

Every one of the 34 traditions — from the largest to the smallest — gets an identical 27-section treatment, so nothing is compared unfairly or given a pass another tradition didn't get. That includes:

- **What's healthy here** — stated first, deliberately, and separately from anything critical. No tradition in the book is treated as only its worst documented moments.
- **History and structure** — who holds authority, how they got there, and whether any mechanism exists to remove them.
- **Money** — where funding comes from, what's disclosed, what isn't.
- **The 30 techniques** — a set of influence and control patterns drawn from domestic-abuse and social-psychology research, applied here to institutions instead of individuals. Every single instance is given a named evidence grade (Documented, Taught, Cultural, Contested, Reformed) tied to an actual citation — this section makes no claim it can't point to a source for.
- **Documented cases** — real, named, sourced incidents: court records, government inquiries, on-the-record reporting. Nothing anecdotal.
- **Voices from inside** — named people from within each tradition who raised hard questions publicly, so the book isn't only outside commentary.
- **Regional variants** — because the same theology plays out very differently depending on which state it sits inside.
- **Leaving safely, and where to get help** — practical, vetted, non-theological — because whatever else this book is, it's also meant to be useful to someone who needs it.
- **Sources** — every numbered citation in the text traces to a named, checkable entry at the end. Nothing ships that I haven't personally verified I could actually read.

The standard I'm holding myself to is: if I can't point to where a claim comes from, it doesn't go in the book. Where I've gotten something wrong, it's corrected and the correction is logged visibly on the page itself, not quietly edited away.
