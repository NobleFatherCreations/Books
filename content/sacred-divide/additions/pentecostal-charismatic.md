| **Foursquare and other founder-denominations** | The Foursquare Church, founded by Aimee Semple McPherson (incorporated 1927) [15] | A founder's constitution, later a board |
| **Charismatic renewal** | Charismatic groups inside Catholic and mainline churches | The host church's own structure |
| **Word of Faith and prosperity ministries** | The line from E. W. Kenyon to Kenneth Hagin; Oral Roberts's "seed faith" [16] | The founder, often with a board the founder chose |
| **Global megachurch networks** | Redeemed Christian Church of God and Living Faith (Nigeria); the Universal Church of the Kingdom of God (Brazil); Hillsong (Australia) [17][18] | A founder or founding family; in Brazil the founder also owns a national TV network [17] |

Pew counted about **584 million** Pentecostal and charismatic Christians in 2011 [14].

## law
| Country | What the law does | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| **United States** | Churches file no public return; the Senate's inquiry into six media ministries (2007–2011) closed without penalties [4] | Jim Bakker's fraud conviction came through the criminal courts, not church regulation [3] |
| **United Kingdom** | Churches that are charities file public accounts; the Charity Commission can appoint an interim manager [9] | It did so at Kingsway International Christian Centre after a 2005 inquiry [9] |
| **Australia** | The charity regulator (ACNC) can accept compliance agreements [10] | Hillsong entered agreements and an undertaking in December 2024 [10] |
| **South Korea** | Breach of trust and tax evasion are criminal offences for pastors as for anyone [5] | The founder of the world's largest congregation was convicted in 2014 [5] |
| **Nigeria** | Section 839 of the 2020 companies law lets the state suspend trustees of charitable bodies, including churches [11] | The Christian Association of Nigeria opposed it [11] |
| **South Africa** | A constitutional commission investigated the "commercialisation of religion" in 2017 [7] | Its licensing recommendation was resisted by churches [7] |
| **Brazil** | Prosecutors can charge church leaders [8] | São Paulo charges against Edir Macedo (2009) expired without a verdict [8] |

## cases
### Defrocked, then convicted (United States, 1987–1989)
- **when:** 1987–1989
- **what:** The Assemblies of God defrocked Jim Bakker in May 1987 [1]. In October 1989 he was convicted on all 24 counts of fraud and conspiracy over the PTL ministry's "lifetime partnerships" [3]. The same denomination dismissed Jimmy Swaggart in 1988 [2].
- **record:** UPI, 6 May 1987 and 5 October 1989 [1][3]; *Christianity Today*, May 1988 [2]
- **outcome:** The rare case of a denomination's own removal mechanism being used — against two of its most famous preachers [1][2].
- **tactics:** 26, 3
- **grade:** Documented

### The world's largest congregation and its founder (South Korea, 2014)
- **when:** 2014
- **what:** Cho Yong-gi, founder of Yoido Full Gospel Church, was convicted of breach of trust and tax evasion causing the church a loss of ₩13.1 billion [5].
- **record:** *Korea Times*, 20 February 2014 [5]
- **outcome:** Three years' imprisonment, suspended for five, and a ₩5 billion fine [5].
- **tactics:** 26, 30
- **grade:** Documented

### The guesthouse collapse (Nigeria, 2014)
- **when:** 2014
- **what:** A guesthouse at T. B. Joshua's Synagogue Church of All Nations in Lagos collapsed on 12 September 2014, killing 116 people, most of them South African pilgrims. A coroner found criminal negligence; Joshua was the only summoned witness who refused to testify [6].
- **record:** *Vanguard*, July 2015 [6]; BBC Africa Eye, *Disciples* (2024) [13]
- **outcome:** Joshua died in 2021; the BBC later documented abuse allegations from more than 30 former members [13].
- **tactics:** 30, 18, 12
- **grade:** Documented

### A regulator takes over a church (United Kingdom, 2005)
- **when:** 2005
- **what:** A Charity Commission inquiry into Kingsway International Christian Centre, a London Pentecostal church, found misconduct and mismanagement [9].
- **record:** Charity Commission inquiry report [9]
- **outcome:** Control passed to an interim manager appointed by the regulator [9].
- **tactics:** 26
- **grade:** Documented

### A student's lawsuit (Australia, 2020–2024)
- **when:** 2020–2024
- **what:** Anna Crenshaw, a student at Hillsong College, was indecently assaulted by a Hillsong staff member, who pleaded guilty in January 2020; no conviction was recorded. She sued two Hillsong entities and the man in the NSW Supreme Court in 2022 [22].
- **record:** Hillsong Church statement, 3 May 2024 [22]
- **outcome:** The case settled as trial was due to begin in April 2024; Hillsong said it "has never disputed the assault occurred" [22].
- **tactics:** 22, 30
- **grade:** Documented

## leaving
Practical guidance, not legal advice.

1. **Stop the pledges first.** Cancel recurring "seed", partnership and building-fund payments in writing and through your bank.
2. **Your health decisions are yours.** No healing promise replaces a doctor; tell your doctor what you were told.
3. **If you work for the church,** get your employment terms in writing before you announce anything.
4. **If a church is a UK charity,** you can report concerns to the Charity Commission [9]; in Australia, the ACNC [10].
5. **If you were abused,** go to the police first, not the pastor.
6. **Find support** [25][26][27].
