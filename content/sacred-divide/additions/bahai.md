## branches
| Body | Where | What differs on this page's questions |
|---|---|---|
| **The Baháʼí Faith** (Universal House of Justice) | Worldwide; seat in Haifa | Nine members elected every five years by national assembly members, with no nominations or campaigning; women are not eligible [1][2][3] |
| **National and local spiritual assemblies** | Every country with Baháʼís | Elected annually; national assemblies are often registered charities [11] |
| **Small dissident groups** (e.g. Orthodox Baháʼís) | — | Designated "covenant-breakers" by the main body |

## cases
### Removed from the rolls (1997–2005)
- **when:** 1997–2005
- **what:** Michael McKenny (1997), Alison Marshall (2000) and the scholar Sen McGlinn (2005) were removed from membership after online discussion and scholarship the institutions objected to [5][6][7].
- **record:** Their own published accounts and documents [5][6][7]
- **outcome:** No appeal outside the institutions that made the decisions [5][6][7].
- **tactics:** 17, 28
- **grade:** Documented

### A scholar resigns under warning (1996)
- **when:** 1996
- **what:** Juan Cole, a historian of the Middle East, resigned from the Faith after being warned over his statements on an online discussion list [4].
- **record:** Juan R. I. Cole, personal statement [4]
- **outcome:** He resigned in 1996 [4].
- **tactics:** 17, 19
- **grade:** Documented

### A second decade in prison (Iran, 2008–2022)
- **when:** 2008–2022
- **what:** Mahvash Sabet and Fariba Kamalabadi, former members of the "Yaran" who administered Iran's Baháʼí community, served ten years from 2008 to 2018 [12].
- **record:** USCIRF statement, 2022 [12]
- **outcome:** In 2022, after an hour-long trial in Tehran's Revolutionary Court, both were sentenced to another ten years [12].
- **tactics:** —
- **grade:** Documented

## regional
### Iran
- **apex:** None permitted; the Yaran were jailed [12].
- **law:** No recognition; prosecution for community activity [8][12].
- **documented:** The UN Special Rapporteur's 2024 report [8]; the 2024 surge in arrests [9].
- **exit:** —
- **regulator:** The state is the persecutor [8].
- **tell:** The birthplace of the Faith treats its administration as a threat to national security.

### United Kingdom
- **apex:** The elected National Spiritual Assembly [11].
- **law:** Charity law [11].
- **documented:** Public accounts [11].
- **exit:** Legally free.
- **regulator:** The Charity Commission [11].
- **tell:** Elections without campaigns, and accounts anyone can read.

### Worldwide (the institutions)
- **apex:** The Universal House of Justice [1].
- **law:** The Faith's own administrative order [1][3].
- **documented:** The removals from the rolls [5][6][7].
- **exit:** Resignation is possible; removal is decided without an outside appeal [5][6][7].
- **regulator:** None outside the institutions.
- **tell:** One of the most democratic structures in this book, with no outside route of appeal.

## leaving
Practical guidance, not legal advice.

1. **You can resign in writing;** removal from the rolls is a separate process decided by the institutions [5][6][7].
2. **Resigning is not the same as being declared a "covenant-breaker".**
3. **In Iran,** membership itself carries legal risk; seek advice before travelling [8][9].
4. **Find support** [13][14][15][16].
