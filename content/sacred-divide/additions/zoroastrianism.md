## cases
### Married out, and shut out (India, 2010–present)
- **when:** 2010–present
- **what:** Goolrokh Gupta, a Parsi woman who married a Hindu man under the Special Marriage Act, was barred by the Parsi trust in Valsad from Parsi institutions, and feared she would be kept from her parents' funeral rites; the Gujarat High Court held that she had ceased to be Parsi [4][6].
- **record:** *Goolrokh Gupta v. Burjor Pardiwala* (Supreme Court of India) [4]
- **outcome:** In 2017 the Supreme Court let her attend her parents' rites; the wider question went to a larger bench [4].
- **tactics:** 14, 24
- **grade:** Documented

### A prayer at the agiary (India, 2026)
- **when:** 2026
- **what:** A Parsi woman who married a Hindu man challenged a Nagpur panchayat rule that excludes women who marry out, and their children, while imposing nothing comparable on men [5].
- **record:** LiveLaw, 25 May 2026 [5]
- **outcome:** A bench led by the Chief Justice explored interim permission for her to pray at Nagpur's only agiary and asked the panchayat to respond [5].
- **tactics:** 14
- **grade:** Documented

### Who counts as Parsi (India, 1908)
- **when:** 1908
- **what:** The Bombay High Court ruled on who counts as a Parsi for the community's institutions [6].
- **record:** *Petit v. Jijibhai* (1908) [6]
- **outcome:** The ruling set the boundary still argued over today [4][6].
- **tactics:** 14
- **grade:** Documented

## leaving
Practical guidance, not legal advice.

1. **Marrying out** is legal everywhere; in India it can affect a woman's access to fire temples and funeral rites [4].
2. **Ask before you assume:** some trusts and temples have changed practice; the Supreme Court is still deciding [4][5].
3. **Find support** [11][12][13].
