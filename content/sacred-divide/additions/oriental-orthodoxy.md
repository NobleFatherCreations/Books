## law
| Country | What the law does | The accountability question |
|---|---|---|
| **Egypt** | Personal-status law is religious: the Coptic Church controls divorce and remarriage for Copts, and the Supreme Constitutional Court upheld that in 2010 [4]; a 2016 law (Law 80) regulates building and repairing churches [8] | A Copt in a failed marriage has no civil route out [4] |
| **Eritrea** | Only state-registered faiths may practise; the state removed the Orthodox patriarch in 2006 [2] | He died in 2022 after more than 15 years under house arrest [2] |
| **Ethiopia** | The Derg nationalized rural land in 1975, including the church's [5] | The state settled the 2023 synod schism [7] |
| **Armenia** | Criminal cases against senior clergy and the Catholicos, 2025–26 [12][13] | Twenty-five bishops publicly backed the Catholicos [13] |
| **India** | Secular courts decide church property: the Supreme Court gave the Malankara Orthodox faction the right to administer about 1,100 churches (2017) [3] | Kerala's government has said it cannot implement the ruling [3] |

## voices
- **Abune Antonios.** Eritrea's patriarch, removed in 2006 after refusing, among other things, to excommunicate members of a Sunday-school renewal movement; he died under house arrest in 2022 [2].
- **The twenty-five Armenian bishops** who publicly backed their Catholicos against the government in 2025–26 [13].
- **The Coptic man who asked the courts to let him remarry.** The Supreme Administrative Court agreed in 2010; the Pope refused, and the Supreme Constitutional Court sided with the church [4].
- **The Oromia bishops of 2023,** whose break over language and representation forced the church and the government to the table [7][17].

## regional
### Egypt
- **apex:** The Coptic Pope — Tawadros II, chosen by altar lot in 2012 [6].
- **law:** Church-controlled personal status [4]; the 2016 church-construction law [8].
- **documented:** The 2011 Alexandria and 2017 Palm Sunday bombings [9][10]; the 2018 monastery murder [11]; the remarriage cases [4].
- **exit:** Leaving the church can mean leaving the only lawful route to marriage and divorce available to you [4].
- **regulator:** The courts — which returned marriage to the church [4].
- **tell:** A minority church that needs the state's protection, and a state that leaves the church in charge of Christian marriage.

### Ethiopia
- **apex:** The Patriarch and Holy Synod.
- **law:** The 1975 land nationalization [5].
- **documented:** The 1979 execution of Patriarch Theophilos [5]; the 2023 schism and settlement [7]; the Tigrayan church's separate administration [17].
- **exit:** Where church and ethnicity coincide, leaving carries family costs.
- **regulator:** The federal government, in practice [7].
- **tell:** The largest Oriental church has twice had its future decided in the prime minister's office.

### Eritrea
- **apex:** The patriarch — since 2025, Abune Basilios [18].
- **law:** State registration of faiths [2].
- **documented:** Abune Antonios's removal and house arrest [2].
- **exit:** Practising outside the registered faiths risks detention [2].
- **regulator:** None independent.
- **tell:** The plainest case in this book of a state choosing a church's head.

### Armenia
- **apex:** The Catholicos of All Armenians — Karekin II, elected in 1999 [15].
- **law:** Criminal prosecutions of clergy, 2025–26 [12][13].
- **documented:** The 2026 trial of the Catholicos and six bishops [13].
- **exit:** Legally free.
- **regulator:** The courts, under a government in open conflict with the church [12].
- **tell:** Here the pressure runs the other way: a national church with an elected, largely lay assembly, facing a state that wants its leader gone.

### India
- **apex:** Two rival authorities in Kerala: the Malankara Orthodox Catholicos and the Jacobite faction under the Syriac patriarch [3].
- **law:** The 2017 Supreme Court judgment [3].
- **documented:** Decades of litigation over about 1,100 churches [3].
- **exit:** Legally free.
- **regulator:** The courts and the state government [3].
- **tell:** A judgment that settled ownership on paper, which the state says it cannot enforce.

## leaving
Practical guidance, not legal advice.

1. **In Egypt,** marriage and divorce for Copts run through the church; take legal advice before you act on either [4].
2. **In Eritrea,** religious practice outside the registered faiths risks detention [2]; if you are abroad, take asylum advice before returning.
3. **In the diaspora,** leaving is legally free; the costs are family and community ones.
4. **If you were abused,** go to the police, not the priest or bishop.
5. **Find support** [20][21][22].
