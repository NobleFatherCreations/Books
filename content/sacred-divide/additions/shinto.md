## cases
### The dormitory sale and the whistleblower (Japan, 2015–2022)
- **when:** 2015–2022
- **what:** Jinja Honcho sold a staff dormitory in Kawasaki for ¥184 million in October 2015; it was resold the next month for ¥212.4 million and six months later for ¥305 million. Takao Ina, a senior official, alleged a breach of trust by the leadership in December 2016; Jinja Honcho dismissed him in August 2017 and demoted a colleague [12].
- **record:** Tokyo District Court (March 2021), upheld on appeal and final in 2022 [1][12]
- **outcome:** The dismissal was declared void [1].
- **tactics:** 17, 28, 30
- **grade:** Documented

### Two presidents at once (Japan, 2022–2024)
- **when:** 2022–2024
- **what:** Two men each claimed to be the next president of Jinja Honcho [2].
- **record:** *Tokyo Shimbun* [2]; Jinja Honcho notice [3]
- **outcome:** The Supreme Court rejected the rival claim on 2 October 2024 [2][3].
- **tactics:** 30
- **grade:** Documented

### A succession feud ends in murder (Japan, 2017)
- **when:** 2017
- **what:** Nagako Tomioka, chief priestess of Tokyo's Tomioka Hachimangu, was killed by her brother, a former chief priest who had left the post in 2001 and had threatened her in writing in 2006 [13].
- **record:** NPR, 8 December 2017 [13]
- **outcome:** Three people died, including the attacker; police treated it as a succession feud [13].
- **tactics:** —
- **grade:** Documented

## leaving
Practical guidance, not legal advice.

1. **Shinto has no membership to leave;** the pressure comes from neighbourhood festival levies and shrine-support associations, which you can decline.
2. **Public bodies may not fund shrine rites;** the Supreme Court has said so twice [4].
3. **If you work for a shrine body,** employment law protects whistleblowers — the courts voided a dismissal [1].
4. **Find support** [14][15][16].
