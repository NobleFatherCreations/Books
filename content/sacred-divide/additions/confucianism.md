- **id:** `confucianism`
- **status:** researched and fact-checked 2026-09-27. `[n]` cites `sources/confucianism.md` (1–8 from pass 1; 9–15 added with this file).
- **note:** Confucianism has no church, clergy or membership; its authority lives in families and in law. The cases below are therefore laws and court rulings, not scandals.

## branches

Formal identification as "Confucian" is rare; Confucian-origin family practice is widespread [5].

## law

|---|---|---|

## moneyNumbers
Confucianism has no donation economy of its own; its costs are paid in family labour, deference and life choices (see Money above).

- **Eldercare by law (China):** adult children who neglect parents can be ordered by a court to visit [3][12].
- **Confucius Institutes:** a state-funded network of language and culture centres abroad since 2004 [7].
- **Lineage halls and ancestral rites** hold family funds in some regions (see Money above).

## cases
### The court-ordered visit (China, 2013)
- **when:** 2013
- **what:** A 77-year-old woman sued her daughter and son-in-law, who had stopped visiting her, under the new elderly-rights law [12].
- **record:** Beitang District court, Wuxi, reported by AFP, July 2013 [12]
- **outcome:** The court ordered them to visit at least once every two months [12].
- **tactics:** 22
- **grade:** Documented

### The family head abolished (South Korea, 2005–2008)
- **when:** 2005–2008
- **what:** Women's-rights activists and the National Human Rights Commission challenged the *hoju* system, which made a man the legal head of each family [10][11].
- **record:** Constitutional Court of Korea, March 2005 [10]; National Human Rights Commission opinion, 2003 [11]
- **outcome:** Found unconstitutional; replaced with individual registration from 1 January 2008 [10].
- **tactics:** 23, 24
- **grade:** Documented

### Married to someone of the same clan (South Korea, 1997)
- **when:** 1997
- **what:** Tens of thousands of couples who shared a surname and ancestral seat could not legally marry [9].
- **record:** Constitutional Court of Korea, 16 July 1997; *Christian Science Monitor*, 4 August 1997 [9]
- **outcome:** The court overturned the ban [9].
- **tactics:** 14
- **grade:** Documented

## leaving
Practical guidance, not legal advice.

1. **There is no membership to leave;** the pressure comes from family expectations about care, marriage and career.
2. **In China,** the law can require you to visit your parents [3][12].
3. **If family pressure becomes abuse or forced marriage,** use specialist help [14].
4. **Find support** [13][15].
