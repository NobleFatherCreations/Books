## 2. A day inside {#a-day-inside}

*Sepideh · A Tuesday in Ayyám-i-Há · A city in Iran*

Prayers at dawn, alone, quietly, in a flat where the window faces a courtyard. Her grandmother taught her the Arabic. Her grandmother was in Evin for four years.

She is twenty-two and cannot attend university. Not because of her marks — her marks were excellent — but because of the box on the form. She studies instead with the community's own institute, over patchy internet, with a lecturer in another city who has been arrested twice.

At ten her father's shop is closed for the fourth time this year by an inspector who does not write anything down. Her father makes tea for the inspector. The inspector drinks it. This is how it goes.

In the afternoon she teaches a children's class — six kids, a rug, songs about unity and the oneness of humanity, and she believes every word, and she has no idea how she would explain to a foreigner that this is the most dangerous thing she does all week.

At six she reads an essay a Bahá'í academic abroad has written about the Faith's early history. It is careful and it is accurate and it has not been through review, and there was a note attached about that, and Sepideh does not know what she thinks. She thinks the essay is good. She thinks the review policy exists. She holds both.

At night she and her mother count the cemetery. The graves were bulldozed in 2021 and it is not clear whether they will be allowed to bury her grandmother where her grandfather is. Sepideh prays for the officials by name, because the Writings say to, and she does it without irony, and then she lies down and thinks: everything trying to erase us is outside this room. That is true and it is nearly the whole story. Nearly.

## 3. The forefront {#forefront}

### The strongest objection, answered

**The objection.** Bahá'ís are executed and imprisoned in Iran. Criticism of this Faith punches down.

**What is true in it.** Entirely true, and this profile leads with it. This community is far more often the target of coercion than its author, and its structural safeguards — no clergy, elected bodies, member-only funding — are genuine and unusual.

**The answer.** Which is why the section is short and limited to three mechanisms, all of which the Faith publishes openly. A religion teaching independent investigation of truth cannot also hold approval rights over its members' published conclusions. That is an internal inconsistency, not an attack.

## 5. History {#history}

Founded by Baháʼu'lláh in 19th-century Iran/Ottoman exile out of the Bábí movement; leadership passed through appointed interpreters to the elected Universal House of Justice (1963); severely persecuted in Iran. [GOVERNMENT REPORT: UN Special Rapporteur on Iran, 2024]

### Timeline

```timeline
1844 | The Báb declares his mission in Shiraz | A messianic movement inside Shi'i Iran; thousands of followers killed in the ensuing persecution.
1863 | Baháʼu'lláh declares his prophetic station in Baghdad exile | The founding claim, made in exile and under state hostility.
1868–1892 | Imprisonment in Akka; writings composed; Covenant established | Succession is designated in writing specifically to prevent schism — an unusual founding safeguard.
1892–1921 | ʻAbduʼl-Bahá leads; Western expansion begins | The Faith becomes global and interracial early, well ahead of its era.
1921–1957 | Shoghi Effendi as Guardian; administrative order built | Elected institutions replace clergy — a deliberate structural anti-clericalism.
1957–1963 | Interregnum; Universal House of Justice first elected 1963 | Authority becomes fully institutional and elected; Covenant-breaker designations used against rival claimants. [OFFICIAL POLICY]
1979–present | Islamic Republic persecution: executions, imprisonment, university exclusion, cemetery desecration | One of the most systematically persecuted religious minorities in the world today. [GOVERNMENT REPORT: UN Special Rapporteur reporting]
1980s–1990s | Bahá'í Institute for Higher Education founded after university bans; faculty arrested | A community builds an underground university because the state bars its youth from education. [GOVERNMENT REPORT]
1990s–2000s | Disputes over pre-publication review and academic freedom; some scholars disenrolled or resign | The tradition's internal control question surfaces around its own historians. [ACADEMIC SOURCE / FORMER MEMBER TESTIMONY]
2000s–present | Ruhi/Institute process global expansion; continued Iranian persecution | Growth through structured study circles; persecution unabated.
```

### Moments in the room

::: card
#### 1963 — The first election of the House of Justice

Members of the national assemblies convened and elected the nine-member Universal House of Justice — without nominations, campaigning, or candidate information, by design, to prevent factionalism.

**Why it matters.** A genuinely electoral apex, and the design that prevents factionalism is the same design that prevents an informed vote. Both descend from one intentional choice.
:::

::: card
#### 1979–1988 — The Iranian executions

Following the revolution, Bahá'ís in Iran faced executions, imprisonment, property seizure, and exclusion from higher education. The persecution is thoroughly documented by UN bodies and continues.

**Why it matters.** Recorded first and plainly, because this page's later questions about internal governance must not be readable as ammunition for a state that is killing people. Real persecution and internal critique are both true, and the codex refuses to trade one for the other.
:::

::: card
#### 1990s–2000s — The review policy meets the internet

As Bahá'í academics published historical work outside the review process, several were subject to administrative sanction and the boundary of permissible scholarship became a live public dispute.

**Why it matters.** The mechanism the page's unanswered question turns on: an electorate that cannot read unreviewed material about its own candidates is not being asked to choose.
:::

## 10. Genealogy {#genealogy}

::: card
#### Covenant-breaker designation and mandatory shunning

**Origin.** Established in the Faith's early succession disputes to prevent the schisms that fractured earlier religions. [OFFICIAL POLICY]

**What it was for.** A genuine and understandable problem: the founder watched rival claimants tear at the community and wrote in a mechanism to protect unity.

**Why that reason expired.** It works — the Faith is unusually unified. But the price is a policy requiring members to sever contact with a designated person including their own family. Unity purchased by mandated shunning is not unity; it is the appearance of unity with the disagreement removed from view.

**Who benefits now.** The administrative order's continuity and narrative control. The cost is paid by designated individuals and by their relatives, who must choose.
:::

::: card
#### Pre-publication review

**Origin.** Instituted as the Faith spread, to prevent inaccurate representations while it was small and widely misunderstood. [OFFICIAL POLICY]

**What it was for.** A young religion facing hostile misrepresentation wanted its own accounts to be accurate. Defensible at the time.

**Why that reason expired.** It is now an explicit, written requirement that members submit manuscripts about their own religion for institutional review before publishing — one of very few such systems in any modern religion. A faith that teaches independent investigation of truth cannot also vet the conclusions.

**Who benefits now.** Institutional control of the historical narrative. Scholars have reported pressure and disenrollment episodes over critical historical work. [ACADEMIC SOURCE / FORMER MEMBER TESTIMONY]
:::

::: card
#### Exclusion of women from the Universal House of Justice

**Origin.** Stated in the authoritative texts without explanation, in a religion whose central social teaching is the equality of men and women. [OFFICIAL POLICY]

**What it was for.** No purpose is given. That is itself the notable fact.

**Why that reason expired.** The Faith taught women's equality in 19th-century Iran, at real cost, and was right decades ahead of its neighbors. The single exclusion sits inside that record unexplained, which means it is defended by authority alone.

**Who benefits now.** Nothing identifiable, materially. It is the cleanest example in the codex of a rule sustained purely because questioning it is out of bounds.
:::

::: card
#### Prohibition on partisan political involvement

**Origin.** Instituted to keep a global, multi-ethnic community from fracturing along national and factional lines. [OFFICIAL POLICY]

**What it was for.** Coherent and, in many contexts, protective — particularly for members under hostile governments.

**Why that reason expired.** Partially: it also removes an entire community from ordinary civic contestation, including on matters affecting them, and it makes institutional direction the only permitted channel for collective action.

**Who benefits now.** Institutional unity, and in hostile states, arguably members' safety. This one is genuinely double-edged and the codex says so.
:::

## 11. Reach {#reach}

### Information

- Pre-publication review requires members to submit manuscripts about the Faith to institutional review before publishing — an explicit, written information control unusual among modern religions. [OFFICIAL POLICY]
- Covenant-breaker designation obliges members to shun the designated person entirely, including family — the sharpest sanction in the system. [OFFICIAL POLICY]
- Partisan political involvement is prohibited, which protects unity and also removes members from ordinary civic contestation. [OFFICIAL POLICY]

### Children

- Children's classes and junior youth spiritual empowerment programs are central to the Institute process; participation is voluntary and typically warm.
- Declaration of faith at 15 is treated as the age of maturity — notably, an explicit age of consent for religious commitment, better than most traditions here. [OFFICIAL POLICY]
- In Iran, Bahá'í children have faced school harassment and their families' businesses closed — harm imposed from outside, not within. [GOVERNMENT REPORT]

### Bodies

- Equality of men and women is explicit scripture and is practiced substantially in community life — one of the strongest positions in this codex.
- The exception: women are ineligible for the Universal House of Justice, the highest institution. Doctrinally unexplained, and the clearest internal inequality. [OFFICIAL POLICY]
- Chastity outside heterosexual marriage is required; same-sex marriage is not recognized, and LGBTQ members must remain celibate to stay in good standing. [OFFICIAL POLICY]
- Divorce is permitted after a year of patience — comparatively unrestrictive.

## 12. The 30 techniques {#techniques}

Thirty named techniques from domestic-abuse and social-psychology research, applied to institutions, in the eight stages of the cycle. Each carries an evidence grade for this tradition.

### Stage 1 · Idealize {#stage-1}

::: stage
**You arrive with a need and are met with more warmth than you have had in years.**

Unity, interfaith warmth, racial harmony, global peace, no clergy, no hellfire. It is the gentlest entry in this codex and the offer is largely what it appears to be.

*What it asks of you:* Notice how little pressure you felt. Then ask what happens if you organize a disagreement.
:::

::: tactic n=1
#### 1 · Love Bombing {#t-1}

*Overwhelming affection on arrival to create emotional dependency before terms are revealed.*

**How it shows here**

- Seekers are welcomed with unity language, interfaith warmth, firesides, consultation, global peace ideals, racial harmony, and the promise of a religion beyond sectarian conflict.
- The first impression is often unusually gentle: no clergy, no hellfire preaching, no crude tribalism, no obvious coercive ritual.
- The deeper obligations appear later: administrative order, obedience to institutions, restrictions on partisan politics, sexual norms, covenant loyalty, and strong discouragement of public dissent.

**The strongest defense.** The Baháʼí Faith emphasizes unity, peace, and independent investigation of truth.

**The counter.** “Independent investigation” is compromised when the acceptable conclusion is already mapped. If questioning is praised on the way in but treated as disunity once inside, then unity is not peace. It is managed agreement.

**Evidence grade.** [[Cultural]] Firesides and study circles are warm and locally run; the entry experience is among the gentlest here.
:::

::: tactic n=2
#### 2 · Weaponized Generosity {#t-2}

*Giving help that installs unspoken obligation.*

**How it shows here**

- Baháʼí communities may offer warmth through firesides, study circles, children’s classes, junior youth programs, consultation, service projects, and unity-centered friendship.
- Seekers may be welcomed into a global vision: peace, racial unity, interfaith harmony, equality, and independent investigation of truth.
- The obligation can appear later through institutional obedience, participation in community plans, avoidance of partisan politics, sexual norms, administrative loyalty, and discouragement of public dissent.
- Service becomes a spiritual currency: the person who received community is expected to build community, teach the Faith, host gatherings, and align with institutional goals.

**The strongest defense.** Baháʼís do not have clergy and emphasize independent investigation, consultation, and service.

**The counter.** A religion does not need clergy to create obligation. Administration can function as authority just as effectively as priesthood. If the community gives warmth to seekers but later treats dissent as disunity, then unity has become the invoice for belonging.

**Evidence grade.** [[Cultural]] Firesides and study circles are warm and locally run; the entry experience is among the gentlest here.
:::

### Stage 2 · Hook {#stage-2}

::: stage
**You are given a future that cannot be verified, and a rope for whenever you drift toward the door.**

World unity, the peace of humanity, the Faith's global plan advancing through your service — a future genuinely aimed at the good, and permanently ahead of you. Retrieval for the ordinary lapsed is warm and easy; for the designated, it requires recantation.

*What it asks of you:* The promise here is unusually decent. The question is still who decides when you have contributed enough.
:::

::: tactic n=3
#### 3 · Future Faking {#t-3}

*Promising a future that keeps you invested but never has to arrive.*

**How it shows here**

- The Baháʼí Faith offers a sweeping future: world unity, peace, racial harmony, religious reconciliation, global governance, moral maturity of humanity, and the eventual unification of the human family.
- The believer is invited to build the future now through service, study circles, children’s classes, junior youth programs, teaching, consultation, and obedience to administrative institutions.
- The promise is historically enormous but personally demanding: your small acts are part of the spiritual evolution of civilization.
- Dissent can be reframed as delaying unity. Public criticism may be treated as disunity, ego, covenant-breaking, or failure to understand the long arc of divine order.
- The future is always coming through the administrative plan. If it has not arrived, humanity is immature, the believers must serve more, or the process needs more time.

**The strongest defense.** Baháʼís work sincerely for peace, unity, racial justice, and global cooperation.

**The counter.** Noble goals can still function as future faking. If the promise of world unity is used to suppress present dissent, protect administration from criticism, or demand endless unpaid service, then peace has become a horizon that moves whenever accountability gets close.

**Evidence grade.** [[Taught]] World unity and the Faith's global plan are taught as the frame for service; retrieval for the ordinary lapsed is informal.
:::

::: tactic n=4
#### 4 · Hoovering {#t-4}

*Pulling someone back after they've started to leave, through guilt, love, or fear.*

**How it shows here**

- Someone distancing from the Baháʼí community may be gently contacted through invitations to firesides, study circles, devotionals, children’s classes, consultation, or service projects.
- The pull is usually soft: “We miss your spirit,” “The community needs your gifts,” “Unity requires everyone.”
- Doubt may be reframed as disunity, impatience, ego, misunderstanding of the Covenant, or failure to appreciate the long process of building world peace.
- Former members may be urged not to criticize publicly because public criticism is seen as harmful to unity.
- The person may be pulled back through idealism: racial unity, global peace, interfaith harmony, and the idea that leaving means stepping away from humanity’s future.

**The strongest defense.** Baháʼís emphasize unity and service, so reconnecting people is loving and constructive.

**The counter.** Unity becomes hoovering when it cannot tolerate honest departure. If the person is welcomed back only through silence, administrative loyalty, and renewed service, then the invitation is not reconciliation. It is reintegration.

**Evidence grade.** [[Taught]] World unity and the Faith's global plan are taught as the frame for service; retrieval for the ordinary lapsed is informal.
:::

### Stage 3 · Devalue {#stage-3}

::: stage
**You are taught that you are broken, that your perception is unreliable, and that both exits from the trap lead back inside.**

Framed as disunity rather than deficiency. You are not insufficiently pious; you are insufficiently unified.

*What it asks of you:* 'Disunity' is a category that makes disagreement into a moral failure without ever calling you a sinner.
:::

::: tactic n=5
#### 5 · Devaluation {#t-5}

*Reducing your sense of worth so you become dependent on the institution for identity.*

**How it shows here**

- The Baháʼí Faith can devalue dissent by framing criticism as disunity, ego, impatience, covenant weakness, or failure to understand the long arc of global transformation.
- The individual is invited to serve humanity, but that ideal can make personal grievances seem petty.
- If someone questions administration, sexual rules, political restrictions, or institutional decisions, they may be treated as spiritually immature or harmful to unity.
- The self is diminished beneath the future of world peace.
- The believer may internalize the idea that their pain matters less than the community’s image of harmony.

**The strongest defense.** Baháʼís value independent investigation of truth and consultation.

**The counter.** Independent investigation is hollow if dissent is honored only before enrollment. If questioning is praised while it leads inward but pathologized when it leads outward, then unity has become a beautiful name for compliance.

**Evidence grade.** [[Taught]] Insufficiency is framed as disunity rather than depravity, in institutional guidance.
:::

::: tactic n=6
#### 6 · Gaslighting {#t-6}

*Making you doubt your own experience so you trust the institution's interpretation instead.*

**How it shows here**

- A person criticizes administration and is told they are harming unity.
- Doubt may be reframed as impatience, ego, misunderstanding, or failure to grasp the Covenant.
- Someone questions sexual rules, political restrictions, or institutional decisions, and the response becomes, “You are focusing on imperfections instead of the larger unity of humanity.”
- Public dissent may be treated as spiritual immaturity.
- The believer’s moral alarm is softened into “disunity.”

**The strongest defense.** Baháʼís value consultation, unity, and independent investigation of truth.

**The counter.** Consultation is not real if the conclusion must preserve institutional harmony. If independent investigation is praised only until it produces public disagreement, then unity has become a method for controlling perception.

**Evidence grade.** [[Codified]] Pre-publication review requires institutional approval of members' writing about the Faith. *(sourced)*
:::

::: tactic n=7
#### 7 · Double Bind {#t-7}

*A trap where both options lead to compliance. No exit serves you.*

**How it shows here**

- Bahá'ís are taught independent investigation of truth as a founding principle — investigation that must arrive at the Covenant. Investigate and dissent, and you risk the machinery reserved for 'covenant-breakers,' including shunning.
- The Faith teaches the harmony of science and religion, but scholars publishing inconvenient history have faced review, withdrawal demands, or enrollment consequences.
- Consultation is prized; but once institutions decide, unified support is expected — the consulting voice must now defend the outcome it argued against.

**The strongest defense.** Unity is the Faith's central healing principle for a fractured world; covenant protections prevent schism.

**The counter.** A principle of independent investigation with a single permitted destination is an itinerary, not an investigation. Unity purchased by shunning the disunified is agreement's costume worn over enforcement.

**Evidence grade.** [[Taught]] Insufficiency is framed as disunity rather than depravity, in institutional guidance.
:::

### Stage 4 · Confuse {#stage-4}

::: stage
**The rewards become unpredictable, the standard keeps moving, the answers stop meaning anything, and the accusation gets turned around.**

Persistent dissent is reframed as a threat to the Covenant. The subject becomes your loyalty rather than your point. Scholars publishing critical history described review pressure and disenrollment.

*What it asks of you:* Watch for the moment the topic changes from what you said to what your saying it means about you.
:::

::: tactic n=8
#### 8 · Intermittent Reinforcement {#t-8}

*Unpredictable reward that creates addictive emotional dependency.*

**How it shows here**

- Service to the Faith is rewarded irregularly with appointment, delegation, or mention — institutional regard arriving unpredictably enough that active believers keep intensifying service to re-trigger it.
- The believer's standing brightens with teaching activity and quiet compliance and dims — without official statement — around persistent public dissent, a temperature everyone reads and no document admits.
- Prayers and pioneering sacrifices are repaid by 'confirmations' — doors opening, hearts softening — counted when they occur and reinterpreted as tests when they don't.

**The strongest defense.** Bahá'í administration is elected, unpaid, and explicitly forbids campaigning or status-seeking.

**The counter.** Forbidding the pursuit of status does not abolish the economy of it; it drives the economy into weather — felt warmth, felt distance — where nothing can be appealed because nothing was ever said.

## 23. The questions {#questions}

1. Your Faith teaches independent investigation of truth. Explain pre-publication review of members' writing about the Faith.
2. If a designated covenant-breaker's family must shun them, ask which teaching about unity requires a mother to stop speaking to her son.
3. Women's equality is central scripture. Ask an institution to explain the one exclusion without invoking its own authority.
4. Ask for the accounting of Huqúqu'lláh. Note whether the question is answered or reframed as a spiritual matter.
5. A scholar publishes accurate history the institutions dislike. Describe the process by which he is heard rather than removed.
6. You were taught that unity is the highest value. Ask whether unity that requires shunning is unity or only silence.

### In closing

Your religion built real safeguards most traditions in this codex lack: no clergy, elected bodies, member-only funding, consultation as a method, and a documented history of getting race and gender right early and at cost. Those are not small. They are why this profile is short.

But a faith confident in its own truth does not need to review what its historians write, and unity that requires a family to stop speaking is not the unity Baháʼu'lláh described — it is the appearance of it. Your tradition teaches you to investigate independently. That instruction was not meant to stop at the institutions' door.

## 24. Leaving safely here {#leaving}

Practical guidance, not legal advice.

1. **You can resign in writing;** removal from the rolls is a separate process decided by the institutions [5][6][7].
2. **Resigning is not the same as being declared a "covenant-breaker".**
3. **In Iran,** membership itself carries legal risk; seek advice before travelling [8][9].
4. **Find support** [13][14][15][16].
