## 2. A day inside {#a-day-inside}

*Sepideh · A Tuesday in Ayyám-i-Há (the intercalary days before the Bahá'í fast) · A city in Iran*

Prayers at dawn, alone, quietly, in a flat where the window faces a courtyard. Her grandmother taught her the Arabic. Her grandmother was in Evin (the prison in Tehran) for four years.

She is twenty-two and cannot attend university. Not because of her marks — her marks were excellent — but because of the box on the form. She studies instead with the community's own institute, over patchy internet, with a lecturer in another city who has been arrested twice.

At ten her father's shop is closed for the fourth time this year by an inspector who does not write anything down. Her father makes tea for the inspector. The inspector drinks it. This is how it goes.

In the afternoon she teaches a children's class — six kids, a rug, songs about unity and the oneness of humanity, and she believes every word, and she has no idea how she would explain to a foreigner that this is the most dangerous thing she does all week.

At six she reads an essay a Bahá'í academic abroad has written about the Faith's early history. It is careful and it is accurate and it has not been through review, and there was a note attached about that, and Sepideh does not know what she thinks. She thinks the essay is good. She thinks the review policy exists. She holds both.

At night she and her mother count the cemetery. Agents took over the cemetery in 2021 and graves were razed in 2024, and it is not clear whether they will be allowed to bury her grandmother where her grandfather is. Sepideh prays for the officials by name, because the Writings (the Bahá'í scripture) say to, and she does it without irony, and then she lies down and thinks: everything trying to erase us is outside this room. That is true and it is nearly the whole story. Nearly.

## 3. The forefront {#forefront}

It is about the two or three mechanisms that activate the moment someone organizes a disagreement.
:::

### The strongest objection, answered

**The objection.** Bahá'ís have been executed in Iran and are imprisoned there. Criticism of this Faith punches down.

**What is true in it.** Entirely true, and this profile leads with it. This community is far more often the target of coercion than its author, and its structural safeguards — no clergy, elected bodies, member-only funding — are genuine and unusual.

**The answer.** That is why the section is short and limited to three mechanisms, all of which the Faith publishes openly. A religion teaching independent investigation of truth cannot also hold approval rights over its members' published conclusions. That is an internal inconsistency, not an attack.

## 4. What healthy looks like here {#healthy}

::: lede
The Bahá'í International Community states up to 8 million members worldwide [23]. It is a 19th-century religion that teaches the oneness of God, religion and humanity, with a global elected administrative order and no clergy.
:::

- The absence of clergy, the elected administration, member-only funding and the ideal of consultation (group decision-making by open discussion) are real structural safeguards, and most members experience a service-oriented, non-authoritarian community life.
- The mechanisms this page examines, covenant-breaker designation and pre-publication review, apply mainly to organized dissent and to published writing, and not to ordinary community life (section 7).
- Baháʼís in Iran face persecution by the state, which this page records first and treats as the largest harm it describes (sections 5 and 17) [8].

## 5. History {#history}

Baháʼu'lláh founded the Faith in 19th-century Iran and Ottoman exile, out of the Bábí movement. Leadership passed through appointed interpreters to the elected Universal House of Justice (1963), and the community has been severely persecuted in Iran. [GOVERNMENT REPORT: UN Special Rapporteur on Iran, 2024]

### Timeline


### Moments in the room

::: card
#### 1963 — The first election of the House of Justice

Members of the national assemblies convened and elected the nine-member Universal House of Justice — without nominations, campaigning, or candidate information, by design, to prevent factionalism.

**Why it matters.** The apex is genuinely electoral, and the design that prevents factionalism is the same design that prevents an informed vote. Both descend from one intentional choice.
:::

::: card
#### 1979–1998 — The Iranian executions

Following the revolution, more than 200 Bahá'ís in Iran were executed or killed, and the last reported execution was in 1998 [19]. Persecution has continued as arrest, imprisonment, property seizure, exclusion from work and higher education, and the razing of graves; as of 11 June 2026, 63 Bahá'ís were detained for their beliefs [20]. It is thoroughly documented by UN bodies.

**Why it matters.** Recorded first and plainly, because this page's later questions about internal governance must not be readable as ammunition for a state that executed more than 200 of them and still imprisons them. Real persecution and internal critique are both true, and the codex refuses to trade one for the other.
:::

::: card
#### 1990s–2000s — The review policy meets the internet

As Bahá'í academics published historical work outside the review process, several were subject to administrative sanction and the boundary of permissible scholarship became a live public dispute.

**Why it matters.** The mechanism the page's unanswered question turns on: an electorate that cannot read unreviewed material about its own candidates is not being asked to choose.
:::

## 6. Branches & variants {#branches}

The Faith is overwhelmingly a single unified body, and the Covenant structure has been unusually effective at preventing schism. Small dissident groups (for example, the Orthodox Bahá'ís) exist and are designated covenant-breakers.

| Body | Where | What differs on this page's questions |
|---|---|---|
| **The Baháʼí Faith** (Universal House of Justice) | The body operates worldwide, and its seat is in Haifa. | It has nine members, elected every five years by national assembly members, with no nominations or campaigning; women are not eligible [1][2][3] |
| **National and local spiritual assemblies** | Assemblies exist in every country with Baháʼís. | Local and national assemblies are elected annually, and national assemblies are often registered charities [11] |
| **Small dissident groups** (e.g. Orthodox Baháʼís) | The groups' locations are not recorded on this page. | The main body designates them "covenant-breakers". |

## 10. Genealogy {#genealogy}

::: card
#### Covenant-breaker designation and required avoidance

**Origin.** The designation was established in the Faith's early succession disputes to prevent the schisms that fractured earlier religions. [OFFICIAL POLICY]

**What it was for.** The designation answered a genuine and understandable problem: the founder watched rival claimants tear at the community and wrote in a mechanism to protect unity.

**Why that reason expired.** It works — the Faith is unusually unified. But the price is published guidance that members avoid a designated person, relatives included [17]. Unity purchased by required avoidance is not unity; it is the appearance of unity with the disagreement removed from view.

**Who benefits now.** The administrative order benefits, through its continuity and narrative control. The cost is paid by designated individuals and by their relatives, who must choose.
:::

::: card
#### Pre-publication review

**Origin.** The review was instituted as the Faith spread, to prevent inaccurate representations while it was small and widely misunderstood. [OFFICIAL POLICY]

**What it was for.** A young religion facing hostile misrepresentation wanted its own accounts to be accurate. That was defensible at the time.

**Why that reason expired.** It is now an explicit, written requirement that members submit manuscripts about their own religion for institutional review before publishing — one of very few such systems in any modern religion. A faith that teaches independent investigation of truth cannot also vet the conclusions.

**Who benefits now.** The institutions benefit, through control of the historical narrative. Scholars have reported pressure and disenrollment episodes over critical historical work. [ACADEMIC SOURCE / FORMER MEMBER TESTIMONY]
:::

::: card
#### Exclusion of women from the Universal House of Justice

**Origin.** The rule is stated in the authoritative texts, which give the reason as "a wisdom of the Lord God's, which will erelong be made manifest" [18], in a religion whose central social teaching is the equality of men and women. [OFFICIAL POLICY]

**What it was for.** The texts say the reason is a wisdom to be made manifest later, and no published text says what it is. That is itself the notable fact.

**Why that reason expired.** The Faith taught women's equality in 19th-century Iran, at real cost, and was right decades ahead of its neighbors. The single exclusion sits inside that record with its stated reason deferred to the future, which means it is defended by authority alone.

**Who benefits now.** Nothing identifiable benefits, materially. It is the cleanest example in the codex of a rule sustained purely because questioning it is out of bounds.
:::

::: card
#### Prohibition on partisan political involvement

**Origin.** The prohibition was instituted to keep a global, multi-ethnic community from fracturing along national and factional lines. [OFFICIAL POLICY]

**What it was for.** The prohibition was coherent and, in many contexts, protective, particularly for members under hostile governments.

**Why that reason expired.** Partially: it also removes an entire community from ordinary civic contestation, including on matters affecting them, and it makes institutional direction the only permitted channel for collective action.

**Who benefits now.** Institutional unity benefits and, in hostile states, arguably members' safety does too. This one is genuinely double-edged and the codex says so.
:::

## 11. Reach {#reach}

### Information

- Pre-publication review requires members to submit manuscripts about the Faith to institutional review before publishing — an explicit, written information control unusual among modern religions. [OFFICIAL POLICY]
- Covenant-breaker designation obliges members to avoid personal relations with the designated person, relatives included, under published guidance [17] — the sharpest sanction in the system. [OFFICIAL POLICY]
- Partisan political involvement is prohibited, which protects unity and also removes members from ordinary civic contestation. [OFFICIAL POLICY]

### Children

- Children's classes and junior youth spiritual empowerment programs (programs for young adolescents) are central to the Institute process; participation is voluntary and typically warm.
- Declaration of faith at 15 is treated as the age of maturity — notably, an explicit age of consent for religious commitment, better than most traditions here. [OFFICIAL POLICY]
- In Iran, Bahá'í children have faced school harassment and their families' businesses have been closed. This is harm imposed from outside, not within. [GOVERNMENT REPORT]

### Bodies

- Equality of men and women is explicit scripture and is practiced substantially in community life — one of the strongest positions in this codex.
- The exception: women are ineligible for the Universal House of Justice, the highest institution. The stated reason is deferred to the future [18], and it is the clearest internal inequality. [OFFICIAL POLICY]
- Chastity outside heterosexual marriage is required; same-sex marriage is not recognized, and LGBTQ members must remain celibate to stay in good standing. [OFFICIAL POLICY]
- Divorce is permitted after a year of patience, which is comparatively unrestrictive.

## 12. The 30 techniques {#techniques}

Thirty named techniques from domestic-abuse and social-psychology research, applied to institutions, in the eight stages of the cycle. Each carries an evidence grade for this tradition.

### Stage 1 · Idealize {#stage-1}

::: stage
**You arrive with a need and are met with more warmth than you have had in years.**

The offer is built on unity, interfaith warmth, racial harmony, global peace, no clergy and no hellfire. It is the gentlest entry in this codex and the offer is largely what it appears to be.

*What it asks of you:* Notice how little pressure you felt. Then ask what happens if you organize a disagreement.
:::

::: tactic n=1
#### 1 · Love Bombing {#t-1}

*Overwhelming affection on arrival to create emotional dependency before terms are revealed.*

**How it shows here**

- Seekers are welcomed with unity language, interfaith warmth, firesides (informal gatherings in homes where the Faith is introduced), consultation, global peace ideals, racial harmony, and the promise of a religion beyond sectarian conflict.
- The first impression is often unusually gentle: no clergy, no hellfire preaching, no crude tribalism, no obvious coercive ritual.
- The deeper obligations appear later: administrative order, obedience to institutions, restrictions on partisan politics, sexual norms, covenant loyalty, and strong discouragement of public dissent.

**The strongest defense.** The Baháʼí Faith emphasizes unity, peace, and independent investigation of truth.

**The counter.** “Independent investigation” is compromised when the acceptable conclusion is already mapped. If questioning is praised on the way in but treated as disunity once inside, then unity is not peace. It is managed agreement.

**Evidence grade.** [[Cultural]] Firesides and study circles are warm and locally run; the entry experience is among the gentlest here.
:::

::: tactic n=2
#### 2 · Weaponized Generosity {#t-2}

*Giving help that installs unspoken obligation.*

**How it shows here**

- Baháʼí communities may offer warmth through firesides, study circles, children’s classes, junior youth programs, consultation, service projects, and unity-centered friendship.
- Seekers may be welcomed into a global vision: peace, racial unity, interfaith harmony, equality, and independent investigation of truth.
- The obligation can appear later through institutional obedience, participation in community plans, avoidance of partisan politics, sexual norms, administrative loyalty, and discouragement of public dissent.
- Service becomes a spiritual currency: the person who received community is expected to build community, teach the Faith, host gatherings, and align with institutional goals.

**The strongest defense.** Baháʼís do not have clergy and emphasize independent investigation, consultation, and service.

**The counter.** A religion does not need clergy to create obligation. Administration can function as authority just as effectively as priesthood. If the community gives warmth to seekers but later treats dissent as disunity, then unity has become the invoice for belonging.

**Evidence grade.** [[Cultural]] The welcome is real and locally given; whether help later becomes an obligation is shown in community practice, and no written rule governs it.
:::

### Stage 2 · Hook {#stage-2}

::: stage
**You are given a future that cannot be verified, and a rope for whenever you drift toward the door.**

The offer is world unity, the peace of humanity and the Faith's global plan advancing through your service, a future genuinely aimed at the good and permanently ahead of you. Retrieval for the ordinary lapsed is warm and easy; for the designated, it requires recantation.

*What it asks of you:* The promise here is unusually decent. The question is still who decides when you have contributed enough.
:::

::: tactic n=3
#### 3 · Future Faking {#t-3}

*Promising a future that keeps you invested but never has to arrive.*

**How it shows here**

- The Baháʼí Faith offers a sweeping future: world unity, peace, racial harmony, religious reconciliation, global governance, moral maturity of humanity, and the eventual unification of the human family.
- The believer is invited to build the future now through service, study circles, children’s classes, junior youth programs, teaching, consultation, and obedience to administrative institutions.
- The promise is historically enormous but personally demanding: your small acts are part of the spiritual evolution of civilization.
- Dissent can be reframed as delaying unity. Public criticism may be treated as disunity, ego, covenant-breaking, or failure to understand the long arc of divine order.
- The future is always coming through the administrative plan. If it has not arrived, humanity is immature, the believers must serve more, or the process needs more time.

**The strongest defense.** Baháʼís work sincerely for peace, unity, racial justice, and global cooperation.

**The counter.** Noble goals can still function as future faking. If the promise of world unity is used to suppress present dissent, protect administration from criticism, or demand endless unpaid service, then peace has become a horizon that moves whenever accountability gets close.

**Evidence grade.** [[Taught]] World unity and the Faith's global plan are taught as the frame for service; retrieval for the ordinary lapsed is informal.
:::

::: tactic n=4
#### 4 · Hoovering {#t-4}

*Pulling someone back after they've started to leave, through guilt, love, or fear.*

**How it shows here**

- Someone distancing from the Baháʼí community may be gently contacted through invitations to firesides, study circles, devotionals (prayer gatherings), children’s classes, consultation, or service projects.
- The pull is usually soft: “We miss your spirit,” “The community needs your gifts,” “Unity requires everyone.”
- Doubt may be reframed as disunity, impatience, ego, misunderstanding of the Covenant, or failure to appreciate the long process of building world peace.
- Former members may be urged not to criticize publicly because public criticism is seen as harmful to unity.
- The person may be pulled back through idealism: racial unity, global peace, interfaith harmony, and the idea that leaving means stepping away from humanity’s future.

**The strongest defense.** Baháʼís emphasize unity and service, so reconnecting people is loving and constructive.

**The counter.** Unity becomes hoovering when it cannot tolerate honest departure. If the person is welcomed back only through silence, administrative loyalty, and renewed service, then the invitation is not reconciliation. It is reintegration.

**Evidence grade.** [[Taught]] The gentle invitations to return rest on the Faith's teaching that unity and service frame community life; nothing written prescribes contacting those who drift away.
:::

### Stage 3 · Devalue {#stage-3}

::: stage
**You are taught that you are broken, that your perception is unreliable, and that both exits from the trap lead back inside.**

Insufficiency is framed as disunity rather than deficiency. You are not insufficiently pious; you are insufficiently unified.

*What it asks of you:* 'Disunity' is a category that makes disagreement into a moral failure without ever calling you a sinner.
:::

::: tactic n=5
#### 5 · Devaluation {#t-5}

*Reducing your sense of worth so you become dependent on the institution for identity.*

**How it shows here**

- The Baháʼí Faith can devalue dissent by framing criticism as disunity, ego, impatience, covenant weakness, or failure to understand the long arc of global transformation.
- The individual is invited to serve humanity, but that ideal can make personal grievances seem petty.
- If someone questions administration, sexual rules, political restrictions, or institutional decisions, they may be treated as spiritually immature or harmful to unity.
- The self is diminished beneath the future of world peace.
- The believer may internalize the idea that their pain matters less than the community’s image of harmony.

**The strongest defense.** Baháʼís value independent investigation of truth and consultation.

#### 7 · Double Bind {#t-7}

- Consultation is prized; but once institutions decide, unified support is expected — the consulting voice must now defend the outcome it argued against.

**The strongest defense.** Unity is the Faith's central healing principle for a fractured world; covenant protections prevent schism.

**The counter.** A principle of independent investigation with a single permitted destination is an itinerary, not an investigation. Unity purchased by shunning the disunified is agreement's costume worn over enforcement.

**Evidence grade.** [[Taught]] The bind rests on the teaching that independent investigation leads to the Covenant and that unified support follows a decision; the entry cites no document that states the bind itself.

## 13. The loops {#loops}

::: lede
The seven loops below show how the practices connect, so that each step makes the next one easier and the last step feeds the first. In this tradition several of them run weakly and one barely runs, which each card says. The loops are analysis built from findings recorded elsewhere on this page [PATTERN OBSERVED]; each step names the section where its fact is recorded.
:::

::: card
#### 1 · Money to Doctrine to Money

Huqúqu'lláh and Fund contributions are member-only, which is a real safeguard, but no public source shows whether the accounting is member-facing.

**How it runs.**

1. The Faith asks members for Huqúqu'lláh, 19% of surplus wealth, the Right of God, paid to the Faith's head (section 9).
2. Funds accept contributions from members only, so no outside donor shapes the institutions (sections 9 and 16).
3. The Universal House of Justice holds final authority over doctrine, administration and the Faith's global plans, and Huqúqu'lláh is paid into its funds (sections 7 and 9).
4. No source on this page records how Huqúqu'lláh is accounted for to members, and giving amounts are confidential, so a member-facing accounting is not established (sections 9 and 16).
5. The money supports the institutions and their expansion plans, which members are then asked to serve through unpaid labor and pioneering (section 9), and the giving continues.

**Techniques that feed it.** [26 · Financial Control](#t-26), [3 · Future Faking](#t-3), [22 · Benevolent Control](#t-22).

**Why it closes.** The body that sets the obligation also receives the payment and holds final authority over doctrine. The refusal of outside money protects the Faith from donors and leaves open whether members can see how their own payments are used.

**Where it could be broken, and by whom.** It weakens wherever the money becomes visible. National assemblies registered as charities file public accounts, as the UK assembly does (section 8); whether Huqúqu'lláh is accounted for to members is in the institutions' hands. A member can ask for the accounting (section 23, question 4). This paragraph is analysis, not a documented finding.

**An example from this page.** The UK national assembly's accounts are public. In the period to March 2025 it received £10.09m of its £10.73m income as donations and spent £10.81m (section 9) [11]. No equivalent account of Huqúqu'lláh is recorded.
:::

::: card
#### 2 · Fear to Dependence to Fear

This loop barely runs here, because fear is not the mechanism.

**How it runs.**

1. The offer to the seeker has no hellfire (section 12, stage 1; technique 1).
2. Insufficiency is framed as disunity rather than as sin or depravity, so there is no equivalent of a debt that must be absolved (section 12, stage 3; technique 5).
3. The one sanction that can produce fear is covenant-breaker designation, which is reserved for organized dissent and not for ordinary members (sections 7 and 15).
4. Ordinary resignation is administratively simple and members generally retain relationships (sections 3 and 15).
5. The distress that remains is the shame of seeming selfish or disunified, which service restores (section 12, technique 20).

**Techniques that feed it.** [5 · Devaluation](#t-5), [20 · Trauma Bonding](#t-20), [21 · Learned Helplessness](#t-21).

**Why it closes.** It does not close for most members, because no authority sells relief from a fear it creates. It closes only for a member who has begun to organize a disagreement, where unity language produces shame and service restores worth.

**Where it could be broken, and by whom.** For the member who feels it, the loop breaks where objections are heard without being translated into unity language, which lies with the assemblies (section 18 names this as a point where an assembly member could refuse to treat a persistent question as a loyalty matter). This paragraph is analysis, not a documented finding.

**An example from this page.** Technique 20 describes a member who feels distress when dissent seems to threaten unity, then relief when they consult, soften, serve and return to institutional harmony.

#### 3 · Children to Members to Children

Children's classes and junior youth programs are warm and voluntary, and declaration at fifteen is an explicit age of consent.

**How it runs.**

1. Children's classes and junior youth programs are central to the Institute process, and participation is voluntary and typically warm (section 11).
2. The classes teach unity and the oneness of humanity, and young people come to believe it (section 2).
3. Declaration of faith at 15 is treated as the age of maturity, an explicit age of consent for religious commitment (section 11).
4. Members who have been formed this way are asked to run the classes and programs in turn, as Institute and children's-class tutors (sections 12 and 18).
5. Service roles in children's classes and junior youth work can come to replace personal boundaries, and the next group of children is taught by them (technique 24).

**Techniques that feed it.** [13 · Normalization / Desensitization](#t-13), [15 · Triangulation](#t-15), [24 · Identity Erosion](#t-24), [29 · Replacement](#t-29).

**Why it closes.** The formation work is staffed by the people it formed. Each group of children becomes the tutors of the next, and the plan for the programs sits with the institutions.

**Where it could be broken, and by whom.** It weakens where service remains voluntary and a member can decline a request without losing standing. The age-of-consent rule in section 11 is the loop's own safeguard, and it is kept by the assemblies and by parents. Section 18 names refusal as an option open to tutors. This paragraph is analysis, not a documented finding.

**An example from this page.** Sepideh teaches a children's class of six, with a rug and songs about unity and the oneness of humanity, and believes every word (section 2).

#### 4 · Aid to Legitimacy to Leverage to Aid

Global service and interfaith work generate standing, but there is little corresponding legal privilege to convert it into.

**How it runs.**

1. Communities offer firesides, study circles, children's classes, service projects and interfaith warmth (section 12, techniques 1 and 2).
2. That work earns the Faith standing for unity, peace and racial harmony (section 12, stage 1).
3. The standing has little legal privilege to turn into. In Iran the Faith has no recognition (section 8), and in the UK the national assembly is a charity under charity law (section 8).
4. The standing turns instead into growth through structured study circles (section 5, timeline).
5. Growth supplies more members to serve and more service to offer, and the cycle repeats (section 12, technique 29).

**Techniques that feed it.** [1 · Love Bombing](#t-1), [2 · Weaponized Generosity](#t-2), [22 · Benevolent Control](#t-22).

**Why it closes.** The loop closes through membership and not through law. Standing produces service, and service produces the standing that draws in the next seeker, with obligations arriving after the welcome (section 12, technique 2).

**Where it could be broken, and by whom.** It is weak at the conversion step, and charity regulators already supervise national assemblies where they are registered entities (section 8). It would weaken further if seekers were told the later obligations at the point of welcome, which lies with the assemblies.  This paragraph is analysis, not a documented finding.

**An example from this page.** The UK national assembly files public accounts under charity law, the one place on this page where the Faith's standing meets a legal duty (sections 8 and 9) [11].

#### 5 · Unpaid Labor to Assets to Power to More Labor

Members give substantial unpaid service and pioneering relocation, and the institutional plans direct both.

**How it runs.**

1. Committed members are expected to give substantial unpaid labor and to relocate (section 9).
2. No clergy salary exists, and assembly members serve unpaid and without campaigning (sections 9 and 18).
3. Service becomes a spiritual currency: the person who received community is expected to build community, teach the Faith and host gatherings (section 12, technique 2).
4. Service roles can replace personal boundaries, and nonparticipation can feel like a failure to support humanity's future (section 12, techniques 24 and 30).
5. A member who stops serving is replaced by someone eager to take the role, and the institutional expansion plans continue (section 9; section 12, technique 29).

**Techniques that feed it.** [26 · Financial Control](#t-26), [2 · Weaponized Generosity](#t-2), [24 · Identity Erosion](#t-24), [29 · Replacement](#t-29).

**Why it closes.** The labor produces the growth that the plans measure, and the plans set the next demand for labor. Because departing members are replaced, the demand continues without anyone deciding to continue it.

**Where it could be broken, and by whom.** It weakens where a member's limits are accepted, which lies with the members and the assemblies that direct them. Section 18 records that assembly members and tutors could refuse requests that become loyalty tests. This paragraph is analysis, not a documented finding.

**An example from this page.** Assembly members serve without campaigning, unpaid, in genuine good faith, and see that there is no appeal above the system (section 18).

#### 6 · Scandal to Removal to Reform Theatre to Continuity

Dissent is handled by designation and removal from the rolls, not by public scandal, so there is nothing public to remove.

**How it runs.**

1. A scholar or member publishes or discusses material that the institutions object to (section 19).
2. The member is removed from the rolls (1997, 2000 and 2005) or resigns under warning (1996), with no appeal outside the institutions (section 19).
3. Institutional decisions on individuals are private, while doctrine and policy are public (section 15).
4. The individual departs and the review policy remains (section 14).
5. The next dissent is resolved administratively and quietly, and pre-publication review is not revisited (section 14, prediction).

**Techniques that feed it.** [17 · Smear Campaign](#t-17), [18 · Silent Treatment / Stonewalling](#t-18), [28 · Discard](#t-28), [30 · Plausible Deniability](#t-30).

**Why it closes.** Each case ends with the person and not with the policy. Because decisions on individuals are private and no body outside the institutions can review them, the structure that produced the case survives it unchanged.

**Where it could be broken, and by whom.** Section 20 names the change: publication of the reasoning behind the ineligibility ruling and open pre-election information about candidates. Both lie with the institutions. Charity regulators reach only the national assemblies' filings (section 8). This paragraph is analysis, not a documented finding.

**An example from this page.** Michael McKenny (1997), Alison Marshall (2000) and Sen McGlinn (2005) were removed from membership, and Juan Cole resigned in 1996 after being warned (section 19) [4][5][6][7].

#### 7 · Persecution to Solidarity to Insulation to Unaccountability

Iranian persecution is real and severe, and it makes any internal criticism feel like punching down.

**How it runs.**

1. Baháʼís in Iran face imprisonment, university exclusion and cemetery desecration, and the persecution is documented by UN bodies (sections 5 and 8).
2. This page records that persecution first, because its later questions about internal governance must not be read as ammunition for a state that harms the community (section 5).
3. Within the community, public criticism is treated as harming unity and the Faith (section 12, techniques 12 and 17).
4. Members close ranks around the institutions, and approached critics are asked to consult and to protect unity (section 12, technique 16).
5. Questions about review, shunning and the ineligibility ruling go unanswered, and the next question meets the same response.

**Techniques that feed it.** [12 · DARVO](#t-12), [17 · Smear Campaign](#t-17), [16 · Flying Monkeys](#t-16), [27 · Manufactured Crisis](#t-27).

**Why it closes.** The larger harm is external and real, so each internal question can be answered by pointing to it. The more severe the persecution, the stronger the pull to treat criticism as a betrayal.

**Where it could be broken, and by whom.** It weakens where both facts are held at once, as sections 3 and 17 do, and where the institutions answer the internal questions on their merits. The institutions can do that, and so can members who ask. Section 20 lists what would change the page. This paragraph is analysis, not a documented finding.

## 16. The ledger {#ledger}

### Who benefits

- The administrative order benefits through its continuity and narrative control, and unity is an institutional asset.
- Externally, the Iranian state benefits from persecuting Baháʼís as an internal enemy — a reminder that this community is far more often victim than perpetrator of coercion. [GOVERNMENT REPORT]

### Money out, leverage back

- The clearest fact here runs the other way: the Iranian state persecutes Bahá'ís systematically — executions after 1979, and now imprisonment, exclusion from universities, cemetery desecration — using religion as an instrument against a defenseless minority. This community is far more often coercion's target than its author. [GOVERNMENT REPORT]
- Funds accept contributions from members only, which genuinely prevents donor capture — an anti-leverage design worth naming as a model. [OFFICIAL POLICY]
- Huqúqu'lláh (19% of surplus wealth) flows to the Faith's central institutions; whether it is accounted for to members is not established from any public source. [OFFICIAL POLICY]

### Who pays

- Iranian Bahá'ís pay: more than 200 were executed after 1979, and they are now imprisoned and barred from higher education by the state. This community is far more often the victim of coercion than its author. [GOVERNMENT REPORT]
- Designated covenant-breakers and their families pay, because published guidance keeps other members at a distance from them.
- LGBTQ members pay, because they are required to be celibate for life.
- Women pay, because they are ineligible for the highest institution.
- Bahá'í scholars pay when their critical historical work meets review pressure.

## 23. The questions {#questions}

1. Your Faith teaches independent investigation of truth. Explain pre-publication review of members' writing about the Faith.
2. If a designated covenant-breaker's relatives are expected to avoid them, ask which teaching about unity asks a mother to hold apart from her son.
3. Women's equality is central scripture. Ask an institution to explain the one exclusion without invoking its own authority.
4. Ask for the accounting of Huqúqu'lláh. Note whether the question is answered or reframed as a spiritual matter.
5. A scholar publishes accurate history the institutions dislike. Describe the process by which he is heard rather than removed.
6. You were taught that unity is the highest value. Ask whether unity that asks members to avoid one another is unity or only silence.

### In closing

Your religion built real safeguards most traditions in this codex lack: no clergy, elected bodies, member-only funding, consultation as a method, and a documented history of getting race and gender right early and at cost. Those are not small. They are why this profile is short.

But a faith confident in its own truth does not need to review what its historians write, and unity that asks relatives to avoid one another is not the unity Baháʼu'lláh described — it is the appearance of it. Your tradition teaches you to investigate independently. That instruction was not meant to stop at the institutions' door.
