## 2. A day inside {#a-day-inside}

- **name:** Keiko
- **when:** A Tuesday before an upper-house election
- **where:** Saitama

1. She does gongyo (the daily recitation of passages from the Lotus Sutra, together with the chanting of Nam-myoho-renge-kyo, a phrase meaning devotion to the Mystic Law of the Lotus Sutra) at six. It has steadied her through two miscarriages and a layoff. It works. She is sure of that.
2. At ten the district leader calls with the list: twelve friends to contact for the candidate.
3. She buys a second copy of the newspaper for her sister, who does not read it.
4. At lunch a colleague asks, lightly, whether her religion tells her how to vote. She says no, and it is technically true.
5. At the discussion meeting in the evening, a young mother cries about her child's illness and is told to chant with more determination.
6. Walking home Keiko rehearses the calls she will make tomorrow, and notices she has never once asked who decided that this was the candidate.

## 3. The forefront {#forefront}

:::

## 4. What healthy looks like here {#healthy}

::: lede
Soka Gakkai reports 8.27 million member households in Japan and around 3 million members elsewhere, in 192 countries and territories (2021 announcement) [1]. It is a lay Buddhist organization practising the chanting of Nam-myoho-renge-kyo in the tradition of the 13th-century teacher Nichiren. It is known for peace, culture and education work (Soka University, UN-affiliated NGO activity) and a strong local discussion-meeting culture [1].
:::

- The organization carries out peace, anti-nuclear and education activism.
- The founder refused to bow to State Shinto (the state-sponsored form of Shinto promoted in wartime Japan), at the cost of his life [7].
- It has been a lay-led religion without priestly fees since the 1991 break [9].
- The organization made a public apology in 1970 and adopted a formal policy separating religion and party after the scandal [3].
- Where it operates as a registered charity, as SGI-UK does, its accounts are public [2].

## 5. History {#history}

The movement's key stages:
- **1930:** The educators Tsunesaburo Makiguchi and Josei Toda founded the movement as the Soka Kyoiku Gakkai, an education reform society [7]. It later aligned with Nichiren Shoshu (the Japanese Nichiren school whose priesthood the lay group followed) [9].
- **1943–44:** Makiguchi was arrested in 1943 for refusing to accept a State Shinto talisman and died in prison in November 1944 [7].
- **1950s:** Under Toda, the movement grew explosively through aggressive conversion (shakubuku).
- **1960–2023:** Daisaku Ikeda led the movement from 1960, founded Soka Gakkai International (SGI, the network of Soka Gakkai organizations outside Japan) in 1975, was honorary president from 1979, and died on 15 November 2023 [6].
- **1991:** The Nichiren Shoshu priesthood excommunicated the organization on 28 November 1991 [9].
- **Since 2006:** Minoru Harada is president [1].

## 9. Money {#money}

### Where it comes from

- The organization solicits financial contributions (zaimu, 財務) from members. Former members and commentators describe the major annual drives as larger in the past [8]. Current practice is not established from a primary source.
- The daily *Seikyo Shimbun* claimed a circulation of about 5.5 million in 1997 [8]. That figure is unverifiable: the paper is outside Japan's official circulation-audit system [8]. Members' work selling subscriptions is documented in scholarship [5].
- Income also comes from publishing, cultural centres and Soka University [1].

## 10. Genealogy {#genealogy}

1. **A religion with a party**
   - *Origin:* The rule began with Toda's and Ikeda's vision of kosen-rufu, which included politics, and Komeito was founded in 1964 [3].
   - *Then:* It gave protection from a hostile state, in memory of the founder's death in prison [7].
   - *Expired?* A party in a governing coalition for most of 26 years [4][19] is not a persecuted minority's shield.
   - *Benefits now:* The party gains legislative power and the organization gains standing.
2. **The mentor–disciple relationship**
   - *Origin:* It began in Nichiren's teaching, reframed around Ikeda [6].
   - *Then:* It gave inspiration and unity.
   - *Expired?* Since 2023 [6], the question is who inherits the authority.
   - *Benefits now:* The organization's leadership benefits.

## 13. The loops {#loops}

::: lede
The seven loops below show how the practices connect, so that each step makes the next easier and the last step feeds the first. The loops are analysis built from findings recorded elsewhere on this page [PATTERN OBSERVED]; each step names the section where its fact is recorded. The page rates the second loop, Fear, as weak.
:::

::: card
#### 1 · Money

Contributions and newspaper sales fund the organization that teaches their value [5][8].

**How it runs.**

1. The organization presents contributions (zaimu) as support for kosen-rufu (section 9).
2. Members give contributions and sell subscriptions to the *Seikyo Shimbun*, work that scholarship documents (section 9).
3. The money funds the organization, its publishing arm, its cultural centres and Soka University (sections 7 and 9).
4. The newspaper is members' main source of news (sections 7 and 11).
5. Members read it, are recognized for subscription results, and the giving continues (section 9; technique 8).

**Techniques that feed it.** [3 · Future Faking](#techniques), [8 · Intermittent Reinforcement](#techniques), [26 · Financial Control](#techniques).

**Why it closes.** The organization that asks for the money also publishes the paper members read and sell. Neither side is audited in public: the Japanese organization's accounts are not public (section 1 scorecard) and the paper's circulation is outside the official audit system (section 9).

**Where it could be broken, and by whom.** It weakens wherever the money becomes visible. Section 20 names the changes that would break it: published accounts and an audited circulation. SGI-UK already files public accounts (section 8). A member can ask for them before giving. This paragraph is analysis, not a documented finding.

**An example from this page.** In section 2, Keiko buys a second copy of the newspaper for a sister who does not read it.
:::

::: card
#### 2 · Fear

The page rates this loop weak: hardship is answered with more practice, and no formal penalty for leaving was found.

**How it runs.**

1. Hardship or doubt arises (section 2; technique 6).
2. It is read as insufficient practice or weak faith (techniques 5 and 6).
3. The answer offered is "chant more" (technique 25; section 14).
4. The remedy is more of the same practice, directed by the organization.

**Techniques that feed it.** [5 · Devaluation](#techniques), [6 · Gaslighting](#techniques), [25 · Spiritual Bypassing](#techniques).

**Why it closes.** Only partly. The practice supplies both the diagnosis and the remedy, but the page found no formal shunning (section 15) and learned helplessness is ungraded (section 12).

**Where it could be broken, and by whom.** It weakens where hardship is met with help other than more practice, and where doubt is allowed to stand, as technique 6 puts it. This paragraph is analysis, not a documented finding.

**An example from this page.** In section 2, a young mother cries about her child's illness at the discussion meeting and is told to chant with more determination.
:::

::: card
#### 3 · Children

Multi-generational membership [5].

**How it runs.**

1. Membership runs through families, and youth-division activities draw in children (section 11).
2. Social life is centered on the district (technique 14; section 15).
3. Family, district and mentor align (technique 15), so leaving carries a family cost as well as a social one (section 15).
4. Children raised in the district take part in the same activities as adults, and the pattern repeats.

**Techniques that feed it.** [13 · Normalization](#techniques), [14 · Isolation](#techniques), [15 · Triangulation](#techniques).

**Why it closes.** The people who make up the family are the same people who make up the district, so leaving one means loosening the other.

**Where it could be broken, and by whom.** It weakens where membership is a choice made by an adult and where families accept a member who steps back. The page records no formal shunning (section 15), so the break lies with families and districts. No documented harms specific to children were found (section 11). This paragraph is analysis, not a documented finding.

**An example from this page.** The ledger of exit in section 15 records a family cost: multi-generational expectations.
:::

::: card
#### 4 · Aid

Peace work and UN status build legitimacy.

**How it runs.**

1. The organization carries out peace, anti-nuclear and education activism, including Soka University and UN-affiliated NGO activity (section 4).
2. That work is part of the organization's public record (section 4).
3. The standing it earns supports the answer to critics, who are cast as enemies of peace (technique 11).
4. Engagement with the public is presented as ordinary faith practice, "engaged Buddhism" (technique 13).

**Techniques that feed it.** [11 · Projection](#techniques), [13 · Normalization](#techniques).

**Why it closes.** The peace work is real, and the same record becomes the defense against criticism. Each criticism can then be read as an attack on the work.

**Where it could be broken, and by whom.** It weakens where critics are answered with records rather than with the peace record, and where standing is separated from party politics. Section 3 shows the answer the page itself gives: the findings rest on the organization's own apology and on university-press scholarship. This paragraph is analysis, not a documented finding.

**An example from this page.** Section 4 lists peace, anti-nuclear and education activism among what works here.
:::

::: card
#### 5 · Labor

Election labor built political power [5].

**How it runs.**

1. District leaders organize meetings and campaigns (section 18).
2. Members contact friends for votes, unpaid (section 9; technique 16).
3. Women's Division leaders are asked to deliver votes and subscriptions (section 18).
4. The votes gave Komeito legislative influence, and the organization standing (sections 10 and 16).
5. Recognition for results draws further labor (technique 8).

**Techniques that feed it.** [8 · Intermittent Reinforcement](#techniques), [16 · Flying Monkeys](#techniques), [19 · Manufactured Consent](#techniques), [27 · Manufactured Crisis](#techniques).

**Why it closes.** The labor is unpaid and organized by the religion's own structure (technique 30), while the product, votes, accrues to the party and the organization rather than to the members who give it.

**Where it could be broken, and by whom.** Section 18 names where it could be refused: district leaders could refuse to pressure members to vote, and Women's Division leaders could refuse to turn faith into a quota. Komeito's exit from the coalition in October 2025 (section 5) changed what the votes buy. This paragraph is analysis, not a documented finding.

**An example from this page.** In section 2, Keiko's district leader calls with a list of twelve friends to contact for the candidate.

#### 6 · Scandal

2. The matter became a public church–state question (section 5).
3. Ikeda made a formal apology on 3 May 1970, and a policy separating religion and party was adopted (section 5).

**Where it could be broken, and by whom.** Section 20 names the changes that would break it: published accounts, an audited circulation, and a public statement that members' votes are their own. #### 7 · Persecution

The founder's martyrdom [7] and the 1991 excommunication [9].

**How it runs.**

1. Makiguchi refused a State Shinto talisman and died in prison in November 1944 (sections 4 and 5).
2. Nichiren Shoshu excommunicated the organization on 28 November 1991 (section 5).
3. The movement describes itself as persecuted, and critics are cast as enemies of peace (technique 11; section 3).
4. Criticism is answered as "persecution by rivals" (technique 11).
5. Findings are received as attacks, and the next finding is met the same way.

**Techniques that feed it.** [11 · Projection](#techniques), [17 · Smear Campaign](#techniques).

**Why it closes.** The founding history is real, so the claim of persecution cannot be dismissed. It can also be applied to any criticism, which makes the next finding easier to set aside.

**Where it could be broken, and by whom.** It weakens when members read findings as the organization's own record, not an outsider's attack. Section 3 notes that the findings here rest on the organization's own 1970 apology and on university-press scholarship, and that critics include scholars (technique 11). This paragraph is analysis, not a documented finding.

## 16. The ledger {#ledger}

### Who benefits

- The organization benefits, and so does the party it founded, Komeito (founded 1964) [3].
  - Komeito was the Liberal Democratic Party's coalition partner from 1999 until it withdrew on 10 October 2025, apart from 2009–12 in opposition [4][19].
  - The two were formally "separated" in 1970 after the book-suppression scandal [3].
- The organization's publishing and institutional enterprises benefit too [5].

### Money out, leverage back

- Organized votes gave a religious corporation legislative influence for decades [3][4][5].

### Who pays

- Members pay, pressed into election work and subscription sales [5].
- How far contributions strain households is not established on this page; no primary or scholarly source for it was found.

## 23. The questions {#questions}

1. Does your district ask you to campaign? Who decided that was faith?
2. Can you see the organization's accounts?
3. How many copies of the newspaper does your household buy, and why? Why is its circulation unaudited [8]?
4. Who holds authority now that the mentor has died [6]?
5. In 1969–70 the organization pressured a publisher to stop a critic's book [3]. What written rule prevents that today?
6. The founder refused the state. Is a party in government what he refused, or what he wanted?

### In closing

1. Your founder's courage is the standard: refuse the state's claim on conscience [7]. That includes a religion's claim on a member's vote.
2. You can chant every morning and vote however you choose.

## 24. Leaving safely here {#leaving}

Practical guidance, not legal advice.

1. **Leaving is legally free,** and no formal shunning doctrine was found [5].
2. **Stop contributions and subscriptions** in writing if you are asked to renew.
3. **Expect social costs** in close local districts and multi-generation families [5].
4. **Find support** [13][14][15].
