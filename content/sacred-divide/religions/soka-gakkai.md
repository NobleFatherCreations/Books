## 2. A day inside {#a-day-inside}

- **name:** Keiko
- **when:** A Tuesday before an upper-house election
- **where:** Saitama

1. Gongyo at six, the chanting that has steadied her through two miscarriages and a layoff. It works. She is sure of that.
2. At ten the district leader calls with the list: twelve friends to contact for the candidate.
3. She buys a second copy of the newspaper for her sister, who does not read it.
4. At lunch a colleague asks, lightly, whether her religion tells her how to vote. She says no, and it is technically true.
5. At the discussion meeting in the evening, a young mother cries about her child's illness and is told to chant with more determination.
6. Walking home Keiko rehearses the calls she will make tomorrow, and notices she has never once asked who decided that this was the candidate.

## 4. What healthy looks like here {#healthy}

::: lede
Soka Gakkai reports 8.27 million member households in Japan and around 3 million members elsewhere, in 192 countries and territories [1]. It is a lay Buddhist organization practising the chanting of Nam-myoho-renge-kyo in the tradition of the 13th-century teacher Nichiren. It is known for peace, culture and education work (Soka University, UN-affiliated NGO activity) and a strong local discussion-meeting culture [1].
:::

- Peace, anti-nuclear and education activism.
- The founder refused to bow to State Shinto, at the cost of his life [7].
- A lay-led religion without priestly fees since the 1991 break [9].
- A public apology (1970) and a formal policy separating religion and party, adopted after the scandal [3].
- In many countries, a light-touch, voluntary practice.

## 5. History {#history}

The movement's key stages:
- **1930:** founded by the educators Tsunesaburo Makiguchi and Josei Toda, as a lay group within Nichiren Shoshu.
- **1943–44:** Makiguchi was arrested in 1943 for refusing to accept a State Shinto talisman and died in prison in November 1944 [7].
- **1950s:** under Toda, explosive growth through aggressive conversion (shakubuku).
- **1960–2023:** Daisaku Ikeda led from 1960, was honorary president from 1979, founded SGI (1975), and died on 15 November 2023 [6].
- **1991:** the Nichiren Shoshu priesthood excommunicated the organization on 28 November 1991 [9].
- **Since 2006:** Minoru Harada is president [1].

## 7. Structure {#structure}

### Size and shape

- **adherents:** 8.27M households (Japan, self-reported); ~3M outside Japan [1].
- **regions:** Japan; the US, Brazil, Korea, Taiwan, Europe, India [1].
- **branches:** Soka Gakkai; Nichiren Shoshu, the priesthood, separate since 1991 [9].
- **trend:** aging in Japan; growth in some countries abroad.
- **participation:** high local activity in Japan — meetings, the newspaper, election work [5].

### Authority

- A president (Minoru Harada, since 2006) [1]. The late Ikeda remains the central "mentor", and his writings (*The Human Revolution*) are foundational [6].
- A dense local structure of districts, chapters, and youth and women's divisions [5].
- A registered religious corporation in Japan, subject to the Religious Corporations Law as revised in 1995 [11].

## 9. Money {#money}

### Where it comes from

- Financial contributions (zaimu, 財務) solicited from members; former members and commentators describe the major annual drives as larger in the past [8]. Current practice is not established from a primary source.
- The daily *Seikyo Shimbun* has long claimed a circulation of about 5.5 million. That figure is unverifiable: the paper is outside Japan's official circulation-audit system [8]. Members' work selling subscriptions is documented in scholarship [5].
- Publishing, cultural centres and Soka University [1].

## 10. Genealogy {#genealogy}

1. **A religion with a party**
   - *Origin:* Toda's and Ikeda's vision of kosen-rufu, including politics; Komeito founded 1964 [3].
   - *Then:* Protection from a hostile state, remembering the founder's death in prison [7].
   - *Expired?* A party in national government for 26 years [4] is not a persecuted minority's shield.
   - *Benefits now:* The party's legislative power and the organization's standing.
2. **The mentor–disciple relationship**
   - *Origin:* Nichiren's teaching, reframed around Ikeda [6].
   - *Then:* Inspiration and unity.
   - *Expired?* Since 2023 [6], the question is who inherits the authority.
   - *Benefits now:* The organization's leadership.

## 23. The questions {#questions}

1. Does your district ask you to campaign? Who decided that was faith?
2. Can you see the organization's accounts?
3. How many copies of the newspaper does your household buy, and why? Why is its circulation unaudited [8]?
4. Who holds authority now that the mentor has died [6]?
5. In 1969–70 the organization pressured a publisher to stop a critic's book [3]. What written rule prevents that today?
6. The founder refused the state. Is a party in government what he refused, or what he wanted?

### In closing

1. Your founder's courage is the standard: refuse the state's claim on conscience [7]. That includes a religion's claim on a member's vote.
2. You can chant every morning and vote however you choose.
