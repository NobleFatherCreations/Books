## neutral
Roughly one million members *(verify)*, mostly in India (Gujarat, Maharashtra) and Pakistan, with communities in East Africa, the Gulf, the UK, the US and Australia. A Shia Ismaili community led by a single spiritual leader, the Daʿi al-Mutlaq ("the absolute missionary"), who acts on behalf of a hidden Imam. Known for strong community institutions, business networks, distinctive dress, and a comprehensive welfare system.

## origin
Descends from the Fatimid Ismaili tradition via the Mustaʿli line (1094) and the Tayyibi line in Yemen. After the Imam al-Tayyib went into concealment (12th c.), authority passed to the Daʿi al-Mutlaq. The daʿwa's seat moved from Yemen to Gujarat in the 16th century *(verify date)*. The Dawoodi–Sulaymani split (1592) gives the community its name. The 51st and 52nd Daʿis (20th century) built its modern centralized institutions. The 53rd Daʿi has led since 2014, after a succession disputed in court.

## authority
- One Daʿi al-Mutlaq with authority over religious and communal life, believed to act for the hidden Imam. [OFFICIAL POLICY]
- Each local community (jamaat) is administered by an ʿamil appointed by the Daʿi; permissions (raza) for marriages, burials, religious functions and other life events flow through this office. [OFFICIAL POLICY / COURT RECORD]
- An oath of allegiance (misaq) is taken around puberty, binding members to the Daʿi. [OFFICIAL POLICY]
- The central administration (the Dawat-e-Hadiyah) holds trusts, property and institutions. [OFFICIAL POLICY]

## money
- Obligatory dues including an annual wajebaat assessed per member, plus other levies and offerings *(verify names and rates)*. [OFFICIAL POLICY / FORMER MEMBER TESTIMONY]
- Large trusts and redevelopment projects, e.g. the Saifee Burhani Upliftment Trust's redevelopment of Bhendi Bazaar, Mumbai. [OFFICIAL POLICY / INVESTIGATIVE REPORT]
- Interest-free community loans (qardan hasana): a genuine welfare system that also sits inside the same permission structure. [OFFICIAL POLICY]

## exit
- Excommunication (baraat) with social boycott, historically cutting a member off from mosques, cemeteries, marriage and business within the community. [COURT RECORD]
- The Bombay Prevention of Excommunication Act (1949) outlawed this. The Supreme Court of India struck it down in 1962 as violating the community's religious freedom (*Sardar Syedna Taher Saifuddin Saheb v. State of Bombay*). The question returned to the Court and was referred to a larger bench *(verify current status)*. [COURT RECORD]
- Maharashtra's Prohibition of Social Boycott Act (2016) criminalizes social boycott generally. [OFFICIAL POLICY]

## whoBenefits
- The office of the Daʿi and the central administration, which hold the permission system, trusts and dues. [COURT RECORD / OFFICIAL POLICY]
- Appointed ʿamils, whose standing rests on it.

## healthy
- A welfare system (interest-free loans, housing, community kitchens) that is genuinely effective.
- Strong literacy and commercial education, including for women.
- Community-wide initiatives (cleanliness, environmental drives).
- Internal reform voices and survivor-led campaigns *from inside* the community (Sahiyo, WeSpeakOut, Progressive Dawoodi Bohras).

## children
- Khatna (female genital cutting, typically clitoral hood cutting at about age seven) has been widely practised. It is documented in criminal courts in the US and Australia and in survivor-led surveys *(verify: Sahiyo 2017 survey figures)*. [COURT RECORD / ACADEMIC SOURCE]
- The misaq is taken around puberty, binding a child to the Daʿi before adulthood. [OFFICIAL POLICY]

## gender
- Khatna is the defining documented gender harm; the community's public position has shifted in some jurisdictions to "follow the law of the land" *(verify statements)*. [LEADERSHIP STATEMENT]
- Distinct women's dress (the rida). An all-male religious hierarchy. [OFFICIAL POLICY]

## opening
You may belong to one of the most generous, well-organized communities in this book: loans without interest, a kitchen that feeds everyone, and a prayer life that shapes every day. Nothing here dismisses that. This page is about a permission system that reaches from your wedding to your grave, and about a practice done to little girls that three countries' courts have now examined.

## genealogy
1. **The permission (raza) system**
   - *Origin:* The Daʿi's authority as deputy of the hidden Imam, extended to every religious act. [OFFICIAL POLICY]
   - *Then:* Order in a dispersed trading community with no state.
   - *Expired?* When burial needs permission, the office holds a key to the grave.
   - *Benefits now:* The central office.
2. **Excommunication and boycott**
   - *Origin:* The community's right to discipline members, protected by the Indian Supreme Court in 1962. [COURT RECORD]
   - *Then:* Doctrinal integrity.
   - *Expired?* Used against reformers who questioned finances and permissions (per the reform record), it becomes a tool against criticism, not heresy.
   - *Benefits now:* The office, against its critics.
3. **Khatna**
   - *Origin:* Pre-modern practice; framed as purity or tradition. [ACADEMIC SOURCE]
   - *Then:* —
   - *Expired?* Criminal courts in the US and Australia have ruled on it; survivors from inside the community have organized against it.
   - *Benefits now:* No one. It persists because it is not publicly renounced.

## loopHere (the seven loops)
1. **Money:** Dues fund the office that grants the permissions members need to live.
2. **Fear:** Loss of the Daʿi's favor, and burial outside the community.
3. **Children:** Misaq at puberty; khatna at seven.
4. **Aid:** Welfare builds loyalty and public standing.
5. **Labor:** Community labor for events and trusts.
6. **Scandal:** Prosecutions abroad; the practice continues quietly *(verify)*.
7. **Persecution:** Real anti-Shia and anti-minority hostility invoked against reformers.

## day
- **name:** Zainab
- **when:** A Tuesday in Muharram
- **where:** Mumbai

1. Her daughter turned seven in March. The question arrives at breakfast, from her mother-in-law, gently, as all of it arrives: has she thought about the thing that is done at seven?
2. Zainab has read the Sahiyo stories on her phone, late at night, in English so her mother-in-law won't see them.
3. At noon the ʿamil's office calls about the wajebaat assessment. Her husband handles it. He comes back quieter than he went.
4. In the evening, the majlis. The whole community in rida and topi, the sermon from the Daʿi relayed on screens, the grief for Husayn that she has felt every year of her life.
5. Her cousin, whose father was put under baraat for signing a reformist letter in the eighties, is not there. He is never there. Zainab thinks of the funeral they had to hold in another city.
6. At night she lies awake with the decision about her daughter, which everybody says is a small one, and understands that the smallness is the whole mechanism.
