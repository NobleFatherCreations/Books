## neutral
A new religious movement founded in Seoul in 1954 by Sun Myung Moon, and led since his death in 2012 by his widow Hak Ja Han Moon [1]. Membership figures are disputed. The movement has claimed up to 3 million worldwide (and at times far more); independent estimates run from about 250,000 to just over 1 million [19]. It is known for its "Blessing" mass wedding ceremonies, its teaching of Moon and Han as the "True Parents", and a wide business and media network [1].

## origin
Founded in Seoul in 1954 as the Holy Spirit Association for the Unification of World Christianity [1]. Moon's *Divine Principle* teaches that the Fall corrupted humanity's lineage, and that the True Parents restore it through the Blessing (arranged marriage) [1]. The movement reached Japan in 1959 [12] and the US in the 1960s–70s. It built businesses and anti-communist political networks, which a US congressional subcommittee investigated in 1976–78 [3]. It took the name Family Federation for World Peace and Unification in the 1990s [1]. After Moon's death, sons led rival organizations.

## authority
- The "True Parents" hold supreme spiritual authority. Since 2012 that is Hak Ja Han Moon as "True Mother" [1].
- National churches are led by appointees. Japan has been the movement's largest source of funds, according to experts cited in reporting [18].
- Marriage matching places members' family formation under institutional authority [1].
- A 1978 US congressional report concluded that the church and Moon's other religious and secular organizations "constitute essentially one international organization", moving people and money freely across borders [3].

## money
- **Donations in Japan, including "spiritual sales"** — selling ordinary goods at extreme prices by invoking ancestral karma. Reported examples include a 43-million-yen statuette [12].
  - Japan's National Network of Lawyers Against Spiritual Sales (founded 1987) says it has filed suits seeking 123.7 billion yen in damages for former followers [12].
  - Consumer law in Japan now treats "spiritual sales" as a recognized legal category [11].
- The church in Japan offered to deposit up to 10 billion yen (about $66 million) as a compensation fund in 2023, while the dissolution case was pending [17].
- A business network including *The Washington Times* (founded 1982) and other commercial holdings [1].
- The founder was convicted in 1982 of filing false tax returns; the conviction was affirmed on appeal (*United States v. Sun Myung Moon*, 718 F.2d 1210, 2d Cir. 1983) [2].

## exit
- Leaving can mean losing an arranged spouse, family (especially for second-generation "Blessed" children) and community [13]. [FORMER MEMBER TESTIMONY]
- Teachings on leaving the restored lineage carry spiritual threat. [FORMER MEMBER TESTIMONY]
- **Balance:** for decades in Japan, some families hired "deprogrammers" who confined members against their will. A Tokyo court awarded damages in January 2014, and the Tokyo High Court increased the award in November 2014, to a member held by relatives and deprogrammers from 1995 to 2008; the ruling was later upheld by the Supreme Court [16]. The practice declined after the UN Human Rights Committee urged Japan in 2014 to prevent coercion over belief [16].

## whoBenefits
- The central leadership and Korean headquarters, funded mainly from Japan [18].
- Affiliated businesses [1].
- Politicians who received organizational support:
  - In Japan, an internal survey of the Liberal Democratic Party (Sept 2022) found 179 of its 379 national lawmakers had some tie to the group, ranging from event attendance to election help [10].
  - In South Korea, the leader was convicted in 2026 of giving bribes and illegal political funds [14].

## healthy
- Members' genuine commitment to marriage, family and interfaith events.
- Accountability mechanisms working as designed:
  - Japan's courts and legislature [4][5][8]
  - Korea's courts [14]
  - a US appeals court [2]
- The church's own 2023 compensation offer [17] and its participation in collective mediation with donation victims [5]. The court judged these insufficient, but they are real steps.

## demographics
- **adherents:** disputed. Up to 3M claimed; independent estimates 250,000–1M+ [19]. In 2022 church leadership claimed about 600,000 members in Japan [19].
- **regions:** South Korea (headquarters), Japan (largest financial base) [18], the US, the Philippines and elsewhere.
- **branches:** the Family Federation (Hak Ja Han); rival organizations led by two of Moon's sons (Sanctuary Church; Family Peace Association) [1].
- **trend:** dissolved as a religious corporation in Japan (pending the Supreme Court) [5][6]; leader convicted in Korea [14]; an aging first generation.
- **participation:** intense for core members; the second generation is openly divided [13].

## info
- The *Divine Principle* and the True Parents' speeches are authoritative [1]. Former members describe critical media being framed as satanic persecution. [FORMER MEMBER TESTIMONY]

## children
- Second-generation "Blessed" children are born into the lineage and expected to marry within it [1].
- From 2022, second-generation members in Japan spoke publicly about poverty and neglect caused by parents' donations, and about restricted religious freedom [13]. On 27 Dec 2022 Japan's Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare issued a Q&A to local governments on child abuse related to religious belief [20]. UN human-rights experts later criticized related materials for risking the conflation of legitimate religious practice with abuse [21]. Record both.
