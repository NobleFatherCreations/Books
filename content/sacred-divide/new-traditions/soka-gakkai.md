## neutral
Japan's largest new religious movement, claiming about 8 million households there *(verify: self-reported 8.27M)* and some 2 million+ members abroad *(verify)*. A lay Buddhist organization practising the chanting of Nam-myoho-renge-kyo, following the 13th-century teacher Nichiren. It is known for its peace, culture and education work: Soka University, the Min-On concert association *(verify)*, and UN-affiliated NGO activity.

## origin
Founded in 1930 by educators Tsunesaburo Makiguchi and Josei Toda as the Soka Kyoiku Gakkai, a lay group within Nichiren Shoshu. Makiguchi died in prison in 1944 after refusing to enshrine a State Shinto talisman. Under Toda (1951–58) it grew explosively through aggressive conversion (shakubuku). Daisaku Ikeda led from 1960 (honorary president from 1979) until his death in 2023, founded SGI (1975), and presided over the break with the Nichiren Shoshu priesthood, which excommunicated the organization in 1991.

- A president (Minoru Harada since 2006 *(verify)*), but the central figure is the late honorary president Ikeda, presented as the "mentor", with his writings (*The Human Revolution*) as foundational. [OFFICIAL POLICY / ACADEMIC SOURCE]

## opening
You may chant every morning and feel your life change. You may have marched for nuclear disarmament. Your movement's founder died rather than bow to a state god. This page asks what happens when a religion builds a political party, and whose work turns out the votes.

## genealogy
1. **A religion with a party**
   - *Origin:* Toda's and Ikeda's vision of kosen-rufu, including politics; Komeito founded 1964. [ACADEMIC SOURCE]
   - *Then:* Protection from a hostile state, informed by the founder's death in prison.
   - *Expired?* A party in national government for over two decades *(verify)* is not a persecuted minority's shield.
   - *Benefits now:* The party's legislative power, and the organization's protection.
2. **The mentor–disciple relationship**
   - *Origin:* Nichiren's teaching, reframed around Ikeda. [ACADEMIC SOURCE]
   - *Then:* Inspiration and unity.
   - *Expired?* After 2023, the question is who inherits the authority.
   - *Benefits now:* The organization's leadership.

## hardQuestions
1. Does your chapter ask you to campaign for Komeito? Who decided that was faith?
2. Can you see the organization's accounts?
3. How many copies of the newspaper does your household buy, and why?
4. Who holds authority now that the mentor has died?
5. When Komeito voted on laws about religious corporations, whose interest did it represent?

## closing

2. You can chant every morning and vote however you choose.
