## for-you structure
Jinja Honcho, the Association of Shinto Shrines, coordinates about 80,000 shrines. When its board failed, the courts stepped in. A staff member who questioned a 2015 property sale was dismissed, and the courts voided the dismissal. In 2024 the Supreme Court rejected a rival claim to the presidency. If you buy amulets or pay festival levies, your money supports this body. Did you know it had been in court over its own leadership?

## for-you law
Japan's 1947 constitution separates religion and state (Articles 20 and 89) because State Shinto had been used to enforce emperor worship. The Supreme Court has held that public offerings to Yasukuni (1997) and free municipal land for a shrine (2010) were unconstitutional. If you are a Japanese voter, shrine-linked political networks are lobbying to revise that constitution. Do you know which of your representatives they support?

## for-you money
Amulets, fortune slips, ceremony fees, corporate purification services and neighborhood festival levies. The money table adds one line most shrine visitors don't know: devotional revenue that supports "a constitutional-revision agenda most donors never voted on." When you buy an omamori, part of the system you are funding also lobbies. Would you still buy it if you knew exactly where the money went?

## for-you cost
There is no membership to leave. The glance table says the cost is paid in neighborliness and reputation: a household that opts out of shrine festivals or levies gets marked. The unanswered question asks it directly: if taking part is "just culture" and belief is optional, why does refusing cost you belonging? Could your household decline the levy for three years and go unremarked?

## for-you who-gets-hurt
The table puts women first: purity exclusions at some sites, kept in place by custom with no doctrine to appeal to. Women are still barred from landing on the sacred island of Okinoshima, a World Heritage site. Then come households that don't take part, marked in tight neighborhoods, especially minorities and religious dissenters. If you are a woman who has been turned away from a sacred site, who could you ask to explain why?
