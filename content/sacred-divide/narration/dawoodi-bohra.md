## for-you structure
One office, the Daʿi al-Mutlaq, has authority over the permissions that shape a Bohra life: marriage, burial and pilgrimage. When the 52nd Daʿi died in 2014, the succession went to court, and the Bombay High Court dismissed the challenge in 2024. If your family's permissions all run through one office, who could you appeal to if that office were wrong about you?

## for-you law
India has changed its mind about excommunication three times. A 1949 Bombay law outlawed it. The Supreme Court struck the law down in 1962 as an infringement of religious freedom. Maharashtra banned social boycott in 2016, and in 2023 the Supreme Court sent the 1962 ruling to nine judges. On khatna, the diaspora courts have also moved: the first U.S. federal FGM prosecution was in Detroit in 2017. If you live in India, your rights here are still being decided. Are you following the case?

## for-you money
The community is known for order and care: nobody hungry, nobody in debt to a bank. It is funded by annual dues (wajebaat) and other levies, and reformist accounts say that a paid-up card is required for rites, which the page flags as testimony awaiting a primary source. The questions section asks it simply: where are the accounts? If your family's rites depend on having paid, do you know where the payment goes?

## for-you cost
Excommunication (baraat) carries social boycott, including denial of mosque access and community burial. That makes it a cost for the family as well as the person, across generations. If you are thinking about speaking up on khatna or anything else, this is the price the page says reformers have paid. Knowing that, who would you want standing beside you?

## for-you who-gets-hurt
The first row is girls. Khatna is usually performed around age seven, long before consent is possible. In Sahiyo's 2017 survey of 385 women, 80% had been cut and 81% wanted the practice to end. If you are a Bohra parent of a daughter, you are the person this section is written to. Many Bohra women have already said no, publicly, from inside the faith.
