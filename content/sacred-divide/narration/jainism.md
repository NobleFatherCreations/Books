## for-you structure
Authority in Jainism rests with the acharyas, who lead the monastic orders through moral standing rather than office. Nobody below can remove them. There is no church to resign from and no bureaucracy to appeal to. The page's point is that in a community this close-knit, visibility does the enforcing: everyone can see who fasts, who gives and who doesn't. If you have ever done something because people would notice if you didn't, this page is about that feeling.

## for-you law
Indian courts are deciding questions at the heart of Jain practice. In 2015 the Rajasthan High Court treated santhara, the ritual fast to death, as suicide; the Supreme Court stayed that ruling weeks later, and the appeal is still pending. No law sets a minimum age for a child's renunciation, and bills have failed since 1955. In 2026 a Surat court stayed a seven-year-old girl's initiation on her father's plea. If you were a legislator, what minimum age would you set? Would you set one at all?

## for-you money
Non-possession is a central Jain ethic, and ritual honors are auctioned to the highest bidder, in public. The day inside describes an auction where the winning figure is read aloud "and everyone in the hall now knows what everyone in the hall can afford." The scorecard finds no public accounts. When your family bids, what are you actually buying: merit, or standing?

## for-you cost
Leaving costs you marriage networks and business trust at once, because the community's commercial ties are its enforcement mechanism. Nobody forces anything, the page agrees. But if your work, your credit and your children's marriages all depend on one community's good opinion, how free is your choice?

## for-you who-gets-hurt
The table puts women first: doctrinal limits on their capacity for liberation, ritual exclusion, and dowry despite the ethic of non-possession. Then children, fasting competitively for family status and in some cases renouncing the world as minors. The glance table asks the question in the tradition's own terms: a tradition this serious about harm to the smallest insect, "who examines the harm to the girl fasting for her family's standing?"
