## for-you structure
Islam is often described as having no clergy and no church between a believer and God. The say-do table records the gap: in many states, government ministries license the preacher, approve the sermon and pay the salary. The most-cited seat of Sunni learning is headed by a Grand Imam whom Egypt's 2014 constitution makes irremovable. If your imam's Friday sermon was written or approved by a ministry, would you know? Would it change how you heard it?

## for-you law
Everything on this page depends on your passport. The same act, leaving Islam, can carry the death penalty in about ten states and nothing at all in others. India's Supreme Court struck down instant triple talaq in 2017. Moroccan judges approved 81% of 32,000 requests for underage marriage in 2019. If you are a Muslim woman, which of these legal systems decides your marriage, your divorce and your children? Did you choose it?

## for-you money
Zakat, sadaqa and waqf are among the most generous giving systems in any religion. The money section also records that most of it moves "without a public ledger," and that state-run waqf boards have recurring corruption findings. The land your great-grandparents endowed may today be administered by trustees you didn't choose. When you pay zakat through an institution this year, ask one question: can I see where last year's went?

## for-you cost
The Quran says there is no compulsion in religion. Yet in about ten states leaving Islam is a crime that can carry death, and in many more marriage, custody and inheritance depend on religious status. The genealogy section records that these apostasy laws are used against critics, minorities and personal enemies far more than against actual apostates. If a cousin of yours told you privately they no longer believed, what would you do with that knowledge?

## for-you who-gets-hurt
The table puts women first, under guardianship, unequal divorce, custody and inheritance rules, where there is no civil alternative. Then LGBTQ Muslims, ex-Muslims and doubters, minority sects such as Ahmadis, and children in residential religious schools with weak reporting routes. Most of these harms come from state law or community enforcement rather than scripture. Which of them has your community ever spoken about publicly?

## q1
why: The Quranic principle is explicit, and so are the penal codes. The question puts them side by side and asks why the scholars paid by those states do not point out the contradiction.
example: The say-do table sets "There is no compulsion in religion" beside the record: several states maintain criminal penalties for leaving it, and the scholars on those states' payrolls do not say otherwise.

## q2
why: The page's genealogy test applies here. Guardianship was framed as protection for women who could not contract or inherit alone. Once that disability is gone, the rule has lost its stated reason.
example: The genealogy section concludes that keeping guardianship "after removing the disability it addressed converts protection into ownership," and notes that Saudi Arabia's own recent reforms concede the point.

## q3
why: Silence after abuse protects someone. The question asks whether it was the child, or the school and its reputation.
example: The who-gets-hurt table lists children in residential religious schools, with "documented abuse with weak reporting pathways," where the school is also the family's only affordable education.

## q4
why: A state that enforces a religious rule by force, and punishes those who report on the enforcement, is protecting its power, not the rule. The question separates the two.
example: The genealogy section records that state enforcement of modesty by morality police is "a 20th-century invention, principally post-1979," and that a young woman died in morality-police custody in 2022.

## q5
why: If a scholar's income depends on a government, his rulings and that government's policy become hard to tell apart from outside. The question asks what test a believer could use.
example: The reach section records that in many Muslim-majority states sermon content is written or approved by a government ministry: "information control by the state, wearing clerical dress."

## q6
why: A rule that says criticism arms the enemy ends a question before anyone looks at the evidence. The question asks who benefits from that silence.
example: The reach section records that community pressure discourages engagement with critical scholarship, and that "orientalist" works in some settings as a label that ends the conversation.
