## for-you structure
Every cardinal who elects a pope was appointed by a previous pope, and canon law has no procedure for removing one. Most Catholics know the first fact. Few have thought about the second. If you are Catholic, the chain of accountability above your parish priest ends in a single office that no one can correct against its will. Does that change how you think about the phrase "the Church has spoken"?

## for-you law
Where you live decides a lot. In Germany the state collects church tax through the tax office: €6.75 billion for the Catholic dioceses in 2025. Leaving is a formal civil declaration that ends the payment. In Ireland, mandatory reporting now reaches into church settings. If you are a German taxpayer, did you know that leaving is a form at the registry office? If you live somewhere else, who in your country could compel a diocese to produce its files?

## for-you money
Your parish collection, the diocesan appeal and Peter's Pence all feed an institution with one of the largest real-estate portfolios in the world. The say-do table records that part of Peter's Pence was invested, including in the London property at the center of a cardinal's criminal conviction. Before the next second collection, ask your diocese one question: where can a parishioner read last year's audited accounts?

## for-you cost
For the devout, the cost of leaving is framed as eternal: excommunication and the loss of the sacraments. Even for those who stay, the annulment system decides whether a second marriage counts. If you are divorced and remarried, or love someone who is, this is the section that explains why communion depends on a tribunal.

## for-you who-gets-hurt
Four national inquiries (Australia, France, Ireland and the United States) established that children in Church institutions carried the heaviest weight in this whole book. If you went to Catholic school, or your children do, the question is not whether your parish is safe today. It is whether you would know if it weren't. Who would a child in your parish tell, and who would that person be required to tell?

## q1
why: "Nothing to hide" and "a government had to subpoena the documents" cannot both be true. An institution with nothing to hide does not need state power to produce its own records.
example: France's CIASE report, Australia's Royal Commission and U.S. grand juries each obtained internal Church documents through state authority. None of them began with a synod or a papal inquiry acting first.

## q2
why: This question isolates one documented administrative decision, reassignment instead of reporting, and asks why the officials who made it, again and again, kept their offices.
example: The say-do table sets "Zero tolerance for abuse, since 2002" beside national inquiries whose own files documented transfers rather than reports, some of them after 2002.

## q3
why: The Becciu conviction shows the capacity exists: a working criminal court inside the Vatican that can convict a cardinal. So not using it for cover-ups is a choice about priorities, not a limit of power.
example: Cardinal Becciu was convicted of financial crimes by the Vatican's own tribunal in 2023, the first cardinal tried there. A partial retrial began in 2026. No bishop has been convicted by that machinery for concealing abuse.

## q4
why: The question pairs two outcomes to expose an inconsistency. A private choice, remarrying after divorce, carries a sacramental penalty. An institutional choice, moving a known abuser, has not carried an equivalent one.
example: The cost section records that the annulment system controls whether a remarriage is legitimate. The cases and inquiries on this page document the transfers.

## q5
why: This is a logic test. If a rule is spiritually necessary, there can be no valid exception. If there are valid exceptions, the rule is a discipline, which is a different and more honest thing to call it.
example: The branches section records that Eastern Catholic churches, in full communion with Rome, ordain married men. Their sacraments are recognized as valid.

## q6
why: When criticizing the institution is treated as betraying God, the cost of asking any question rises as high as it can go. The question asks you to separate the two.
example: The closing of this page's own questions section notes that millions of Catholics forced the inquiries and ran survivor networks while keeping their faith. The institution and the faith were separable for them.
