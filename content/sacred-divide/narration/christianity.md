## for-you structure
Most readers picture "the Church" as one body with one head. For most Protestant congregations the real top of the chain is the local pastor and a board, often a board the pastor helped choose. If your pastor did something seriously wrong, do you know, in writing, who could remove him? Have you ever seen your church's bylaws? You are allowed to ask for them.

## for-you law
If you are American, here is a fact most churchgoers don't know: churches are the one kind of charity that does not have to file the public Form 990 that lets anyone look up a charity's income and top salaries. Your pastor's housing allowance is also tax-free. Nobody is breaking the law here. But the law is the reason you can't look your own church up. Did anyone ever tell you that?

## for-you money
If you tithe, you may be giving more of your income to your church than to almost anything else except housing and tax. The scorecard finds no public evidence of what leaders are paid. Ask for last year's budget and the senior pastor's total pay, including housing. A church that has nothing to hide can answer that in writing. Watch whether you get an answer, or a sermon about trust.

## for-you cost
The price of leaving is usually paid in relationships, not money: friends who cool, a small group that stops texting, sometimes a public "church discipline" announcement. If you work for a church, a school or a ministry, your job is on the line too. If you have ever held back a doubt because of who you'd lose, then this cost has already been shaping what you say.

## for-you who-gets-hurt
The table puts children first, and for a reason. Teaching about eternal punishment, and shame about sex, reach children before they can weigh either. If you are a parent, ask what your child has heard about hell by the age of seven, and from whom. If you are a woman in a marriage your church calls "complementarian", ask where the counsel goes if the marriage becomes unsafe. The table's answer is: back to submission.

## q1
why: Churches are exempt from filing disclosures that almost every other charity has to file. So the only way members will ever see the accounts is if the church volunteers them. The question asks why so few churches do, when "nothing to hide" costs nothing to prove.
example: The say-do table sets "We have nothing to hide" beside the record: the sector lobbied for and kept the only charitable exemption from public financial disclosure in U.S. law (§6033).

## q2
why: Preaching sacrificial giving puts a cost on the congregation. Publishing the preacher's own pay is the only way to show that the sacrifice is shared. The question is about consistency, not about the size of the salary.
example: The scorecard marks Pay as N: no public source anywhere establishes what leaders are paid. The money section also records that clergy housing allowances are tax-free under IRC §107.

## q3
why: An investigation run by someone who reports to the accused is not independent, whatever its conclusions. This question checks structure, not honesty. Who could the investigator afford to find against?
example: The cases section records that Southern Baptist executive staff kept a private list of accused ministers for years while telling survivors that such a list was structurally impossible. It took an independent firm, Guidepost Solutions in 2022, to find it. It also records two founders whose misconduct was confirmed only after they had left or died.

## q4
why: A rule invoked only when leaders are criticized works as a shield, not as a teaching. The question asks you to notice when the rule is used, rather than argue about whether it is scriptural.
example: The reach section records that critics and former members are pre-labeled as bitter, deceived or spiritually attacked. That discounts their testimony before anyone has heard it.

## q5
why: "You're free to leave" is only true if leaving costs something bearable. Asking for two real names turns a slogan into a test anyone can run in their own congregation.
example: The cost section's ledger lists family and community loss as "Often" documented. In high-control congregations it adds formal shunning and discipline announcements.

## q6
why: Doubt threatens beliefs, but it also threatens attendance and giving. The question separates the two, so that a member can ask which one is actually being protected when doubt is discouraged.
example: The say-do table records that "giving is between you and God," while attendance and giving are tracked, and eligibility for leadership roles often depends on both.
