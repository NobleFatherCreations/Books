## for-you structure
There is no church here, no clergy and no membership. The top of the chain is simply the senior generation of your own family, which the glance table calls "the apex, the court, and the enforcement arm in one." You were born into this position, and nobody removes you from it except by time. The page calls it the purest ownerless control in the book. If there is no one to appeal to, what happens when your parents are wrong?

## for-you law
States have written this ethic into law, and taken it back out. China's 2013 elderly-rights law requires adult children to visit their parents, and a Wuxi court ordered a woman to visit her 77-year-old mother at least once every two months. South Korea abolished the *hoju* system, which made a man the legal head of every family, and replaced it with individual registration in 2008. Should a court be able to order you to visit your parents? If you are South Korean, did you know your grandmother lived under the *hoju* system?

## for-you money
Nothing on this page asks for donations. The cost is paid in labor, deference and life choices: eldercare, the careers and marriages you are steered into, and, most heavily, daughters-in-law's time. In the day inside, a wife texts about her mother's hospital appointment and who will take the day off. "It will be her. It is always her." In your family, who takes the day off? Was it ever discussed?

## for-you cost
You cannot resign from a family. The glance table calls that the trap: there is no membership to give up, so a person who disagrees can only be a bad child, a bad student or "a disharmonious element." If you have ever kept quiet at a family dinner to avoid being that person, this section is describing you.

## for-you who-gets-hurt
Daughters-in-law come first in the table: the heaviest care labor with the lowest standing in the household, no independent income and no way out of the family they married into. Then daughters generally, under son preference that, with modern technology, became sex selection across whole regions. If you are a son, what do you owe the women carrying your family's obligations?

## q1
why: The tradition contains its own corrective, a duty to remonstrate with erring elders. The question asks why that half of the ethic is almost never taught.
example: The say-do table sets "Filial piety is reciprocal duty" beside the record: "Mencius asserted the duty to remonstrate with an erring superior, and that half of the ethic is almost never taught." The history section adds that Mencius even allowed the deposing of an unworthy ruler.

## q2
why: Duty that runs only one way is obedience, not reciprocity. The question asks parents to name an obligation to their children that they would accept being held to.
example: The genealogy section on face and shame records that the rule "now runs in one direction. A parent's conduct is not subject to the family's shame; a child's is."

## q3
why: A rule against bringing shame on the family also works as a rule against telling anyone what happens inside it. The question asks who that protects in your own family.
example: The genealogy section records that "do not bring shame on us" functions as an information rule that keeps abuse inside the house.

## q4
why: If rites require sons, daughters become lesser members of the lineage. The question asks what happened to them, and who remembers them.
example: The money table records ancestor rites and lineage funds controlled by senior males. The who-gets-hurt table records son preference and, with technology, sex selection "into skewed ratios across whole regions."

## q5
why: The examination system ended more than a century ago, but the way it valued people may not have. The question asks whether your worth is still calculated that way.
example: The history section records that the imperial examination system (605–1905) made memorizing a canon "the only route to status" for 1,300 years.

## q6
why: If there were really nothing to leave, disagreeing would be free. The question notices how much it costs.
example: The glance table records: "There is no membership to resign — which is the trap: one can only be a bad child, a bad student, a disharmonious element. Dissent has no legitimate category."
