## for-you structure
Sun Myung Moon founded the church in Seoul in 1954. Since his death in 2012 his widow, Hak Ja Han Moon, has led it, and the history table records that the succession came "by schism." In August 2026 a Seoul court sentenced her to two years for bribery, political-funds violations and embezzlement. The scorecard finds no written way to remove the leader. If you are a member, who inside the church could have stopped what that court found?

## for-you law
Japan has gone further than any other country in this book. After former Prime Minister Abe was assassinated in 2022 by a man who blamed the church for ruining his family, a 2023 law banned high-pressure solicitation of donations. The Tokyo District Court ordered the church's religious corporation dissolved in 2025, and the Tokyo High Court upheld that in 2026. If you are Japanese, a court has now found that how this church raised money harmed the public welfare. What do you think should happen to the families who gave?

## for-you money
Donations in Japan were solicited by appealing to ancestral karma, and "spiritual sales" became a recognized category in Japanese consumer law. Ancestor-liberation payments buy outcomes that no one can verify. The techniques section puts it plainly: "Nothing promised can be checked, and every payment can." If a relative of yours gave everything, do you know whether they could still recover some of it through the courts?

## for-you cost
For the second generation, those "born Blessed" to matched parents, leaving can mean losing your family and your lineage identity at once. From 2022, second-generation members in Japan spoke publicly about poverty and neglect caused by their parents' donations. If you are one of them, you are not alone, and Japan's health ministry issued guidance in 2022 on child abuse linked to religious belief.

## for-you who-gets-hurt
The table's rows are short and heavy: elderly Japanese and their families, pushed into bankruptcy by donations; second-generation children, living in poverty with no way out that doesn't cost them their family; and spouses matched abroad, isolated by language and by legal dependency. If you know someone in a matched marriage abroad, do they have a way home if they need one?
