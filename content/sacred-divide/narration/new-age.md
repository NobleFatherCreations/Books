## for-you structure
There is no church here. The glance table says the real top of the chain is the platforms and publishers that own the teacher economy (streaming services, publishing houses, festival brands), and that the teacher answers to them in conversion rates. That means your teacher's incentives are set by a sales funnel, not by any body that exists to protect you. If a healer harmed you, who would you report it to? The page's answer is that for most of this industry, no one.

## for-you law
When this industry is held to account, it happens through ordinary law. James Arthur Ray was convicted over three deaths in a sweat lodge at one of his retreats. NXIVM's leader was sentenced to 120 years. Ten California counties settled with Goop over unsupported health claims. The US Embassy warned in 2025 against ayahuasca and kambo retreats in Peru after deaths and assaults. Before you book a retreat abroad, have you checked what your own government says about it?

## for-you money
The money line describes a funnel: $97, then $997, then a $10,000 mastermind, plus retreats and certification ladders where you pay to become a certified healer who then certifies others. The page calls that "MLM logic in spiritual clothing." The unanswered question is the one to ask before your next payment: if the healing works, why does the funnel always need another tier?

## for-you cost
There is no formal exit. The costs are sunk costs, identity ("lightworker") and a community, plus a blame reversal in which you are told you manifested whatever went wrong. The sunk-cost effect (Arkes & Blumer, 1985) is well documented: the more you've already paid, the harder it is to stop. If you started today, knowing what you know, would you buy the next level?

## for-you who-gets-hurt
The table puts people in medical crisis first: treatment delayed for alternative protocols, which with cancer is measured in survival. Then women facing boundary violations by unlicensed healers and facilitators, with no licence to revoke and no body to report to, and retreat participants. If someone you love has swapped chemotherapy for a protocol, would you know how to talk to them about it without pushing them further in?
