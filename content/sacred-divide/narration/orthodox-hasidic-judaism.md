## for-you structure
In the Hasidic world the top of the chain is a rebbe's court, passed down by bloodline and settled by succession fights where necessary. The glance table records that no procedure to remove a rebbe exists or has ever existed. Satmar has been split between two brothers since 2006. If you belong to a court, your schooling, your marriage match and often your work run through its institutions. Who, other than the rebbe, could tell the rebbe he was wrong?

## for-you law
If you are a New York taxpayer, this section is partly about your money. The city's 2023 investigation found 18 yeshivas failing to give an education "substantially equivalent" to public schools, and the schools had received more than $1 billion in government funding. In Israel, the High Court ruled unanimously in 2024 that the army must draft Haredi men. Did you know your taxes help fund schools that may not teach your neighbor's son English or arithmetic? Is that a question of religious freedom, or of a child's rights?

## for-you money
The money here runs through tuition, kosher premiums, wedding economies and gemachs (community charitable funds). In Israel it also runs through state stipends for full-time Torah study, negotiated by Haredi parties. Those funds are generous, and the page notes that they also create webs of dependency. If you depend on a gemach, what would happen to that support if you started asking questions?

## for-you cost
This page records one of the highest exit costs of any Western religious community. People who leave can lose their spouse, their children (in custody fights where community funds appear on one side), their parents, their siblings and their work. And because secular education was withheld, many leave without marketable skills. If you are thinking about leaving, organizations like Footsteps exist because thousands have done it before you. If you are staying, what would you want for your own child if they chose to go?

## for-you who-gets-hurt
People who leave (OTD) come first in the table, then women under modesty policing, get refusal and rabbinically mediated reproductive decisions, often with many children, no secular education and no independent income. The question for a reader inside is the one the page's forefront poses: can a child in your community read, and tell someone if something is wrong? If not, who decided that?
