## for-you structure
The Universal House of Justice has nine members, elected every five years by the members of the national assemblies, and its membership is published. That is a real electoral structure, more than most pages in this book can show. Its decisions are held to be doctrinally guided, and women are ineligible by standing ruling. If you are a Bahá'í woman, you can vote in the elections at every level and can never be elected to the highest one. Has anyone ever explained that to you without pointing to the institution's own authority?

## for-you law
In Iran, Bahá'ís are not a recognized minority. They are excluded from universities, and running the community is prosecuted as membership of a "deviated sect." Two former leaders were sentenced in 2022 to a second ten-year term after an hour-long trial. The page calls this the largest harm it records, and it is done by the state, not the Faith. If you are not Bahá'í, did you know? What would it take for your government to raise it?

## for-you money
Two features stand out. The Faith's funds accept donations from members only, which the page calls a genuine integrity feature. And Huqúqu'lláh, 19% on surplus wealth, is paid to the Faith's center. In the UK the national assembly files public accounts. If you pay Huqúqu'lláh where you live, can you read how it was used?

## for-you cost
Ordinary resignation is simple. The heavy cost is reserved for organized dissent: someone designated a covenant-breaker is cut off, and even their family is expected to stop associating with them. Scholars and online critics were removed from the rolls in 1997, 2000 and 2005, with no appeal outside the institutions that removed them. If your son were designated tomorrow, what would unity ask of you?

## for-you who-gets-hurt
Iranian Bahá'ís come first: executed, imprisoned, barred from higher education, their cemeteries destroyed. Then those designated covenant-breakers, and relatives who must choose between them and the Faith. The page holds both truths together: a community severely persecuted from outside that also applies severe sanctions inside. Is it possible to defend the first without defending the second?
