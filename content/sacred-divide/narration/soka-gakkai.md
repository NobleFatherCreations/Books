## for-you structure
Daisaku Ikeda led the movement from 1960 and died in November 2023. Minoru Harada has been president since 2006. The page asks a question most members haven't had to face: now that the mentor has died, who holds authority, and how would a member know? If you chant daily, your practice is yours. Is the organization's direction something you have any say in?

## for-you law
The founder, Makiguchi, was arrested in 1943 for refusing a State Shinto talisman and died in prison. That is the page's standard. Then, in 1964, the religion founded a party, Komeito, which governed in coalition for twenty-six years until October 2025. If you are a Japanese voter, you have lived under a government that included a religion's party. Would you have wanted to know which of your neighbors were campaigning because their faith asked them to?

## for-you money
Members are asked for annual contributions (zaimu), and the page notes that current practice still needs a primary source. The daily *Seikyo Shimbun* claims a circulation of about 5.5 million, but it sits outside Japan's official audit system, so the figure can't be checked. In the day inside, a member buys a second copy for a sister who doesn't read it. How many copies does your household buy, and why? In the UK, SGI-UK files public accounts, which shows it can be done.

## for-you cost
There is no formal penalty for leaving. The cost is the density of it all: meetings, the newspaper, election work and multigenerational family ties. If you stopped doing election work but kept chanting, would anyone treat you differently? The closing line of the questions section puts it simply: you can chant every morning and vote however you choose.

## for-you who-gets-hurt
The table has one row: active members in Japan, carrying election work, newspaper subscriptions and contributions, compounded by family ties across generations. That is not the harm of other pages in this book. It is the cost of an organization asking a lot, over many years, and members finding it hard to say no. Where is your limit, and have you ever told anyone?
