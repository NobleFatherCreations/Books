## for-you structure
The next Church president is chosen by seniority, not by vote or discussion. The glance table calls it "a queue, not a choice," one you can see coming decades ahead, and the office ends only at death. The page finds no written way to remove a president. If you ever disagreed with one, where exactly would you take it?

## for-you law
If you are American, the most important thing on this page came from a federal regulator, not from the Church. The SEC found that the Church's investment arm hid its portfolio behind 13 shell companies from 1997 to 2019. In Utah, a 2024 law protects clergy who choose to report abuse, but it does not require them to report what they hear in confession. If a child in your ward disclosed abuse to a bishop, do you know whether the law would require him to call the police?

## for-you money
Tithing is ten percent, and paying it is a condition of the temple recommend. That means it decides whether you may attend your own child's sealing. The genealogy section records that the Church stopped publishing financial statements to U.S. members in 1959. A whistleblower revealed a reserve fund of more than $100 billion in 2019. You are asked for a tenth of your income. Do you know where to read the books? The page's answer is that you can't.

## for-you cost
Picture a father waiting in the temple lobby while his daughter is sealed, because he doesn't hold a recommend. That scene opens this page's "day inside," and for many families it is the real cost of leaving, or of simply not paying. In the Mormon corridor, leaving can also cost friendships and business. If a sibling of yours stopped believing, would they still be invited in to the wedding?

## for-you who-gets-hurt
The table's first row is children in worthiness interviews, questioned alone about sexual matters by an untrained volunteer adult who is also a neighbor and the family's ecclesiastical leader. If you are a parent, you can ask to sit in, or decline on your child's behalf. What do you think would happen if you did? The second row is survivors whose disclosures are routed through a legal-liability helpline. Who is that helpline built to protect?

## q1
why: If the members who funded a portfolio learned its size from a whistleblower and a regulator, then the Church's own reporting did not tell them. The question asks why disclosure took outside force, and whether anyone was held responsible.
example: The law table records the SEC's finding: the portfolio was hidden behind 13 shell companies from 1997 to 2019, with penalties of $4m and $1m in 2023 [1][2]. The glance table notes "no resignation."

## q2
why: Tying a spiritual principle to a payment makes the payment the gate to a family's most important ceremony. The question asks whether that link is spiritual, or financial.
example: The money table records that tithing is linked directly to temple recommend worthiness, so that access to family sealings depends on payment. The day inside shows a father waiting in the lobby during his own daughter's sealing.

## q3
why: Any other institution that works with children has safeguarding rules against one-to-one questioning about sex. The question asks the Church to meet the standard any school or sports club would.
example: The structure section records worthiness interviews of minors, historically with sexually explicit questions. The who-gets-hurt table adds that the interviewer is an untrained volunteer who is also the family's ecclesiastical leader.

## q4
why: If material called lies later appears in the Church's own publications, the people disciplined for raising it were right before the institution was. The question asks whether the institution has said so.
example: The say-do table records that material once called anti-Mormon propaganda was published by the Church itself from 2013 onward. The history section records the excommunication of the "September Six" historians and intellectuals in 1993.

## q5
why: A policy announced as revelation and reversed four years later left real families in between. The question asks who accounted to them.
example: The genealogy section records the 2015 policy excluding children of same-sex couples from baptism, reversed in 2019, "without accounting to the families harmed in between."

## q6
why: The first call after a disclosure decides whose interests are protected. The question asks who a call to a Church attorney protects, and who a call to the police protects.
example: The structure table lists the abuse-reporting helpline as risk management, staffed by Church-retained attorneys, "who gets called before the police." The history section records litigation over the helpline from 2018 onward.
