## for-you structure
Sunni Islam has no pope, and that is real. But the page follows the question of who decides who counts as a scholar, and finds that the trail "ends at a ministry almost every time." The Grand Imam of al-Azhar was appointed by Egypt's president, and the 2014 constitution makes him irremovable. When you follow a fatwa, do you know who appointed the person who issued it, and who pays him?

## for-you law
Sunni Islam has no single legal system; the state supplies one. In Saudi Arabia a leading scholar was arrested in 2017 after declining to publicly back the state's position on Qatar, and prosecutors later sought the death penalty. In Malaysia the Federal Court ruled in 2007 that only a sharia court could recognize a woman's leaving Islam. In the UK you have full civil rights, but your marriage may not be civilly registered. Do you know whether yours is? Is your wife's or your daughter's?

## for-you money
On top of the giving every Muslim shares, the page adds state salaries for clerics, Gulf endowments for institutions abroad, and celebrity-preacher media economies. The say-do table records that states seized the endowments that once funded independent scholarship and replaced them with salaries. When a scholar or a mosque abroad is funded by a foreign government, that is information you are entitled to. Does your mosque tell you who funds it?

## for-you cost
The page records that community and family enforcement usually exceeds anything institutional. Leaving, or simply becoming more liberal, can cost marriage prospects, family standing and, in diaspora communities, a person's entire social world. If someone in your family started asking questions, would they come to you, or would they hide it from you? What does your answer say about the cost here?

## for-you who-gets-hurt
The table names women (under guardianship over marriage, mosque exclusion by architecture, and counsel to endure domestic violence), boys in hifz-only schooling who reach adulthood without literacy or an employable credential, independent scholars, Sufi and minority communities, and girls withdrawn from school at puberty. If you are a parent choosing a madrasa, ask: will my son also learn to read, write and count well enough to earn a living if he ever wants to?

## q1
why: This is the load-bearing question of the page. Every finding about state-controlled religious authority depends on it. If a scholar's salary comes from a government, what test could a believer use to tell a ruling from a policy?
example: The say-do table sets "Scholars are independent of rulers" beside the record: colonial and modern states seized the endowments that funded independent scholarship and replaced them with salaries. The cases section records Salman al-Awda, arrested in 2017 after declining to back a state position.

## q2
why: Education that leaves a graduate employable only by religious institutions is not free in the way it is presented. It makes the student dependent on one employer for life. The question is structural; it is not a claim about any single school's intent.
example: The genealogy section records that where hifz-only curricula displace literacy, numeracy and employable skills, "the charity becomes a trap." The who-gets-hurt table adds that this "makes exit financially impossible."

## q3
why: This question can be answered from inside the fiqh tradition itself. If one of the four recognized schools permits it, a community's refusal is a communal choice, not a requirement of the text.
example: The branches table records that the Hanafi school lets an adult woman contract her own marriage, "the reason this page treats guardianship as contested, not divine."

## q4
why: The long gap between scholarly condemnation and legal reform is itself evidence. It measures how much of the practice was about religious law, and how much was family custom in religious language.
example: The cases section records Qandeel Baloch, killed by her brother in 2016. He was sentenced to life, and in 2022 the Lahore High Court freed him after their parents pardoned him, despite the 2016 law.

## q5
why: Reporting obligations for child abuse exist in every jurisdiction this page covers. The question is whether religious schools are treated as exempt in practice.
example: The reach section records documented physical abuse and, in some residential madrasas, sexual abuse "with weak reporting pathways."

## q6
why: The closing question aims at the silencing mechanism itself. A rule that raising a problem "helps the enemies of Islam" ends the question before any evidence is examined.
example: The cases section records Nasr Hamid Abu Zayd, a Cairo University professor whom a court declared an apostate for his scholarship on the Qur'an, and ordered separated from his wife in 1995. The legal route used was a suit on behalf of public morals.
