## for-you structure
No one speaks for Hinduism. The four Shankaracharya seats carry enormous prestige and no command, and the page treats that decentralization as genuinely freeing. It also says decentralization can be "a deniability structure": when nobody is in charge, nobody takes responsibility for what doctrine sanctified. The unanswered question is exactly that. If a practice in your family harms someone, who in your tradition could you ask to stop it, and who would take responsibility if it didn't?

## for-you law
Much of this page is Indian constitutional law. Article 17 abolished untouchability in 1950, yet dowry deaths still numbered 6,450 in 2022. The Supreme Court struck down Sabarimala's exclusion of women of menstruating age in 2018. If you live in the US, Seattle banned caste discrimination in employment in 2023, and California's governor vetoed a statewide ban. If you are a manager in a company with South Asian staff, did you know caste discrimination can happen at work, and that in most places no law names it?

## for-you money
India's richest temples hold wealth that is hard to picture. Tirupati's own 2022 white paper declared a net worth of ₹2.26 lakh crore and 10.25 tonnes of gold on deposit. Many large temples are run by state-appointed boards, which means governments manage, and draw on, temple wealth. When you make an offering, do you know whether a state board or a private trust receives it, and whether you can read its accounts?

## for-you cost
There is no excommunication in Hinduism, and the say-do table says that is true institutionally and "irrelevant to the person disowned by their family for an inter-caste marriage." Caste works as an enforcement grid assigned at birth. If your son or daughter wanted to marry across caste, what would your family do? What would you do?

## for-you who-gets-hurt
Dalits come first in this table: the tradition's oldest and largest structural harm, compounded by landlessness and by local police aligned with dominant castes. Then women, facing dowry, menstrual exclusion, widow stigma and documented abuse by gurus. If you are not Dalit, ask yourself honestly: in your family's village, or your family, is there still a separate cup?

## q5
why: If a guru cannot be questioned, there is no mechanism for detecting a bad one until a court does it. The question asks what the tradition's own mechanism is.
example: The techniques section records that harder structures, including guru obedience, often appear only after a newcomer has encountered the tradition's gentler face. The cases section shows courts, not religious bodies, delivering the verdicts.

## q6
why: The claim that caste is culture, not religion, protects the religious texts from criticism. The question asks why the text that ranks people is treated as scripture, and the constitution that unranked them is not.
example: The history section records that the Dharmashastra literature, including the Manusmriti, gave caste "scriptural sanction," and that Ambedkar publicly burned a copy at Mahad in 1927.
