## for-you structure
David Miscavige has chaired the Religious Technology Center since 1987. The RTC holds the trademarks and "the tech" itself, which puts its chairman at the top regardless of who holds church presidencies. The glance table says no public record identifies anyone who could remove him, and in a structure this thoroughly lawyered, that absence is itself the finding. If you are a Scientologist, who would you go to if you believed the chairman was wrong?

## for-you law
If you sign a membership agreement, you may be signing away your right to sue. A federal appeals court enforced the church's religious arbitration against former members in 2021, while California's courts refused to do so for claims arising after members had left. In France, the Paris Celebrity Centre was convicted of organized fraud and fined €600,000. Before signing anything, would you read the arbitration clause, and would you know what it meant?

## for-you money
The glance table calls this the most explicitly priced spiritual path in the book: fixed fees for each auditing level, rising to hundreds of thousands of dollars at the upper levels, plus IAS fundraising on top. The questions section asks the obvious thing. If the tech works, why does the price rise as you become more competent rather than fall? If you have paid for a level, what did the next one cost, and who set that price?

## for-you cost
Disconnection is written policy: members must cut off anyone declared suppressive, including parents, spouses and children. People who left the Sea Org were historically billed "freeloader debt" for services received. If someone you love is a Scientologist and has stopped speaking to you, the page's say-do table records that this comes from the founder's policy letters, not from their personal choice. Does knowing that change how you feel about the silence?

## for-you who-gets-hurt
Sea Org members come first, some recruited as minors, working for decades at nominal pay under a billion-year contract and leaving with no outside credentials, contacts or savings. The day inside describes a man who signed at seventeen while his mother, who signed her own contract at nineteen, waited outside. If you know a teenager being recruited, what would you want them to know before they sign?
