Most people looking for what to do after leaving an abusive relationship open this book in the middle of something. A message just arrived. A court letter came. It's 2am and the house is too quiet. So the book starts with you, not with chapter one.

Pick the line that fits. You can pick a different one next week.

=== if-youre-not-safe-right-now
title: If you're not safe right now
question: Where do I go for domestic abuse help today?
seo: Domestic Abuse Help: Where to Go Right Now
short: If you are in danger now, call your emergency number. For domestic abuse help that isn't an emergency, a domestic abuse helpline can talk you through what to do next. They are free and confidential, and many are open all hours. You don't need to be sure it's serious enough to call.
---
If someone is hurting you, threatening you, or outside your door, call your emergency number now. Nothing in this book matters more than that call.

You don't need to be sure it's "bad enough." Call handlers would much rather hear from you early.

## Emergency numbers
- United States and Canada: 911
- United Kingdom: 999. If you can't speak, call 999 from a mobile, then press 55 when the recording asks. This tells the operator you need help. It doesn't share your location, so say where you are if you can. It doesn't work from a landline.
- Australia: 000
- Ireland: 112 or 999
- Anywhere else: search "[your country] emergency number" now, and save it in your phone under a plain name.

## Domestic abuse helplines
These lines are free and confidential. They can help you plan, find a refuge or shelter, and explain your options where you live.

- **United States:** National Domestic Violence Hotline, 1-800-799-7233, or text START to 88788.
- **United Kingdom:** National Domestic Abuse Helpline (England), 0808 2000 247. Scotland: 0800 027 1234. Wales: Live Fear Free, 0808 80 10 800. Men: Men's Advice Line, 0808 801 0327. LGBT+ people: Galop, 0800 999 5428. Honour-based abuse and forced marriage: Karma Nirvana, 0800 5999 247.
- **Canada:** find your nearest shelter at sheltersafe.ca, or call 211 to be connected to local services.
- **Australia:** 1800RESPECT, 1800 737 732.
- **Ireland:** Women's Aid, 1800 341 900. Men's Aid, 01 554 3811.

Anywhere else, search: "[your country] domestic abuse helpline." The national charity will come up near the top.

## What happens when you call a helpline
A trained person answers. They won't judge you, and they won't tell you to leave or to stay. They'll ask whether you're safe to talk, then listen.

You don't have to give your name. You don't have to know what you want. You can say, "I don't know if this counts," and they'll take it from there. Most people who call say something like that.

They can help you think through what happens next. That might be a safety plan, a refuge or shelter, legal help, or someone to talk to again next week. If you need to stop, hang up. You can call back whenever you want.

## If you can't speak or can't call
- **United States:** in many areas you can text 911. If it isn't available where you are, you'll get a message back telling you to call. The National Domestic Violence Hotline also has live chat on its website.
- **United Kingdom:** emergencySMS lets you text 999, but you have to register first. Text "register" to 999 now, while it's quiet, so it works when you need it. The National Domestic Abuse Helpline has live chat at set hours.
- **Canada:** text service for 911 is for people who are Deaf, hard of hearing or speech-impaired, and they register in advance.
- **Australia:** the National Relay Service connects calls for people who can't hear or speak on the phone. 1800RESPECT has online chat.
- **Anywhere:** if you call and can't talk, stay on the line. Tap the phone, cough, or answer yes and no questions by pressing keys if asked.

## If you have to leave right now
Take only what you can carry without being noticed:

- your phone and charger
- keys
- any cash and one bank card
- your ID, if you can reach it quickly
- medicine you need today
- the children, and one comfort item each

Everything else can wait. Documents can be replaced and things can be collected later, often with a police escort. Go to a public place, a friend, or a neighbour, then call.

## If they're at the door
- Don't open it.
- Call your emergency number. Say: "My ex-partner is outside my door. I'm frightened. I have [a protective order / children here]."
- Move to a room with a lock and a way out, away from the kitchen, with your phone.
- If they get in, get out if you can. Your safety comes before your things and your home.

Afterwards, write down what happened, with times. [What to document and how](ch:what-to-document-and-how) shows how.

## If you're thinking about harming yourself
Call a crisis line now:

- United States and Canada: call or text 988
- United Kingdom and Ireland: Samaritans, 116 123
- Australia: Lifeline, 13 11 14

Then read [When it's more than aftermath](ch:when-its-more-than-aftermath), which was written for this moment.

## If this device isn't private
The **Leave** button at the top of every page blanks this page at once. Press **Esc** on a keyboard to do the same. The browser's back button can still bring the book back, so close the tab when you safely can.

A private or incognito window keeps this book out of your history. Browsers can still be monitored in other ways, so if someone has had access to your phone, use a phone they've never touched: a friend's, a work phone, a library computer. [Surveillance and technology](ch:surveillance-and-technology) explains how to check.

## What to do next
When the danger has passed, make a plan for the next time. [A safety plan for after you've left](ch:a-safety-plan-for-after-youve-left) walks through it, and [Find help](ch:find-help) has more numbers and the words to search for local services.

*The Sovereign Divine Feminine* has a short version for the hardest moments: [The 30-Minute Rescue](/feminine/the-30-minute-rescue#top).

=== how-to-use-this-book
title: How to use this book, and what it is not
question: What will this post-separation abuse book do, and what won't it do?
seo: The Long After: A Free Post-Separation Abuse Book
short: The Long After is a free post-separation abuse book for anyone who has left a partner, a marriage or a controlling family and found that leaving wasn't the end. Read it in any order. It is not therapy, not legal advice and not a timeline.
---
This is a post-separation abuse book: it is for the part after you leave. You left a partner, a marriage, or a family that controlled you. Maybe it was last week. Maybe it was eight years ago and a chapter here still lands. Either way, something kept going after the door closed, and you want to understand it and deal with it.

## How it works
Every chapter stands alone. Most people arrive in the middle, from a search, at a bad moment. So each chapter opens with **the short answer**: the plain version, in a few lines. Everything under it's the detail.

The headings are the questions people ask. If you came here wondering "does it ever end?" or "is this a crime?", there's a heading for that.

When you need a tool rather than a chapter, the [Toolbox](ch:the-record) has things to print or copy: a four-line record, a safety plan, a device check, a court-day list, and replies you can send.

If you don't know where to start, go to [Where are you right now?](ch:where-are-you-right-now).

## How each chapter is built
Every chapter has the same frame, so you always know where you are:

- **The short answer.** A few lines at the top. If that's all you read, you'll still have the main thing.
- **Questions as headings.** Each heading is a question people ask. Scan them and skip to the one that's yours.
- **"Say" boxes.** Words you can use, in messages, on the phone or face to face. Change them to sound like you.
- **Links that open the right page.** A phrase in colour takes you to the chapter that covers it. Links to the *Pattern Decoder* open the page for that tactic. Links to *The Sovereign Divine Feminine* open the exact passage.
- **Honest checks.** In some chapters, a short set of private questions marked *An honest check, just for you*. They appear where hurt, longing or anger can bend how things look. They're about what's in your hands now, never about blame for what was done to you. Nobody sees your answers.
- **Sources.** At the bottom of every chapter, listed plainly, so you or an advocate can check them.
- **Next, if this is you.** Two or three pages that usually come next for someone in your place.

## If reading is hard right now
After abuse, many people find it hard to read for long. Concentration goes. Words slide off the page. That's common, and it usually comes back.

- **Read the short answers only.** Every chapter works at that level.
- **Use [The card](ch:the-card).** It's the whole book in a few lines.
- **Read with someone.** A friend can read a chapter aloud, or read it first and tell you the part you need.
- **Print one page.** Paper can be easier than a screen, and you can underline it. Keep it somewhere private.
- **Stop when you've had enough.** The book doesn't need finishing.

## The words this book uses
- **"The person you left," "your ex," "the other parent."** These all mean the same person. The book uses whichever fits the sentence.
- **"Abuse."** This book uses the word for patterns of control, fear and punishment, with or without violence. Most legal definitions now do the same.
- **"Survivor" and "victim."** The book uses neither much. You don't have to call yourself anything.
- **Gender.** Abuse happens to people of every gender, from people of every gender. The examples change from page to page on purpose.

## What this book is not
**It isn't therapy.** If you need steady support from someone trained, a book can't replace that. [Finding a therapist who understands this](ch:finding-a-therapist-who-understands-this) explains what to look for.

**It isn't legal advice.** Laws differ by country, and often by state or region. Every legal chapter tells you what a thing is, gives examples from a few countries, and tells you exactly what to search to find your local version.

**It isn't a timeline.** No page here tells you how you ought to feel by six months, or a year, or five. Any number like that would be wrong for a lot of the people reading it. People heal at different speeds, and the speed says nothing about how well you're doing.

**It isn't a verdict on whether you're "over it."** There's no test to pass. [What "recovered" means](ch:what-recovered-actually-means) offers a working definition, and it isn't a finish line.

**It will never describe ways to harm yourself.** If you're in crisis, [When it's more than aftermath](ch:when-its-more-than-aftermath) is written for you. You don't need to read anything else first.

## Who wrote it, and how it's checked
The Long After is written by Shae Stovell as part of a free library. Every chapter lists its sources. Where experts disagree, the chapter says so. Where the book is giving its own judgment, it says that too. [What this book might have wrong](ch:what-this-book-might-have-wrong) collects the weak spots in one place, and [Every source in this book](ch:all-sources) lists all of the sources together.

## Keeping it private
Nothing you do here is saved. There are no accounts and no tracking of what you read. The **Leave** button at the top clears the page instantly. If someone might check your phone, a private window helps, and [Surveillance and technology](ch:surveillance-and-technology) explains the rest.

## Sharing this book safely
You can send any page to someone by copying its address. Each chapter has its own.

- **To a friend who's in it:** send one chapter, not the whole book. [What post-separation abuse is](ch:what-is-post-separation-abuse) or [Hoovering](ch:hoovering) are good first pages. Think about who else reads their messages.
- **To someone helping you:** [Helping someone in year one](ch:helping-someone-in-year-one) was written to be forwarded.
- **To a professional:** [For professionals](ch:for-professionals) explains how the book can be shared with clients.
- **In print:** every page prints cleanly, with its sources.

## If you print pages
Paper is easy to find. Keep printed pages somewhere the person you left can't reach: at work, with a friend, or in a folder that doesn't look like what it is. Shred what you don't need.
