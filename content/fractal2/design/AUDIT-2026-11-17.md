## 2. The ending blocks

### Section 10, the window (717 to 774)

What is on screen (720, 728, 735, 745, 750): a 3 by 10 grid of same-size dark rectangles, each with a technique name in small serif type, packed edge to edge from the top of the phone screen down to just above the caption. Behind them a ringed glow. As the voice speaks, tiles fade or slide off. At 757.9, on "My original frequency.", the whole frame goes white (760). By 770 it is a lone sun on black.

What is wrong:
1. **It is a UI grid, not a stack.** The script says blocks "stacked against the window". The build tiles them in rows and columns of equal size, with even gutters and centered labels. That is a tag cloud, a keyboard, a settings screen. Nothing is stacked, nothing has weight, nothing sits on anything.
2. **There is no window.** No frame, no opening, no glass, no wall. The metaphor the scene is named for is missing.
3. **Thirty labels at once.** The labels are about 9 CSS px on the phone. Thirty unreadable names compete with the caption. It is noise, and it repeats what table 1 and table 4 already said.
4. **Basalt is wasted.** It is applied per block at tile scale, offset and turned so "no two blocks share a face". At that size the stone is just dark grain. The plate never reads as stone, and the offsets break any sense of one wall cut into blocks.
5. **The payoff is hidden, then burned.** The reason the scene exists is to reveal the light original (`frequency-8`) behind the dark. It sits behind opaque tiles until they are mostly gone, then the PEAK3 bloom whites out the frame (760). The line "My original frequency." lands on a featureless white blob. Then the twin is faded away in 4.5 s because, per deviation 6, it "read as a flat cream dish". The fix for a flat plate was to remove it rather than light it.
6. **Caption contrast risk.** At 760 the light caption sits on a bright cream glow. It reads in the frame only because the type is large. It very likely fails 4.5:1. No check caught it.
7. **The tiles crowd the caption.** On the phone the lowest row ends a few pixels above the caption block (745).

### Section 11, the map (774 to 1120)

What is on screen:
- 775 to about 840: the owner's map returns and the combination threads run through it (778, 800, 820). This part is good.
- About 846 to 1095 (about 250 s, over four minutes): a single sun on black. A loop of chain links appears around it at times (900, 950, 1000) and shrinks (950). Otherwise frames 850, 1040, 1060, 1080 are the same picture: one dot and a caption.
- 1097 to 1120: the map and the ring of thirty dots return with the Enter button (1110). Good.

What is wrong: the script's "The map recedes. One thread remains, close, wrapped around the light" was taken literally and stretched across the most emotional four minutes of the film. No plate appears in section 11 at all. The owner gave eleven images and none is used here. The viewer stares at the same dot for longer than the whole of sections 9 and 10 together. The chain loop is the only event, and it is a thin white outline that reads as clip art.

## 3. Root causes

Each choice is steelmanned first, then judged.

1. **Plates as low-opacity textures (0.15 to 0.5) under one dominant light.**
   Steelman: the film has one visual idea, a single light in the dark; textures keep that idea pure, keep captions readable, and avoid a slideshow feel.
   Verdict: **does not stand.** The owner asked to see his images. A plate at 0.15 to 0.38 on black under a bloom is not an image, it is a grade. Strength must be 0.8 to 1.0 when a plate is the hero.

2. **Scaling plates past the frame (mirror 1.4x, rings 5.6, city 16x zoom, dust 1.25x).**
   Steelman: no visible edges, so the plate feels like a world rather than a picture in a box.
   Verdict: **does not stand** for mirror, rings, city. Each plate's identity is its whole shape: the mirror's tarnished frame, the full disc of rings, the city's red core inside blue inside gold. Cropping removes exactly what makes them recognizable. Keep the overscan only for dust.

3. **The sun drawn on the plate's center.**
   Steelman: the light is the film's protagonist; it must be in every frame.
   Verdict: **does not stand as built.** The center of fractal-8 (the hollow with a gold point), frequency-8 (the soft glow), city (red core) and rings is exactly where the bloom sits, so the most recognizable part is burned out. During a plate hero hold, cap the bloom radius inside the plate's innermost band (0.157 of the plate half width for the twins) and drop sun intensity to 40 percent.

4. **"The owner's map stays the picture in sections 2, 3, 6 tables and 11" applied to everything.**
   Steelman: it is the owner's own instruction, and the map is the real subject.
   Verdict: **the instruction stands; its spread does not.** It names specific beats. The build also put the map over the city in section 5, shrank the twins inside the map in tables 3 and 4, and left section 11's middle four minutes empty rather than "not a table". Sections 1, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, and the non-table stretches of 6 and 11 are free for the plates.

5. **Twins used only as masks: an annulus in table 3, a horizontal half at 34 percent in table 4, a disc behind the sun at the STOP (deviations 3, 4, 5).**
   Steelman: the twins register band for band, so the shader can swap rings in place, which is clever and keeps one map on screen.
   Verdict: **does not stand as the only use.** Clever registration that no viewer can see is not a design. The masked uses can stay as secondary touches, but the pair needs one full, side-by-side, hero moment.

6. **Short windows.** SEAM4 is about 5 s with 3 s at full. Each table 3 band shows for well under a second before the squash.
   Verdict: **does not stand.** A new image needs at least 6 s fully visible to be read, 8 s if it is the hero.

7. **Deviation 6: the light twin faded out in 4.5 s after PEAK3 because it read as a flat cream dish.**
   Steelman: the observation is correct; frequency-8 is flat (spec 5.1 says so).
   Verdict: **does not stand.** The answer to a flat plate is lighting (a radial grade and the light's falloff), not deletion. It is the "My original frequency" image. It must be held.

8. **Deviation 7: the old `frequency` plate at 15 percent in section 9; `frequency-8` kept only for "matched comparisons".**
   Verdict: **does not stand.** Both light plates ended up invisible. A light plate cannot be shown at low opacity on black; it turns grey. Show it at full strength in its own part of the screen, away from the caption.

9. **The window as a tile grid, basalt per tile.**
   Steelman: thirty named blocks honor "the thirty inversions"; the grid is orderly and legible in code.
   Verdict: **does not stand.** See section 2. Rebuild.

10. **Section 11 middle as a lone light.**
    Steelman: intimacy; the voice turns to direct address, so the picture steps back.
    Verdict: **does not stand at 250 s.** Stepping back for 20 s is a choice. For four minutes it is an empty screen.

11. **Gates that measure presence.** The 101 checks confirmed that textures loaded and that windows had non-zero opacity. None compared a rendered frame with the plate.
    Verdict: **the process failure behind all of the above.** See section 5.

12. **Nobody held the frames next to the plates.** The build was checked against its own spec, and the spec had drifted from the owner's ask ("I want to see my images") to "the plates are wired in".

## 4. Fix specification, ranked by visual value

Conventions. Times are film seconds; anchors by caption text from `cues.json` win over the numbers if the clock moves. "Short side" = min(W, H) of the viewport. Desktop: caption column is x from 0.595 W (`F.COL`) to the right edge; the plate stage is the left part, centered at x = 0.30 W, y = 0.50 H. Phone: captions sit about 0.60 H to 0.80 H; the plate stage is centered at x = 0.50 W, y = 0.34 H. Hero strength means the plate is drawn at that opacity with no extra darkening gain, the sun capped (rule 3 above). Every hero plate gets a 2 percent per second slow push (scale 1.00 to 1.04 over the hold) so it is never a frozen still.

Caption contrast rule for every scene below. Caption ink is about #EDE6D8 (relative luminance about 0.79). For 4.5:1 the background behind every caption glyph box, measured with the caption hidden, must have p95 relative luminance at or below 0.13. Dark plates (fractal-8, city, rings, mirror, shaft, sky) pass if their area under the caption box is graded down by a scrim: linear gradient, transparent at 0.52 H to #07060A at 0.70 opacity at 0.60 H on phone; on desktop a left-to-right gradient, transparent at 0.55 W to 0.70 opacity at 0.595 W. Light plates (frequency, frequency-8) must never sit under a caption: desktop, disc right edge at or left of 0.57 W; phone, disc bottom edge at or above 0.56 H, with the scrim below it.

### Rank 1. Section 10, the window, rebuilt around frequency-8 (717 to 774)

Concept: a real window, bricked up with basalt. The light original is behind it. Blocks come out one at a time; the light comes through the holes; when the last block goes, the owner's light twin is fully seen and held.

- **Wall and window.** Fill the frame with one continuous basalt surface (the plate drawn at cover scale 1.0, strength 0.85, one texture, no per-block offsets). Cut a window opening: desktop, a rectangle 0.44 W wide by 0.78 H tall, centered at (0.30 W, 0.50 H), with an arched top (semicircle of the opening's width); phone, 0.86 W wide by 0.50 H tall, centered at (0.50 W, 0.32 H), same arch. The surround is a 0.018 short side deep reveal, lit on the side facing the sun.
- **What is behind the glass:** `frequency-8` at strength 1.0, disc diameter equal to the opening width times 0.96, centered in the opening, not visible yet.
- **The blocks.** Thirty basalt blocks fill the opening as a dry-stacked wall in courses, bottom up: 6 courses on desktop (5 blocks each), 8 courses on phone (3 to 5 each), block widths varying 0.6x to 1.5x of the course average so joints are staggered like real masonry. Every block samples the SAME basalt texture at its own position (so the wall reads as one stone cut into pieces), plus a 1 px top highlight and a 2 px dark joint. No labels on resting blocks.
- **Light through the joints.** From 717, a thin line of warm light (#FFE7B8, 0.35 opacity) shows in every joint, so the wall is visibly holding back light. This is the first moment of the scene and replaces the grid.
- **Lifting.** One block per beat in the existing `bkLeave(k)` order, top course first. Each lift: the block's technique name appears engraved on its face (MSDF, small caps, 16 CSS px minimum, dark ink with a 1 px light lower edge) for 0.5 s, then the block slides 0.03 short side toward the camera and drops out of frame downward over 0.9 s with a soft shadow. Through the hole, that patch of `frequency-8` appears at full strength and a light shaft (additive cone, 0.25 opacity, length 0.4 short side) points toward the viewer. Only the lifting block is labeled. Never more than two labels on screen.
- **PEAK3 (756.8, "and let the light through").** Last block leaves at PEAK3 minus 1.0 s. The window now shows the whole `frequency-8` disc. Bloom: a single 0.8 s swell to 1.4x sun intensity, but radius capped at the plate's band 1 edge (0.157 of plate half width). Never whites out the frame.
- **The hold (757.9 to 774), "My original frequency. Who I am underneath all of it. Pulsing quietly, and waiting."** The wall and window frame dissolve (0 to 1 over 2.0 s from 757.9); `frequency-8` moves to the plate stage center and scales to diameter 0.62 short side on desktop (left stage) and 0.86 W on phone (top stage) over 3.0 s, ease in-out. Strength 1.0. Light it so it is not flat: multiply by a radial grade, 1.10 at center to 0.82 at the rim, plus the light's falloff. Pulse plus 5 percent brightness on "Pulsing" (one 1.2 s pulse at that caption's t0 + 0.4). Hold until 774. Caption stays in its column (desktop) or below the disc on the scrim (phone).
- **Into section 11 (774 to 777, "I came back up carrying a map.").** `frequency-8` crossfades into the owner's map in registration (reuse the table 3 radial remap of the owner's ring radii onto the plate's radii), band by band from the center out, over 2.5 s, so the light original visibly becomes the map. This is the "the light rises; the map assembles" beat.
- **Remove:** the 3 x 10 tile grid, per-block basalt offsets, the all-white bloom frame, the 4.5 s fade-out of the light twin.

## 5. New machine checks

All checks render real frames (the same renderer as the previews) at both 780 x 1688 and 1440 x 900, at 0.5 s steps through each plate window. Each scene in the fix spec gets a role in a table: `hero`, `pair`, or `texture`, with its minimum hold.

1. **Plate recognizability (per frame).** For a hero plate, warp the plate image to its expected screen footprint (center, scale, rotation from the `PL.A0` values for that frame), unlit. Render the frame with captions hidden. Downsample both to 256 px on the long side, grayscale. Compute SSIM over the footprint and the normalized cross-correlation of Sobel edge magnitude. A frame passes when SSIM is at least 0.50 and edge NCC is at least 0.45. Texture plates use edge NCC at least 0.20 only.
2. **Hold time.** For each hero plate, the longest run of consecutive passing frames must be at least the role's hold (hero 8 s, pair 7 s with both discs passing in the same frames, STOP split 4 s). Fails the build, no warning level.
3. **Footprint coverage.** The fraction of the plate's own image (its full disc or full rectangle) that falls inside the viewport during the hold must be at least 0.90 for hero plates. This catches mirror at 1.4x, rings at 5.6, city at 16x.
4. **Effective strength.** Per pixel in the footprint: layer opacity times gain times (1 minus the coverage of anything drawn above it: map, blocks, bloom, words). Mean at least 0.75 for hero, at least 0.25 for texture. This is computed from the shader inputs, so it also runs without rendering.
5. **Bloom burn.** In any hero footprint, no more than 10 percent of pixels may have luminance at or above 0.95 (0 to 1). Whole-frame check everywhere: no frame may have more than 35 percent of pixels at or above 0.90. This catches the white frame at 760.
6. **Empty-screen ledger.** Mark a frame "empty" when, outside a disc of radius 0.08 short side around the sun and outside caption boxes, mean luminance is below 0.03 and no plate or map passes check 1 at the texture level. No run of empty frames may exceed 20 s, except section 1's first 10 s. This catches 846 to 1095.
7. **Owner image ledger.** For each of the ten plates: seconds passing check 1 at its role level. Print the table in the gate output and fail if any plate tagged hero is under its hold, or if basalt is not recognized as stone (edge NCC at least 0.30 over the wall area) for at least 10 s.
8. **Caption contrast on the real frame.** For every caption, at its fully readable moment, render with the caption hidden, take each glyph box's background, compute p95 relative luminance, and require a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 against the caption ink. Light plates must also keep their footprint out of the caption column (desktop x at or beyond 0.595 W) and caption band (phone).
9. **Grid smell for the blocks.** From the block layout data: fail if 12 or more blocks share a width within 5 percent, or if 3 or more column edges align across 4 or more rows (that is a grid, not masonry). Fail if more than 2 block labels are visible in one frame, or if any label is under 16 CSS px.
10. **Contact sheet gate.** Write one sheet per scene: the plate on the left, the frame at the middle of its hold on the right, for both viewports. The gate fails until a reviewer has marked each pair "seen" in a sidecar file. Presence checks alone can no longer pass a build.
