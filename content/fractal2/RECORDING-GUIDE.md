# Recording the Fractal² narration — a plain guide

You are recording your own voice reading `content/fractal2/narration-script.md`. It is about 42 minutes when finished, in 12 short parts. You do not need a studio. A phone and a quiet closet are enough.

## 1. What to record with

**Best simple option: your phone.**
- iPhone: the **Voice Memos** app. First go to Settings → Voice Memos → Audio Quality → **Lossless**.
- Android: the built-in **Recorder** app (or "Easy Voice Recorder"). In its settings choose **WAV** or the highest quality offered.

**If you own a USB microphone**, use it with any recorder on your computer (QuickTime on a Mac: File → New Audio Recording, quality **Maximum**).

Do not use a headset microphone, earbuds, or a video call app. They thin the voice.

## 2. Where to record

- The smallest, softest room you have. **A closet full of clothes is ideal.** A bed with a duvet over your head and the phone also works, and nobody will know.
- Not a kitchen, bathroom or empty room. Hard walls add echo, and echo cannot be removed afterwards.
- Turn off anything that hums: fridge nearby, fan, air conditioning, computer fans. Close the windows. Put the phone in **Airplane Mode** so nothing buzzes.
- Record at a quiet time of day. If a car passes or a dog barks, stop, wait, and say the sentence again.

## 3. How to hold it

- Phone **about a hand-span from your mouth** (15 to 20 cm, 6 to 8 inches).
- **Slightly to the side**, pointed at the corner of your mouth, not straight in front. This stops the popping on P and B sounds.
- Rest the phone on something soft (a folded towel on a shelf) instead of holding it. Hands make noise.
- Stay the same distance for the whole session. If you stop for the day, take a photo of your setup so you can match it tomorrow.
- Read from a second screen or printed pages. If you print, do not turn pages while speaking.

## 4. Before each session

- Drink water. Avoid milk and coffee right before; they make the mouth click.
- Stand, or sit upright. The voice carries more weight standing.
- **Record 10 seconds of silence first**, in the room, saying nothing. Keep it as its own file called `room-tone`. It is used to make the joins between parts invisible.
- Say one test sentence, play it back on headphones, and check: no echo, no hum, no distortion when you get loud. If loud parts crackle, move the phone a little further away.

## 5. How to read it

- **Slower than feels natural.** About 138 words a minute. If it feels slightly too slow while you are doing it, it is right.
- `[breath]` means take a real breath. `[pause 2]` means count two seconds of silence in your head. The silences matter: the pictures on the page move during them.
- **If you stumble: stop, wait two seconds, and say the whole sentence again.** Do not start the part over. The mistake is cut out afterwards.
- The small numbers at the start of each line (`012`) are not read. They are for syncing.
- **Section VI ("Why I can draw this") is your own account.** Read it exactly as written, plainly. Do not add to it and do not perform it. If it is hard to read, record it last, alone, and take as many tries as you need. Keep every try; choose later.
- The combinations section (Part seven) is the heart. Give it room. The list of six steps should sound like someone counting, not announcing.

## 7. How to send the files

1. Put all the files in **one folder in your Google Drive** named `Fractal2 narration`. Include `room-tone`.
2. Do **not** convert, trim, compress or "enhance" them. Send exactly what the recorder made (`.m4a` or `.wav`).
3. Do not send them through a messaging app; those shrink audio. Drive, or a direct upload in the chat, keeps the quality.
4. In a new chat, say: **"The narration files are in my Drive folder Fractal2 narration."**

## 8. What happens next

- The parts are cleaned gently (hum and clicks only; your voice is not altered), levelled to the same loudness, and joined with room tone.
- Each line is matched to its place on the page, so the page follows your voice and the captions come from the page's own words.
- You get a private preview to listen to before anything is published. Nothing goes live until you say so.
