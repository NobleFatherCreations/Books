## The floor — outranks everything else

- You do not owe anyone certainty before you protect yourself.
- If someone is using calibration to make you doubt an accusation you made about them, that is evidence.
- In danger — violence, a child at risk, isolation, leaving made impossible, physical fear — stop weighing. Act, then understand.

## The gradient

::: gradient
- rude
- thoughtless
- inconsiderate
- unkind
- harmful
- controlling
- abusive
:::

The hinge is freedom: does this hurt, or does it reduce the moves available to you?

## The two-sided question

1. If I am wrong that this is manipulation, what does that cost me?
2. If I am wrong that this is innocent, what does that cost me?

The larger number moves your threshold, not your conclusion.

## Two base rates

- **Wide** — how common is this in people generally. For strangers, colleagues, the world.
- **Local** — how common is this in *this room*. For the environment you are assessing.

Name which room you are in before you read the signal.

## Before you trust your own reading

- **Whose voice does this alarm sound like?** Name the era. A mismatch between the size of your reaction and the size of the event is the clearest sign you are reading history, not the present.
- **Sensor or smoke alarm?** Can you name what set it off, in specifics? If ten minutes produces only atmosphere, it is a smoke alarm — worth noticing, not yet worth acting on beyond caution.
- **Investigation that never reaches a verdict** is grief wearing analysis’s clothes, not analysis.
- **Revise memory by memory** after a revelation, not wholesale — one new fact rarely reaches your whole history at once.
- **Slept, eaten, frightened, alone with it, how long in this, what time is it?** No verdicts at 2am.

## Your reference sample

Healthy relationships contain regular conflict, disappointment, unresolved things, and periods of distance. What actually distinguishes them: things can be named, repair happens, “no” is survivable, your freedom is not decreasing. Grade the aftermath, not the trouble.

## The three explanations

For any behavior, generate one **hostile**, one **neutral**, one implicating **you or the situation**. Then ask: what observation would tell them apart?

## Four dimensions that turn an incident into a finding

1. **Frequency** — how many times, in what window.
2. **Duration** — over what span.
3. **Escalation** — growing, stable, or shrinking.
4. **Response to feedback** — what changed after it was named.

No pattern required for: violence or threat of it, sexual coercion, harm to a child, or a deception that changed the shape of your life.

## Before any test — is testing safe?

- Any history of violence or threats? Stop.
- What happened last time you refused something?
- What do they control — housing, money, status, children?
- Is the fear physical rather than social? You already have your answer.

## The naming test

Say the act, then the effect. No diagnosis, no motive. Then stop talking.

- **Healthy:** eventually engages the content — disputes, explains, apologises, returns to it.
- **Coercive:** attacks the naming. The subject becomes your memory, character, health, loyalty, or timing.
- **The real test is weeks later:** can you raise it a second time?

## Repair — the four components

1. Specific acknowledgment of the actual thing.
2. No conditions, no counter-charges.
3. Changed behavior over weeks.
4. No repayment demanded for having apologised.

## The cost of “no”

Refuse something small and reasonable. Watch for temperature, access, and framing — not for an argument. Track the pattern, not one reaction. If you cannot bring yourself to run it, that is the finding.

## Turning the instrument on yourself

- **Run the self-audit on the last six months.** When did you last go quiet at someone rather than say the thing? What do people close to you apologise for most often? Behavior, not intention — if the answer is nothing, you did not run it.
- **Clinical vocabulary ends arguments; it does not win them fairly.** Translate the accusation into plain observable description first. If it cannot be said in ordinary words, you may have a label, not a finding.
- **Who benefits from your verdict?** Certainty that rises without new evidence is applause, not proof. Find one person with no stake and tell them the whole thing.
- **Being wrong well:** name the specific accusation, withdraw it without hedging, do not perform the apology, accept their timetable.

## Assessment is not decision

“What I believe is ___, confidence ___.” “What I am going to do is ___, because ___.” Two sentences. Neither requires the other.

## Your own track record

Three lines, whenever you reach a real conclusion about someone: what you concluded, how sure (a percentage), what would show you wrong. Add a fourth line later — what actually happened. After eight or ten entries your own tilt appears, and it is worth more than any single test in this book, because it corrects every judgment you will ever make.

## Certainty is the tell

Check the **shape**, not the content: total explanatory coverage · nothing could disprove it · disagreement means you are asleep · confidence rising without evidence rising · doubt costs you your place. Applies to this book too.

=== limits
title: The limits of this instrument
short: What this book cannot do, where it fails, and what would change its mind.
---
A book that demands evidentiary standards and publishes none for itself fails its own test. So here is the honest accounting.

## What this book cannot do

**It cannot tell you whether a specific person is manipulating you.** It has never met them, and no framework applied at a distance can substitute for evidence you gather yourself. Anyone offering that certainty in a book is selling something.

**It cannot make you safe.** Calibration is a thinking skill. Safety is a logistics problem — money, housing, documents, distance, and people whose job this is. Chapter thirty-seven points at them and stops, because those answers are local and this book is not.

**It cannot diagnose anyone, including you.** Nothing here is clinical. The vocabulary this library uses is descriptive, not diagnostic, and chapter twenty-nine is about what happens when that line is crossed.

**It cannot resolve the unresolvable.** Some questions never close. Chapter thirty-three is an admission, not a technique.

## Where it is most likely to fail

**On someone currently inside a coercive situation.** This is the book’s central risk and it is real. Every argument here — consider alternatives, check your instrument, wait for a pattern — is available for misuse against exactly the reader who most needs to act. The safeguards in chapters two, twenty, thirty-six and thirty-seven exist because of this, and they may not be enough for every reader. If these chapters are making you slower and more apologetic rather than clearer, something has gone wrong and you should stop.

**On grief.** Chapter eight names the problem and cannot solve it. Analysis holding up a loss will not be dislodged by better analysis.

**Across cultures it does not know.** The norms in chapter twenty-six are illustrative, not exhaustive, and the book’s own assumptions about directness, apology, and emotional expression are not neutral.

**On institutions, at depth.** Chapter twenty-seven is an introduction. The real instrument for that is The Sacred Divide and its records toolkit.

## What would change this book’s mind

Stated plainly, so it can be held to them:

- If readers of the calibration material reliably became *slower to leave harmful situations* than readers of the recognition material alone, the central premise would be wrong and the book should not exist in this form.
- If the naming test turned out not to discriminate — if healthy and coercive responses to plain description were indistinguishable in practice — movement four would need rebuilding.
- If the four pattern dimensions failed to predict outcomes better than a single strong instance, chapter thirteen would be overstating its case.
- If the “three explanations” exercise were shown to function mainly as a rationalisation device rather than a discrimination device, chapter fifteen would need to go.

## What this book is confident about

Two things only. That recognition without calibration produces isolation, and that the aftermath of a harm — whether it can be named, whether repair happens, what a refusal costs — carries more information than the harm itself.

Everything else here is an instrument offered for testing, including by you.
