## 1. Voice (overrides V5 section 1)

**Owner:** "Think Shane Gillis esque." Use the register, never any comedian's material: dry, deadpan, observational, unbothered, an everyman who notices what people actually do and says it plainly. He laughs at the reader and at himself, never down at anyone hurting.
- **Humour has two forms only:**
  - **(a) Mirror lines:** the joke lands on the reader's own behaviour, so they recognise themselves and check themselves ("You've also blamed a bad week on someone's character. Probably this month.").
  - **(b) Jokes inside the argument**, as observations ("Manipulation needs planning and upkeep. Most people can't keep a houseplant alive.").
- **Owner, verbatim:** "the humor should be blunt but also point out a truth in a funny way, or bring the reader to self reflection and accountability." Every joke must carry a truth. If deleting it loses nothing, delete it.
- **Sections stop flat.** End on the last fact, the last action, or a mirror line. **Never** end a section or an example on a line that restates the lesson ("That's repair." "Fast was information." "A tactic survives it.").
- **Hard limits (unchanged, absolute):**
  - No humour in the three safety chapters (`this-book-in-the-wrong-hands`, `before-you-test-is-testing-safe`, `when-weighing-stops`), in any `::: warn` block, in the safety gate, or in any case involving violence, sexual abuse, self-harm or children at risk.
  - Never laugh at someone being harmed. No mirror line may suggest a victim caused their abuse.
  - No punching at a group (gender, culture, neurotype, class, religion, sexuality). No swearing.

**The owner's Human Voice and Anti-Slop Protocol applies to every sentence you write:**
- No negative parallelism ("It's not X, it's Y", "This isn't about X; it's about Y", "Less X, more Y"); state what the thing is.
- No ceremonial closers or fake profundity; stop when the point is made.
- No throat-clearing openers.
- Break rule-of-three habits: use one detail, two or four, unless there really are three things.
- No "-ing" tails ("…highlighting the importance of").
- No fractal summaries inside the body (the short answer, the tool or ask block and the quiz are the sanctioned repeats).
- **Banned words:** delve, tapestry, testament, landscape, paradigm, symphony, beacon, crucible, nuance/nuanced, multifaceted, pivotal, foster, elevate, unleash, unlock, harness, navigate, underscore, realm, embark, game-changer, cutting-edge, bustling, vibrant, resonate; moreover, furthermore, additionally, consequently, indeed, notably.
- Concrete over abstract (no dynamics, elements, aspects, factors, journey, space).
- Plain verbs. Vary sentence length. Take a stand: "tends to" only for research findings.
- Contractions by default; uncontracted only for emphasis.
- Show the behaviour; don't label it.
- Before handing back, rewrite any line that sounds like a LinkedIn post, a TED talk or a press release.

**Also remove:**
- the book praising itself ("the most economical test in the book");
- "isn't X. It's Y" sentences;
- stylistic triplets;
- "judgment": use "judgement", except inside Decoder link text, which keeps the Decoder's own name.

## 2. Retention: replace every `::: handle`

There are no more "X, not Y" slogans. Two formats, chosen by chapter:

**8 core chapters get a tool card:**

```
::: tool THE DIAL
When someone hurts you and you reach for "abuse" or "fine".
Ask yourself: did my freedom shrink, or just my mood?
:::
```

- **Line 1:** "When" plus the moment, in the reader's words, as it feels (not the concept).
- **Line 2:** "Ask yourself:" plus one question you can answer on the spot.

**Every other ordinary chapter gets one self-check:**

```
::: ask
Ask yourself: would you accept that excuse if they gave it?
:::
```

- One question, aimed at the reader at least half the time (accountability), answerable in the moment.
- **Placement (both formats):** where the chapter's idea lands, roughly where the handle sat. Never the last block of the chapter.
- **Recall:** add one item to the chapter's `::: sort` (or `::: check`): the situation as the prompt, the tool or question as the answer.
- **In the gradient chapter**, "dial" means only the seven-step scale; call freedom, repetition and response **"the three tests"**.
- **Shuffle every recall sort** whose answers run in order.

## 3. The worked examples (CAST.md is binding)
- **Rename** exactly as CAST.md says. New names must not appear in any other chapter's examples (`voice-check.py` fails on repeats). Don't invent rare or literary names for unnamed side characters; use everyday ones and check them.
- **Gender swaps:** follow each SWAP note. Keep the facts, the stakes and the outcome. Fix every pronoun.
- **Country-neutral:** no currency symbols (say "fifteen thousand", "a month's rent"), no country-specific institutions (housing association becomes landlord, solicitor becomes lawyer, council becomes landlord or the city), no local slang ("half nine" becomes "nine thirty"), no named regions. **Keep every concrete universal detail** (the pan in the sink, the IKEA story, the 11.40pm text, the chips can become fries or a takeaway). British spelling stays.
- **Shape:** build the case in the shape CAST.md gives it:
  - **tool-in-action:** as now.
  - **starts-in-aftermath:** open after the event and work back.
  - **mostly-dialogue:** the case told mainly in lines people say.
  - **unresolved:** it stops before the answer; the reader is left with the method, not a verdict.
  - **misuse-caught:** the person uses the method wrongly, notices, and corrects; or the gate stops them before a test.
  - **short:** under 120 words.
- **Endings:** no "Yes, it was X." No closing line that states the lesson. End on the last thing that happened or was said. Where the verdict matters (a harm case), say it once, plainly, inside the body.
- **Life stages:** follow CAST.md notes (over 55, parents of teenagers, a disabled tenant, a retired couple, flatmates). Make it matter to the case, not a label.
- The first line stays exactly `*Made up, but you've met these people.*`. Keep cases under about 220 words.
