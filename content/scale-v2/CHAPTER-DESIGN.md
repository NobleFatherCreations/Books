# The Weighing: chapter design (one shape per chapter)

Binding for the writing pass. Owner (2026-11-23): "The sections within each chapter shouldn't follow a static flow / audit and design a unique approach to each chapter that ensures usability and retention tricks for people to remember in the moment." No two neighbouring chapters share a shape. Topic map: `COVERAGE-MAP.md`.

## The memory system (book-wide)

1. **One handle per chapter.** A short line someone can say under stress, such as "Toast or fire?" or "Resemblance is cheap." It sits in a `::: handle` block where the chapter's idea lands (not always at the end). The field card collects all of them, in order: the book on one screen.
2. **One anchor image per chapter**: a concrete picture the idea hangs on (smoke alarm, two rooms, a file folder, a dial). Reused, never swapped for a new metaphor mid-chapter.
3. **A recall moment.** Somewhere after the middle, the reader is asked to produce the idea before seeing it again ("Before you scroll: name the four dimensions"), with the answer in a `::: sort` or `::: check` reveal. Retrieval beats rereading.
4. **A worked case that sometimes ends "yes, it was"**: at least one case in three ends with real manipulation confirmed, so the book never teaches doubt alone.
5. **Numbers as anchors**, only when sourced (for example, 54% accuracy at spotting lies: Bond and DePaulo 2006).
6. **Search headings in the reader's words** where they fit; inbound-linked headings stay word for word.

## Block palette (writers use only these; design styles each one)

`::: handle` the line to remember · `::: scene <label>` worked case (label chosen at freeze; for now the line `*LABEL*`) · `::: try <label>` exercise · `::: warn [danger] <label>` the floor and safety lines · `::: tree` decision path (nested `- If … → …` list) · `::: sort` sort-these items, `- item || answer` (answers hidden behind a tap) · `::: myth` pairs `- **Myth:** … / **True:** …` · `::: script` words to say: `**You:** …`, `**If they say:** …` · `::: check` a self-check with `☐` items · `::: compare <label>` a two-column table · `::: pull` · `::: aside` · `::: gradient` · `::: bridge` one line to a sister book.

Voice: blunt, funny, precise (owner). **No jokes** in or beside the heavy chapters (2, 20, 37) or any `warn` block.

## Start here
- **what-are-you-trying-to-weigh**: six tiles (a partner, someone new, family, a friend, a boss or a group, yourself). Each opens a 3-stop route through the book. "In danger right now?" sits above the tiles. Handle: "Start where it hurts."
- **how-to-use-this-book**: three promises and three refusals (no tracking, no verdicts about people it hasn't met, no permission slips to stay). It links to the limits page and the 60-second weigh.

## I. Why weigh at all
1. **you-learned-to-see**: *shape: the split screen.* One scene told twice, through the detector, then through the weigher. Handle: "Resemblance is cheap. Explanations cost." Image: smoke alarm and toast. Heading: "What is calibration?" Recall: "which question did the detector ask?"
2. **this-book-in-the-wrong-hands** (heavy): *shape: the three rules as a card that comes first*, then the misuse lines an abuser would say, quoted beside the rule that beats each one (`compare`). No humour. Handle: "You don't owe anyone certainty before you protect yourself." Bridge: Long After if-youre-not-safe-right-now.
3. **the-two-errors**: *shape: the price tag.* Two receipts side by side, then a `tree`: "Where are you right now?" decides which error is more expensive. Handle: "Price both mistakes." Heading: "False positive and false negative in relationships." Case ends "yes, it was".
4. **most-people-most-days**: *shape: the two rooms.* Wide room and local room, a `sort` of ten behaviours ("which room's base rate applies?"). Handle: "Name the room first." Heading: "What is a base rate?"
5. **the-gradient**: *shape: the dial.* The seven positions as a visual `gradient`, eight real lines to place on it (`sort`), then what moves a behaviour right (freedom, repetition, response). Handle: "Seven positions, not two." Heading: "Rude or abusive? The difference."
6. **NEW red-flag-flaw-or-dealbreaker**: *shape: three bins.* Definitions in one line each, then a `sort` of 15 first-date and early-relationship behaviours into bins, with answers showing the bins move with context. Handle: "Flaws annoy you. Red flags predict." Headings: "Red flag or just a flaw?", "Red flag vs dealbreaker". Links: the-gradient, weighing-someone-new, Decoder love bombing.
7. **NEW conflict-or-control**: *shape: two weather maps.* Ordinary conflict comes and goes like storms; coercive control is the climate. A `compare` table (who is afraid, who adapts, does freedom shrink), then the freedom test. Handle: "Storms pass. Climate stays." Headings: "Is it a toxic relationship or coercive control?", "Normal fighting or abuse?" Sources: Johnson typology, Stark (coercive control). Links: Feminine, Long After abuse-or-a-hard-breakup, Decoder coercive control.
8. **NEW a-boundary-or-a-rule**: *shape: grammar lesson.* "A boundary has 'I' as its subject. A rule has 'you'." Twelve sentences to rewrite (`sort`), then `script` for saying a real boundary. Handle: "Whose behaviour does it govern?" Headings: "Boundaries vs control", "Is my partner setting boundaries or controlling me?"

## II. The instrument is you
9. **your-detector-has-a-history**: *shape: the archaeology dig.* Three layers (the room you grew up in, the worst relationship, the internet), each with "whose voice is this alarm?" Handle: "Whose voice does this alarm sound like?" Bridge: Long After the-trauma-bond.
10. **the-gut-sensor-or-smoke-alarm**: *shape: the ten-minute test.* A `tree`: can you name what set it off, in specifics? Then a sorted list of real gut reports (sensor or alarm). Handle: "Name the smoke or call it an alarm." Heading: "Should you trust your gut about someone?"
11. **NEW gut-feeling-or-anxiety**: *shape: a symptom table*, intuition versus anxiety versus OCD doubt versus trauma alarm versus attachment panic, by tell (specific or vague, calms with information or demands more, points outward or loops). Handle: "Intuition informs. Anxiety interrogates." Headings: "Gut feeling or anxiety?", "Trusting your gut when you have anxiety". Careful clinical phrasing; links to finding help (Long After finding-a-therapist).
12. **NEW when-people-say-youre-overreacting**: *shape: the size-match.* The size of the event against the size of the reaction, as a `tree` with three honest outcomes: overreacting, under-reacting, "overreacting" used to silence. Handle: "Match the size, then check who's measuring." Headings: "Am I overreacting?", "How do I know if I'm overreacting in a relationship?" Links: Decoder gaslighting, minimization.
13. **the-biases-in-everyone** (NEW): *shape: a field guide to your own brain.* Eight biases, each one card: the name, one sentence, a 2-line example, a one-line counter-move. Recall quiz at the end (`sort`: name the bias). Handle: "Your brain has defaults. Check them." Sources: attribution error (Ross 1977), hostile attribution bias (Dodge), negativity bias, hindsight bias (Fischhoff).
14. **grief-in-the-costume-of-analysis**: *shape: a letter.* Written partly to the reader as the investigation they keep opening; the tell list as a `check`. Handle: "Investigation that never reaches a verdict is grief." Bridge: Long After the-grief-nobody-will-let-you-have.
15. **the-retrospective-rewrite**: *shape: the photo album.* Pick three memories; re-sort each one (still true, needs revising, can't know) in a `sort`. Handle: "Revise memory by memory, not all at once."
16. **the-state-youre-judging-from**: *shape: the pre-flight checklist.* An actual `check` (slept, eaten, frightened, alone, how long in this, what time is it?), then why each one bends judgment. Handle: "No verdicts at 2am."
17. **the-reference-sample**: *shape: the myth-buster.* `myth` pairs about healthy relationships (they don't fight, they always agree), then the four things that do distinguish them. Handle: "Grade the aftermath, not the trouble." Heading: "What does a healthy relationship actually look like?"

## III. What counts
18. **what-is-evidence**: *shape: the courtroom ladder.* Four tiers as rungs; ten pieces of "evidence" to place (`sort`). Handle: "Record, observation, inference, feeling: know which rung you're on."
19. **one-thing-is-not-a-pattern**: *shape: the fill-in form.* The four blanks (frequency, duration, escalation, response to feedback) as a fillable sentence, plus the no-pattern-needed list in `warn`. Handle: "How often, how long, getting worse, what changed when named?" Case ends "yes, it was".
20. **the-file-that-builds-itself**: *shape: the folder.* A scene where kindness gets filed as evidence, then the two-column log (`compare`). Handle: "If kindness goes in the file, the file is rigged." Heading: "Confirmation bias in relationships."
21. **the-discipline-of-three-explanations**: *shape: a drill*, three timed rounds on three events, with a model answer revealed after each. Handle: "Hostile, neutral, you. Then: what would tell them apart?"
22. **the-boring-explanations**: *shape: the top-ten countdown* of boring causes, most common first. Handle: "Boring first."
23. **intent-impact**: *shape: three boxes*, what they did, what it did, what they meant, with the third box crossed out. `script` for impact statements. Handle: "Defend what happened, not what they meant." Bridge: Loop (cites this sentence).
24. **NEW can-you-tell-when-someone-is-lying**: *shape: myth-buster, then method.* Busts the eye-movement, fidgeting and eye-contact myths with the research, then what does work: detail, checkable facts, consistency over time, the record. Handle: "Don't read faces. Check facts." Headings: "How to tell if someone is lying", "How to tell if someone is lying over text". Sources: Bond and DePaulo 2006; Vrij on cognitive load and asking for detail.
25. **NEW reading-a-text-message**: *shape: annotated screenshots in words.* Six messages, three readings each, with what you'd need to know. Reply speed, read receipts, "k.", emoji. Handle: "Text strips the face. Don't fill it in with your worst guess." Headings: "What does this text mean?", "Lying over text". Bridge: Loop.
26. **NEW i-dont-remember-it-that-way**: *shape: the two-witness exercise.* How memory rebuilds itself; honest disagreement versus gaslighting, as a `compare` (one-off versus pattern, curiosity versus attack on your sanity, records welcome versus records attacked). Handle: "Two honest memories can disagree. Gaslighting attacks the rememberer." Heading: "Gaslighting or just remembering differently?" Sources: memory reconstruction (Loftus).
27. **NEW screenshots-and-what-they-leave-out**: *shape: the crop.* One conversation shown cropped three ways; checklist for screenshots, recordings and AI-made media. Handle: "Ask what's outside the frame." Bridges: Loop synthetic-evidence, Feminine synthetic-evidence.
28. **secondhand-accounts**: *shape: the telephone game*, a story passed through three people, the distortion at each hop, then the questions to ask the messenger. Handle: "Who saw it, and what do they want?"
29. **judging-strangers-through-screens**: *shape: the clip test*, "could context change this?" as a `tree`, with the honest exception. Handle: "Eight seconds is not a person."

## IV. The tests that work
30. **before-you-test** (heavy): *shape: a gate.* A `check` at the top that stops the reader if any line is ticked, then why. No humour. Handle: "If testing could cost your safety, don't test. Leave or get help." Links: when-weighing-stops, Long After safety plan.
31. **the-naming-test**: *shape: a lab protocol*: the setup, the words (`script`), what to watch, two outcomes, and the second test weeks later. Handle: "Say the act and the effect. Then stop talking." Case ends "yes, it was".
32. **repair-and-apologys-counterfeit**: *shape: the forgery check*, a real apology against six counterfeit ones (`sort`). Handle: "Specific, unconditional, changed, no bill." Bridge: Repair what-an-apology-is-for.
33. **the-cost-of-no**: *shape: the price list*: temperature, access and framing, tracked over time. Handle: "Refuse something small. Watch the price." Heading: "What happens when you say no to a controlling person?"
34. **is-it-you-or-is-it-everyone**: *shape: the audience test*, the same behaviour in public, in private and with others, in a three-column `compare`. Handle: "Only you, only unwatched: that's information."
35. **power-changes-the-meaning**: *shape: the swap*, the same sentence from a peer, a partner who controls the money, a boss and a landlord. Handle: "Same words, different weight." Bridge: At Will.
36. **difference-is-not-malice**: *shape: the translation table*, behaviours that read as cold, rude or evasive across culture, neurotype, class, generation and language. Handle: "Different isn't dangerous. Check the aftermath."
37. **institutions-weigh-differently**: *shape: an org chart*: incentive, structure and record, not intent. Handle: "Ask what the system rewards." Bridge: Sacred Divide.
38. **NEW when-you-both-say-victim**: *shape: the fear test.* Who is afraid of whom, who adapts, who can say no, reactive abuse explained, and why "mutual abuse" is usually wrong but not always. Handle: "Ask who is afraid, not who shouted." Headings: "Reactive abuse", "Am I the toxic one or the victim?" Bridges: Silence if-you-have-done-both, being-provoked-into-being-the-bad-one; Decoder DARVO.
39. **NEW weighing-someone-new**: *shape: the first-90-days timeline.* What's normal at week 1, month 1 and month 3; pace as a signal; love bombing versus enthusiasm (`compare`). Handle: "Fast is information. Watch the first no." Headings: "Love bombing or genuine interest?", "Trusting your gut when dating". Bridge: Long After dating-when-youre-ready.
40. **NEW weighing-family**: *shape: the holiday table*, one family dinner with four people, weighed one by one. Difficult versus controlling, roles, and scripts with family. Handle: "Difficult costs you an evening. Controlling costs you yourself." Headings: "Am I overreacting about my parents?", "Is my parent controlling?" Bridge: Slow Take.
41. **NEW weighing-a-boss**: *shape: the performance review, reversed*: the reader reviews the manager on six lines. Bad versus coercive. Handle: "Bad bosses are bad at work. Coercive ones are good at you." Bridge: At Will a-bad-job-versus-a-coercive-one.
42. **NEW weighing-a-friend**: *shape: the ledger*, a two-season ledger of give and take, with "hard season" versus "using" markers. Handle: "Seasons end. Patterns don't."
43. **NEW weighing-a-group**: *shape: the welcome pack*, what a healthy group and a high-demand group hand you in month 1 and month 12; the BITE model labelled as a model. Handle: "Can you leave and still be spoken to?" Bridges: Sacred Divide, Loop.

## V. The mirror
44. **am-i-the-one-doing-it**: *shape: the mirror interview*, eight questions asked in the second person, answered honestly in a `check`. Handle: "Behaviour, not intention." Headings: "Am I the toxic one?", "How do I know if I'm being toxic?" Bridge: Repair the-difference-and-why-its-practical.
45. **the-vocabulary-as-a-weapon**: *shape: translation exercise*: clinical label into plain observable description (`sort`). Handle: "If you can't say it in plain words, it's a label, not a finding." Bridge: Loop.
46. **who-benefits-from-your-verdict**: *shape: the stakeholder map*, everyone in the reader's corner and what each one gains. Handle: "Find one person with no stake."
47. **NEW when-youre-the-one-accused**: *shape: the 24-hour plan*: first hour, first day, first week. Weigh the accusation with the same tools, without collapse or counter-attack. Handle: "Listen before you defend. Weigh before you answer." Bridge: Repair.
48. **being-wrong-well**: *shape: the script-led chapter*: the four moves as a `script` with good and bad versions. Handle: "Name it, withdraw it, don't perform it, accept their timing."

## VI. Living calibrated
49. **assessment-is-not-decision**: *shape: two sentences*, the fill-in pair, plus a new section on reversible versus irreversible decisions and small safe experiments. Handle: "What I believe. What I'll do. Neither needs the other."
50. **when-you-cant-ever-know**: *shape: the open file*, how to close a case without a verdict (a ritual in `try`). Handle: "Close the file without a verdict."
51. **scoring-your-own-past-calls**: *shape: the logbook*, worked example entries, then the tilt reveal. Handle: "Write the call before you learn the answer." Links: track-record sheet.
52. **certainty-is-the-tell**: *shape: the five-point shape check*, applied to a cult pitch, a manosphere thread and this book itself. Handle: "Check the shape, not the content." Bridges: Loop, Silence the-manosphere-will-offer-to-help.
53. **NEW can-people-change**: *shape: the before-and-after audit*: what real change looks like at 1, 6 and 12 months, against performed change. Handle: "Change is a pattern, not a promise." Headings: "Can a manipulative person change?", "Should I give them a second chance?" Bridge: Repair what-changes-and-what-doesnt.
54. **NEW weighing-an-expert**: *shape: the second-opinion checklist*: therapists, couples counselling where there is control (why many services advise against it), online diagnosis, court reports. Handle: "Credentials aren't a verdict. Ask what they saw." Bridge: Long After finding-a-therapist.
55. **helping-someone-else-weigh**: *shape: the friend script*, a `script` of better questions, plus the escalator and minimiser failure modes. Handle: "Steady beats fierce." Bridge: Long After helping-someone-in-year-one.
56. **NEW weighing-after-youve-left**: *shape: the recalibration plan*: weeks 1 to 12 of turning the alarm down without switching it off. Handle: "Turn it down, never off." Bridge: Long After.
57. **when-weighing-stops** (heavy): *shape: the stop sign*, the list of conditions first and the helplines next, with no exercise and no humour. Handle: "In danger, act first. Understand later." Links: Long After if-youre-not-safe-right-now.
58. **NEW changing-your-mind-out-loud**: *shape: the announcement*, scripts for telling people you got it wrong, or right, about someone. Handle: "Update in public, briefly."
59. **trust-as-a-skill-you-rebuild**: *shape: the staircase*, small trusts, checks and bigger trusts (trust as a dial, not a switch). Handle: "Trust in steps you can afford to lose." Closes the book.

## Toolbox
- **the-60-second-weigh**: the whole method on one screen: floor, room, gradient, three explanations, four dimensions, one test, decide.
- **field-card**: all handles, in book order, grouped by movement (covers every movement).
- **questions-people-ask**: about 30 answers in search phrasing, each 40 to 80 words plus a chapter link (FAQ schema).
- **a-to-z**: the book's terms, linked to Decoder names.
- **weighing-sheet** and **track-record-sheet**: printable `::: sheet` pages.
- **limits**: updated numbers; add what would change the book's mind about the new chapters.
- **all-sources**: every cited source, generated from chapter Sources sections.
