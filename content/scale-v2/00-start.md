## In danger right now?

::: warn danger Read this first
If someone has hurt you, threatened you, cut you off from help, made leaving dangerous, or you're afraid in your body and not just your head, stop weighing. You don't need to be sure before you protect yourself. Get safe first. If it's an emergency, call your local emergency number.

## My partner, and I can't tell if this is normal

You love them, and something keeps happening that you can't name. You need to know whether you're in ordinary rough weather or something that's quietly shrinking your life, and stop three is a test, so read [Before you test](ch:before-you-test-is-testing-safe) before you run it.

1. [Conflict or control](ch:conflict-or-control): storms pass, climate stays.
2. [One thing is not a pattern](ch:one-thing-is-not-a-pattern): the four questions that turn a bad night into evidence or let it go.
3. [The cost of "no"](ch:the-cost-of-no): refuse something small and watch what it costs you.

## Someone new, and it's moving fast

Three weeks in, they've said "soulmate" and you've said "um." Maybe it's love bombing, maybe it's an enthusiastic person, and your gut has opinions you can't yet check.

1. [Weighing someone new](ch:weighing-someone-new): what's normal at week one, month one and month three.
2. [Red flag, flaw or dealbreaker](ch:red-flag-flaw-or-dealbreaker): which bin each worry belongs in.
3. [The gut: sensor or smoke alarm](ch:the-gut-sensor-or-smoke-alarm): whether your gut is reporting something or remembering someone.

## My parents or family, and I'm told I'm overreacting

Family comes with thirty years of history and a seating plan. The hard part is telling a difficult parent from a controlling one when everyone at the table says you're too sensitive.

1. [Weighing your own family](ch:weighing-family): one dinner, four relatives, weighed one at a time.
2. [When people say you're overreacting](ch:when-people-say-youre-overreacting): match the size of the event to the size of the reaction, then check who's measuring.
3. [A boundary, or a rule for someone else](ch:a-boundary-or-a-rule): how to say what you'll do without being told you're controlling them.

## A friend who has started to feel off

Every message is a favour, or a sulk, or a silence. They might be in a hard season, or they might have worked out that you never say no.

1. [Weighing a friend](ch:weighing-a-friend): the two-season ledger of give and take.
2. [The boring explanations](ch:the-boring-explanations): tired, broke, grieving, bad at texting, checked first.
3. [The naming test](ch:the-naming-test): say what happened and how it landed, then watch what they do with it.

## My boss, or a group I belong to

Power changes what the same words mean, and so does a group that's hard to leave. You need to know whether this is bad management or a bad crowd, or something built to keep you there.

1. [Weighing a boss](ch:weighing-a-boss) or [Weighing a group](ch:weighing-a-group), whichever it is.
2. [Power changes the meaning](ch:power-changes-the-meaning): the same sentence from a peer, a partner, a boss and a landlord.
3. [Institutions weigh differently](ch:institutions-weigh-differently): ask what the system rewards, not what anyone intends.

## Am I the problem?

Asking is a decent sign, and it isn't proof either way. You need an honest look at your own behaviour, plus a check on who has been holding the mirror.

1. [Am I the one doing it?](ch:am-i-the-one-doing-it): eight questions about behaviour, not intentions.
2. [When you both say you're the victim](ch:when-you-both-say-victim): ask who is afraid, not who shouted.
3. [Being wrong well](ch:being-wrong-well): what to do if the mirror shows you something.

This is a book about being right about people, which is harder than being suspicious and harder than being nice.

Most of us were handed one of two settings. Some people trust everyone until it costs them a decade. Others learned the vocabulary of manipulation last spring and have since diagnosed their partner, their mother, two coworkers and a barista who said "no worries" in a tone. Both settings get people hurt. This book teaches the dial in between.

## What is The Weighing?

The Weighing is a free book on judging people accurately. It teaches you to weigh what someone did against how common it is, how often it happens, what changes when you name it and who has the power. The goal is fewer mistakes in both directions: missing real manipulation, and seeing it where it isn't.

It's built for the moment you need it. A text you can't read. A fight you can't size. A friend telling you you're overreacting, and a quiet voice wondering if they're right.

## Three promises

**It shows its working.** Where a chapter states a research finding, it names the source at the bottom. Where the evidence is thin, it says so in one plain line. [The limits of this instrument](ch:limits) collects everything the book might have wrong and what would change its mind.

**It takes both errors seriously.** The book will talk you down from the 3am investigation, and it will also tell you plainly when something is control. Some of its examples end with an innocent explanation. Some end with "yes, it really was manipulation." A book that only taught doubt would be a gift to the people it's meant to catch.

**It gives you something to use in the moment.** Every chapter has one line short enough to say under your breath while someone is talking at you, and one small thing to try, usually in under five minutes.

## Three refusals

**It won't track you.** Nothing you read or tap is stored. There are no streaks or progress bars, and you never make an account. The **Leave** button at the top blanks the page at once. If someone checks your phone, a private window helps too.

**It won't give verdicts about people it hasn't met.** It can't tell you your partner is a narcissist, and neither can the stranger in the comments who has seen one screenshot. What it can do is help you weigh what you've actually seen, and decide what you'll do about it.

**It won't be a permission slip to stay somewhere unsafe.**

::: warn danger The floor
The doubt tools in this book are for ordinary life. They don't apply to someone in danger. You don't owe anyone certainty before you protect yourself. If someone hands you this book to prove you're overreacting, that act is itself evidence: reread [This book in the wrong hands](ch:this-book-in-the-wrong-hands). In danger, stop weighing and go to [When weighing stops](ch:when-weighing-stops).
:::

## How do the chapters work?

Every chapter stands on its own. Most people arrive in the middle, from a search, mid-situation, so you can start anywhere. The headings use the words people actually type ("Am I overreacting?", "Red flag or just a flaw?"), so you can scan for yours.

- **The line to remember.** Each chapter has one memory line in its own box, placed where the idea lands. "Boring first." "Name the room first." You're meant to say it aloud.
- **Tap to reveal.** Some chapters ask you to sort, place or name things before you see the answer. Answer in your head, then tap. Pulling the idea out of your own memory makes it stick in a way rereading never will.
- **Made-up examples.** Short scenes with invented people and everyday detail: the "k." text, the cancelled dinner, the boss who copies everyone in. Some turn out innocent. Some turn out to be exactly what they looked like.
- **One thing to try.** Most chapters end with a small action, usually under five minutes. Nobody is marking it.
- **What to read next.** The last paragraph points to the chapter that usually comes next for someone in your place.

[The field card](ch:field-card) collects every chapter's memory line in book order. It's the whole book on one screen, and the page to screenshot. [The 60-second weigh](ch:the-60-second-weigh) is the method in seven steps for when you have a minute and a decision.

## Who is it for?

Anyone trying to read a person and not wanting to get it wrong. Someone in a relationship that confuses them. Someone dating again after a bad one, whose alarm now goes off at a late reply. Someone whose friends have all reached a verdict and who'd like to reach their own. Someone who has started to wonder if they're the problem, which is a fair question and gets a fair chapter.

It's for people of every gender, in relationships of every kind. The examples switch on purpose.
