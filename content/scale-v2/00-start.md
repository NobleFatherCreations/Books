Somebody did something, or keeps doing something, and you'd like to know whether it's a bad week or a bad person before the group chat finishes voting. This page skips the theory and sends you to the three chapters that matter for your situation.

## In danger right now?

::: warn danger Read this first
If someone has hurt you or threatened to, a child is at risk, you're being cut off from people who could help, leaving is being made hard or dangerous, or you're afraid in your body and not just your head, stop weighing. You don't need to be sure before you protect yourself. Get safe first. If it's an emergency, call your local emergency number.

[When weighing stops](ch:when-weighing-stops) says what to do instead. The Long After's [If you're not safe right now](/longafter/if-youre-not-safe-right-now) has help lines by country and what to do if this device isn't private.
:::

Everyone else: pick the heading that sounds most like what's going on. You don't have to read the book in order, and you can come back tomorrow and pick another. If two headings both sound like you, start with the one you'd rather not read.

## Is this normal in my relationship?

Fights are normal, loud ones included, if they're about something specific, they pass, and you can both still argue back. It's a different thing if you've started editing yourself before you speak, to dodge their reaction, and your life is quietly getting smaller. These three chapters tell the two apart.

You love them, and something keeps happening that you can't name. Stop three is a test, so read [Before you test](ch:before-you-test-is-testing-safe) before you run it.

1. [Conflict or control](ch:conflict-or-control): bad weather that blows over, or a climate you've started dressing for.
2. [One thing is not a pattern](ch:one-thing-is-not-a-pattern): the four questions that turn a bad night into evidence or let it go.
3. [The cost of "no"](ch:the-cost-of-no): refuse something small, then read the price list of what it costs you.

## Is it love bombing, or are they just keen?

Early on you can't weigh the whole person, because there isn't enough of them yet. You can weigh the pace, and what happens to it when you slow things down. Someone keen lets you set the speed. Someone love bombing needs you to keep up. Start with these three.

Three weeks in, they've said "soulmate" and you've said "um." Your gut has opinions you can't check yet.

1. [Weighing someone new](ch:weighing-someone-new): what you can know in the first ninety days, and what happens when you touch the brake.
2. [Red flag, flaw or dealbreaker](ch:red-flag-flaw-or-dealbreaker): which bin each worry belongs in.
3. [The gut: sensor or smoke alarm](ch:the-gut-sensor-or-smoke-alarm): whether your gut is reporting something or remembering someone.

## Am I overreacting about my family?

Maybe, and there's a way to check. A difficult relative costs you an evening, and you go home still yourself. A controlling one costs you decisions: your money, partner or plans need their sign-off, and saying no gets punished. Weigh each person separately, and measure your reaction as carefully as theirs.

Family comes with thirty years of history and a seating plan. The hard part is doing any of this while everyone at the table says you're too sensitive.

1. [Weighing your own family](ch:weighing-family): one dinner table, every chair weighed on its own, yours included.
2. [When people say you're overreacting](ch:when-people-say-youre-overreacting): measure the event and your reaction with separate rulers, then check who's holding them.
3. [A boundary, or a rule for someone else](ch:a-boundary-or-a-rule): whose behaviour your sentence governs, and whose theirs does.

## Is my friend using me?

Look at two seasons of the friendship. In a hard season the giving tips one way for a reason you can name, and it tips back later. Using stays one-sided even when their life is fine. Check the boring causes first, then ask for something small and see what comes back.

Every message is a favour or a sulk. They might be in a hard season, or they might have worked out that you never say no.

1. [Weighing a friend](ch:weighing-a-friend): the two-season ledger of give and take.
2. [The boring explanations](ch:the-boring-explanations): tired, broke, grieving, bad at texting, checked first.
3. [The naming test](ch:the-naming-test): say what happened and how it landed, then watch what they do with it.

## Is it my boss, or the whole place?

Often both, and it helps to know which part is which. Power changes what the same words mean, because refusing your boss costs more than refusing a friend. A group or workplace can also be built to keep you there, whatever anyone in it intends. These chapters separate a bad manager from a bad system.

1. [Weighing a boss](ch:weighing-a-boss) or [Weighing a group](ch:weighing-a-group), whichever it is.
2. [Power changes the meaning](ch:power-changes-the-meaning): the same sentence from a peer, a partner, a boss and a landlord.
3. [Institutions weigh differently](ch:institutions-weigh-differently): a system has no mind to read, so ask what it rewards and who it protects.

## Am I the problem?

Asking is a decent sign, and it isn't proof either way. You need an honest look at your own behaviour, plus a check on who has been holding the mirror. Most people who've been the problem were sure, at the time, that they were the reasonable one. So were most people who weren't.

1. [Am I the one doing it?](ch:am-i-the-one-doing-it): the dashcam, eight questions about what you actually did, as a camera would have recorded it.
2. [When you both say you're the victim](ch:when-you-both-say-victim): count the fear, meaning who is afraid of whom and who adapts in advance.
3. [Being wrong well](ch:being-wrong-well): what to do if the mirror shows you something.

This is a book about being right about people, which is harder than being suspicious and harder than being nice.

Most of us were handed one of two settings. Some people trust everyone until it costs them a decade. Others learned the vocabulary of manipulation last spring and have since diagnosed their partner, their mother, two coworkers and a barista who said "no worries" in a tone. Both settings get people hurt. Most of us have run both, sometimes in the same week. This book is about the middle setting, which takes more work than either.

## What is The Weighing?

The Weighing is a free book on judging people accurately. It teaches you to weigh what someone did against how common it is, how often it happens, what changes when you name it and who has the power. The goal is fewer mistakes in both directions: missing real manipulation, and seeing it where it isn't.

It's built for the moment you need it. A text you can't read. A fight you can't size. The 2am search for "covert narcissist signs". A friend telling you you're overreacting, and a quiet voice wondering if they're right.

## Three promises

**It shows its working.** Where a chapter states a research finding, it names the source at the bottom. Where the evidence is thin, it says so in one plain line. [The limits of this instrument](ch:limits) collects everything the book might have wrong and what would change its mind.

**It takes both errors seriously.** The book will talk you down from the 3am investigation, and it will also tell you plainly when something is control. Some of its examples turn out innocent and some turn out to be control, and it says which. A book that only taught doubt would be a gift to the people it's meant to catch.

**It gives you something to use in the moment.** Eight chapters hand you a named tool, such as the dial or the dashcam, with the moment it's for and one question to ask yourself when that moment comes. Every other chapter gives you a single "Ask yourself" question, pointed at you as often as at them.

## Three refusals

**It won't track you.** Nothing you read or tap is stored. There are no streaks or progress bars, and you never make an account. The **Leave** button at the top blanks the page at once. If someone checks your phone, a private window helps too.

**It won't give verdicts about people it hasn't met.** It can't tell you your partner is a narcissist, and neither can the stranger in the comments who has seen one screenshot. It can help you weigh what you've actually seen, and decide what you'll do about it.

**It won't be a permission slip to stay somewhere unsafe.**

::: warn danger The floor
The doubt tools in this book are for ordinary life. They don't apply to someone in danger. You don't owe anyone certainty before you protect yourself. If someone hands you this book to prove you're overreacting, that act is itself evidence: reread [This book in the wrong hands](ch:this-book-in-the-wrong-hands). In danger, stop weighing and go to [When weighing stops](ch:when-weighing-stops).
:::

## How do the chapters work?

Every chapter stands on its own. Most people arrive in the middle, from a search, mid-situation, so the way to use this book is to start wherever you are. The headings use the words people actually type ("Am I overreacting?", "Red flag or just a flaw?"), so you can scan for yours.

- **A tool, or a question.** Eight chapters carry a tool card: a name, the moment it's for ("When…"), and a question to ask yourself on the spot. Planning what you'll do when a moment arrives makes you likelier to do it. The other chapters carry one "Ask yourself" line, placed where the idea lands. Expect it to be about you about half the time.
- **Tap to reveal.** Some chapters ask you to sort, place or name things before you see the answer. Answer in your head, then tap. Pulling an answer out of your own memory makes it stick better than reading it again.
- **Made-up examples.** Short cases with invented people and everyday detail: the "k." text, the boss who copies everyone in. In some, the person gets the method wrong and catches it. A few stop before the answer, because real life does that too.
- **One thing to try.** Most chapters end with a small action, usually under five minutes. Nobody is marking it.
- **What to read next.** The last paragraph points to the chapter that usually comes next for someone in your place.

The Toolbox at the back holds [the kit](ch:field-card): the eight tools on one page, looked up by what's happening to you, for printing or a screenshot. [The 60-second weigh](ch:the-60-second-weigh) is the method in seven steps for when you have a minute and a decision.

## Who is it for?

Anyone trying to read a person and not wanting to get it wrong. Someone in a relationship that confuses them. Someone dating again after a bad one, whose alarm now goes off at a late reply. Someone whose friends have all reached a verdict and who'd like to reach their own. Someone who has started to wonder if they're the problem, which is a fair question and gets a fair chapter.

It's for people of every gender and age, in relationships of every kind. The examples switch on purpose: men get controlled in them, parents and flatmates and people past sixty weigh in them, and plenty of them have no partner in the story at all.
