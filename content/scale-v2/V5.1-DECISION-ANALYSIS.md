## 1. Steelman: the strongest case against each answer

## 2. Auditing the ~100,000 words I didn't read line by line

**What was done:** every sentence was scanned by script against your protocol. I read all 64 worked examples, every opening sentence, and samples of every rule hit. A script can count patterns, but it can't judge whether a paragraph is clear, true, funny, kind or necessary. That needs reading.

**Why I didn't read it in one go:** 100,000 words is about 135,000 tokens. Read in one session, the earliest chapters get compressed out of memory before the last ones are read, so the audit gets shallower as it goes.

**Three ways to do it:**

| Method | How | Quality | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Me, file by file in this session | Read one source file per turn; write findings to a ledger after each, so nothing depends on memory | Good; consistent single judgement | Slow: 15+ long turns |
| B. One reviewer agent per file, one at a time | Each gets a fresh mind, the full file, and a fixed rubric (protocol + STEELMAN-2 + example checklist), and writes `content/scale-v2/review/NN.md`. I verify a sample of every report against the text before trusting it | Best: full attention on every word | 15 agent runs |
| **C. Audit-then-rewrite in the same agent (recommended)** | Each writing agent in the v5.1 pass first writes its file's audit (every improvement, with the line), *then* rewrites, then a steelman agent checks the whole diff | Same depth as B, and every finding is acted on | 15 runs total instead of 30 |

**To get it, say:** "Use one agent per file to audit then rewrite The Weighing per V5.1, pilot first." (The repo's rules let me dispatch agents only when you ask, one at a time.) If you want the audit *before* deciding on the rewrite, say "Use one agent per file to audit only" (method B), and I'll bring back the ledger.

## 3. The final redesign (content and design together)

**Concept sentence:** *The Weighing is an instrument you can trust at 2am: quiet, exact, and blunt, with one moving part, the gradient.*

**Content (v5.1):**
- **Voice:** blunt deadpan with mirror jokes and the limits above.
- **Retention:** 8 core tool cards, a self-check question in every other chapter, a "which tool?" item in each quiz.
- **Examples:** a new cast (CAST.md): 8 gender swaps, everyday names, country-neutral, varied shapes, misuse cases, wider lives, no lesson endings.
- **Mechanical fixes:** "three tests"; shuffled quizzes; "judgement"; no self-bragging; stylistic triplets broken; contractions.
- **Search:** the 22 descriptions, plus the tools become citable named methods ("the dashcam rule") with their own A to Z entries.

**Structure:**
- **Cover:** a full-bleed cover moment, the one crisis line, then **Start here** (pick your situation, which gives three chapters).
- **Contents:** six movement cards instead of an 11-screen list. Each opens to its chapters, with the chapter number and a one-line question.
- **Chapter:** short answer, argument, tool card or self-check question, worked case, quiz in batches of 4, a "try", then the handoff.
- **Toolbox:** the field card rebuilt as **the kit** (8 tools, plus "what's happening to you?" lookups), printable on one page and screenshot-able on a phone; the 60-second weigh; the sheets.
- **Safety:** "Help" in the header on every page, plus Leave; the crisis page gets one clear "Find help where you live" action near the top; the wrong-hands refrain becomes one small recognisable box.

**Design (the R1 faults fixed):**
- **Signature move: the gradient,** drawn as a seven-step scale. It appears on the cover, in the gradient chapter, and whenever a case gets placed ("this one sits at *unkind*"). Still in reduced motion, crisp in print.
- **Type:** two families (a reading serif, plus a compact sans for cards, labels and the scale). The heading weights are preloaded with a size-matched fallback, which fixes the 0.3 layout jump.
- **Colour:** a calm dark ground; one restrained accent per movement (the handoff's budget); tokens for corner radius and shadow.
- **Phone:** the header hides on scroll (Leave stays in the dock); dock-safe scroll padding fixes focus being hidden; quizzes come in batches of 4; a phone-sized cover image.
- **Discreet mode:** your call (asked with the comps).
- **Process:** three comps, you pick by picture, one critic, then guards for every fix.

Scores 1 to 5, judged against the book's stated goal: help people weigh accurately, remember it in the moment, find it, and trust it.

**Reader-value total (first nine rows):** current about 29/45, redesign about 41-43/45.

**Analysis:**
- **The biggest gains** are the two things the book exists for: remembering the method when it matters (retention, the kit) and seeing yourself in it (examples). Those are content changes; design alone can't deliver them.
- **The biggest risk** is the humour register in a safety-adjacent book. The tiered retention model and written limits reduce it; the two-chapter pilot is the real control.
- **The biggest cost** is time while v4 stays live. Every week v5 isn't live is a week of search surface lost (the v5 pages target phrases worth roughly 10,000+ monthly searches that v4 barely covers).
- **Sequencing that recovers most of the time:**
  1. CAST.md, the brief and the builder `tool` block (me, no agents).
  2. **The pilot agent runs while I build the three design comps on the same pilot chapters.** Your one review message covers both: tone (two chapters) and look (pick A, B or C).
  3. The other 14 files, one agent at a time, while I build your pick into the builder.
  4. Steelman 3, then gates, then the critic, then release on "deploy The Weighing".
- **Alternative if speed matters most:** ship v5 now with the design pass and the mechanical fixes only, then do the voice and examples pass as v6. Faster to live (about 1-2 sessions), but the tool cards and the kit then need a second design round later. Overall, I don't recommend it: it ships the weakest part of the book (the examples and retention) to search first.

**Recommendation:** the redesign, with the four adjustments from section 1:
- tiered tools;
- humour limits;
- name and setting rules that keep concrete detail;
- unresolved and misuse cases in proportion.

Run the audit and rewrite together (method C), and use the parallel sequence.
