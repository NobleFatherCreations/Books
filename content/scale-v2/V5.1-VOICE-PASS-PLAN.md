# The Weighing v5.1: voice and examples pass, analysis and plan (2026-11-24)

Owner answers: content/design/OWNER-TASTE.md (2026-11-24). Findings: STEELMAN-2.md. This plan is for approval before any text changes.

## 1. Retention: how to use all three methods without cluttering the page

The three methods do different jobs, so they combine instead of competing:
- **A named tool** (a concrete picture: the dial, the dashcam, the test weight) is what you *remember*. Concrete images are recalled far better than abstract phrases (dual coding).
- **A "when" trigger** is what *fires* it at the right moment. Planning "when X, I'll do Y" reliably raises follow-through (implementation intentions, Gollwitzer and Sheeran's 2006 meta-analysis: medium to large effect).
- **A self-check question** is what you *do* when it fires, aimed at you, so it builds accountability.

**Design: one three-line card per chapter, replacing the `::: handle` line** (a new `::: tool` block):

```
THE DIAL
When someone hurts you and you reach for "abuse" or "fine".
Ask yourself: did my freedom shrink, or just my mood?
```

Rules for the card:
- **Placement:** where the chapter's idea lands (mid-chapter, where the handle sits now), never as the last line, so it can't act as a mic-drop.
- **Names come from images already in the chapter** (CHAPTER-DESIGN.md gives each chapter an anchor image), so nothing new to learn. Everyday objects only. No two chapters share a tool.
- **The trigger uses the reader's words**, the situation as it feels ("you've read the text eleven times"), not the concept.
- **The question is answerable in the moment** and points at the reader at least half the time (accountability), the other person the rest.
- **Recall:** the existing `::: sort` quiz gains one item per chapter: the trigger, with the tool as the answer ("You're sure you're the calm one in the relationship. Which tool?" || "The dashcam.").
- **The field card is rebuilt from the cards, ordered by trigger** (what's happening to you), so in the moment you look up your situation and get the tool and the question. One source: each card lives in plan.json as `tool: {name, when, ask}` and the builder renders both the chapter card and the field card from it.
- **Risk: 59 tools is a lot.** Mitigation: they're grouped six ways (the movements) on the field card, and the 60-second weigh keeps only the eight most-used.

## 2. Humour and section endings
- **Voice target:** dry, deadpan, observational, unbothered, willing to laugh at the reader and the narrator; blunt about what people actually do. (The owner's reference is Shane Gillis's tone: we use the register, never any comedian's material.)
- **Two forms only:**
  - **Mirror lines:** the joke lands on the reader's own behaviour, so they recognise themselves ("You've also blamed a bad week on someone's character. Probably this month.").
  - **Jokes inside the argument**, as observations.
- **Sections stop flat** when the point is made, on the last fact or a mirror line. No line that restates the lesson.
- **Hard limits:** no jokes in or next to the three safety chapters, the safety gate, warn blocks, or any case involving violence; never laugh at someone being harmed; mirror lines never imply a victim caused their abuse.

## 3. The 64 worked examples
- **Gender rebalance (8 cases, facts unchanged):**
  1. conflict-or-control: he adapts to her moods.
  2. the-state-youre-judging-from: her midnight "talks".
  3. one-thing-is-not-a-pattern: a woman controls her girlfriend's clothes.
  4. i-dont-remember-it-that-way, case 2: she gaslights him.
  5. the-discipline-of-three-explanations: she isolates him from his friend.
  6. can-people-change, case 1: an ex-girlfriend who controlled.
  7. weighing-family: a controlling mum.
  8. weighing-a-boss: a female manager.

  Physical-violence cases (when-weighing-stops, before-you-test, when-you-both-say-victim) stay as written.
- **Names:** one central cast list (content/scale-v2/CAST.md, to write before the pass). Everyday names across cultures, each used once book-wide; the rare European names go. A checker fails on any repeat.
- **Place-neutral settings:** remove currency symbols, country-specific institutions (housing association, solicitor, council), local slang ("half nine", five-a-side) and named places (Pembrokeshire). British spelling stays.
- **Shapes:** each case gets an assigned shape in CAST.md so the book varies: about 20 tool-in-action (as now), 10 that start in the aftermath, 8 told mostly in dialogue, 10 left unresolved, 6 where the method is misused and caught (including one where the safety gate stops a test before it happens), 10 short sharp cases.
- **Endings:** no "Yes, it was X." and no line that states the lesson; end on the last thing that happened. Where the verdict matters (harm cases), the verdict moves into the body, said once, plainly.
- **Wider lives:** at least 12 cases move to people over 55, parents of teenagers, carers, disabled people, single people, and housemates or colleagues rather than couples.

3. **Builder:** add the `::: tool` block and the plan.json `tool` field, render the field card from it, and add a checker for name repeats and lesson-endings.
4. **Pilot:** one agent on 01-i-b (the gradient chapter and its neighbours). The owner reads two rendered chapters to confirm the tone before the other 14 files.
5. **The other 14 files**, one agent per file in reading order, each through `accept-pass.sh`.
6. **Steelman 3:** one agent over the whole diff (safety, accuracy, repeated jokes, humour near safety).
7. **Rerun every check;** re-snap the text; design resumes at R2.

**Cost:** 16 to 17 agent runs. The last pass hit the 5-hour usage cap twice; work is saved per file, so a cap only pauses it.
