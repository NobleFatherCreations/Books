# The Weighing: steelman 2, whole book (2026-11-24)

Scope: all 68 pages (114,552 words of body text, Sources excluded). Method, so nothing is claimed that wasn't done:
- **Every sentence** scanned against the owner's Human Voice and Anti-Slop Protocol (2026-11-24) and the no-ai-slop / copy-editing skills.
- **Read in full by hand:** all 64 worked examples (12,020 words), every page's opening sentence, and samples of every rule hit. Close reads of the gradient, am-i-the-one-doing-it and the crisis pages.
- **Not read line by line by hand:** the remaining prose (about 100k words). That was the job of the per-file writing agents and STEELMAN-1.

## A. The worked examples (64 cases, 389 sort items)

What's right: every case is specific and ordinary (a lasagne joke, a text with a full stop, rent paid late, mould reported four times); many are funny; no name repeats across chapters; outcomes are balanced (about half end "it was harmful", half end "it was ordinary"), which is the book's argument; same-sex couples appear; sort-item pronouns are balanced (He 26, She 20, They 6).

Improvable, most important first:
1. **Who does harm is lopsided.** Of about 29 cases that end in real harm, about 25 have a man doing it (Neil, Mark, Rob, Chris, Joel, Marcus, Kieran, Dev, Ollie, Stuart, Victor, Aziz, Lionel, Wendell, Malik, Rohan, Anders, Ciaran, Magnus, Achille, Yaw...). Men appear as the person harmed about twice (Eamon; Oskar, briefly). Men who weigh mostly learn they overreacted or were the one doing it (Dan, Theo, Tomás, Kofi, Hollis, Mateus, Lorcan). A male reader, including a father looking for this map, rarely sees himself as the one being controlled. Fix: swap the genders in 6 to 8 harm cases (a controlling woman, a man being isolated, a controlling same-sex partner, a controlling mother), keeping every case's facts.
2. **The names are memorable but not "you've met these people."** Many are rare or literary in English-speaking countries (Dagny, Cosmo, Hollis, Corin, Lucian, Clemence, Achille, Anouk, Ilse, Juno, Bram, Signe, Oona, Rasmus, Wendell, Lorcan, Mairead, Ruairi, Saoirse), while very common names (Sarah, Mike, Emma, Josh, Laura, Dave) are almost absent. Inclusion is good (Kofi, Ngozi, Ifeoma, Zainab, Priya, Yusuf, Elif, Kenji); the fix is fewer rare European names, more everyday ones, keeping the range.
3. **The settings are British** (£15,000, "half nine", five-a-side, the loo, chips, housing association, solicitor, Pembrokeshire, cricket), while the measured search demand is US and the book cites US services. Decide the market and make settings match (or keep them place-neutral).
4. **Every case has the same shape:** a name and a worry, "runs the [tool]", the steps, a verdict, a closing line. Sixty-four times. Vary it: start in the aftermath; tell one entirely in dialogue; show someone using the tool wrongly and catching it; leave a few unresolved (Rasmus and Bea already are; most aren't).
5. **The endings explain the moral.** "Yes, it was X." closes 18 cases, and many end on a kicker that states the lesson ("Two witnesses, and no prosecutor." "That's repair." "Fast was information." "The gate kept a test away from someone who had already given her the answer."). Protocol rule 3: let the story land. Cut the kicker; end on the last thing that happened.
6. **Nobody gets the tool wrong.** In all 64, the reader-proxy uses the method correctly and it works. Add two or three where the method is misapplied (counting a pattern from three moments that were all one bad week; running the cost of no on someone dangerous and being stopped by the gate before, not after).
7. **Narrow life stages:** mostly 20s to 40s, couples and dating. Few older people, teenagers or their parents, carers, disabled people, or people with no partner at all. Adjust when swapping cases.

## B. The voice, measured against the owner's protocol

Clean across the whole book: 0 banned crutch words, 0 filler transitions (moreover, additionally...), 0 "-ing" tails, 0 "ironic/profound" labels, 3 throat-clearing phrases (all literal), 15 runs of four medium sentences in 114k words (rhythm varies well), contractions are the norm.

Improvable:
1. **Mic-drop section endings:** about a quarter of 588 sections end on a short punchline ("A tactic survives it." "Your nerves can't." "Your mum charges you for them."). Sharp, but the protocol bans ceremonial closers. Owner decision.
2. **"X, not Y" memory lines:** 44 handles are built this way ("Seven positions, not two." "Behaviour, not intention."), plus about 26 "isn't X. It's Y" sentences in the body. The handles are the book's retention device; the protocol bans negative parallelism. Owner decision; the body sentences should go either way.
3. **Rule of three:** 279 lists of three; most are real lists, about a third are stylistic triplets ("short, specific and slightly uncomfortable", "real, recent and theirs", "small, reversible and yours"). Break the stylistic ones.
4. **The book bragging about itself:** about 7 lines, mostly chapter openings ("This is the most economical test in the book", "the only mechanism in the book that keeps improving", "frees more people than any other page in this book", "the question the whole book has been walking toward"). Cut to the claim.
5. **Deliberate repetition:** each page states its answer up top (short answer), argues it, compresses it (handle), quizzes it (recall), and the field card repeats it. The protocol bans fractal summaries; the brief asked for retention and the top answer serves search and AI. Owner decision.
6. **The safety refrain** appears in 47 of 59 chapters ("reread This book in the wrong hands"). Keep for safety and standalone visits; render it as one recognisable box in design.
7. **"Dial" means two things** in the gradient chapter (the seven-step scale, and the three factors).
8. **Recall sorts can be guessed by order:** the gradient's runs rude to abusive. Shuffle; check the other 49 sorts.
9. **Hedges:** "tend to" 14 times, mostly correct for research claims; cut the 3 that aren't about research.
10. **Uncontracted forms:** 233, mostly for emphasis ("is not a number"); contract the rest.
11. **Spelling:** judgment 24 / judgement 19; weaponized (link text) vs weaponised (prose).
