## Part B — RUNS IN EVERY DIRECTION: the rule, plus the worst offenders rewritten in full

**The rule, applied to every remaining card:** name at least three specific
people who run this play, drawn from roles the book has already established
(vendor, camp lead, tour crew, ceremony holder, harm-reduction volunteer, sound
tech, officer, DJ). At most one universality sentence, and it goes last, as the
conclusion — not the whole card.

There are roughly 100 cards left to touch across chapters 9–24. Rather than
draft all 100 blind, here are the eight worst (by formula-phrase density,
measured in the audit), rewritten in full to demonstrate the pattern. The
remaining cards in those same chapters, and the other eight chapters flagged,
follow identically — that part is mechanical enough to script the same way the
cast-and-cross-reference pass was.

## Part C — SOBER TUESDAY: venue rotation map, plus the three worst rewritten

**The rule:** cap the office at one card in three. Rotate the rest through
venues the book hasn't used yet — the DMV, a family Thanksgiving, a landlord's
email thread, a car dealership, a church volunteer committee, a Facebook
Marketplace meetup, a CrossFit box, an MLM "team call," a hospital waiting
room, a youth sports league board. Chapter 17 already proves this works — its
landlord, gym membership, and free trial cards are the funniest run in the back
half specifically because they don't all live at the same desk.

## Part D — How every one of these gets checked for tone, slang, and relevance

This is the process, applied to the drafts above and to whatever gets written
from the same rules afterward — not a one-time pass, a standing checklist.

**1. POV lock before drafting.** Field Specimens and RUNS cards are narrator
voice, not one character's. So instead of a person's vernacular bank, the
narrator draws from that specific chapter's own Track titles and cold-open
nouns — chapter 12's specimen pulls "batch," "cap," "test," "source" straight
from its Tracks (THE SKIPPED TEST, THE FORTY-A-CAP REFRAME) rather than
generic language. That's why "My Guy" reads native to chapter 12 and wouldn't
work dropped into chapter 18.

**2. Slang-currency filter.** The narrator's own voice (Specimens, RUNS,
TUESDAY) stays deliberately dry and doesn't reach for trend slang at all — that
dryness is the joke, the same way a nature documentary narrator doesn't say
"lowkey." Currency risk is isolated to one character on purpose: Beans, who is
nineteen turning twenty-three across the book and is the only voice who should
sound generationally current. Anything written for him gets run against a
"still legible in three years" test — favor constructions that read as
personality ("bro is not," "it's giving," deadpan understatement) over slang
tied to one specific meme cycle, which ages out fastest.

**3. Role-reuse check.** Every new named archetype has to already exist as a
role somewhere in the book's cast (vendor, camp lead, tour crew, ceremony
holder, harm-reduction volunteer, sound tech, officer, DJ) before it's allowed
into a new card. That's why "the femme camp lead who's been feeding your row
for three years" in the chapter 13 rewrite isn't invented — it's the same
figure chapter 2's Kandi Debt Racket already established. Reused roles read as
native to the book; new ones read as bolted on.

**4. Repetition ledger.** Formula phrases ("regardless of," "does not have a
gender," "runs in every direction") get tracked across the whole set of new
cards so no phrase repeats more than once every several chapters — the finding
that started this was exactly this kind of phrase running unchecked for
sixteen chapters straight.

**5. The object rule for humor.** Every addition gets checked against the
book's own working comic principle, confirmed in the audit: the joke targets
the operator's props (the letterpress card, the bottle-cap crown, the lanyard
worn wrong), never the person being targeted and never the scene itself. THE
MISSING STAIR draft above was rewritten twice to make sure the joke stayed on
the institution's workaround, not on anyone's injury.

**6. no-ai-slop pass.** Every drafted card gets run through the same skill used
on the cold opens, checking specifically for the patterns that already showed
up once in this book — binary contrasts, colon-reveals, fake-profound kickers.
New content is the highest-risk material for reintroducing exactly what the
last four rounds removed.

**7. Continuity re-check.** Once anything here is applied, `scripts/wook-
continuity-check.py` runs again before anything ships. New cards that mention a
chapter number or a count ("twenty-six chapters," "since chapter 9") have to
match the book's current framing, or they become the next round's bug.
