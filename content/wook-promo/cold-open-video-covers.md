# Cold Open Readings — Video Cover Prompts

**Four covers for Instagram Reels / TikTok video readings.**
Chapters 5, 10, 12 and 14 of *The Festie Codex — The Gate*.

These are built to read as **one series**, so a viewer who sees the second one
recognizes it before they read a word. Everything below is derived from the
book's own chapter-poster system and palette, not invented.

---

## Before you generate anything: two rules

**1. Generate the art, set the type yourself.**
Every image model still mangles text. Do not ask it for the title. Generate a
clean image with deliberate empty space, then set the type over it in Canva,
Figma or Express using the book's real fonts. Each prompt below tells you which
zone to keep empty, and the exact text to lay in.

**2. No faces. Ever. In any of the four.**
This is the series' signature and it is also the book's argument: the person
running the play looks like everyone else. Objects, hands, wrists, backs of
heads, a blurred crowd. The moment you put a face on a cover, you have told the
viewer who to watch for, and the whole thesis collapses. It also sidesteps the
uncanny-AI-face problem and any question of depicting a real-looking person as a
predator.

---

## Series template

Every cover shares these, so change nothing here between the four:

| Element | Value |
|---|---|
| Canvas | 1080 × 1920, 9:16 |
| Ground | Deep ink `#17122B` |
| Paper accents | Cream `#F4EDDA` |
| Type faces | Shrikhand (display), Space Mono (labels), Bricolage Grotesque (body) |
| Treatment | Risograph / screenprint. Visible halftone grain, slight misregistration, one dominant accent per cover |
| Borders | 3px hard ink outline on any card, 5px hard offset shadow, no blur, no gradient glow |
| Composition | Subject occupies the middle third. Top 320px and bottom 480px stay clear |
| Mood | Late, warm, slightly wrong. Never glossy, never stock-photo festival joy |

### Safe zones, and why the bottom must stay empty

TikTok's caption block, username and button rail cover roughly the bottom 480px
and the right 180px. Instagram Reels covers the bottom ~420px. Put nothing you
need read down there.

```
0–320px      TOP: series label + cold-open title (your type, over empty space)
320–1440px   MIDDLE: the image. Subject centered, breathing room on all sides
1440–1920px  BOTTOM: leave empty. Platform UI eats it.
Right 180px  Keep clear of any detail you need seen
```

### The type you lay over each one

Same three lines every time, only the middle changes:

```
Line 1  (Space Mono, 28px, letterspaced .18em, cream, 60% opacity)
        COLD OPEN READING · THE FESTIE CODEX

Line 2  (Shrikhand, 96–120px, accent color, two lines max, hard ink shadow)
        [THE COLD OPEN TITLE]

Line 3  (Space Mono, 24px, cream)
        CH [N] · [CHAPTER TITLE]
```

## 1 · Chapter 5 — "The Eight-Ball"

**Accent: purple `#8B6BFF`** · Set One, Know the Pattern

The scene: a converted warehouse east of the LA arts district. Three stories of
raw rebar ceiling. Fog collects at the top and makes the lights look underwater.
A man is standing at his usual elevated vantage point, clocking three hundred
people in about forty-five seconds. We are looking out from where he stands.
That is the whole horror of it: the cover is his point of view.

> Risograph screenprint poster, vertical 9:16. Elevated point-of-view looking
> down over a crowded converted warehouse party from a mezzanine railing in the
> foreground. Three stories of exposed raw rebar and steel ceiling above. Dense
> fog pooled at the ceiling so the stage lights read as if seen from underwater,
> soft violet and deep indigo shafts. Below, a crowd of three hundred rendered
> only as anonymous silhouettes and motion blur, no discernible faces, no
> individual features. Deep ink background #17122B, dominant violet #8B6BFF,
> small warm cream #F4EDDA highlights on the rebar. Heavy halftone dot grain,
> two-color screenprint misregistration, flat printed texture. Empty dark space
> across the top quarter and bottom quarter of the frame. Cinematic, cold,
> observational. No text, no faces, no logos.

**Type overlay:** `THE EIGHT-BALL` · `CH 5 · YES IS A SOBER WORD`
**Alt subject if you want a second option:** a single unopened bag on a cream
card, shot flat, the only lit object in a dark frame.

## 2 · Chapter 10 — "We've Been Having Problems"

**Accent: teal `#3FD9B0`** · Set Two, Read the Room

The scene: the bag is in the rear passenger footwell of a Subaru and it is
heavier than the conversation at the gas station made it sound. Ahead is the gate
line. The object is the protagonist here, so shoot the object.

> Risograph screenprint poster, vertical 9:16. Interior of a car, looking down
> into the rear passenger footwell. A plain canvas tote bag sitting on the floor
> mat, slumped and heavier than it should be, rendered in flat printed color. Late
> afternoon light coming through the window in one hard diagonal, dust visible in
> the beam. Through the windshield in the far background, deeply out of focus, a
> long line of stopped cars at a festival gate. Deep ink background #17122B,
> dominant cool teal #3FD9B0 on the bag and light shaft, cream #F4EDDA on the dust
> motes. Heavy halftone grain, screenprint misregistration, flat matte texture.
> Empty space at the top and bottom of the frame. Quiet, tense, dread. No people,
> no faces, no text, no logos.

**Type overlay:** `WE'VE BEEN HAVING PROBLEMS` · `CH 10 · THE TAMPON BAG`
**Why this one works as a thumbnail:** an ordinary tote in a footwell means
nothing until you read the title, and then it means everything. That gap is the
click.

## 3 · Chapter 12 — "Forty A Cap"

**Accent: yellow `#FFD94A`** · Set Two, Read the Room

The scene: eight hundred dollars fronted for eighty caps from a source he has
used for two years. Marked up to forty. Never tested. The cover should be the
math, laid out like evidence.

> Risograph screenprint poster, vertical 9:16. Overhead flat-lay on a dark
> surface, arranged like a police evidence photograph. A neat grid of small
> unmarked capsules, a folded stack of twenty-dollar bills, and a sealed drug
> test kit still in its wrapper pushed deliberately to the far edge of the frame,
> just out of the arrangement. Hard single-source overhead light, sharp shadows,
> no hands, no people. Deep ink background #17122B, dominant acid yellow #FFD94A
> on the capsules, cream #F4EDDA on the paper. Heavy halftone dot grain,
> two-color screenprint misregistration, flat printed poster texture. Generous
> empty dark space at top and bottom. Clinical, cold, ominous. No text, no faces,
> no logos, no branding on any object.

**Type overlay:** `FORTY A CAP` · `CH 12 · THE BATCH`
**The detail that does the work:** the unopened test kit pushed to the edge. It
is the only thing in the frame that could have changed the outcome, and it is
the one thing not being used. Keep it in the prompt.

## 4 · Chapter 14 — "The Wristband Cop"

**Accent: orange `#FF9A3D`** · Set Two, Read the Room

The scene: the wristband is real. He paid two hundred and forty dollars on the
same Eventbrite page as everyone else. The glitter is real, from a jar that said
holographic gold biodegradable, applied after three YouTube tutorials. That is
the joke and the threat in one image.

> Risograph screenprint poster, vertical 9:16. Extreme close crop on a single
> forearm and wrist, cropped at the elbow and above the hand so no face and no
> body are visible. A genuine woven cloth festival wristband on the wrist,
> slightly frayed. Loose holographic gold glitter scattered across the skin and
> the forearm hair, applied a little too evenly, a little too deliberately. The
> sleeve of a plain festival merchandise t-shirt at the edge of the frame. Warm
> low golden hour light from one side. Deep ink background #17122B, dominant warm
> orange #FF9A3D, cream #F4EDDA glints on the glitter. Heavy halftone grain,
> screenprint misregistration, flat printed texture. Empty dark space top and
> bottom. Unsettling, deadpan, too-perfect. No face, no text, no readable logos.

**Type overlay:** `THE WRISTBAND COP` · `CH 14 · THE UNDERCOVER`
**Optional second frame for the carousel:** the same forearm, same crop, but a
government-issue watch just visible under the cuff. Do not explain it. Let the
comments find it.

## Negative prompt (use on all four)

```
faces, portrait, person looking at camera, eyes, smiling, crowd of happy
people, stock photo, lens flare, bokeh glow, neon glow, HDR, hyperrealistic
3D render, glossy, plastic skin, watermark, signature, text, letters, words,
typography, captions, logos, brand names, EDM festival cliche, rainbow
gradient, tie-dye, peace signs, extra fingers, deformed hands
```

## Per-tool notes

- **Midjourney** — append `--ar 9:16 --style raw --stylize 150`. Low stylize
  keeps it from prettifying the grain into something glossy. Add `--no text` in
  addition to the negative list; it obeys that flag more reliably than prose.
- **Adobe Firefly** — set Content Type to *Art*, Visual Intensity low, and pick
  a Reference Image after your first good result so the other three inherit the
  same grain. Firefly is the strongest of these at flat screenprint texture.
- **DALL·E / ChatGPT** — drop the word "risograph" if it goes too literal and
  gives you a picture of a printing press. Say "flat two-color screenprint
  poster with visible halftone dots" instead.
- **Any tool** — generate the four in one sitting with the same seed family. A
  series that drifts in grain or contrast stops reading as a series.

## Posting order

If you are releasing these as a run rather than all at once, lead with the ones
that need the least setup to land:

1. **Ch14 The Wristband Cop** — funniest premise, instant hook, most shareable.
2. **Ch10 We've Been Having Problems** — the strongest dread, and the most
   comment-bait ("this happened to my friend").
3. **Ch12 Forty A Cap** — most useful, most likely to be saved and sent.
4. **Ch5 The Eight-Ball** — heaviest. Post it once the account has context,
   because it is the one that needs the audience to already trust you.
