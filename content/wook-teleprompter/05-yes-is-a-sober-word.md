Not to sell. That’s the first thing I want you to understand. I’m not a dealer. I have a real job. I am a person who is drawn to people who are open and I want to facilitate their openness and the eight-ball is how I do that. Nothing sinister. Just chemistry. Just a man who has good stuff and likes to share.

Three hundred people in a converted warehouse space east of the arts district. The ceiling is three stories of raw rebar and it does something beautiful with the fog machines — the fog collects at the top and makes the lights look like they’re underwater. I’ve been at enough of these to know which vantage point gives you the full room. I go there first. I always go there first.

Black mesh top. Glitter on the collarbones. Earrings that came from a festival vendor, the kind where you can tell by the weight. That’s my window. The almost-leaving. I know exactly what almost-leaving looks like and I know it doesn’t close the same way a fully-leaving does.

You good?

Like it’s optional. Like I don’t care.

She nods. She takes it. And then, before she has even registered the hit, she says the sentence I am farming the whole night for.

Oh my god thank you, you’re so nice.

I have not had to.

The structure does the pushing. The chemistry does the pushing. The original frame — I am nice, she is grateful — does the pushing. And she is making choices inside that structure that she believes are hers because they feel like hers and they are coming from her body and her voice and her hands.

I am not like those guys. Those guys who grab, who insist, who make it obvious, who leave a trail of something that looks like what it is. Those guys are predators.

I’m just generous.

I bring an eight-ball to every party.

That man slept great.

The price was their yes before they knew they were saying it.

Yes is a sober word.

Even when you are not sober. Even when the music is doing what it does. Even when the bag comes out without asking and the frame is already set and the older-brother character has been running for an hour before the question becomes a question.

Yes is a sober word in the sense that it must be made by the part of you that can still see the exit.

A body shape is not a yes. A laugh is not a yes. A sure through chattering teeth is not a yes. Gratitude is not a yes. Being alone in a kitchen with someone who has been nice all night is not a yes.

### TRACK 01 — THE DOSE CREEP
*(incremental boundary erosion)*

Here's the behavior. You agreed to a small thing.

Half a tab of lucy. One bump. A microdose. A two-finger pour. A short ceremony. A light cup of tea. An edible labeled starter dose by a man who is not a labeling authority and whose certification, if asked, is “I’ve been doing this for years.”

Then the math starts moving.

“That batch runs weaker, you’ll want another.” “This is basically the same as what you already did.” “You’re tolerating it well — you can handle a little more.” “That dose is for tourists.” “You’re not feeling it because you’re blocking.” “Just lick the rim.” “This is basically just kava.”

The Dose Creep is not always coercive in tone. Sometimes it is friendly, paternal, maternal, spiritual. The voice is calm. The pace is slow. The escalation looks like generosity. But the contract you agreed to is not the contract being run. You said yes to half a tab of lucy in a tested batch from a known source. You did not say yes to the follow-on that the other person decided, inside their own head, was the logical extension of your original yes.

Here's the read on it. Watch for math that changes after consent. Did the dose described match what was offered? Did the follow-on offer come before or after you hit your original ceiling? Are they tracking your dose or losing track on purpose? Are they refusing to name milligrams, grams, drops, or duration? Are they using the word “intuitive” to mean “I’m not measuring”?

Vague dosing is not mysticism. Vague dosing is plausible deniability.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Pre-Set Dose Plan. Set the plan before the lasers start. Before the event. Before the camp opens. Before the cooler is touched. Not at eleven p.m. with a man named Atlas pouring tea.

Step 1 — Decide your ceiling sober. Write it down. “Tonight: one tab, tested, nothing else.” “Tonight: one bump at midnight, water after, that’s the set.” “Tonight: sober. Cacao only. No exceptions.” The note lives in your phone where your altered self can read it.

Step 2 — Tell one anchor. The anchor knows the plan. The anchor is allowed to say “that’s not the plan” and the pre-agreement is that you listen when they say that.

Step 3 — Refuse renegotiation in altered states. “I don’t update my dose plan after I’ve started dosing.” Or the shorter version: “Future me already voted.”

Step 4 — Treat every upsell as data. If someone keeps pushing past your stated ceiling, they are not being friendly. They are trying to lower your capacity. That is a Track, not a vibe.

Quick vibe check. People can offer you things kindly. You can decline kindly. The test is what happens after the third no. Green: accepts and redirects. Yellow: keeps the option warm. Red: escalates, reframes your no as your problem, finds a new angle.

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever pushed someone past their stated dose because you wanted company at the peak? Have you ever told someone “you’re not feeling it” because their slowness was inconveniencing your timeline? Be honest about which side of this you have been on. Then stop running it.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **First-Timer:** A veteran’s tolerance is not your floor. What takes them to baseline will take you somewhere else entirely. Set your ceiling based on your body and your experience level, not theirs.

**Sober and Recovery Lane:** “Just a sip” is not a small ask. It is the whole ask. Your sobriety is not an inconvenience to the group’s chemistry plan. You do not owe anyone a modified version of your sobriety to make the night more comfortable for them.

**Ceremony Lane:** A facilitator who says the dose is determined intuitively and cannot be specified in advance is not running a ceremony. They are running a dose-accountability bypass. Real ceremony practice has named protocols. Ask for them before you sit down.

This runs in every direction. The Dose Creep is run by male producers who pour generously, femme facilitators who say “the medicine knows what you need,” nonbinary elders whose ceremonial authority is used to override questions, touring veterans whose confidence is the credential, and camp leads who treat their kitchen supply as a social tool. The pour is not gendered. The push past no is not gendered.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. If a waiter refilled your wine glass twice after you said you were fine and then described the third refill as “your body wanted it,” you would leave a different tip. The restaurant does not get a pass because the candles were nice.

## 🎬 VIDEO 4 — Track 02 — THE SOBER-FRIEND SKIM

### TRACK 02 — THE SOBER-FRIEND SKIM
*(capacity-asymmetry targeting)*

Here's the behavior. Your sober anchor is the entire safety architecture for the night.

The Wook knows this. So they remove it.

They get the anchor talked into “just one.” They flirt with the anchor. They pull the anchor into a forty-five-minute conversation that is genuinely interesting and also precisely as long as it needs to be. They tell the anchor someone else is checking on you. They invite the anchor on a side mission that leaves you alone in the bass crowd at peak. They convince the anchor that everyone is “in good hands” — which is the specific phrase that means no one has that specific job anymore.

The Sober-Friend Skim doesn’t always look like seduction. Sometimes it looks like recruitment: “You should come sit by the fire.” “Your friend is fine, she’s with my crew.” “Come to the afters, we’ll meet up with her later.” Suddenly the only person whose job was to track you is somewhere else, untracking.

Here's the read on it. Is one person consistently pulling your anchor away from their anchor role? Is the anchor being convinced their job is unnecessary? Is the anchor being told you are “with someone” when you are not? Watch the geometry: the Wook needs your anchor removed before moving to the next Track. The skim is often visible from outside as a sequence.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Two-Anchor System. One anchor is a single point of failure. Two anchors is a system.

Step 1 — Designate two anchors before the event. Anchor A and Anchor B. They both know the plan, both have check-in windows, both have a code word.

Step 2 — Stagger their availability. If A is dosing tonight, B is sober or lower. If both want to dose, stagger the timing so they are never both at peak simultaneously.

Step 3 — The code word is a sentence that means “I need you now, no questions.” Text it. Say it. It does not require context. The anchor responds immediately with location and movement toward you, not with “are you okay?”

Step 4 — The anchor’s job is not to be fun. Their job is structural. Be very clear about this before the event. Anchor is a role, not a hangout.

**Step 5 — If the anchor disappears:** locate a DanceSafe or Zendo volunteer, a harm-reduction tent, a ranger, a staff member. These are your community anchors. Know where they are before you need them.

Quick vibe check. Anchors are human and can genuinely lose track. Someone pulling the anchor into a long conversation may not be running a play. The test is pattern: does it happen consistently, with the same person or type, at the same phase of the night?

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever kept someone’s anchor occupied while something else was happening? Have you ever told a sober person their friend was “fine” before checking? Have you ever recruited someone away from their care role for your own evening’s convenience?

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **New to the scene:** You may not have an established anchor network yet. DanceSafe, Zendo, and harm-reduction volunteers at any festival with safety infrastructure are your structural anchor. Locate them on the festival map before your first dose.

**Queer/Trans Festie:** In spaces where your full identity may not be safe with a random security guard, build your anchor network from your specific community before the event. The anchor should understand your context, not just your chemical plan.

**Disabled and ND Festies:** Capacity asymmetry compounds with any pre-existing condition that affects processing speed, executive function, or social boundary-reading. Your anchor needs to know your specific tells — not generic altered-state tells, yours. Brief them before you go in.

This runs in every direction. The Sober-Friend Skim is run by men, women, and everyone across the spectrum. It requires no particular gender to execute — only the social skills to occupy an anchor’s attention and the situational awareness to time the occupation correctly. Both skills are distributed equally.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. Your work friend who always wants a “quick word” with your mentor right before your performance review. Long enough that the meeting runs short and you don’t get to the hard thing. The anchor was occupied. The floor was left open.

## 🎬 VIDEO 5 — Track 03 — THE “WE ALREADY STARTED” PIVOT
*~749 spoken words · est. 5:00 at 150wpm*

### TRACK 03 — THE “WE ALREADY STARTED” PIVOT
*(sunk-cost applied to consent)*

Here's the behavior. “We already started this together.”

“You can’t half-ceremony.”

“It’s bad luck to leave the medicine unfinished.”

“But you said yes to this earlier.”

“After what we’ve already done — you’re going to stop now?”

The “We Already Started” Pivot is one of the oldest moves in coercion. It treats a small yes as a binding contract for every yes after it. It tells you the door closed when you walked through it, so do not bother turning around. Your previous consent becomes a claim on your current capacity. Your earlier presence becomes evidence of your future compliance.

The door is always open. There is no ceremony, no hookup, no commitment, no shared experience, no chemical partnership that closes the door. The door is open at 2 a.m. It is open at peak. It is open in the middle of a set they drove four hours to see. It is open in their tent. It is open after their hands are already on you. The door is always open and the Pivot is a set design that is supposed to make the door invisible.

Here's the read on it. Is your earlier consent being cited as evidence you cannot withdraw? Are they framing pause or re-evaluation as failure, fear, weakness, or betrayal? Are they making you feel like slowing down is doing something to them? Is the language about the container — “you agreed to this space” — being used to lock you in the space?

A real container honors the pause. A coercive container punishes it.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Re-Consent Pause. **Step 1 — Use the line:** “I am stopping. My earlier yes does not cover this.” No further explanation required. No apology. No justification for changed feelings.

Step 2 — Move toward people or light. Wooks lose power in the presence of witnesses. Harm-reduction tents, kitchen lights, crowds, rangers. Move toward them.

Step 3 — Document before the altered state clears. Time, location, what was said, what was offered, what you stopped. Even fragmented. Even voice-noted while walking.

Step 4 — Tell your anchor. Even if you are embarrassed. Especially if you are embarrassed.

Quick vibe check. You are allowed to stop in the middle. You are allowed to stop after the hookup has started, after the ceremony has begun, after the dose has hit, after you said yes three times in a row, after the whole night was built around the assumption that you were in. There is no contract. There was never a contract. The only contract that exists is the one you can give with the part of you that can see the exit.

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever told someone they couldn’t stop? Have you ever used “we already started” to push past discomfort — a partner’s, a participant’s, a friend’s? Have you ever framed a pause as a rejection of you rather than a recalibration for them? If yes, you ran this Track. Name it. Stop running it.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **Poly and Kink Scenes:** Prior negotiation does not override active withdrawal. The negotiated agreement is the floor, not the ceiling. “We already discussed this” does not mean “you already gave consent for all future instances.”

**Ceremony and Plant Medicine Lane:** “You agreed to surrender when you sat down” is one of the most dangerous sentences in the scene. Surrender in ceremony means releasing the ego’s resistance to the medicine’s work. It does not mean releasing your capacity to stop. These are different things. Any facilitator who conflates them is running the Pivot inside a sacred container.

This runs in every direction. The Pivot runs across every gender and every scene. Male ceremony facilitators running it from a position of spiritual authority. Femme partners running it inside a relationship where commitment has been mistaken for consent. Nonbinary scene figures running it inside a community where questioning the container feels like questioning the values. The Pivot is ideologically flexible and will wear whatever costume is most available.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. “But we’ve been in this project together for eight months — you can’t pull out now.” Yes you can. Eight months of investment does not obligate the ninth month. The Pivot wears a blazer in the conference room and a poncho in the ceremony space. Same move.

### TRACK 04 — THE PEAK PICKPOCKET
*(consent under cognitive overload)*

Here's the behavior. The peak is when your judgment is most beautiful and least operational.

It is also when Wooks move.

They take your phone “to find your friends” and never give it back, or they scroll, or they text from your number, or they note your passcode, or they read your DMs while you are somewhere else being made of light. They take your bag “to hold it safe” and go through it. They take your wallet “for the ride” and pocket the cash. They take your body. Your kiss. Your photo. Your password. Your story.

They ask you for your Venmo while you are dissolving and you tell them because at peak the brain’s executive function is running at reduced capacity and the social script of answering a question runs on autopilot when the full assessment system is elsewhere. They get you to send a text. Sign a thing on their phone. Agree to a deal. Name a price. Hand over a key. Nod into a camera that is recording. Say yes to the plan that did not exist twenty minutes ago.

Some peak pickpockets are physical. Some are financial. Some are emotional. Some are reputational — a photo you didn’t agree to, a video, a story told to someone important in the scene while you were not able to manage your own narrative. All of them rely on the same fact: at peak, you cannot lock the door the way you normally would.

Here's the read on it. Did they wait until peak to ask what they had not asked before? Are they handling your things “for you” in ways that limit your access to them? Did they introduce decisions — housing, money, sex, access, commitment — that should have been made sober? Are they using the peak as the moment to ask for things they have been working toward all night?

The peak is the destination. The building happened earlier.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Peak Perimeter. Build this before you peak, not during.

**Step 1 — Establish physical perimeter:** bag on body or with named anchor only. Phone in body pocket or with anchor. Cash in separate location from cards. No new transactions during peak — this is a standing policy your anchor enforces.

**Step 2 — Establish social perimeter:** your anchor has license to say “she’s not available for that right now” on your behalf. Not to speak for you about your feelings. To run interference on requests that require your executive function while your executive function is on leave.

**Step 3 — Establish decision perimeter:** Nothing that costs money, body, reputation, career, or commitment gets decided at peak. If someone has a “now or never” offer at two a.m. on the bass floor — the answer is never. Real offers survive until morning.

**Step 4 — After peak, before sleep:** brief survey. Phone intact? Bag intact? What was agreed to? The anchor runs this with you. Four questions. Five minutes.

Quick vibe check. Peak states produce genuine insight, genuine connection, genuine beauty. Some of what happens at peak is exactly what it is: real. The test is not did something happen at peak. The test is: did someone wait for your peak to ask for something they had not asked for when you were at baseline? If yes, they chose the timing on purpose.

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever waited for someone’s peak to have a conversation you wanted to have? Have you ever introduced a question — about intimacy, about money, about a plan — at the specific moment when someone’s ability to assess it clearly was most reduced? If yes, that is the Peak Pickpocket run inside a relationship you thought was clean. Notice the timing.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **First-Timer:** You do not yet know what your peak looks like from the inside, which makes you maximally susceptible to the pickpocket. Your anchor is your entire protection here. Establish them and brief them specifically: “If anyone asks me for money, physical access, or commitments after midnight, please interrupt.”

**BIPOC Festie:** Reputational pickpocketing — the photo, the video, the story told about you while you were not able to manage your own narrative — lands differently when your image in predominantly white spaces is already managed under additional scrutiny. Your narrative is yours. Build the perimeter around it.

**Vendor and Artist Lane:** Business agreements made at peak are not binding. If you were at peak when you agreed to a split, a booking, a collaboration, a price — you have grounds to revisit it. The verbal agreement made at two a.m. in a fog machine is not a contract.

This runs in every direction. The Peak Pickpocket does not require proximity. The financial pickpocket can be across the room, working the Venmo from a text. The reputational pickpocket can be across the festival, running your story through four people before you have sobered up enough to tell it yourself. The physical pickpocket can be someone your body trusts, which makes the ask feel like a natural continuation rather than a targeted move. The peak is the window. The shape of the pickpocket varies.
