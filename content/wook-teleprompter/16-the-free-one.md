🎛️ THE TAPERS’ SECTION

Cialdini · Gilbert · The Dose-Consent Gap

Short. Then back to the fire.

Robert Cialdini’s reciprocity is the most ancient lever in the book: humans are wired to return favors, to balance social ledgers, to feel the weight of an unreturned gift as a specific social discomfort. The gift economy runs on this legitimately. The Gift Economy Trap runs on it as a mechanism: front-load the gifts, make them personal and calibrated, and allow the reciprocity pressure to build until it is discharged in the way the gift-giver has already decided to receive it.

Daniel Gilbert’s affective forecasting research shows consistently that humans are poor predictors of their future emotional states under altered conditions. The person at the fire at ten p.m., sober, cannot accurately forecast how they will feel at one a.m. after two hours of a state they did not choose to be in. The ten-p.m. self is making decisions that the one-a.m. self will inherit. The one-a.m. self did not get a vote.

**The dose-consent gap:** when a person’s capacity is altered by a substance they did not consciously choose to ingest, their subsequent decisions cannot constitute informed consent under any ethical framework. The warmth felt toward the person who provided the substance is a chemical artifact of the substance, not an independent evaluation of the person. The decision made in that state is not the decision the sober person would have made. The sober person’s decision is the relevant one.

You are in.
