## The Sunrise Set + The Kandi Trade (closer)

Here's the Sunrise Set. Zero slang — just the true thing. This work helps people.

That has to be said first and it has to be said without hedging, because a chapter like this one can easily be read as an argument against something that has genuinely changed lives, including lives of people reading this, including people who will finish this chapter and feel defensive on behalf of an experience that was real.

Your experience was real. Noor’s was real. Nothing in this chapter takes it from either of you.

People sit down with these medicines and come back different in ways that hold for years — grief that finally moves, a drink that finally loses its grip, a father who finally gets forgiven or finally gets put down. Good practitioners do careful, difficult, unglamorous work, and there are more of them than there are Amarus, and they are the reason any of this has a reputation worth exploiting in the first place.

And that is exactly why the standard matters. **The people most damaged by the men in domes are not the skeptics. They are the practitioners who do this properly** and who spend their careers absorbing the consequences of whoever poured last month.

Here is the thing worth holding: every safeguard in this chapter is cheap.

A second sober adult in the room. A touch policy written on two pieces of paper. A dose expressed as a number. A name you can complain to that is not his. One person outside who expects to hear from you by noon. An integrator who does not profit from the ceremony.

Six items. None of them costs a thousand dollars. None of them requires regulation that does not exist, a license nobody issues, or permission from anybody. Every one of them can be adopted by any facilitator on earth this afternoon, and the ones who are good are mostly already doing four or five of them and will be delighted to be asked.

Which means the question is never really whether you can trust someone. Trust is not a method and it never has been. The question is whether the room is built so that trust is not the only thing standing between you and a bad outcome.

A real container has a window and a second adult and a piece of paper.

Everything else is a rug.

And here's the Kandi Trade — the vow.

- I will get the touch policy in writing, before, while I can still read.
- I will set my default to no in February, so that it holds at midnight without me.
- I will ask what the dose is in units, and I will decide about the second cup before I drink the first.
- I will ask who I complain to who is not him, and I will notice if there is no one.
- I will have one person outside who knows the address, the legal name, and the hour I am due back.
- I will book my integration before I go, with someone who does not profit from the ceremony.
- I will not take a life decision, a purchase, or a booking within seventy-two hours of coming down.
- And I will remember that a yes at hour three is not a yes I gave badly — that it was never mine to give in that state, that the responsibility belongs entirely to whoever built the evening around collecting it, and that the night can have been beautiful and still have contained something that was not.

PROTECT THE
