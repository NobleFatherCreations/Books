## The Fanny Pack (chapter recap)

Chapter 17. The front pocket. The one with the printed touch policy in it.

**The Consent That Expires (capacity, not willingness):** Consent obtained before an altered state cannot cover what happens during it. Yes is a sober word. A yes at hour three is not a yes you gave badly — it is not a yes, and the responsibility sits entirely with whoever built an evening where it could be collected.

The Unverifiable Lineage (credential laundering): You are not entitled to a man’s teachers. You are entitled to know who you complain to. “If something goes wrong here, who do I go to who is not you?” A lineage that produced exactly one practitioner is a biography.

**The Cup You Didn’t Measure (unquantified dosing):** The four questions from Chapter 16 apply in a nicer room. “Everyone gets what they need” is not a protocol, it is the refusal to have one. Decide about the second cup before you drink the first.

The Sealed Room (no witnesses, no record, no exit — all for good reasons): Confidentiality covers other participants’ material. It has never covered the conduct of the person running the room.

**The Integration Funnel (the aftercare is the business):** The ceremony is the customer acquisition cost. Your first interpreter should not be your only interpreter. Tuesday Quarantine applies to every offer made in that doorway.

**The whole test, per Bell:** how many sober adults are in the room, is the touch policy written down, and did you get it before you drank.

You can have had a profound, real, genuinely valuable night *and* have been harmed during it. Those are not in competition. The belief that naming the second cancels the first is the most effective silencing device in this world and it runs almost entirely inside your own head.

Set the default to no in writing, in February. It survives you, which is the only kind of boundary worth having before a state that dissolves boundaries.

💬 POCKET SCRIPTS — SAY THESE

**The five-sentence February email:** “Can you send me your written touch policy? What’s the dose and is it the same for everyone? Who else is facilitating and what’s their training? If I have a concern afterward, who do I raise it with besides you?”

**The boundary that survives your own incapacity:** “My consent for physical contact is no, for the whole ceremony. If I seem to ask for it in the moment, the answer is still no.”

**Pre-committing the dose, out loud, to a named person:** “One cup. Whatever I say at midnight, the answer is one cup.”

**Declining the second cup, decided in advance:** “I’ve already decided about seconds — thank you.”

**Declining the doorway offer:** “Thank you — I’ve already got someone lined up for integration.”

**The accountability question:** “If something goes wrong here, who do I go to who is not you?”

**The reference ask:** “Could you connect me with two past participants I could email?” — then: “Anything I should know?”

**The daylight contact instruction:** “If you haven’t heard from me by noon Sunday, call this number, and then this address.”
