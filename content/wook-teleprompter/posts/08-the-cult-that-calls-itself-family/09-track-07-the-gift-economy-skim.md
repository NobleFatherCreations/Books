### TRACK 07 — THE GIFT ECONOMY SKIM
*(sacred labor extraction)*

Here's the behavior. The camp has values. The values include service, mutual aid, contribution, gift economy, leave-no-trace, and “being of use to the magic.” Some of these values are completely genuine and run through the camp’s actual behavior like a spine. And some of them have been quietly turned into a labor extraction engine that benefits the inner ring at the expense of the middle ring and the first-season arrivals.

The pattern is not symmetrical.

Dinah, Hugo, and Tomás do most of the structural work. The inner ring does some visible, photographable work. The people who are adjacent to the inner ring do varying amounts. And the inner-ring members do none of the structural work and are not held accountable for this, because asking them to contribute proportionately would be “transactional,” or “default-world thinking,” or “not in the gift economy,” or “not in alignment with their capacity this season,” which is a phrase that seems to apply to the inner ring exclusively.

When the middle-ring workers are tired, they are told they are “leading from love.” When they ask for help, they are told “the camp is not built on guilt.” When they stop showing up, the camp’s values mysteriously shift to “honoring people’s capacity.” When they leave entirely, the camp produces a beautiful Instagram post about their departure that frames it as personal growth and doesn’t mention the labor distribution.

Then the camp recruits three new workers.

The Gift Economy Skim is the engine of cartel longevity.

Here's the read on it. Follow the labor. Who does the build, the kitchen, the strike, the storage run, the post-event cleaning? Then follow the access and the credit. Who gets the artist passes, the annual poster, the year-end acknowledgment, the inner-circle conversations? If the labor names and the credit names are different people, you are watching the skim operate.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Labor Cap. Step 1 — Before the season, set a labor cap. Specific, written, personal: “I’m available for X hours of build and Y hours of kitchen. That’s my contribution ceiling this season.”

Step 2 — Name the cap to the camp lead before the event. Not apologetically. As a logistical fact.

**Step 3 — When you hit the cap:** “I’ve hit my labor cap for this season.” Full stop. No negotiation in the context of festival conditions where saying no feels like abandoning family. The cap was set sober, in writing, in advance.

**Step 4 — Ask for the labor map:** “Can we do a quick labor audit before the season starts — who does what, for how many hours, across build, kitchen, and strike?” A camp that resists the labor audit is a camp that knows the audit will be damning.

**Step 5 — If the labor map reveals the Skim:** “I’m not willing to do more structural labor than inner-ring members this season. If there isn’t a way to equalize that, I need to reconsider my role here.”

Quick vibe check. Some people genuinely cannot do physical labor. Disability, chronic pain, recovery from injury, mental health conditions, and caregiving responsibilities all affect what people can contribute physically. That is real and the camp should accommodate it. The test is whether the accommodation applies equally or whether it applies selectively based on inner-ring status.

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever benefited from an asymmetrical labor structure and told yourself you contributed in other ways without examining whether those other ways were actually equivalent? Have you ever invoked “gift economy” to avoid a direct conversation about who is doing more work than their fair share? If yes: “leading from love” is a sentence that describes someone else’s contribution while you sit on cushions.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **Artists, Live Painters, Glovers, Hoopers:** If your craft is being treated as your camp contribution while you are also being asked to do structural labor, name that. Your performance is not equivalent to build labor. You can do both. You do not owe both.

**First Season:** You are most vulnerable to the Skim in your first year because the social equation is not yet clear and the lovebomb is still active. The labor cap set before the event is the only protection that is active before you need it.

This runs in every direction. The Gift Economy Skim requires people doing the structural labor and people not doing it and a values framework that prevents the discrepancy from being named. All three elements run across every camp identity. The mechanism is available to any camp that has values language, a labor-intensive existence, and an inner ring.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. The team where “we’re a startup family” is the reason nobody is paid overtime. The gift economy of startup culture is the Tuesday version of the same skim, with equity instead of artist passes and “mission alignment” instead of “being of use to the magic.”
