### TRACK 03 — THE SLEEPER CELL
*(long-game embedded predation)*

Here's the behavior. The Sleeper Cell is the one that makes everybody tired before the conversation starts.

They have been around forever. They helped build the shade dome in 2014. They know the ranger captain. They were at the first warehouse before the bathrooms had doors. They loaned the generator during the rain year. They introduced half the camp. They have photos with every beloved person and one story about saving someone during a bad trip that has become load-bearing architecture. Their reputation enters the room before the harmed person does. That is the weapon.

The Sleeper Cell does not need to grab your bag. They already helped decide where bags go. They do not need to isolate you loudly. They become the center of gravity — if you pull away, the whole camp tilts. If you speak up, people look at the calendar instead of the conduct. “But they’ve always been solid.” “They were there for me during the rough years.” “They do so much for the community.” “I’ve never seen that side.” No kidding, Moonbeam. That is what a side is.

The Sleeper Cell runs a long con through selective goodness. Generous in public. Strategic in private. Indispensable during crisis. Vague during accountability. Wounded when questioned. Surrounded by people who owe them one from six summers ago. They build a house out of receipts other people remember emotionally. Then they move the walls.

Here's the read on it. Watch for reputation shielding.

A good reputation can coexist with accountability. A shielded reputation cannot.

**Ask:** Do concerns about this person disappear into their history of good deeds? Are harmed people treated like threats to community stability? Does the group ask for impossible proof but accept the Wook’s vibes as evidence? Does everyone know “something happened” but nobody knows what can be said? Are newer people warned privately but not protected publicly? Does the person always survive the fallout cleaner than the people naming harm?

The Sleeper Cell’s tell is not that everyone hates them. The tell is that too many people have a private story and no shared language.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Three-Source Soundboard. Do not try to solo a Sleeper Cell with a kazoo.

Step 1 — Find three sources outside the same social circuit:

**Source A:** someone from your camp or crew.

**Source B:** someone from another camp, stage, or city.

**Source C:** someone default-world adjacent — therapist, organizer, former partner, sober friend, promoter, harm-reduction lead, housemate.

They cannot all be in the same group chat. That is not three sources. That is one group chat wearing three hats.

Step 2 — Ask only behavior questions. Not: “Do you think they’re a predator?” Ask: “Have you seen them retaliate when told no? Have you seen them isolate newer people? Have you seen them control access to rides, substances, housing, gigs, or camp resources? Have concerns been raised before? What happened after concerns were raised?”

Step 3 — Separate story from pattern. One bad breakup is data. Three people describing the same move in different costumes is a setlist.

Step 4 — Build a boring timeline. Dates. Locations. Behaviors. Witnesses. Outcomes. No adjectives if you can help it. “Creepy” loses in committee. “Asked three volunteers under twenty-five to share private sleeping space after late shifts in May, June, and August” survives the meeting.

Step 5 — Move protection before prosecution. You may not have enough to get the Wook removed by Friday. You may have enough to keep them away from first-timers, sleeping areas, intoxicated people, volunteers, green rooms, cashboxes, and people they have already targeted. Community safety does not have to wait for a courtroom-shaped thunderbolt.

Quick vibe check. Long-standing community members are not suspicious because they are long-standing. Do not punish people for being useful, beloved, awkward, old-school, or bad at Instagram apologies. The question is not: “Do they have power?” The question is: “What happens when their power is questioned?”

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever hidden behind your history? Have you ever said, “After all I’ve done for this camp,” when someone named a real harm? Have you ever let your old good deeds stand between you and a current repair? If your reputation cannot survive accountability, it is not a reputation. It is staging.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **Veteran:** Your nostalgia may be someone else’s gag order. The fact that they were kind to you in 2012 does not erase what they did last season.

**Builder/Sound Camp Crew:** Indispensable people become dangerous when nobody cross-trains their role. If only one person knows the generator, the patch bay, the trailer lock, or the stage plot, you built a throne by accident.

**Camp Lead:** Private warnings without public protocols create a predator aquarium. Everyone swims around the shark and calls it community.

**Sober/Disabled/Neurodivergent Readers:** If one embedded person controls your access needs, transportation, medication storage, sensory retreat, or sober support, their reputation can become your cage without them ever intending it.

This runs in every direction. The Sleeper Cell can be the beloved elder, the charming femme organizer, the queer house-scene connector, the disabled activist nobody wants to question, the sober leader with a decade chip, the touring bassist, the sound guy, the camp mom, the medicine man, the medicine woman, the medicine person with a headlamp and zero receipts. Identity can explain context. It does not cancel impact.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. In default world, this is the longtime employee who helped found the company and whose tenure makes every HR complaint into an institutional crisis. The performance review goes back to 2009. The harm happened in March. These are not the same thing.
