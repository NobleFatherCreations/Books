## The Mirror + The Discog

HAVE YOU BEEN THE WOOK? Mirror Check At The Disco Ball

Everybody wants this book to be about that guy.

The problem is the disco ball. It throws pieces of everyone.

Have you ever made your crisis the camp’s weather? Have you ever turned your favor into debt? Have you ever used “family” when you meant “free labor”? Have you ever gotten someone high and then treated their looseness as consent to your plan? Have you ever kept someone close because they made you feel important, even after they tried to step back? Have you ever let people believe a story because correcting it would cost you status?

Do not collapse into shame. Shame is just ego wearing wet socks.

Look at the behavior. Name the move. Stop running it. Repair what can be repaired. Accept what cannot.

That is the difference between being a person who has caused harm and being a Wook who made harm into a lifestyle brand.

Here's the Wook Discog for this chapter. Studio Debut — The Baby Wook learning the moves

Hopper, age twenty-two, at his first big festival. He does not yet know he is running plays. He is just — naturally — drawn to people who are newer, brokerer, and more impressionable than he is. He shares a joint. He helps with a shade structure. He notices that when he helps people, they are more likely to do things for him. He does not think of this as a system. It is just how things work. He is twenty-two and charismatic and everybody seems to like him, and that feels good, and the things that feel good are the things that stick. The Studio Debut is still mostly accidental. It is still improvisation. The harm is real and the intent is not fully formed. That is the record that does not have a press kit.

Live Album — The Seasoned Operator

Hopper at thirty-two. He has a crew. He has a route — specific lots, specific festivals, specific windows when the crowd turns over and the fresh tickets arrive. He knows the difference between a kid who will hold the tank and a kid who will run. He reads nervousness, gratitude, and hunger with the ease of someone who has been reading them for a decade. He has a cover story for every situation. The good deeds are real. The math is also real. He does not think of himself as a predator. He thinks of himself as a guy who knows how things work. The Live Album sounds great at volume and loses something in the quiet.

Greatest Hits Compilation — The Institutional Scaled-Up Version

Hopper at fifty. He does not hold the tank. He does not need to. He has four people who owe him well enough to hold the tank without a conversation. He has a legend in three regions and a story about saving somebody that has been passed around for fifteen years and grows slightly with each telling. He is invited to sit on the safety committee of a mid-size festival that has not yet figured out that the committee is where a Sleeper Cell goes when the lot gets too hot. The Greatest Hits Compilation comes with liner notes nobody wrote.
