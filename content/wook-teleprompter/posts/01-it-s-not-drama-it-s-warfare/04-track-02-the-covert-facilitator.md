### TRACK 02 — THE COVERT FACILITATOR
*(ingratiation grooming)*

Here's the behavior. The Covert Facilitator is where the scene gets stupid, because this one looks useful.

They have the extra wristband. The spare tent. The artist contact. The golf cart ride. The shade cloth. The plug. The green room password. The volunteer lead’s ear. The invite to the “real afters.” The van seat. The generator adapter nobody else packed because nobody else thinks in adapters. They solve problems with one hand and invoice your soul with the other.

At first, it feels like rescue. Your shift runs late — they cover it. Your ride flakes — they “got you.” Your camp forgot stakes — they have extras. Your set time moves and the green room is locked — they have the code. Each rescue is real. The kindness inside it is real. And every single one goes on a tab you didn’t see being opened.

The thing about the Covert Facilitator is that the debt is not financial. Financial debt has numbers. This debt is relational. You owe them your loyalty, your positive word-of-mouth, your silence during difficult moments, your presence at their set, your endorsement to the people they want to reach, your willingness to look the other way when someone else describes an experience that sounds familiar. The payment is always extracted at the worst possible time — when you most need to speak, when you most need to leave, when you most need to be believed.

They do not make you feel trapped. They make you feel grateful. Trapped people run. Grateful people stay.

Here's the read on it. A clean favor increases your options. A Wook favor narrows them.

**Ask:** Can you leave this person’s orbit without social cost? Can you say no to their offer without losing standing? Has their help ever arrived at a moment that also happened to create dependence? Do they remind you — verbally or atmospherically — of what they have done for you?

**Watch for the Inventory:** not a bill, just the frequent, casual accounting of their generosity. “After everything I set up for your camp.” “I called in a favor for that.” “I was the one who got you that access.” The Covert Facilitator has a spreadsheet where you have memories. That asymmetry is the weapon.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Clean Plate Protocol. Step 1 — Before accepting significant help (a ride, a couch, a wristband, a slot, housing, a connect), ask yourself: what would they need from me that I would find hard to say no to? Not paranoid. Just awake.

Step 2 — Name the terms out loud. “I really appreciate this. I want to make sure we’re both clear this isn’t creating an obligation.” Watch the reaction. A clean helper says “of course.” A Wook says “of course” and goes quiet in a way that means the opposite.

Step 3 — Pay it forward, not back. If someone gives you a clean thing, you give something equal forward — to someone else, if possible. This breaks the bilateral debt loop.

Step 4 — At any point when you feel the pull of I can’t say no because of what they did — that is the tab calling. Name it internally and act in your own interest anyway.

Step 5 — One safe person is not a network. It is a choke point. If one person has become your only access to transportation, housing, work, belonging, or care — audit how that happened and start building parallel access this week. Not this season. This week.

Quick vibe check. Some people are just generous. Some communities run on gift culture that is genuinely reciprocal. Not every favor carries a tab. The tell is not the giving. The tell is what happens when you stop receiving — do they stay equally warm? Do they maintain equal investment? Or does the whole thing recalibrate around what you can give back?

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever reminded someone of a favor while they were in the process of saying no to you? Have you ever leveraged someone’s gratitude to override their hesitation? Have you ever made your help feel contingent on loyalty? The Covert Facilitator is the hardest Wook to see from the inside because the giving feels genuinely good — and it often is genuinely good — and the invoice comes so quietly.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **Queer or Trans Festie:** If your only safe person at an event is the person who booked you or housed you or connected you, their reputation becomes your cage. Build lateral networks before you need them.

**BIPOC Festie:** In predominantly white festival spaces, the person who “takes you under their wing” first carries particular power. That power is not automatically clean. Watch the terms.

**Sober/ND Festie:** If someone controls your access to sober space, sensory retreat, or the logistical structure that makes the event safe for your body — your gratitude is real and the leverage is real. They do not have to be malicious. The architecture of dependence is still the architecture of dependence.

**Artist/DJ:** The booker who helps you break through and never stops reminding you they broke you through has installed themselves as a permanent tax on your career.

This runs in every direction. The Covert Facilitator can be your boyfriend with the van, your girlfriend with the guest list, your nonbinary camp lead with the only shower key, your polycule hub with all the invites, your sober mentor with the moral high ground, your love-and-light camp mom who reframes every boundary you set as you ‘not being open,’ your favorite DJ’s manager with a laminated smile. Power is not gendered. Access is access.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. At work, this is the coworker who covered your shift twice and now mentions it during every performance review. In your social circle, this is the friend who has bankrolled enough dinners to have standing to say “I think you’re making a mistake” as a full sentence, no follow-up required.
