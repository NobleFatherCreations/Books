## The Mirror + The Discog

HAVE YOU BEEN THE WOOK? Mirror Check At One In The Morning

Almost nobody in this chapter is a Wook. That is the thing that makes it the hardest mirror in the book, because there is no villain to be relieved you are not.

The two security staff who walked up to Tee were doing their jobs and would fail no test anyone could design. The person at the fire who said “you got anything” loves him. The friends of nine years who do not know the protocol exists are good friends. Not one of them did a single thing that would survive being written up.

So the mirror here is not *have you been the predator.* It is: **have you been one of the four seconds?**

Have you decided, in under a second, which of two altered strangers was having a night and which was becoming a problem — and have you ever gone back and checked whether you sort evenly? Almost nobody has checked. The checking is the entire ask of this chapter.

Have you given safety advice that was autobiography? Counted the number of times you say *just* in front of an action that is only simple for you?

Have you handed the bag to whoever was closest?

Have you asked somebody if they were staff, and then, when it landed wrong, spent the next four minutes managing your own embarrassment in a way that made them comfort you?

Have you been in a room where a decision got made — a booking, a placement, a complaint, a hire — and not noticed who was not in it? That is the most consequential item on this list and it is entirely composed of an absence, which is the hardest thing in the world to see, because absences do not show up in a photo.

And the one for readers who are running the protocol, which is a different mirror and it belongs here too: have you decided, at a fire at 1:51 a.m., that it was cheaper to absorb it than to name it — and then been told later, by someone who loves you, that they had no idea? The absorbing is completely rational. It is also how nine years go by with people who would have carried it and were never asked.

There is no accusation in this section. There is one request, and it is small: **go back and check the sort.** Not your beliefs, which are probably fine. The four seconds, which nobody has ever audited, and which decide which door a stranger goes through on the worst night of their month.

Here's the Wook Discog for this chapter. Studio Debut — The Baby Wook learning the moves

Twenty. First year on a volunteer safety team, given a radio and about nine minutes of training. Learns within one weekend — from nobody, from no document, from watching two shifts — which people you walk over and check on and which people you call in about. Could not tell you the rule. Does not know there is a rule. Would sincerely and correctly say that he treats everyone the same, and he is not lying, because the sorting does not happen anywhere he can see it. The Studio Debut is the moment a reflex trained entirely outside this field walks through the gate wearing a hi-vis vest, and nobody notices, because nothing happened.

Live Album — The Seasoned Operator

Year eight. Shift lead. Makes the four-second call somewhere around two hundred times a weekend, and has never once been told that it is a call. Has genuinely de-escalated situations that would have gone badly. Has genuinely gotten people to the Zendo who needed the Zendo. Is, by every measure the event uses, very good at this job — and has never seen an aggregate of his own routing decisions, because no such aggregate exists at any festival in this country, because nobody has ever built one, because building one would require deciding to look.

Greatest Hits Compilation — The Institutional Scaled-Up Version

The aftermovie cut to be beautifully diverse, playing on a loop above an org chart that is not. The safety literature that says “alert a staff member” to forty thousand people and has never once marked the assumption inside it. The incident log showing fewer reports from the people who report least, cited in a meeting as evidence that things are fine. The scene built on Black American music whose booking room, production company, board and safety committee assembled themselves out of who was already there, in perfect good faith, every year, for fifty years. Nobody scaled this up on purpose. It scaled up because nothing was ever counted, and the thing about not counting is that it always produces the same answer.
