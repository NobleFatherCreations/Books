### TRACK 02 — THE “I GOT YOU” DEPENDENCY BUILD
*(supplier-as-caregiver role merge)*

Here's the behavior. Wedge knows your preferred form. He knows your tolerance. He knows which products work well together for you and which combinations he has seen create problems for people with your profile. He texts back fast. He is discreet. He comes to you rather than making you navigate to him. He remembers your camp name without being reminded. He asks how you are doing and means it in the specific way of someone who has a professional interest in your wellbeing that is genuine and also not disinterested.

“I got you” is his most-used phrase. It means something precise in his usage: I am your person for this. You do not need to look elsewhere. You do not need to navigate the uncertainty of a new source. You do not need to do the four-questions protocol from Chapter 11 because I have already answered all four questions for you and the answers are good and I am the person who stands between you and a world where the answers might not be good.

The “I got you” is the warmth. The warmth is real. The warmth is also the cage: a warm place whose walls only become visible when you try to leave it.

Dependency builds over time in the specific way that any relationship deepens — through positive interactions, through reliable service, through the accumulation of being-known. The difference is that Wedge is actively engineering the accumulation. The “I got you” is not the spontaneous expression of care. It is the positioning statement of a supply relationship optimized for retention.

Here's the read on it. Does your source want you informed, capable, and free to verify what they tell you? Or does your source position themselves as the person whose job it is to have already done the verification for you, so that your verification instinct points toward them rather than toward independent confirmation?

A real vendor gives you the tools. A Wook plug gives you the conclusions.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Regular Source Audit. **Every three months:** audit the source relationship.

Step 1 — Can I currently buy what I need from a second source, independently, without significant friction? If no: the dependency has formed. Build the second source now.

Step 2 — Has the quality or quantity varied without disclosure in the last cycle? If yes: the trust asymmetry is being used against you.

Step 3 — Would I tell my source I’m doing this audit? Would they respond well? If the audit feels like a betrayal: the relationship has been engineered to feel that way.

Step 4 — Test the source’s reaction to the two-source discipline. Tell them you’re diversifying. A real vendor says fine. A Wook plug makes you feel disloyal.

Quick vibe check. Some sources genuinely are the best available and the loyalty is earned through consistently excellent product and service. Loyalty that is earned through performance is legitimate. Loyalty that is engineered through dependency architecture is not. The distinction is: what happens when you threaten to leave? Relief that you found what you needed elsewhere is a clean source. Anything else is not.

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever positioned yourself as the person whose job it is to handle the verification for someone else, so that their instinct to verify points toward you rather than away from you? Have you ever made someone feel like diversifying their support network was a betrayal? The “I got you” is also the sentence that sober mentors use, that camp leads use, that romantic partners use. It carries the same leverage regardless of the domain.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **Recovery Lane:** The sponsor relationship has a version of the “I got you” dependency build built into its legitimate structure. Real sponsors support the development of independent sobriety tools. Sponsors who make themselves irreplaceable — the only person you can call, the only person who understands, the only safe person in the scene — have converted a legitimate care role into a structural dependency that serves them.

**Solo Festies:** The Wedge dependency builds fastest on people who don’t have an existing supply network to compare against. Solo festie with no reference class is the ideal target for the “I got you” positioning. You don’t know what else is available. You don’t know what the comparison looks like. You have one data point and the data point is performing very well.

This runs in every direction. The dependency gets built by the plug who remembers your order, the camp connect who always has extra, the touring supplier who checks in between runs, and anyone whose genuine care produces structural dependency whether they meant it to or not. It runs across every supply relationship dynamic regardless of the supplier’s gender or the customer’s.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. The HOA board member who is the only one with the key to the clubhouse, the pool schedule, and the neighborhood Facebook group password — so the whole community’s dependency on him becomes total and the role becomes immune to question. “I got you” as a volunteer-committee control mechanism. Works the same way.
