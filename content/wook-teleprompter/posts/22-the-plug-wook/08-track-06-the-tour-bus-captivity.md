### TRACK 06 — THE TOUR-BUS CAPTIVITY
*(mid-leg supply chain dependency)*

Here's the behavior. You are two events into a five-event run.

You are in someone else’s vehicle. Your supply for the next three events is in Wedge’s Sprinter. Your financial arrangement with Wedge involves a credit structure that makes sense at event one and becomes significantly more complicated by event three when the credit has accumulated to a level that the event-four cash flow will not cleanly cover. You are in a city two hundred miles from your default home base. Your alternative sources have not been cultivated because the first two events with Wedge were good and the first two events with Wedge were the only data you thought you needed.

This is the Tour-Bus Captivity. It is the Sunk Cost Swamp applied to logistics, run at the specific scale of a multi-day touring scenario where the dependency becomes load-bearing before you realize it is load-bearing. The captivity does not require a locked door. It requires: being two events in, having supply in someone else’s vehicle, owing money that is easier to settle by continuing the relationship than by disrupting it, and having no alternative source cultivated.

By event three, the tour is running Wedge’s route.

Not yours.

Here's the read on it. **Before any multi-event run:** map the supply chain independence for the full run. Not just the first event. If you cannot answer the question “if this source falls through at event three, what is my alternative” before event one — you have already begun building the captivity.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Run Independence Plan. **Step 1 — Before a multi-event run:** identify the full run’s supply needs. Total quantity. Total type.

Step 2 — Establish at minimum two verified independent sources capable of covering the full run, either individually or jointly.

Step 3 — No credit arrangements that compound across events. Each event’s financial arrangement closes completely before the next event begins. A credit arrangement that accumulates across events is a dependency instrument.

Step 4 — Your supply does not travel in someone else’s vehicle unless you have verified, documented access to it at all times, at your discretion, without requiring their cooperation.

**Step 5 — The Van Rule:** if your supply is in their van and they are driving, you do not have your supply. What you have is access to your supply contingent on their cooperation. That is not supply independence. That is the captivity wearing a logistics arrangement.

Quick vibe check. Multi-event runs with a single trusted source are common and work well. The test is not whether you work with one source across a run. The test is whether you could leave the arrangement at any point without significant logistical cost. If you can: not captivity. If you can’t: captivity, regardless of how comfortable and warm the arrangement has been.

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever structured a supply arrangement, housing arrangement, transportation arrangement, or professional arrangement in a way that made it difficult for the other person to exit mid-run without significant cost? Have you ever let someone’s credit accumulate across events in a way that created dependency rather than resolved it? The Tour-Bus Captivity runs in every domain where someone else’s logistics have become the infrastructure of your movement.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **Touring Artists:** The tour-bus captivity runs literally in the touring music industry. The band on a management-controlled tour with a management-controlled supply chain in a management-controlled vehicle on a management-controlled route, with a credit arrangement that makes leaving the management before the run ends financially catastrophic. This is the Track at its institutional scale. Build independence into the touring infrastructure before you need it.

**New Vendors:** The vendor who gets a discounted booth spot at a festival circuit in exchange for a multi-event commitment that includes supply arrangements that benefit the booth operator who gave them the spot. The discount is the free first bump. The multi-event commitment is the Captivity beginning.

This runs in every direction. The Captivity runs through the supply arrangement that’s costly to walk away from, the touring crew position that’s hard to quit mid-run, the camp housing that’s tied to staying loyal, and any relationship where the cost of leaving has outgrown the value of staying. It runs in every context where the mid-leg exit is economically or logistically costly.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. The subscription that is trivial to start and requires a phone call during business hours and a thirty-day notice period to cancel. The gym membership that required no commitment to join and requires a certified letter to exit. The Tour-Bus Captivity is the default business model of every service that has decided captive customers are more profitable than satisfied ones.
