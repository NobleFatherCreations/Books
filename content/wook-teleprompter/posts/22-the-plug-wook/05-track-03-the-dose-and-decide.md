### TRACK 03 — THE DOSE-AND-DECIDE
*(incapacitation prior to consent moments)*

Here's the behavior. The timing of the ask is not accidental.

The offer that requires a significant decision — more supply on credit, an upgrade to a larger quantity, the accommodation that seems convenient and turns out to require access, the favor that seems small and turns out to be a choke point — arrives after the product has been administered and before full cognitive function has returned.

“Hey, while I’m here — if you need more before Sunday I can front you and you settle up Monday.” Said at the precise moment when: the product is active, the person is warm and open and not running the full credit-risk calculation, and the relationship’s warmth has been recently reinforced by the positive experience of the product itself.

The Dose-And-Decide is not necessarily violent or coercive. It is timing. The timing is the manipulation. The ask that would produce “let me think about that” at noon on a Tuesday produces “yeah, sure, that’d be great” at eleven p.m. after the second cap is properly seated.

This runs especially in the access-for-access direction: “I can get you into the green room/secret set/backstage/afters/the camp that’s hard to get into if you want to connect on Sunday.” The offer is real. The timing is chosen.

Here's the read on it. Notice when significant offers arrive relative to your capacity state. Ask: would this offer have been made at noon on Thursday? If not — the timing is strategic. The offer may be real and the timing may still be strategic.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Sober Monday Rule. Any offer that arrives while you are under the influence of any substance that meaningfully affects your decision-making:

“That sounds potentially interesting. I’ll check in with you Monday when I can think about it clearly.”

Full sentence. Warm. Non-committal. Does not require you to evaluate the offer now.

If the offer evaporates because you won’t decide on altered time: the offer was the timing and not the thing.

Real offers survive Monday.

Quick vibe check. Some timing is coincidental. Wedge is present at the festival when you are present at the festival. His offers arrive when you are both there. Not all of it is strategic. The test is: does the offer require a fast decision, and does the fast decision benefit Wedge specifically?

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever timed an ask for when you knew the other person’s capacity was most open to it? Have you ever introduced a proposal at a dinner party rather than a Tuesday meeting because the dinner party’s social warmth makes refusal harder? The timing is the leverage. The leverage runs at all scales.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **Ceremony Lane:** The post-ceremony period is the highest-dependency, lowest-capacity window in any psychedelic facilitation context. Integration discussions, housing arrangements, financial commitments, and ongoing relationship structures that get established in the post-ceremony window carry the full vulnerability of the Dose-And-Decide. Real facilitators do not use the post-ceremony window for logistics. Wook facilitators do.

This runs in every direction. The Dose-And-Decide gets run by the plug who waits until you’re already in it, the camp connect who frames the ask as casual, the touring supplier whose timing is never accidental, and anyone whose capacity to negotiate depends on someone else’s capacity to wait. It runs across every supply relationship and every context where one person’s capacity is affected and the other person’s timing is strategic. It is not gendered. It has a very good close rate.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. The job offer with a forty-eight-hour deadline, timed to arrive on a Friday afternoon when your weekend plans are falling through and your current job has just had a bad week. The timing is selected. The offer may be real. The timing is still selected.
