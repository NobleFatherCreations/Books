## Tales From The Mojave Weekend — The Save

Festie POV. Same scenario. Counter-Drops live. Named protagonist. Watermelon Moment present.

Kai Nakamura has been buying from Wedge for three events.

The product has been good. The service has been good. Wedge texts back fast, is discreet, knows Kai’s preferred forms, and has twice provided genuinely useful harm-reduction information that Kai verified independently and found to be accurate. This is a supply relationship that, by every metric Kai currently has available, is performing excellently.

Kai is twenty-nine. He has been attending festivals for six years. He has read enough about supply relationships to have a working theoretical model of dependency architecture. He has not applied the model to Wedge specifically because Wedge specifically has never triggered the model’s detection threshold.

**This is the watermelon moment:** not a dramatic one, not in the chest, but in the head, on the Thursday before event four, when Kai is doing the pre-event prep and realizes, running through the logistics, that he does not currently have a second source. He has been meaning to cultivate one since event two. He has not done it. Wedge’s availability has made it easy to not do it. The “I got you” has been doing its job and its job has been, partly, preventing Kai from developing the redundancy that the Own-Source Discipline requires.

He is not in a crisis. He is in a slowly accumulating dependency that has not yet cost him anything.

He does the audit.

He goes through the Regular Source Audit protocol from Track 2. Three months of transactions. Quality: consistent. Disclosure: apparently complete. The apparently is what he notices on the way through. He has been trusting apparently rather than verified. He has been accepting Wedge’s disclosures as accurate because Wedge’s previous disclosures have been accurate and his own testing has confirmed them. Except for the last event, when he didn’t run his own test because he was running late and Wedge had photos of the test results, and Kai accepted the photos as sufficient.

The photos are from a different batch.

He doesn’t know this yet. He finds it out later. Right now all he has is the observation that he accepted photos rather than running his own test, which violates his own protocol, which violated his own protocol because the relationship’s warmth and track record had lowered his vigilance below his own stated standards.

He texts a person he met at event two whose supply reputation he has heard good things about. He does not explain why he is reaching out. He says he is looking to diversify his sources and heard they might be able to help.

The person texts back and the supply relationship begins.

At event four, he buys from both sources. He tests both independently. The Wedge product tests clean. The new source tests clean. He notes the comparison: same result, slightly different presentation, similar pricing.

He has a second source.

**He sends Wedge a brief, warm message:** “Hey, branching out a bit this run. Will still be in touch.”

Wedge texts back immediately, warm, no temperature drop: “Of course. Smart to have options. I appreciate the heads up. Let me know if you need anything this weekend.”

This is actually a green flag. The warm response to the diversification is the clean source telling on itself. Kai notes it. The source that accepts the diversification without cooling is the source that was never running the dependency architecture.

Over the next two events, Kai rotates between sources. He runs his own test every transaction. He does not extend credit that accumulates. He has no supply in any vehicle that is not his own vehicle.

He is not perfectly safe. There is no perfectly safe. But he has removed the single-source dependency and the accumulated credit and the testing gap, which are the three specific vulnerabilities that the dependency architecture requires.

Wedge remains a good source. The relationship continues. The relationship is now one of two.

The cage’s walls are not gone. But the door is open.
