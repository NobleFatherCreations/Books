### TRACK 06 — FESTIVAL-LEVEL REPORTING PATHWAYS
*(institutional escalation architecture)*

Here's the behavior. The festival’s reporting pathway is listed on the website.

It has been listed on the website since the lawsuit in 2019 that recommended that the festival document its accountability processes. It lists: an email address, a form, and a phone number that goes to a voicemail box that is monitored during festival hours.

The email address goes to the same inbox as the vendor inquiries.

The voicemail box was last checked in April.

The festival’s safety director has three other roles at the festival and is specifically not present during festival hours because during festival hours they are managing the medical tent.

This is the institutional version of the routing around the missing stair: the apparatus of accountability is present. The function of accountability is not.

Here's the read on it. **For any institutional reporting pathway:** test it. Not theoretically — specifically.

Does the email address go to a real person? Call the number. Does someone answer? Send the test form. Does someone respond?

The pathway that looks functional and is not functional is worse than no pathway, because it produces the experience of having reported while producing no institutional response, which is the Conduct Committee Cold Shoulder from Track 8 in its gentlest form.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Reporting Pathway Audit. Step 1 — Identify the festival’s reporting mechanisms. Not from the website — from a direct inquiry to the safety coordinator at the event.

**Step 2 — Ask specifically:** Who reviews reports during the event? Who reviews them post-event? What is the response timeline? What are the possible outcomes?

**Step 3 — Before submitting a formal report:** establish that the pathway is functional by confirming the above. If it is not functional, ask who the alternative is.

**Step 4 — Parallel-track reporting:** festival safety AND harm-reduction organizations AND the scene’s informal networks, simultaneously. Do not wait for the institutional pathway to respond before protecting people informally.

Step 5 — Document your own report. Date, content, pathway used, response received. Your documentation is the record if the institutional pathway fails.

Quick vibe check. Some festivals have genuinely functional accountability infrastructure. The audit is not an accusation of the festival. It is the confirmation of what exists so that you know what you can rely on.

Turn it around — the mirror set. **If you run or work for a festival:** is your reporting pathway functional? When was it last tested? Who reviews reports during the event? If you cannot answer those questions without research: the pathway may be a website item rather than a functional system.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **Performers and Artists with Leverage:** A performer with a contract can require documented accountability mechanisms as a contract condition. The contract is leverage to require the festival to build what it should have built. Use it.

This runs in every direction. The Reporting Pathway Audit applies the same way to the festival’s official channel, the camp organization’s internal process, the practitioner collective’s ethics board, and the booker network’s informal grapevine. Different institutional contexts. Same audit mechanics.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. The HR complaint process that routes to the manager who is the subject of the complaint. The EEO form that goes to a shared inbox with the legal department that represents the company. The reporting pathway that is legally present and functionally absent. Test before you need.
