🎛️ THE TAPERS’ SECTION

Diffusion of Innovation · Survivor Network Theory · Institutional Betrayal

Brief. The chapter is long. The theory is proportionate.

Diffusion of innovation is the sociological concept describing how new practices spread through communities: from the early adopters, through the early majority, to the late majority, until the practice becomes normative. The Sister Lou briefing, the Own-Source Discipline, the Napkin Protocol — these are harm-reduction innovations at varying stages of diffusion in the scene. The chapter’s job is to accelerate diffusion.

Survivor network theory is the documented finding that survivor disclosure networks produce dramatically better outcomes when the disclosures are connected across isolated survivors rather than held individually. The Signal thread of six women is not more powerful than any individual survivor’s account. It is exponentially more powerful. The connection is the amplification. The connection is the work.

Institutional betrayal is Jennifer Freyd’s concept: the additional harm that institutions produce when they fail to respond appropriately to disclosures — when they prioritize institutional protection over accountability, when they use the mechanisms of accountability as protection against accountability. The scene’s institutions — festivals, camps, booker networks, practitioner organizations — are capable of institutional betrayal. Naming the mechanism is the first step to building the infrastructure that prevents it.
