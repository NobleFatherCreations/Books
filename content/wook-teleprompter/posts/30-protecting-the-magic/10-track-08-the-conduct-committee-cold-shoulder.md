### TRACK 08 — THE CONDUCT COMMITTEE COLD SHOULDER
*(institutional betrayal / DARVO at scale)*

Here's the behavior. She submitted the report.

She submitted it through the festival’s formal process — the email address, the form, the documented pathway. She included the specific behavior, the specific date, the specific witnesses she could name. She submitted it in March. It is now October.

**She has received:** one auto-reply confirming the form was received, one follow-up email from someone named “Festival Community Team” asking if she would like to speak with a mediator, and nothing else.

The person she reported is on the lineup at the festival’s next event, announced in September. The lineup announcement includes his photo and a quote about the festival’s “commitment to creating space for authentic artistry.”

**This is the Conduct Committee Cold Shoulder:** the institutional response to a harm disclosure that uses the apparatus of accountability to produce the appearance of accountability while producing no accountability. The form that goes to the vendor inbox. The mediation offer that substitutes a facilitated conversation for an investigation. The eight-month silence that is not a decision to not act — it is a decision to let inaction become the institutional response through the passage of time.

**DARVO in its institutional form:** the festival Denies the severity of the harm, Attacks the reporter’s standing by offering mediation as if the harm were a dispute between equals, Victimizes itself by framing any criticism of the response as an attack on the festival’s “authentic artistry” commitments.

Here's the read on it. **The Cold Shoulder has specific markers:** response to the form but no follow-up communication about outcome, indefinite timelines without updates, mediation offered as a substitute for investigation, continued platform for the named person without any communication about why.

**When you encounter these markers:** the institutional process has failed. Parallel-track.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Parallel-Track Protocol. Step 1 — Submit the formal report AND inform one harm-reduction organization, one survivor network, and one scene accountability body simultaneously. Not after the institutional process fails — simultaneously. The parallel track is not backup. It is infrastructure.

Step 2 — Set a response timeline expectation in the submission: “I am submitting this report and requesting a response within thirty days regarding the status of any investigation.” The stated expectation creates documentation of the institutional failure if the response does not come.

Step 3 — At thirty days without a substantive response: send the escalation. “I submitted a report on [date] and have not received a substantive response. I am following up and requesting a status update by [specific date].”

Step 4 — At sixty days without a substantive response: the institutional process has failed. The parallel tracks are now your primary channels.

Step 5 — Document everything. Every submission, every response, every follow-up, every silence. The documentation is the record if the institutional failure itself becomes the subject of accountability.

Quick vibe check. Some institutional delays are genuine capacity problems, not Cold Shoulders. The festival safety coordinator who is managing a medical emergency is not able to process your form simultaneously. The sixty-day timeline accounts for genuine capacity constraints while distinguishing them from institutional abandonment.

Turn it around — the mirror set. If you work for or administer an institutional accountability process: is your process a Cold Shoulder? When was the last form reviewed? What was the last outcome? If you cannot answer: check.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **Scene Journalists and Documentarians:** The documented institutional failure — the eight-month silence while the named person remains on the lineup — is a documented public accountability failure. The documentation you accumulated in the process is the story if you choose to tell it.

This runs in every direction. The Cold Shoulder gets run by the festival committee that’s “still reviewing” six months later, the collective’s ethics board that never quite convenes, the camp organization whose process exists on paper and nowhere else, and institutions of every size, every stated value, and every explicit accountability commitment. The mechanism doesn’t care about the mission statement. The Parallel-Track Protocol applies universally.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. The HR department that received the complaint in Q1 and is “still in the process of reviewing” in Q4. The insurance company that received the claim eight months ago and has been “working on it.” The Cold Shoulder is the institutional face of every system that benefits from the passage of time as a resolution mechanism.
