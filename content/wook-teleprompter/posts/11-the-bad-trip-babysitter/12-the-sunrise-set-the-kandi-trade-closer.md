## The Sunrise Set + The Kandi Trade (closer)

Here's the Sunrise Set. Zero slang — just the true thing. Some nights end at the medical tent.

That is not a failure. That is the night doing what nights sometimes do, which is to hand you something you did not plan for and ask you to respond with the best version of yourself that is available at three a.m. with eleven percent battery.

The girl in the unicorn onesie is the best version. She did not plan to be there. She was in the porta line. She saw what was happening and she asked the one question that was available to her, which was the right question, which was: “Do you need help?”

The answer is yes.

The answer is almost always yes.

You are allowed to need help. You are allowed to be the person who asks for it on behalf of someone who is gripping your arm and telling you not to. You are allowed to say “I hear you” and then walk toward the vest and the clipboard and the kind face and the words “we don’t know what” and the four-hour rule and the recovery position and the pre-loaded decision that you made three days ago in the parking lot in fifteen minutes over a boring note on your phone.

The magic is not bulletproof. It is not supposed to be bulletproof. It is supposed to be beautiful, which it is, and it is supposed to be held by a village that knows the recovery position and has the Zendo starred on the map.

You are part of the village.

You knew that already.

Now you know the rest.

And here's the Kandi Trade — the vow.

- I will ask the four questions before any cap. What it is. Whether it’s tested and with what. The dose. The source. If none of those have a specific answer, I pass.
- I will have the Zendo starred on my map before the first night.
- I will run the crew safety brief. Fifteen minutes. Every event.
- I will know the recovery position. Left side, upper knee forward, head back, airway clear.
- I will remember the four words. “We don’t know what.”
- I will say yes when the unicorn onesie asks if I need help.
- I will send the harm-information text before I talk myself out of it.
- I will be the sober anchor when it is my turn to be the sober anchor.
- And I will remember that the warm forty-five-minute conversation and the untested cap are not opposites — they are the same move, and knowing that is what makes me part of the village and not part of the problem.
