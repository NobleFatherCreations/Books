## The Mirror + The Discog

HAVE YOU BEEN THE WOOK? Mirror Check At The Porta Line

The Wook in this chapter is warm. The Wook in this chapter is genuinely good company. The Wook in this chapter may not know they are the Wook in this chapter, which is the chapter’s hardest sentence to read.

Have you ever given someone something at a festival without knowing specifically what it was? Have you ever said “it’s fine, my guy is solid” without having tested it yourself? Have you ever handed something over in forty-five minutes of good conversation because the conversation was good and the gesture felt generous and the logistics — testing, dosing, disclosure — seemed like an interruption of the warmth?

The warmth was real. The logistics were missing. The person in the porta line three hours later has no way to separate those two facts.

This is not a verdict. Most people who have ever handed something over have not intended harm. The scene runs on a culture of generosity that is real and beautiful and that also, in the absence of the four questions, produces the Medical Tent Math at three a.m.

Run the four questions. Every time. Even when the conversation is warm. Especially when the conversation is warm.

Because the warmth is the funnel.

Here's the Wook Discog for this chapter. Studio Debut — The Baby Wook learning the moves

Twenty-two, at their third festival, offering their friends a substance someone else gave them because sharing felt like the right move and the vibe was good. Has not yet thought about testing. Has not yet connected “I gave them this” to “I am responsible for knowing what this is.” The harm, if it comes, will be the first real data point about what responsible means in this context. The Studio Debut does not have receipts. It does not know yet that receipts are the job.

Live Album — The Seasoned Operator

The forty-five-minute warm guy at the bass stage. He has been doing this for years. Not dealing — sharing. He has a source he trusts. He has not tested in three months because his source has been solid for three months and the testing feels like distrust of his source. He does not think of himself as a risk vector. He thinks of himself as generous. He has given caps to maybe two hundred people over three years and nothing bad has happened. The Live Album cannot hear the silence that precedes the bad batch. The Live Album will hear it once.

Greatest Hits Compilation — The Institutional Scaled-Up Version

The festival that does not provide DanceSafe or Bunk Police access because it “doesn’t want to encourage drug use.” The event that has a no-tolerance policy that prevents its medical staff from receiving accurate information about what people have taken. The promoter who does not publish Good Samaritan amnesty information because it “sends the wrong message.” The Greatest Hits Compilation is not a person. It is a policy. The policy is running the Free Cap Funnel at institutional scale by creating the information vacuum the Funnel requires. The gray lips at three a.m. are downstream of the policy as much as they are downstream of the guy who did not test.
