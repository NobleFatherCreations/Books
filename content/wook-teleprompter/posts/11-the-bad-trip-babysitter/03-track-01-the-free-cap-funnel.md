### TRACK 01 — THE FREE CAP FUNNEL
*(false-intimacy bonding via gifted substance)*

Here's the behavior. He does not approach strangers. He builds relationships first.

Forty-five minutes. An hour. Maybe longer. The conversation is real — he is genuinely warm, genuinely interested, genuinely good at reading what a person wants to feel and giving it to them. He knows the bands you’re there to see. He has a story about the last time they played that you could not have. He laughs at your jokes with the specific quality of someone who was actually listening. By the time the bag comes out, you have been talking long enough that he is not a stranger. He is the cool guy you met at the bass stage who you told that thing to.

**And then:** “Here, on the house. You’re my dude.”

The cap is untested. He has not told you this. He may not know this — or he may know this and have decided that his source’s two-year track record is sufficient assurance, which is the sentence that precedes the worst batches in the history of the scene, and which will be the chapter’s north star when we get to Chapter 15.

The Free Cap Funnel is the Chapter 5 Kandi Debt Racket applied to substances with the specific amplification that the debt is not about money. The debt is about belonging. The warmth he gave you over forty-five minutes was real and it has created the social weight of a friend, and refusing something a friend offers is a different calculation than refusing something a stranger offers.

That calculation is the funnel.

The Discernment Hook

The manipulation in this chapter is upstream of the crisis. By the time Marco’s lips go gray, the Wook is at a different stage and the evidence of the play is gone. This is what makes the Free Cap Funnel harder to name than the Overt Lot Rat or the Covert Facilitator: it looks indistinguishable, in the moment, from genuine generosity. The forty-five minutes was real. The warmth was real. The cap is also real and it is also untested and also the person who gave it to you has no receipts and is not in the porta line with you.

The read is not in the cap. The read is in the test kit that was not run and the name of the substance that was not provided and the conversation that was forty-five minutes of warm and three seconds of logistics.

Here's the read on it. Before accepting any substance from anyone at any stage of familiarity: What is it specifically? Where did it come from? Has it been tested with what kit? What is the expected dose? What is the expected onset? What is the expected duration?

These are not paranoid questions. They are the same questions a pharmacist asks. The festival setting does not change the pharmacology.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Four Questions Before Any Cap. Step 1 — “What is it?” Specific substance name. Not “just molly.” What is it, specifically.

Step 2 — “Has it been tested? With what?” Marquis for MDMA. Fentanyl strips for everything. Both.

Step 3 — “What’s the dose?” Milligrams. Not “it’s light” or “it’s standard.” Numbers.

Step 4 — “What’s your source?” Not suspicious. Just: the information you would want before any medical decision.

If any of these cannot be answered specifically — the cap is not yours tonight. This is not rude. This is the same care you would apply to a pill from a pharmacy.

Quick vibe check. Some people offer genuinely tested, sourced, accurately dosed substances to people they have gotten to know and the whole thing is clean and fine and the forty-five minutes was a real conversation with a real person and the cap is exactly what it was described as. That exists. The four questions still apply, because the four questions are how you know.

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever given someone a substance without telling them what it was specifically? Have you ever said “it’s fine, it’s the same stuff” without having tested it yourself? Have you ever been the warm forty-five-minute conversation before the cap you did not know was cut? If yes: the warmth was real. The receipts were also missing. Both were real.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **First-Timer:** The first person who offers you something at your first festival feels like the scene vouching for you. The scene is not vouching for you. One warm person is vouch-ing for you. Run the four questions regardless of how the vouching feels.

**BIPOC Festie:** In scenes where racial dynamics mean you are already navigating who to trust and who might be a plant, the warm-generous-stranger is an especially complex read. The four questions are a color-neutral tool that gives you information regardless of the social dynamics.

**Harm-Reduction Crew:** If you are staffing a DanceSafe or Zendo booth, the Free Cap Funnel is where a significant percentage of your most serious cases originate. When someone comes to you after taking something from a warm stranger, the first question is: “Do you know specifically what it was?” The answer is usually no. Build the response from there.

This runs in every direction. The Free Cap Funnel is run by the shirtless guy with the cooler who’s suddenly your best friend, the camp mom two rows over, the touring DJ’s merch girl, and the sober-looking stranger who bought you water first. Forty-five minutes of warmth, then the bag. Warmth is not gendered. Neither is the absence of a test kit.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. The colleague who brings donuts on the morning of a difficult meeting. The warm gift that arrives before the ask. The forty-five minutes before the cap is the Kandi Debt Racket applied to chemistry. The chemistry does not care about the warmth.
