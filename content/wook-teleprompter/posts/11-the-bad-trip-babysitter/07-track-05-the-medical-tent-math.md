### TRACK 05 — THE MEDICAL TENT MATH
*(amnesty calculation under acute stress)*

Here's the behavior. Eli’s brain is doing math it is not equipped to do.

**The math is:** “What is the probability of arrest if I take Marco to the medical tent” versus “What is the probability of serious harm if I do not take Marco to the medical tent” — calculated in real time with eleven percent battery, no training, a grip getting weaker, gray lips, and the couple from Tustin still going at volume about the credit card, Greg.

This is the Medical Tent Math. It is the crisis calculation under acute stress that produces the wrong answer because it is being run on broken hardware with missing information by a person who is also scared and also altered and also in a porta line at three a.m.

The math is the Wook’s best ally in this chapter. Not because the Wook designed the math — he did not design it, he just benefits from it. He benefits from every hour you spend calculating instead of moving. He benefits from every “but what if they—” that keeps Marco in the line and not in the care.

The Medical Tent Math is the shame spiral’s intellectual cousin. The spiral says: don’t get help, you’ll be arrested. The Math says: let me think about this. Both produce the same outcome: delay. And delay in an acute medical situation is the only truly dangerous variable.

Here's the read on it. **The read is simple and non-negotiable:** Gray lips do not have a calculation. Gray lips are not a situation that improves with additional deliberation. The Medical Tent Math is for situations that can absorb a minute of thinking. Gray lips cannot.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Non-Negotiable List. There are situations where you do the Medical Tent Math because you have a few minutes and the information matters. And there are situations where you stop doing the math and move.

When 911 is non-negotiable:

Loss of consciousness

Gray or blue lips

Breathing that is not normal in rate or depth

Seizures

Unresponsive to voice or touch

Body temperature that is visibly extreme (heat stroke, hypothermia)

Chest pain or severe difficulty breathing

Injury with significant blood loss

**In any of these situations:** stop calculating. Move. Call. Get help.

When you go to the harm-reduction tent first:

Intense psychological distress without the above physical signs

Bad trip that has not yet progressed to physical symptoms

Altered state with no physical emergency

Fear, panic, confusion, or paranoia without breathing or consciousness issues

The harm-reduction tent is equipped, trained, and specifically structured to handle these situations without law enforcement involvement.

**The four words:** “We don’t know what.” Say these words when you arrive at any medical resource. Not “he took molly.” Not “she took one cap.” Not “I think it was—.” We don’t know what. Those four words give the medic the appropriate clinical posture without introducing inaccurate information that could affect care.

Quick vibe check. Not every alteration is a medical emergency. Not every bad trip needs a medic. The distinction between psychological distress and physical emergency is real and the chapter names it above. The four-hour rule, the buddy system, and the presence of a sober anchor reduce the frequency with which you face this distinction at three a.m. without any of the preparatory information.

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever watched a friend in a bad state and not acted because you were doing math that the situation did not have time for? Have you ever let the calculation run past the point where the calculation was helping? The math is not your enemy. The delay is.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **Sober Anchor:** You are the one running the clearest version of the Medical Tent Math. Your job is to make the call faster and better than anyone else in the crew can make it right now. That is what the sober anchor role is for.

**Harm-Reduction Volunteer:** You have already resolved the Medical Tent Math in advance through your training. Your job at the scene is to help the person in crisis resolve it as quickly as possible by your presence.

This runs in every direction. The Medical Tent Math runs through the camp lead doing the count at 3 a.m., the sober anchor watching gray lips and deciding, the total stranger who steps in because nobody else moved, and the medic who’s seen this exact hesitation a hundred times. It is universal and human, and the fix isn’t eliminating the math — it’s pre-loading the non-negotiable list so the decision is already made.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. The workplace moment where someone is clearly having a medical event and the first response of everyone around them is to have a quiet conversation about whether calling 911 would embarrass anyone. The math runs. The correct answer is the same.
