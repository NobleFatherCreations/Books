## Tales From The Gate Line — The Save

Festie POV. Same scenario. Counter-Drops live. Named protagonist. Watermelon Moment present.

Sadie Adeyemi is twenty-two. She is at her second festival and she learned one specific thing between her first festival and this one, which is that the Zendo tent exists.

She learned it from a friend who had been to six festivals and who told her, at a pre-event dinner, about a night at a desert regional where her friend’s friend had a bad trip and they spent four hours in the porter line making a decision they could have made in four minutes if they had known the Zendo was forty yards from the main stage and nobody was going to be arrested and the volunteers have done this before and “we don’t know what” is a complete sentence that medical professionals are trained to receive.

Sadie has the Zendo tent on her festival map. She has it starred. She walked past it when she came in and noted its location relative to the main stage and the porta line and the second stage. She has a sober anchor — her friend Theo, who is not drinking this weekend for reasons he did not fully explain and which she did not press because Theo’s reasons are Theo’s — and they have the four-hour check-in rule and a physical meeting spot and a code word that is “have you seen my glasses” because Sadie does not wear glasses and therefore the sentence means nothing except extraction.

At the bass stage she meets a guy who is warm and funny and knows the lineup. They talk for about an hour. He is easy company. At some point he mentions he has something if she wants. He is not pushy about it. He mentions it the way you mention you have a snack in your bag.

This is the watermelon moment. Not dramatic. She is standing at the bass stage with this warm funny person and she has a feeling — not panic, not suspicion, just a slight settling in her chest, a minor weight, the sense that something has shifted in the conversation’s register from “two people enjoying a festival” to “a transaction is being proposed.”

**She says:** “What is it?”

He says molly.

**She says:** “Has it been tested?”

The pause is small. Almost invisible. Just long enough.

**He says:** “Yeah, totally.”

**She says:** “With what kit? Marquis? Fentanyl strip?”

**He says:** “I mean, my guy is really solid, he’s been—”

**She says:** “Thank you, I’m going to pass.” She says it warmly. She is not accusing him of anything. She is just: closing that particular door.

He takes it fine. The conversation continues for another twenty minutes and then she finds Theo and they go to the second stage.

Three hours later, Theo comes to find her. He has heard something — a report moving through the camp network about a batch at the bass stage, a few people in the medical tent, not serious, but enough to have made it to the group chat by midnight.

Sadie reads the report.

She does not feel clever. She feels lucky. The gap between her outcome and Marco’s outcome in the previous cold open is not virtue. It is a question she asked because someone told her to ask it. The asking is the whole thing. The question is the vaccine. The four questions, the starred Zendo on the map, the Theo on the four-hour rule — none of those things are sophisticated. They are ordinary. They are boringly preventable. They are the things that are never taught and never not worth teaching.

**She texts the camp group chat:** “Heard there’s a batch at the bass stage from a warm-friendly-guy-in-the-crowd pattern. Don’t take anything you didn’t bring yourself tonight. Test before you take. Zendo is at the northwest corner of the main field.”

The text takes forty seconds to compose.

She does not know how many people read it before they made a decision.

She knows at least one did.

She knows this because a first-timer named Jake DMs her the next morning: “Thank you for that message. A guy offered me something. I remembered your text. I said no.”

The village is the immune system.

The immune system is a text sent in forty seconds by someone who learned one thing at a pre-event dinner.
