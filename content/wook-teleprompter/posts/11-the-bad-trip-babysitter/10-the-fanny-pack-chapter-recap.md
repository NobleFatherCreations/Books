- THE RECOVERY POSITION
- If someone is unconscious or semi-conscious and breathing:
- 1. Turn them onto their left side.
- 2. Bend the upper knee forward to stabilize — like a running position.
- 3. Tilt the head back slightly to keep the airway open.
- 4. Check that the mouth is clear and nothing is blocking the airway.
- 5. Stay with them. Monitor breathing. Do not leave them on their back.
- The recovery position prevents aspiration — choking on vomit — which is the primary cause of preventable death in unconscious intoxicated individuals.
- WHEN 911 IS NON-NEGOTIABLE
- Loss of consciousness
- Gray, blue, or white lips
- Breathing that is labored, absent, or rattling
- Seizure
- Unresponsive to voice or firm touch
- Extreme temperature (seizure-hot skin, hypothermia-cold)
- Chest pain or inability to breathe
- Significant blood loss
- Do not do the Medical Tent Math when these signs are present. Call. Move.
- GOOD SAMARITAN AMNESTY — KNOW YOUR STATE
- As of 2026, all fifty US states have some form of Good Samaritan law for drug-related medical emergencies. Most protect both the person calling for help and the person in crisis from prosecution for possession when calling in good faith.
- State-specific details: DanceSafe.org/good-samaritan-laws — updated regularly, searchable by state.
- Key points for most states:
- The protection applies when you are calling for medical help, not when you are discovered in possession by law enforcement independently.
- The protection covers the caller and the person in crisis.
- The protection does not cover trafficking or distribution charges.
- You should still consult local legal resources for your specific situation.
- THE FOUR WORDS
- When you reach any medical resource — medic, Zendo volunteer, ranger, harm-reduction worker:
- “We don’t know what.”
- This phrase communicates: the substance is unknown, the dose is unknown, the timeline is uncertain. It gives the medic the correct clinical posture without introducing inaccurate information. It also avoids creating legal exposure through specific assertions about controlled substances.
- Do not say: “She took molly.” Do not say: “He did one cap of MDMA.” Say: “We don’t know what.” And then answer specific questions as accurately as you can.
- TEST KIT BASICS
- Marquis reagent: for MDMA/MDEA/MDA. Purple-black is positive. Orange is amphetamines. No reaction or unexpected color: flag.

- Mecke reagent: for DXM/opiates/MDMA. Additional confirmation.
- Source: DanceSafe.org, Bunk Police. Bring both to every festival.
