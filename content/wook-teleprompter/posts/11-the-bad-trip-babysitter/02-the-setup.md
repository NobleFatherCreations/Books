🎛️ THE TAPERS’ SECTION

Harm Reduction · Acute Crisis Response · Good Samaritan Law

Brief. Then directly into the practical content.

Harm reduction in its clinical form is the public-health principle that people will use substances regardless of legal status or moral instruction, and the goal is reducing the harms associated with that use: testing, information, crisis support, Narcan, clean equipment, non-punitive medical access. In scene language: DanceSafe, Zendo Project, Bunk Police, Wharf Rats, the medical tent, the harm-reduction volunteer with the vest and the fanny pack and the warm face that says “I am not here to judge you, I am here to make sure you don’t die.”

Acute crisis response in festival settings is a specific skill set that is different from default-world first aid in key ways: the substances involved are often unknown, the setting is loud and disorienting and dark, the person in crisis may be terrified of medical intervention for legal reasons, and the babysitter — the friend, the stranger, the unicorn onesie — has often had no training. The protocols in this chapter are derived from Zendo Project, DanceSafe, and MAPS-affiliated training. They are simplified for use by people without formal training in the worst possible moment.

Good Samaritan law applies in all fifty US states in some form as of 2026. Most protect both the person in crisis and the person who calls for help from prosecution for possession when a medical emergency is being reported in good faith. The specifics vary by state — consult DanceSafe’s state-by-state guide, linked in the Safety Appendix. The law exists. Know it before you need it.
