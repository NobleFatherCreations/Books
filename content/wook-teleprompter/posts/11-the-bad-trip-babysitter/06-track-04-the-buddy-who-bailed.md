### TRACK 04 — THE BUDDY WHO BAILED
*(abandonment mid-crisis)*

Here's the behavior. The buddy system is real and it fails in predictable ways.

At the beginning of the night, you and your friend agree to look out for each other. This agreement is sincere. Both of you mean it. The agreement also has an implicit expiration that neither of you named, which is: until something more interesting comes along.

**The something more interesting can be:** a person. A stage. A spontaneous group that formed around a fire. A secret set announced thirty minutes ago. The summit of someone’s emotional availability that you have been waiting for all weekend. A situation that you told yourself would “just be thirty minutes.”

The thirty minutes becomes two hours.

The two hours becomes four a.m.

And somewhere in the intervening time, Marco’s escort to the medical tent became a stranger in a unicorn onesie instead of the friend who said they would be there.

The Buddy Who Bailed is not always a villain. Sometimes they just got lost in the night. Sometimes they themselves got into a situation that needed help. Sometimes the festival is four days long and maintaining continuous buddy-system accountability across four days is harder than it sounds when you are nineteen and the headliner is playing and there is someone you have been talking to all day.

The chapter’s specific context is the Wook-adjacent version: the buddy who bails because the same person who gave Marco the untested cap also, at some earlier point in the evening, made very sure that the buddy was occupied in a different direction. The Skim and the Buddy Who Bailed sometimes run together. The Wook creates the crisis and removes the support infrastructure in the same set of moves.

Here's the read on it. Clock the buddy’s availability. Is your person currently reachable? When did you last check in? What would it take to get to them right now?

If the answer is “I don’t know where they are” — that information is due.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Four-Hour Rule. The four-hour rule is the single most effective harm-prevention protocol in the scene’s informal toolkit, and it is achievable by anyone without training or equipment.

**Every four hours, either:** make physical contact with your buddy, or confirm via text. Not “I’ll text if I need something.” Proactive contact. Both ways. “I’m good, where are you?” Every four hours. No exceptions.

Step 1 — Set the rule explicitly before the event starts, out loud, with your buddy.

Step 2 — Set a physical meeting point that does not require cell service: the big oak by family camp, the southwest corner of the main stage, the water truck. A specific physical location. If your phone is dead, you go there.

**Step 3 — Set the check-in interval:** four hours or shorter. Two hours for higher-stakes settings or higher-dose nights.

Step 4 — Name the code word for extraction. A specific phrase that means “I need you now, come get me, no questions.” Something that sounds normal but is the signal. “Where’s my charger?” “Have you seen my jacket?” Something that can be texted or said without announcing the situation.

**Step 5 — If your buddy misses a check-in:** go looking. Not in twenty minutes. Now.

Quick vibe check. Festivals are large and complicated and losing track of someone for a few hours is not automatically a crisis or a failure of care. The four-hour rule is not about surveillance. It is about maintaining the basic accountability thread that means you know whether your person needs you.

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever been the buddy who bailed? Not maliciously — just: got pulled into something and lost track and it was fine but it was also lucky that it was fine? The four-hour rule is not a punishment for past bailing. It is a structure that makes future bailing less likely to land badly.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **First-Timer:** Your first festival, you do not yet have the navigational knowledge to be confident you can find someone again once you lose them. Do not lose them.

**Solo Festie:** If you are attending solo, the four-hour rule applies to your default-world anchor: one person who knows where you are, who you check in with every four hours, and who calls for help if you miss two check-ins. The anchor does not need to be at the festival. They need to have your location and your check-in schedule.

**Large Festival (50,000+):** The four-hour rule becomes the two-hour rule and cell service becomes unreliable enough that the physical meeting point is mandatory, not backup.

This runs in every direction. The Buddy Who Bailed is the best friend who went to find the afters, the hookup who left for a different stage, the camp mate who assumed someone else had it covered, and the person who genuinely thought you’d be fine. Any gender, any configuration, any level of scene experience. The four-hour rule and the extraction code word don’t care which one left.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. The hiking partner who goes ahead and loses you on the trail. Not malicious. Just: the agreement to stay together dissolved gradually and nobody named the dissolution. The trail version and the festival version have the same solution: explicit check-in intervals and a specific meeting point.
