## Tales From The Anchor Crew — The Save

Festie POV. Same scenario, Sister Lou. Counter-protocols live. Watermelon Moment present.

The Save in this chapter is the chapter.

This is unusual. Every other chapter’s Save has been a named protagonist running the Counter-Drops in real time against a specific Wook. This chapter does not have a Wook. The Save is the briefing working.

**The Save is:** nobody went missing.

**The Save is:** at eleven forty-seven p.m. on Saturday, a crew member sent the code word — “where’s my charger” — and the Inner Anchor was at her location in four minutes, not because the Inner Anchor was nearby, but because the Inner Anchor had specifically stayed within a radius that allowed four-minute response time, which was part of the role’s operational definition that the briefing had established.

**The Save is:** the crew member who sent the code word was Cara. Six months out of the Lantern Family bus. Running the briefing for her own people now, four months in. She ran it before this event. She has been running it since Sister Lou taught her at the Wharf Rats meet in Asheville, because Sister Lou taught the briefing to everyone who asked, and Cara had asked, specifically and immediately, because Cara understood from the inside what happens in the absence of the briefing.

**The watermelon moment:** standing in the briefing circle at seven fifty-nine, twenty minutes before doors, saying her government name aloud to the person on her left, hearing the person on her left repeat it back. The specific physical sensation in her chest of being known correctly, by name, in advance of need. Her real name, not a category. Her name, held by the people who will come for her.

That is the watermelon moment.

That is the whole chapter.

At eleven forty-seven p.m. she sends the code word. The Inner Anchor comes. The older man who was pressing the mason jar recedes. She and the Inner Anchor walk toward the water station together. She sees the older man, two minutes later, at a different merch table, pressing a different mason jar into the hands of a twenty-two-year-old who has not had the briefing.

She walks over.

She introduces herself.

She asks the twenty-two-year-old her name.

**She says:** “Hey. I don’t know that guy. Don’t drink that. Come dance with us. I’ll get you a sealed water from our cooler.”

The twenty-two-year-old looks at the jar. Looks at the guy. Looks at Cara.

She follows Cara.

By eleven-thirty they are on the rail together and the twenty-two-year-old is laughing and the older man is somewhere else and Cara is making a mental note of his face for the Signal thread.

The immune system.

Small acts.

Networked.

Over years.
