## The Mirror + The Discog

HAVE YOU BEEN THE WOOK? Mirror Check At The Briefing

Chapter 27 does not have a traditional Have You Been The Wook mirror.

Because in this chapter, the question is not whether you have run a play on someone. It is whether you have been the briefing or whether you have been the person who said “we’ll figure it out” and meant it and was right, narrowly, for several years in a row, and is now in the statistics that Sister Lou has been collecting for eleven years.

Have you ever been the crew lead who did not run the briefing because the briefing seemed like it would kill the vibe?

Have you ever been the anchor who was skimmed and told yourself the person was probably fine?

Have you ever said “we’ll find each other” and lost someone for four hours?

Have you ever let someone sleep in an unattended tent in family camp without checking on them?

Have you ever been the Outer Anchor who did not respond to the backup call because the phone was on silent?

You have probably been one of these. Most people who have been in the scene for more than three years have been one of these. The briefing exists because of what happens when everyone is one of these simultaneously.

Run the briefing next time.

Not because something has gone wrong.

Because something hasn’t yet.

Here's the Wook Discog for this chapter. The Anchor Crew Discog doesn’t have Wooks.

Studio Debut — The New Crew

First event. First time running the briefing. Government names with some laughter. ICE contacts set with mild embarrassment that they hadn’t been set before. Code word chosen and immediately forgotten by one person who was not paying attention. Buddy assignment made and immediately dissolved by the first interesting person each buddy encountered. The briefing was run. The system ran at approximately forty percent of its designed capacity. Nobody disappeared. This is the Studio Debut. The system at forty percent capacity still produced a better outcome than no system. Next year: sixty percent. The year after: eighty. Year five: Sister Lou’s clipboard.

Live Album — The Seasoned Crew

The system running at operational capacity. Eleven years of iteration. The crew knows the briefing without the sheet. The code word has been used twice — once in a genuine extraction, once by accident when someone was genuinely looking for their charger, which produced a brief operational response and a note in the debrief. The Outer Anchor tier prevented one missing-friend situation in year seven. The Default-World Anchor has never needed to activate. This is what it looks like when the boring meeting has been run for eleven consecutive years. The Live Album sounds like nothing in particular. It sounds like everyone going home Sunday with their phones, their wallets, their keys, their hats, and their memories.

Greatest Hits Compilation — The Festival With Infrastructure

The festival that posts the DanceSafe booth location in the pre-event guide. The festival that provides the Good Samaritan amnesty information at the gate. The festival that offers a free wristband photo station at check-in for ICE contact purposes. The festival that has a safety briefing template available to camp leads in the information packet. The festival that built the harm-reduction tent as a permanent structure rather than as a volunteer-managed afterthought. The Greatest Hits Compilation is not one festival. It is a standard that a handful of festivals have partially reached and that the scene has been building toward for twenty years and that still does not fully exist anywhere. The standard is the target. Sister Lou’s clipboard is the minimum viable version of the target.
