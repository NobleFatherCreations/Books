### TRACK 04 — THE FAKE-ASS LAMINATE FLEX
*(manufactured authority)*

Here's the behavior. The laminate. The credential. The title. The name-drop that nobody can verify. I caught your set at Lightning — which is not a lie that can be checked in real time, which makes it operational. The festival name on the lanyard that could be from this year or 2019 or a different city’s version of the same brand. The backstage wristband on a different arm that could mean anything. The Instagram follower count presented as if reach is the same thing as authority. The person who is introduced by someone else — Rex says Marcus — and whose title is implied by the architecture of the introduction rather than stated outright.

Manufactured authority doesn’t have to forge credentials. It just has to let you assume them.

Here's the read on it. Ask the boring verifying question. Out loud. In the moment. “Which stage did you catch me on?” Not hostile. Not accusatory. Just factual. The answer is informative regardless. If Marcus says “the outdoor stage, second night, the set that ended with the Detroit vocal,” and Solenne has never been to Lightning, she now has very clean information. If he pivots — “the energy, I followed your work” — she also has very clean information.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The One Factual Question. Step 1 — Identify the credential being implied.

Step 2 — Ask one specific, verifiable, non-hostile factual question about it. Not “are you legit?” Ask “which stage?” or “when was that?” or “who else was on that bill?”

Step 3 — Listen for the pivot. Legitimate authority answers specific questions specifically. Manufactured authority becomes general, becomes about feelings, becomes about the bigger picture, becomes about how you clearly don’t trust them which is a problem.

Step 4 — Act on the pivot. A pivot is information.

Quick vibe check. Real industry people also sometimes misremember details. Not every credential error is fraud. The test is not perfection. The test is: how does the error land? Does it make them more human, or does it make the authority shimmer and vanish?

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever name-dropped something you were not entirely sure you had the right to name-drop? Have you ever let someone assume a credential you didn’t correct? If yes, you have run a small version of the Flex. Notice where the line is.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **Artists with smaller platforms:** The imposter credential is extra potent when it comes from someone in a position to give you the thing you actually want. You will let the credential slide because you want what they might be able to offer. That is the design. The credential is the hook; the desire is the water.

**Camp Leads and Organizers:** Someone claiming festival-safety authority or harm-reduction credentials they don’t have is not just a social bluff. It is a safety issue. Ask for the training record.

This runs in every direction. The Flex is run by femme producers who present follower counts as booking authority, by male promoters who name-drop venues they have no current relationship with, by nonbinary healers who present a weekend course as clinical training, by veteran festies who present ten-year attendance as scene governance authority. Credentials are not gendered. Neither is the decision to inflate them.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. The job application that includes “extensive experience in—” for things that lasted three weeks. The LinkedIn connection who says they “collaborated with” someone whose assistant they once emailed. Authority inflation is a default-world sport with a festival-specific dialect.
