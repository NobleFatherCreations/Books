### TRACK 11 — THE “I’M JUST WORRIED ABOUT YOU” MOVE
*(concern trolling)*

Here's the behavior. The concern is the weapon. I just don’t want you to make a mistake. I care about you too much to not say something. I’m saying this because I’m on your side. You know I always want what’s best for you. The “I’m Just Worried” is the Trojan horse of the persuasion toolkit because it disguises control as care. The person claiming to be worried is actually expressing: I need you to do the thing I want, and I am repackaging that need as concern for your wellbeing so you cannot disagree without seeming ungrateful for my care.

**Classic deployment:** Solenne says she wants to think about the twenty-five thousand. Marcus says nothing. Rex, later, says: “I just want to make sure you’re not letting this pass you out of fear. I’ve seen talented people do that. I’d hate for you to be one of them.”

The care is the trap. The concern is the cage.

Here's the read on it. **Ask:** Who benefits if I comply with what the concerned person wants? If the answer is the concerned person, the concern is not about you.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Inversion. **Step 1 — Say:** “I appreciate you looking out for me. What specific information do you have that makes you worried?”

Step 2 — Listen for information vs. feeling. “I’ve seen people miss this and regret it” is a feeling. “Here is the track record of the last three artists in this program with their names and contact information” is information.

Step 3 — Act on the information, not the feeling.

Step 4 — If the concern cannot name a specific harm and can only gesture at your hesitation as the problem, the concern is not about you.

Quick vibe check. Sometimes people are genuinely worried about you and the worry is useful. The distinction: genuine concern gives you information and leaves the decision with you. Concern-trolling gives you pressure and leaves the decision with them.

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever framed a self-interested request as concern for someone else? Have you ever told someone “I just don’t want you to get hurt” when you actually meant “I need you not to do that thing”? The two sentences feel different from the inside. They land the same on the other end.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **Queer Festie:** “I’m just worried about you spending so much time with that person” said by someone who controls your social access in a small scene is not concern. It is a gatekeeping operation wrapped in a hug.

**BIPOC Festie:** “I just want to make sure you know what you’re getting into” said by a white scene elder to a Black or brown artist who is about to sign their own deal is often concern-trolling with a side of paternalism.

This runs in every direction. The “I’m Just Worried” Move is equally available to femmes, mascs, partners, mentors, camp leads, parents of the scene, booking agents, and anyone who has decided that their feelings about your choices are a public health crisis. The concern is not gendered. Neither is the manipulation.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. The manager who says “I just don’t want you to burn out” every time you try to negotiate your workload upward. The parent who says “I just want what’s best for you” before outlining what you should do with your life. The concern is real. It is also doing other work.
