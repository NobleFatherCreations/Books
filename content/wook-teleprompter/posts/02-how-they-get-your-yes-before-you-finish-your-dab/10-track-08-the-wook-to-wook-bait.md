### TRACK 08 — THE WOOK-TO-WOOK BAIT
*(unity / in-group lever)*

Here's the behavior. We’re the same. We came up the same. We’re both in this scene, both doing this thing, both know what it costs, both understand the hustle, and that makes us kin, and kin look out for each other, and looking out for each other in this context means you do the thing I’m asking even though I have not explained the full terms of the thing I’m asking.

The in-group lever runs on the genuine warmth of shared context — you get it, I get it, we don’t need to explain — and then uses that shortcut to skip the due diligence that would normally be required. We’re wooks. We don’t need contracts. That’s a default-world thing. The scene as both the bond and the bypass.

Here's the read on it. Shared context is real. Shared identity is real. The shortcut it creates is the thing to clock. Contracts and clear terms are not default-world things. They are human things that exist because memory is interpretive and interests diverge and people want different things in retrospect. The scene being the scene doesn’t change that. It just adds a layer of identity betrayal to the normal friction when things go sideways.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Scene Tax Audit. Step 1 — Separate what you share (scene, values, aesthetic, history) from what you are agreeing to (terms, access, money, labor, time, risk).

**Step 2 — Ask:** “Would I agree to these specific terms with a stranger from outside the scene?” If yes, maybe fine. If no, the in-group framing is doing work it has no right to do.

Step 3 — Get the terms in writing anyway. Scene family that means it will be fine with that.

Quick vibe check. Intra-scene trust is not automatically manipulation. Communities run on trust. The lever becomes harmful when the trust is used to skip the information necessary to consent.

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever said “we don’t need to do it that way, we’re family” to get out of providing clear terms to someone you knew was taking a risk?

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **BIPOC Festie:** The Wook-To-Wook Bait can operate along racial lines in scenes where shared marginalization is used to establish trust — “we both know what it’s like” — and then leveraged for labor, loyalty, or silence. Shared identity does not mean aligned interests.

**Queer Festie:** Chosen family as a cultural institution is real and life-saving. It is also occasionally used as a lever to establish obligations that biological family would never get away with.

This runs in every direction. The in-group lever is equally available to the male promoter who says “we’re all just trying to make the music happen,” the femme camp lead who says “we’re sisters, we don’t do it that way,” and the queer booking collective that says “this is how we build community.” The warmth of belonging is not gendered. Neither is its misuse.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. “We’re colleagues, we don’t need to put this in an email.” Followed, three months later, by a disagreement about what was agreed to. Put it in an email.
