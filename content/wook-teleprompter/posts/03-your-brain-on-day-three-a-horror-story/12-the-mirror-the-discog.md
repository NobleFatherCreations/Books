## The Mirror + The Discog

HAVE YOU BEEN THE WOOK? Mirror Check At The Yurt Door

Here is the uncomfortable one for this chapter.

You have probably weaponized someone else’s Day Three Brain. Not necessarily deliberately. Not necessarily maliciously. But: have you ever made an important ask — of money, of labor, of sex, of silence, of loyalty — at a moment when the person you were asking was sleep-deprived, emotionally opened up, peak-dosed, coming down, isolated from their crew, or so far into the weekend that their decision-making was running on bass residue and the ambient binaural of your personal mythology?

Have you ever set the anchor — first number, first story, first frame — and then let it sit there doing its quiet work while the other person moved toward it?

Have you ever let someone’s halo — yours — make them feel safer with you than the behavioral evidence justified?

Have you ever brought someone the sunk cost argument when they were trying to leave? “But after everything we’ve built—”

Have you ever let the group chat’s fog about someone else — positive or negative — go unchallenged because correcting it would have been socially expensive?

Most of this is not villain behavior. Most of this is what people do when they want something and the environment makes it easy to get it without being fully honest about the ask. The festival environment makes it easy. The sleep deprivation makes it easy. The peak-state bonding makes it easy. “I didn’t know I was doing that” can be completely true and still be the thing that cost someone $2,400 or a year of their life or a night in a tent they didn’t choose.

Know the bugs.
