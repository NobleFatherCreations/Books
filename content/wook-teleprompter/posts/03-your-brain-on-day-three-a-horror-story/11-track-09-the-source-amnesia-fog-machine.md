### TRACK 09 — THE SOURCE AMNESIA FOG MACHINE
*(source amnesia)*

Here's the behavior. You remember the claim but have forgotten where it came from.

People are saying.

Who?

I heard they’re unsafe.

From whom?

I think they admitted something.

To whom?

Everyone knows about the Denver thing.

What Denver thing?

By day three, the group chat has become a swamp oracle. Someone heard something from someone by the porta line after someone talked to someone near medical after someone’s ex posted a close-friends story with a black background and the single word interesting. Now everyone has a feeling. Nobody has a source. Source amnesia is how rumor becomes weather — warm, ambient, directionless, already there before you had a chance to question it.

The Wook uses it two ways. One: to smear someone without fingerprints. “People have concerns about them” said casually enough and often enough becomes fact without a chain of custody. Two: to manufacture authority around themselves. “Everyone knows I’m good for it.” “People always say this about my work.” “Word around the scene is—” The fog becomes reputation, and the reputation circulates faster than the evidence.

By day three, Nadia knows that Solstice is well-regarded in the transformational space. She does not know by whom, specifically, or what that regard was based on, or whether the previous retreat participants got what they paid for. She knows it the way you know weather — it was already there, ambient, no single source.

Here's the read on it. **Any time you hear:** people are saying, everyone knows, I heard, the community feels, there are concerns, it’s been talked about — stop and ask: Who specifically? When specifically? What exactly was said? Did you hear it directly? Can I speak with the source?

If the source cannot be named, the claim cannot drive the van.

This runs in both directions. Before you act on a warning: find the source. Before you repeat a compliment as established fact: name the source. Before you treat an absence of sources as evidence of consensus: notice the fog.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Receipt Chain. Build the chain.

**Step 1 — Claim:** What is being said?

**Step 2 — Source:** Who said it directly?

**Step 3 — Witness:** Who witnessed the behavior firsthand?

**Step 4 — Date and location:** When and where?

**Step 5 — Evidence:** Texts, messages, photos, payments, schedules, staff notes, prior reports.

If any link in the chain is “I think someone said” or “it’s just known” — the chain breaks at that link. Do not carry it beyond the break point.

This does not mean the fog is lying. It means the fog needs a source before it becomes actionable. Warm feelings about a person’s danger or a person’s safety are not the same as the receipt.

Quick vibe check. Informal networks carry real safety information in scenes where formal reporting is impossible or dangerous. “Don’t be alone with that person” from someone you trust, even without a full sourced chain, is still worth taking seriously as a precaution. The point is not to dismiss the fog. The point is not to let the fog become policy, accusation, or defense without the chain.

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever repeated something without knowing where it started? Have you ever passed on people are saying without checking whether people were actually saying it or whether you’d absorbed it through repetition? If yes, you have been part of a fog machine. Without meaning to. That is how fog machines work.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **Camp Leads:** You are often the terminal for both kinds of fog — smear fog and praise fog. Building a culture of “who specifically and when specifically” for every claim protects harmed people from dismissal and falsely accused people from smear campaigns simultaneously.

**First-Timer:** You don’t yet have a reference class for what counts as a credible source in this scene. “Everyone says” will arrive before you have any way to check it. Default to: who told you this, when, and what did they witness directly.

This runs in every direction. The fog machine is run by femme scene insiders with wide social networks, male elders with long memories and selective recall, queer community linchpins whose word carries weight, sober leaders whose authority has been treated as self-verifying, and first-timers who repeated one thing they heard and had no idea it would replicate. The fog is democratic. It is distributed by whoever talks most, not by whoever knows most.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. “There’s a sense around the organization that—” Where? Who? When did that sense form? The fog machine runs in every office with an open floor plan and a strong gossip culture. Fog is fog regardless of the setting.
