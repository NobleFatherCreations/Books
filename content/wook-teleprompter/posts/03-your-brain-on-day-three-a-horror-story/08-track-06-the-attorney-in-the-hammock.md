### TRACK 06 — THE ATTORNEY IN THE HAMMOCK
*(motivated reasoning)*

Here's the behavior. Your brain starts with the conclusion and builds the case backward.

You want to trust the Wook, so your brain finds the evidence for trust. You want to stay in the camp, so your brain builds the argument for staying. You want the retreat to have been worth it, so your brain assembles the evidence that it was. You want to believe your community is safe, so your brain files the counterevidence in the context that will come out later folder, which is a very large folder that never actually gets opened.

The Attorney In The Hammock is not consciously dishonest. That is what makes it dangerous. You genuinely believe you are evaluating. You are not evaluating. You are litigating, and you are both the attorney and the judge, and the verdict was decided before the arguments began.

Nadia’s brain, by 5:47 a.m., has constructed an entire case for why the Tulum thing is the right move. The case is internally coherent. The case references her throat chakra, her career, her need for expression, River’s read, the timing, the alignment. The case is airtight.

The case was built after the Venmo was already sent.

Here's the read on it. **The Attorney’s signature:** you are more interested in winning the argument than in being right about the facts. Ask: “Am I trying to figure out what’s true, or am I trying to win the case for what I already decided?” Those are different activities and they produce different results.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Devil’s Advocate Brief. Step 1 — Write out your current case for the thing you want to do or believe.

Step 2 — Assign yourself to argue the other side for five minutes. Not to be fair. To find the holes. What does the opposing brief say? Where is your case weakest?

Step 3 — Find the evidence that would break your case. Where is it? Who has it? Can you ask?

Step 4 — If the opposing brief is genuinely weaker than your brief after this process, proceed. If the opposing brief reveals you were missing things, update.

Quick vibe check. Motivated reasoning is not always wrong about the conclusion. Sometimes you wanted to believe something, built the case for it, and then found out that the case was also true. The bug is in the process, not always in the outcome. The process still needs to be corrected.

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever been in an accountability conversation where you were arguing for yourself rather than trying to find out what was true? The Attorney In The Hammock runs defense for the self as often as it runs it for other people. Notice whose interests it’s serving when it activates.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **Camp Leads:** When a camp member brings a harm claim, the Attorney is often the first responder. “But they were drunk.” “But there’s no proof.” “But the other person also—.” These may all be relevant. They may also be the Attorney building a defense for the camp before the facts are in. The Attorney does not have to be dishonest to be wrong.

**Queer/Poly Scene:** Complex relationship structures create fertile ground for the Attorney because there is genuinely always more context. “It’s complicated” can be true and can also be the Attorney’s favorite opener for a case that should not be continued.

This runs in every direction. The Attorney is genderless and runs with equal enthusiasm in the service of beloved femme founders, beloved male elders, beloved nonbinary organizers, beloved anyone. The Attorney does not care who it defends. It cares about not losing.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. The performance review conversation where you walked in knowing your narrative and left having never actually asked what the feedback was trying to tell you. The Attorney was present. The feedback was not received.
