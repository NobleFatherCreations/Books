### TRACK 08 — THE REVERSE HALO MUDSLIDE
*(reverse halo / fundamental attribution error)*

Here's the behavior. It runs in the other direction from Track 3. One bad thing about a person makes everything about them seem bad, wrong, unreliable, or dismissible. Specifically: the person reporting harm is messy, complicated, emotional, in debt, has drama, has done something questionable, has an ex who says things, drinks too much, is loud, cries in public, was high when it happened, has previously lied about something smaller, or simply makes the people who like the Wook uncomfortable in some way that is hard to name but easy to feel.

**The Reverse Halo says:** those things contaminate the claim.

They do not. A person can be complicated, messy, emotionally dysregulated, and also accurate about what happened to them. These are not exclusive categories. The mud from the Mudslide does not fall on the claim. It only falls on the credibility of the person making it, and only in the minds of people running the reverse halo, which is most minds in a scene under social pressure.

The Wook often knows which traits will trigger the Reverse Halo in the specific community. They will, if pressed, name those traits. “She’s really intense.” “He has a history of—.” “They tend to escalate.” None of these sentences address whether the harm occurred. All of them successfully redirect the evaluation.

Here's the read on it. **Ask:** Does this trait actually affect the likelihood of the specific claim being true? Messiness in one domain does not predict dishonesty in another. Emotional dysregulation is not the same as fabrication. Being in debt does not mean being a liar. The specific behaviors have to be evaluated on specific evidence, not on the general impression of the person’s life.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Claim Triage. Separate three things:

1. The person: Who are they? What is their history? Are they credible as a general matter?

2. The specific claim: What behavior is being named? What are the facts asserted?

3. The independent evidence: Texts, witnesses, dates, patterns, prior reports, changed access, money trail, medical or harm-reduction contact.

Evaluate three separately. Do not let the first contaminate the third.

Step 1 — Write the claim in plain behavioral language. No adjectives. “On Saturday, he asked her to leave the medical area with him, she went, she reported that he touched her without consent in the tent.” That is the claim. Not: “she says he assaulted her and she’s been really struggling lately and has a complicated history with people in this camp.” That is the mudslide.

Step 2 — Find the independent evidence. Does it exist? What does it say?

Step 3 — Do not require a perfect victim. Perfect victims exist only in courtroom dramas and TV crime procedurals. In actual life, harmed people are often messy, contradictory, high when it happened, bad at following up, and still telling the truth about the core of what occurred.

Quick vibe check. Credibility does matter. People can misremember, misattribute, exaggerate, or act in bad faith. Claims deserve serious investigation, not automatic belief. The problem is when the investigation is replaced by trait assessment, and the trait assessment concludes the claim is false because the person is imperfect. That is not investigation. That is dismissal wearing investigation’s lanyard.

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever dismissed someone’s concern because they were inconveniently messy? Have you used their drama or their history or their intensity to avoid examining the thing they were actually saying? If yes, you found an escape hatch and called it nuance.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **Women and Femmes:** Being emotional is routinely used to discredit accurate perception. The Mudslide runs on hysterical in clinical language, dramatic in scene language, a lot in polite language. Same function.

**BIPOC Lane:** Anger at harm is often racially coded as aggression, unreliability, or disproportionate response. The Reverse Halo runs specifically on racialized traits in white-majority festival spaces. Check that bias at the gate.

**Queer and Poly Scenes:** Complex relationship webs are consistently used to contaminate claims. “They have a complicated dynamic” does not make the specific behavior disappear. Look at the behavior.

This runs in every direction. The Mudslide runs equally against messy men, dramatic women, complicated nonbinary people, intense queer folks, and anyone whose presentation makes the people in power more comfortable with a dismissal than an investigation. The mud finds the path of least institutional resistance.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. If a witness to a stolen bike has bad credit and cries while describing it, the bike may still be stolen. Look for the bike.
