🎛️ THE TAPERS’ SECTION

Kahneman · Tversky · Festinger

Tiny theory. Then back to the dust.

Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky showed that people do not make clean, logical decisions most of the time. We use shortcuts, frames, anchors, vibes, comparisons, losses, and emotional math. System 1 — fast, automatic, associative — makes most calls. System 2 — slow, deliberate, effortful — is supposed to review them, but System 2 is running on a six-hour-old Rx Bar and thirty minutes of yoga mat sleep and the binaural frequency that the sensory marketing brief said increases suggestion receptivity. In festival language: your brain is not a sober accountant at a standing desk. Your brain is a guy in a wizard hat trying to split gas money after three hours of sleep who also just got told his aura is special.

**Leon Festinger gave us cognitive dissonance:** when new information clashes with what we already believe or what we’ve already done, we are more likely to twist the information than update the belief. In lot language: “I Venmo’d $2,400 to a woman I met ninety-three minutes ago” can become “therefore this must have been right” because the alternative is sitting in a therapist’s office at thirty-eight years old being the cautionary tale in your own quarterly briefing. The dissonance is too loud. The brain smooths it. This is not weakness. This is how Festinger predicted all human brains work. It still costs you $2,400.
