## The Sunrise Set + The Kandi Trade (closer)

Here's the Sunrise Set. Zero slang — just the true thing. The festival ends and the magic doesn’t.

The magic goes somewhere. It goes into the body and the memory and the specific warmth that the experience puts in you that Tuesday’s coffee won’t fully dissolve. It travels home with you. It is in the car for seven hours. It is in the floor-sit with the half-unpacked camping gear. It is in the wristband that you will take off when you are ready and not before.

That magic is yours.

It was made by your specific experience over four days and it lives in your nervous system and nobody gave it to you and nobody can borrow it without your permission.

The window is the time when the magic is most accessible. It is also the time when it is most available to be borrowed. The person who contacts you in the window is borrowing the magic you generated. That is not connection. That is someone else running warm on your fuel.

You are allowed to keep it.

You are allowed to sit in the floor-sit with the half-unpacked gear and eat whatever is left in the cooler and let the magic be yours and not discharge it into anyone’s request or deadline or commitment.

Let it settle.

Let Tuesday happen.

Tuesday is not the end of the magic. Tuesday is when the magic is yours in full — integrated, calibrated, in the correct proportion, available for the decisions you make with the whole of yourself.

Some of the festival warmth will still be there on Tuesday.

The warmth that survives Tuesday is the warmth that was always real.

Wait for it.

And here's the Kandi Trade — the vow.

- I will know I am in the window.
- I will not make major decisions in the window.
- I will notice who timed their contact for the drive home.
- I will put the decision in the notes app and open it Tuesday.
- I will keep my festival warmth while it is mine to keep.
- I will take the wristband off when I am ready.
- I will let Tuesday-me make Tuesday decisions.
- I will remember that nothing real is that urgent on Sunday afternoon.
- And I will be the person who, when I notice someone in their window, says: “Wait for Tuesday. If you still want this on Tuesday, I’m here.”
