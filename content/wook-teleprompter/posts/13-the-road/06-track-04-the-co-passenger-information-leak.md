### TRACK 04 — THE CO-PASSENGER INFORMATION LEAK
*(unsolicited disclosure / passenger override)*

Here's the behavior. **Carter says:** “Music festival up north.”

Carter is not running a play. Carter has never been in this situation. Carter answered a friendly question the way Carter answers friendly questions, which is warmly and completely, because Carter is a person who believes that warmth and completeness are the right way to interact with other people, and Carter is right about this in most contexts and not in this one.

The information that damages a traffic stop encounter is almost never the answer to “do you have anything illegal.” It is the answer to “where are you going, how do you all know each other, how long have you been driving, where did you come from.” These questions map the social network, establish familiarity levels, surface inconsistencies across multiple speakers, and confirm destination information that contextualizes the vehicle.

Carter, saying the name of the festival, has told the officer: this vehicle is festival-bound, these occupants are festival-goers, this is a vehicle that matches the enforcement profile I have been briefed on, and the encounter has additional justification beyond the brake light. Not because Carter is malicious. Because Carter has never been told.

Here's the read on it. The driver should be the only person talking. Not because the passengers are untrustworthy — because multiple speakers produce inconsistencies, and inconsistencies produce probable cause, and probable cause produces the chain. One voice is cleaner than four.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Driver-Only Policy. Before the drive. Out loud: “If we get stopped, I handle it. Everyone else says I’ll let the driver handle this or says nothing. Not because you’re hiding anything. Because one voice is cleaner and four voices create complications.”

The Carter variable is removed by the thirty-second conversation. Carter is not a problem. Carter is the person who was never briefed. Brief Carter.

Quick vibe check. Carter’s friendliness is genuine. The driver-only policy is not about distrust of Carter. It is about the legal architecture of the encounter, which does not distinguish between friendly disclosure and legally consequential disclosure.

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever answered questions in a high-stakes setting because the questions were asked warmly and the alternative felt rude? The Carter variable is universal. The briefing protects everyone.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **First-Timers:** You are more likely to be Carter than any other role in the car. The briefing is specifically for you. Ask for it.

This runs in every direction. The leak comes from the front-seat passenger answering a question nobody asked, the back-seat friend who overshares to seem cooperative, the driver’s girlfriend trying to smooth things over, and Carter, who just wants this to be over and says one sentence too many. It runs in every vehicle configuration. The driver-only policy works regardless of who’s talking.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. The team meeting where one person answers the auditor’s casual question with more information than the prepared answer contained. The prepared answer exists for a reason. Know what it is before the meeting.
