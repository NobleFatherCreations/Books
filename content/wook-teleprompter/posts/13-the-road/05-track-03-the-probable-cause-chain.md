### TRACK 03 — THE PROBABLE CAUSE CHAIN
*(manufactured justification for extended detention)*

Here's the behavior. The officer cannot extend the detention past the legitimate purpose of the traffic stop without additional legal justification. But legal justification can be manufactured from: the smell of marijuana (real or reported), visible paraphernalia in the passenger compartment, air fresheners (accepted as odor-masking evidence in some jurisdictions), nervous behavior, inconsistent answers from different passengers, or a drug-detection dog alert.

The dog is the fulcrum. A drug-detection dog alert — which courts have held creates probable cause for a search — converts a consensual encounter into a search requiring no consent. The dog can be requested while the officer is writing the fix-it ticket. The dog can be requested as a result of anything said during the rapport phase.

The dog does not need to be correct. The dog needs to alert.

**If the dog alerts:** you are in a different situation with different rules. Different from anything in this chapter. Do not answer questions. Do not provide explanations. Say: “I want to speak with an attorney.” Then stop talking. That sentence and silence. Every additional word extends and complicates.

Here's the read on it. If a dog is requested after you have declined a search: the encounter has escalated. Name it to yourself. You are no longer in a traffic stop. You are in an investigative detention. Your rights are different. The attorney sentence applies.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Distributed Carry Decision. **Before the drive:** the car has a conversation about the vehicle’s inventory. Not in the abstract — specifically.

“The car contains [specific items]. Everyone in this car knows what the car contains. If anyone is not comfortable with this, we figure out alternatives before we leave.”

This conversation protects everyone. The person who doesn’t know what is in the car is the most legally vulnerable person in the car — they cannot assert that they didn’t know if they were not given the information to know.

The distributed carry decision is also the ethical minimum: four people in a car are four people in a legal situation. The information belongs to all of them.

Quick vibe check. The dog is not always available. The dog is not always called. The encounter that goes only as far as the fix-it ticket is the common outcome. The probable cause chain is the chapter’s escalation path, not its default. Know the path exists.

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever put other people in a car without giving them the information they needed to make an informed decision about being in it? Have you ever assumed that because nothing has gone wrong before, the information wasn’t necessary? The distributed carry decision is the same obligation as the Chapter 12 gate conversation. It is owed before you leave, not during.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **Undocumented Festies:** The encounter’s escalation path has different consequences for people whose documentation status is uncertain. Know which harm-reduction organizations in your specific area have legal resources for this intersection. Build the resource list before the drive.

This runs in every direction. Probable cause gets manufactured from the air freshener hanging from the mirror, the two cars traveling together, the nervous passenger who won’t make eye contact, and the smell the officer swears he detected before he opened the window. It can be built from behaviors that vary across demographic groups. The dog’s threshold doesn’t discriminate. The officer’s discretion does. Know both.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. The workplace investigation that begins with a friendly “quick chat” and escalates when the wrong thing is said. The escalation path was always there. The friendly framing made it invisible.
