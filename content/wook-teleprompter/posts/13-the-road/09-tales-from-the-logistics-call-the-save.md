## Tales From The Logistics Call — The Save

Festie POV. Counter-Drops live. Named protagonist. Watermelon moment present.

Emmett is twenty-four. He is driving his 2019 Honda Civic. Three people in the car: him, his friend Leila, and Leila’s college friend Bode who Emmett has met once. Eight-hour drive. Good playlist. Everyone comfortable.

**Emmett’s car contains:** camping gear, four days of food, a cooler, and in Emmett’s toiletry case in the back, his personal supply for the weekend.

Three weeks before the festival, Emmett did something he started doing two years ago after a conversation with his cousin who works as a public defender.

He sent a voice note to the group chat. He said: “Hey — logistics call before we leave. Fifteen minutes. This week.”

**On the call:** “I’m carrying personal-use amounts for the weekend. I want everyone in this car to know the inventory of the vehicle they’re in, because if we get stopped, that information is yours, not just mine. If anyone’s not comfortable: genuinely no hard feelings, we figure out alternatives. Also: if we do get stopped, I’ll handle the talking, and we decline any search requests. These are the specific words.” He texted the four sentences.

**Bode, whom Emmett has met once, said:** “I didn’t know you could decline a search. I always thought you had to let them.”

**Emmett said:** “You don’t. Do you want the NORML guide for the state we’re driving through?”

Bode said yes.

On hour six, northbound, a state trooper pulls them over for following too closely. The trooper is friendly. He asks where they’re headed. Emmett says: “Road trip up north.” He does not name the festival. He answers two questions and then, when the trooper says “do you mind if I take a look at the vehicle today”, Emmett says: “I appreciate it, but I don’t consent to a search. Are we free to go once you’ve written the ticket?”

The trooper holds his gaze for one second.

The friendliness does not leave.

“Sure thing. Let me get that written for you.”

Nine minutes later, they are back on the highway.

This is the watermelon moment — not at the stop, but three weeks before it. Emmett in his kitchen sending the voice note and feeling the specific weight of something that had always felt awkward and always been necessary. The pre-commitment that turns mile marker 238 from a decision into a delivery.

**Bode says, from the backseat:** “That was so much calmer than I expected.”

**Emmett says:** “Pre-decided answer. I knew what I was going to say.”

Leila changes the song. They make it. Four days. The sets are extraordinary. They fix the hypothetical brake light that doesn’t exist on Sunday before they leave, just for metaphorical completeness, because Leila thought it was funny and now they’re all committed to the bit.
