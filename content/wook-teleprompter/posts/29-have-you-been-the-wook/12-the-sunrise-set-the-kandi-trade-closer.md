## The Sunrise Set + The Kandi Trade (closer)

Here's the Sunrise Set. Zero slang — just the true thing. He put the book down twice.

He picked it up both times.

He read the chapter.

He opened the note.

That is where accountability begins. Not with a public statement. Not with a ceremony. Not with the very well-written apology letter that you spend six weeks crafting because you need it to land exactly right. With the note. With the name. Written in private. In the backyard. In September.

You are capable of harm.

You have caused some.

You are also capable of accountability.

The inventory is how they become separate things.

The changed behavior is how the separation holds.

The scene is built on the promise of transformation — the idea that in the right conditions, in the right company, at the right hour of the morning, people can become more fully themselves. The scene has made that promise real for thousands of people over five decades. The promise is also available to people who have something on their inventory.

The inventory is not the verdict.

The changed behavior is the future.

You know the name.

Write it down.

And here's the Kandi Trade — the vow.

- I will make the inventory. Private. Honest enough to be uncomfortable when I read it back.
- I will name the pattern where the pattern exists.
- I will ask what it cost them, not what it cost me.
- I will construct the apology for the recipient, not for myself.
- I will ask whether contact is wanted before I make contact.
- I will find the action equivalent of the apology.
- I will run the closed-door audit and answer it honestly.
- I will stop doing the thing first. Then figure out the damage. Then ask what the person needs.
- And I will hold both things: the behavior that went on the inventory and the person who is capable of change.
