## Tales From The Backyard — The Save

Festie POV. Same scenario. Counter-Drops live. Named protagonist. Watermelon Moment present.

He reads the chapter.

He reads the Honest Inventory Track and he takes out his phone and he opens a new note and he titles it “camping gear” and he writes, in the four columns, the entry he has been not writing for two years.

Mira Sandoval. What he did: the amber lantern light conversation, the things he said that were designed to build something, the text he sent three days later that was warm and caring and designed to manage the narrative. What it cost her: she left the camp. She stopped responding to texts. He does not know the full extent. Two years of not looking. Status: nothing done.

He writes a second entry. Older than Mira. 2019. The wire-wrap artist from the Southern California circuit whose name he also knows. He writes the name. The entry is similar in structure and different in specifics.

He writes a third entry.

The third entry is short. Not a full situation. A moment he has been filing as “probably nothing.” He looks at the moment written as four columns and determines, honestly, that the filing was motivated reasoning.

He reads the Pattern Vs. Mistake Audit Track.

He has three entries on the inventory.

All three have a similar shape.

He takes the time with that.

**The watermelon moment:** sitting in the backyard in September with the cold coffee and the note titled “camping gear” and the three entries and the specific weight of looking at a pattern clearly for the first time rather than at individual incidents. The specific physical sensation is in his chest — not the warm one, not the expansion, the other one. The one that arrives when something you have been not looking at is now in plain sight and the not-looking is over.

He reads the Apology Architecture Track.

He does not send anything tonight. That is one of the things the Track says: timing is for the recipient, not the apologizer. He does not know if Mira wants contact from him. He does not know if the wire-wrap artist wants contact. He does not know if the third person does.

He knows what he is going to do first, which is what the Track says to do first: ask whether contact is appropriate, through channels that give the person full freedom to say no without it being awkward or costly.

He does not know how that is going to go. He does not know what the inventory ultimately produces. He does not know what the restitution looks like.

He knows what happened at the amber lantern light. He knows the name.

He knows the name.

He writes it again, at the bottom of the note.

Mira Sandoval.

That is the whole save.

That is the chapter.

The knowing the name and writing it down and looking at it clearly is the first move.

The first move is the accountability starting.
