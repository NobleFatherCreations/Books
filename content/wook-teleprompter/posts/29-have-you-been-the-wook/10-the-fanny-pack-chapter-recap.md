## The Fanny Pack (chapter recap)

Chapter 29. The one you carry for yourself, not for anyone else.

Accountability is not a ceremony. It is: stop doing the thing first. Then figure out the damage. Then ask what the person needs. In that order.

**The Honest Inventory (moral inventory):** Four columns. Name. What I did. What it cost them. Status. Private. Honest enough to be uncomfortable when you read it back.

The Pattern Vs. Mistake Audit (intent/impact/repetition): One instance is a mistake to examine. Multiple instances across multiple people is a pattern to map. The pattern map asks: what did it produce for me? What would I have needed differently?

The Apology That Actually Means It (restorative apology architecture): For the recipient, not for you. No explanation. No request for forgiveness. Does not require a response. Do not send without checking whether contact is wanted.

**The Restitution Question (amends as action):** What action, not what words. The changed behavior over time is the amends.

The Quieter Wook Test (behavior modification vs. concealment): Closed-door audit. Nobody will know, no social cost attached — would you do it again? The test recurs quarterly.

The sixteen moves the book ran on you. All real. All named. The honest version of the book is more useful than the version that pretended otherwise.
