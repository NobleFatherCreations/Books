## The Soundcheck — The Card In The Phone

Before the event. Four minutes, and you will almost certainly never use it, and it costs nothing to carry.

Everything in this chapter depends on information that is impossible to obtain at 5:40 in the morning with a phone at nine percent and no signal. So obtain it in April.

**Step 1 — Put the hotline in your phone as a contact.** In the US: 800-656-4673. Name the contact something you will find while distressed. Not “SA hotline.” Something plain, at the top of your favourites.

**Step 2 — Look up the state coalition for wherever the festival physically is** — not where you live, where the event is — and save that number too. They know which hospitals have examiners and what the non-report rules are in that state. Every state has one and they are free.

**Step 3 — Screenshot all of it.** Screenshots work with no signal. A saved webpage does not. This is the entire reason this step exists.

**Step 4 — Find the nearest hospital with a forensic examiner, once, and write down the town.** Just the town and the drive time. Four minutes of work that saves two hours on the worst morning of somebody’s year.

**Step 5 — Agree the three sentences with your camp, out loud, in daylight.** Not a heavy conversation — ninety seconds, at the same time as the Chapter 14 Thursday Map. “If anyone in this camp has a bad night, the three sentences are: I’ve got you, you don’t have to tell me anything, I’ll drive you and you don’t have to decide anything.” Say it once. Everyone will remember it.

**Step 6 — Decide now who has a functioning car and full gas on Sunday morning.** There is always one. Knowing which one is a four-second question in April and an hour-long problem at 6:31 a.m.

And if you run a festival: the whole of this drill is a laminated card at the medical tent and the harm-reduction tent with four lines on it — the hotline, the state coalition, the nearest facility with an examiner, and the sentence *the exam is not the report.* It costs about forty dollars. Nobody has done it. You could do it this year.
