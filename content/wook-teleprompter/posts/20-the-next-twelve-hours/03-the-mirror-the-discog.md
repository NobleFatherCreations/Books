## The Mirror + The Discog

HAVE YOU BEEN THE WOOK? Mirror Check At Six In The Morning

This chapter has two mirrors and the second one is the hardest thing in this book.

**The first is for the friend.**

Have you asked someone what they took, in the first ten minutes, before anything else? Have you asked what they were wearing, or why they went with him, or whether they said no, or whether they are sure — and felt, while asking, that you were helping them get clear?

Have you made it communal? Woken the camp, gathered the group, made it the thing everyone is dealing with, because that felt like love and like solidarity and like doing something?

Have you gone and found him? Confronted him, or organized a confrontation, or posted about him — before the person it happened to had decided anything at all?

Every item there is done out of love, by decent people, constantly, and every one of them takes options away from someone who has just had options taken away from them. The scene is magnificent at the first seventy-two hours and most of its instincts in the first ninety minutes are wrong.

**The second mirror is the one this book has been building toward since Chapter 5, and it is not comfortable.**

Some of the people reading this have been on the other side of an ambiguous night.

Not as a predator. Almost nobody reading this is Amaru from Chapter 17 or the men in the beige RV. The mirror is not *are you a rapist*, and framing it that way is how everybody gets to skip the question.

The actual question is smaller and much more answerable:

**Was there a night when you proceeded because nothing stopped you?**

Where the other person was very altered, and you were too, and they did not say no, and they did not fight, and you did not check — not because you decided not to, but because checking would have introduced friction into something that was going well, and the not-checking was not a decision you experienced yourself making.

Chapter 5 said it in six words and this chapter is what the other end of those six words looks like at 5:40 in the morning: a woman doing an inventory of objects and distances because her memory has a gap with things in it.

Tonic immobility means that *they didn’t resist* is not evidence of anything. Fragmentation means that *she seemed into it* may be your account of a person who cannot currently produce an account at all. And a yes at that level of altered is the same yes this book spent Chapter 17 taking apart, and it does not become a different kind of yes because the room had a string of lights instead of an altar.

If that landed somewhere, the response is not a confession and it is not a post. Those are for you, not for them.

It is: **change what you do from here.** Ask. Out loud. Every time. Accept that asking is unsexy and do it anyway. Stop at ambiguity rather than proceeding through it. Treat “they didn’t say no” as the absence of information rather than the presence of consent — because that is precisely what it is, and it always was, and the only thing that ever made it feel otherwise was that nothing stopped you.

Here's the Wook Discog for this chapter. Studio Debut — The Baby Wook learning the moves

Twenty-two, and it is not the assailant — it is the friend. Wakes the whole camp. Asks what she took within the first four minutes, because it seems relevant and because they are frightened. Goes to find him at nine in the morning with two other people. Posts a story at eleven with no name in it that everybody decodes by noon. Deletes the group thread later that week “so it isn’t out there.” Every single action taken out of genuine love, at speed, by a person who has never been taught a single thing about this and is doing the only things that feel like doing something. The Studio Debut is the reason Protocol 5 exists, and it is the most common failure in this entire chapter, and it is committed almost exclusively by people who would take a bullet for the person they are harming.

Live Album — The Seasoned Operator

The event, at year fifteen. Has a reporting email address on the website. Takes a statement at the info booth on Sunday from a volunteer with no training who is also managing a lost-and-found queue. Files an incident report that preserves nothing, collects nothing, and starts no clock. Removes him, sometimes, if there is enough. Sends a sincere and genuinely well-meant email in October. Has no relationship with the hospital ninety minutes away, does not know whether it has a trained examiner on weekends, has never printed the state coalition’s number on anything, and would be astonished to learn that the single highest-value thing it could do costs about forty dollars and is a laminated card at the medical tent.

Greatest Hits Compilation — The Institutional Scaled-Up Version

The rural hospital ninety minutes from three major festivals with no SANE-certified examiner on weekend shifts. The neighbouring county where the nearest trained examiner is fifty minutes further and nobody at the first desk knows it. The kits sitting untested in storage, in numbers that have been the subject of multi-year audits in multiple states. The prevention literature — thousands of pages of it, at every festival in the country, all of it about the before, almost none of it about the after. And a scene that will mobilize a hundred people to search a field for a missing friend, brilliantly, at 2 a.m., and has never once put the number for the state sexual assault coalition on the back of a wristband, where it would cost nothing and be there at 5:40 in the morning when the phone is at nine percent.
