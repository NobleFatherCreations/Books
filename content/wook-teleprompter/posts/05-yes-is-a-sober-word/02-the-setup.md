🎛️ THE TAPERS’ SECTION

Harm Reduction · Capacity Theory · DanceSafe Lineage

A little theory. Then back to the dust.

Harm reduction is the idea that people will use substances regardless of moralizing, so the goal is keeping them as safe as possible. Test kits. Hydration. Cool-down spaces. Trusted information. Accurate dosing. Drug interaction awareness. Crisis support without punishment or criminalization. In scene language: Zendo Project, DanceSafe, Wharf Rats, the medical tent, the psychedelic peer support volunteer with a clipboard and a fanny pack with stickers and Narcan in it. These organizations have been building the language this chapter uses for decades. We are not inventing it. We are translating it into the register of the PLURth Angel at four a.m. with a flashlight under the chin.

Capacity theory is the legal and ethical idea that consent requires a person who can understand, weigh, and freely choose. A person who is blackout, unconscious, dissociated, at psychedelic peak, in physical crisis, or inside a coercive emotional frame cannot give meaningful consent regardless of what their body or mouth does. In scene language: a body shape is not a yes. A nod is not a yes. A sure through chattering teeth is not a yes. A laugh is not a yes. Gratitude is not a yes. Being alone with someone in a kitchen at three-thirty a.m. is not a yes.

The DanceSafe and Zendo lineage also gives us this: a capacity-aware crew tests their substances, sets a dose plan, has a sober anchor, agrees on check-ins, and treats consent as something that must be re-asked when the chemistry shifts. A capacity-disrespecting Wook treats your altered state as a green light, your we already started as a contract, and your blackout as a mystery instead of a crime.

That is enough. Put your harm-reduction sticker back on the cooler. Let’s walk.
