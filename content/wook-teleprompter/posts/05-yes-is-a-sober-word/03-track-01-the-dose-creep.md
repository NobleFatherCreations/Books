### TRACK 01 — THE DOSE CREEP
*(incremental boundary erosion)*

Here's the behavior. You agreed to a small thing.

Half a tab of lucy. One bump. A microdose. A two-finger pour. A short ceremony. A light cup of tea. An edible labeled starter dose by a man who is not a labeling authority and whose certification, if asked, is “I’ve been doing this for years.”

Then the math starts moving.

“That batch runs weaker, you’ll want another.” “This is basically the same as what you already did.” “You’re tolerating it well — you can handle a little more.” “That dose is for tourists.” “You’re not feeling it because you’re blocking.” “Just lick the rim.” “This is basically just kava.”

The Dose Creep is not always coercive in tone. Sometimes it is friendly, paternal, maternal, spiritual. The voice is calm. The pace is slow. The escalation looks like generosity. But the contract you agreed to is not the contract being run. You said yes to half a tab of lucy in a tested batch from a known source. You did not say yes to the follow-on that the other person decided, inside their own head, was the logical extension of your original yes.

Here's the read on it. Watch for math that changes after consent. Did the dose described match what was offered? Did the follow-on offer come before or after you hit your original ceiling? Are they tracking your dose or losing track on purpose? Are they refusing to name milligrams, grams, drops, or duration? Are they using the word “intuitive” to mean “I’m not measuring”?

Vague dosing is not mysticism. Vague dosing is plausible deniability.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Pre-Set Dose Plan. Set the plan before the lasers start. Before the event. Before the camp opens. Before the cooler is touched. Not at eleven p.m. with a man named Atlas pouring tea.

Step 1 — Decide your ceiling sober. Write it down. “Tonight: one tab, tested, nothing else.” “Tonight: one bump at midnight, water after, that’s the set.” “Tonight: sober. Cacao only. No exceptions.” The note lives in your phone where your altered self can read it.

Step 2 — Tell one anchor. The anchor knows the plan. The anchor is allowed to say “that’s not the plan” and the pre-agreement is that you listen when they say that.

Step 3 — Refuse renegotiation in altered states. “I don’t update my dose plan after I’ve started dosing.” Or the shorter version: “Future me already voted.”

Step 4 — Treat every upsell as data. If someone keeps pushing past your stated ceiling, they are not being friendly. They are trying to lower your capacity. That is a Track, not a vibe.

Quick vibe check. People can offer you things kindly. You can decline kindly. The test is what happens after the third no. Green: accepts and redirects. Yellow: keeps the option warm. Red: escalates, reframes your no as your problem, finds a new angle.

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever pushed someone past their stated dose because you wanted company at the peak? Have you ever told someone “you’re not feeling it” because their slowness was inconveniencing your timeline? Be honest about which side of this you have been on. Then stop running it.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **First-Timer:** A veteran’s tolerance is not your floor. What takes them to baseline will take you somewhere else entirely. Set your ceiling based on your body and your experience level, not theirs.

**Sober and Recovery Lane:** “Just a sip” is not a small ask. It is the whole ask. Your sobriety is not an inconvenience to the group’s chemistry plan. You do not owe anyone a modified version of your sobriety to make the night more comfortable for them.

**Ceremony Lane:** A facilitator who says the dose is determined intuitively and cannot be specified in advance is not running a ceremony. They are running a dose-accountability bypass. Real ceremony practice has named protocols. Ask for them before you sit down.

This runs in every direction. The Dose Creep is run by male producers who pour generously, femme facilitators who say “the medicine knows what you need,” nonbinary elders whose ceremonial authority is used to override questions, touring veterans whose confidence is the credential, and camp leads who treat their kitchen supply as a social tool. The pour is not gendered. The push past no is not gendered.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. If a waiter refilled your wine glass twice after you said you were fine and then described the third refill as “your body wanted it,” you would leave a different tip. The restaurant does not get a pass because the candles were nice.
