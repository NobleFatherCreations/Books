### TRACK 06 — THE PLUG LEVERAGE
*(supply-as-coercion in consent)*

Here's the behavior. The plug becomes the relationship.

It starts as access. Then it becomes the only access. Then it becomes the only safe access, because theirs is clean and everyone else’s is suspect, and the story about everyone else’s quality is something they tell you with a frequency that is slightly too convenient to be only about your safety. Then the price changes. Then the price is not money.

“You can crash with me, it’s easier that way.” “I’ll just bring it to your tent — come out after.” “Don’t go to anyone else, I’ve seen what they’re moving and it’s dirty.” “I’ll cover you tonight, we’ll figure it out later.” “You owe me, but not in money.”

The Plug Leverage Wook is not always selling substances. Sometimes they are selling festival tickets, backstage wristbands, ceremony invites, gear loans, campsite space, a vehicle seat on the last caravan out. The pattern is identical. They become the supplier of something you need or want. They make themselves the only clean source. Then they price it in something other than money — in loyalty, in silence, in physical availability, in the suppression of a concern you were about to raise.

A real plug is a vendor. A Wook plug is a landlord.

Here's the read on it. Is the supply line tied to favors that grow? Are you being told other sources are unsafe in a way that creates exclusive dependence? Are you being made responsible for managing one person’s goodwill in order to maintain your access? Are you being asked to repay in body, secrecy, labor, or loyalty?

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Supply Independence Plan. Step 1 — Map the dependency. What does this person supply you with? Be specific. Substances, housing, tickets, rides, work, social access. Write the list.

Step 2 — Build two alternates per item. Two test-kit sources. Two ride options. Two housing contacts. Two friends who can spot you financially. The redundancy breaks the leverage.

Step 3 — Refuse non-monetary repayment explicitly. “Money is the only form of repayment I’m doing. If that doesn’t work for you, I need to find another source.” If the deal cannot survive that sentence, the deal was a leash.

Step 4 — Pay forward, not back. If you want to honor a genuine favor, give something equivalent to someone else in the scene who needs it. Wooks are structurally opposed to this because forward payment cannot be collected by them.

Step 5 — Test the relationship by buying nothing for one week. Stop using their supply. Observe what happens to the warmth, the access, the communication. The answer will be clear and it will be fast.

Quick vibe check. Real friends in the supply chain exist. They are delighted when you find a better or safer or cheaper source. They want you safe, not dependent. They do not mention what they have done for you during conversations about what you owe them. The distinction between a vendor-friend and a leverage-plug is: what happens to the friendship when you need nothing from them.

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever held supply over someone’s head — not overtly, just: made yourself the most convenient option and let the convenience do the work of binding them to you? Have you ever priced access in loyalty rather than money because loyalty was what you actually wanted? Name it without theater. Then stop running it.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **Touring Lane:** A tour-bus plug is a tour-bus boss. The person who supplies the whole bus controls the whole bus. Build outside lines before you need them — before you are four dates into a tour and the only option is the person running the leverage.

**Recovery Lane:** The “I got you” plug and the “I got you” sponsor can both be leverage if the price is silence about the relationship. Being clean of one substance does not automatically clean the leverage structure.

**Queer Scene:** Supply leverage in small queer scenes can involve social access, community platforms, safe housing, or event invites rather than substances. The mechanism is identical. The thing supplied differs. The price is the same: compliance or exile.

This runs in every direction. The Plug Leverage runs equally from men, women, nonbinary people, elders, peers, touring artists, promoters, camp leads, ceremony facilitators, housing providers, and the person with the one van going to the festival. Supply is power. Power is not gendered.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. The landlord who controls which unit comes open next and “just happens” to move fastest for the tenants who never call about the leak. The supply is a better apartment. The price is silence. The leverage is called management.
