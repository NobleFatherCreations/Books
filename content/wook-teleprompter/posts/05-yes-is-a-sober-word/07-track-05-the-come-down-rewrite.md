### TRACK 05 — THE COME-DOWN REWRITE
*(post-event narrative control / DARVO precursor)*

Here's the behavior. Sunday morning. Your brain is in the slow reboot. The synapses are tired. The emotional skin is thin. The memories of the previous thirty-six hours exist in a way that is vivid in some patches and completely absent in others, with no reliable way to distinguish the vivid-accurate from the vivid-constructed or the absent-because-it-didn’t-happen from the absent-because-your-brain-was-elsewhere.

This is when they call.

“Last night was so incredible.” “I’ve never felt that kind of connection.” “You were so present, so open.” “We had such a deep moment — I keep thinking about it.” “That’s wild, you seemed so into everything.”

The Come-Down Rewrite is when someone else interprets the night for you while your brain is still rebooting. The interpretation is always slightly — sometimes massively — favorable to them. The corners they round are the corners you would have wanted to keep. The parts they describe as “incredible” are the parts your fragmentary memory cannot fully confirm or deny. And because humans default to the first coherent version of an unclear event — because the brain hates ambiguity and reaches for narrative resolution — the version they give you has a high chance of becoming yours.

If you challenge it, you are confused. Or ungrateful. Or “stuck in your head.” Or “letting the come-down color something beautiful.”

The Rewrite is also a DARVO precursor. If the night involved something you later name as harm, the Rewrite is the first layer of the defense: she said yes, she was so into it, she was totally present, she was the one who— The Rewrite gets established while your memory is most plastic. It gets established before you can consult your anchor. It gets established before you find out what other people saw.

Here's the read on it. Are they the first person to tell you what happened? Are they framing your memory gaps as confirmation of their version — “you were so far gone, you probably don’t remember, but”? Are they discouraging you from talking to other witnesses? Are they framing your discomfort as a misinterpretation of something wonderful?

Memory after altered states is fragile and Wooks know this. So do good friends. The difference is what they do with that knowledge.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Receipt Sweep. Step 1 — Before talking to them, talk to your anchor. Anchor’s version first. Before anyone else’s.

Step 2 — Check your phone independently. Texts you sent. Photos timestamped. Location data. Voice notes you made. Step count. Battery drain patterns. Anything that creates an independent record.

Step 3 — Check with witnesses outside their social circle. The vendor who was nearby. The harm-reduction volunteer. The friend from a different camp. The person sober for the night.

Step 4 — Write your own version before reading theirs. Even if it is fragmented. Especially fragmented. Your fragmented version is still your version and it has standing.

Step 5 — If their version contradicts the receipts, trust the receipts. Not their description of what “clearly” happened. The receipts.

Quick vibe check. People can disagree about a shared night without running a Rewrite. Memory is genuinely fallible on both sides. The test is not do we remember it differently. The test is: do they make space for your version, or do they replace it? Do they offer their account as one account, or as the account?

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever told someone “you said yes” when you were not entirely certain, because the certainty made the morning more comfortable for you? Have you ever been the first voice in someone’s Sunday, soft and sure, interpreting a night when you knew they could not fully check? That is the Rewrite in a relationship that did not feel coercive. It is still the Rewrite.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **Sober Lane:** You are often the only person in the crew with a clean tape. Your version of the night is community infrastructure. Tell it. Do not soften it to protect someone else’s narrative.

**Survivor Lane:** A Come-Down Rewrite that replaces your experience of harm with someone else’s account of beauty is not just gaslighting. It is the first layer of the coercive-control documentation that will be deployed if you ever name what happened. Document your version before their version becomes the shared memory.

This runs in every direction. The Rewrite is run by anyone who benefits from a particular version of the night. It does not require malice — sometimes people genuinely believe their interpretation, which makes it more persistent and harder to counter. The Rewrite runs across every gender because the underlying mechanic — fill the memory gap with the story that serves you — is available to everyone.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. The project retrospective where the person who caused the failure presents their version of events before anyone else has spoken, and by the end of the meeting that version is what everyone remembers. The Rewrite runs in every debrief where one person speaks first and longest.
