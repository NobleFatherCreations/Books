Minimum theory. Maximum practicality. Then immediately back to the dust.

Entrapment as a legal defense is significantly narrower than most people believe. Entrapment requires that law enforcement induced someone to commit a crime they were not otherwise predisposed to commit. Courts have interpreted “predisposition” very broadly. If you have ever possessed a controlled substance, ever distributed to a friend, ever expressed interest in obtaining — you are likely considered predisposed. This means the guy in the sun hat will almost certainly not win an entrapment defense. The defense exists in theory and is almost never successful in practice. Know this before you count on it.
