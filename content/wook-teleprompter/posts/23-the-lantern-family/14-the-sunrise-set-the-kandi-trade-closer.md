## The Sunrise Set + The Kandi Trade (closer)

Here's the Sunrise Set. Zero slang — just the true thing. The napkin is written in pencil, in handwriting that has been practiced for steadiness.

The steadiness is the whole story of this chapter. The steadiness of someone who is still inside a situation that has cost them something they will not fully name for another year, and who has decided that the cost of passing the information forward is less than the cost of not passing it. The steadiness of someone who cannot leave yet but knows where the door is and is drawing a map for the next person.

We can’t leave yet but you still can.

The scene produces Elias. It always has and it probably always will, because the conditions the scene creates — belonging-hunger, altered states, chosen family, the road, the bus, the fire, the music — are the same conditions that produce the most real magic the scene has to offer. El knows this. El has been using this for nineteen years.

The scene also produces the older girl who wrote the napkin.

The scene produces six women on a Signal thread.

The scene produces Cara, who got the forty-five-minute call and went in with the setlist and came home Sunday night in her own car.

The immune system is the scene’s own people, passing information to each other in the specific quiet way of people who love something enough to protect it from the inside.

Write the napkin.

Pass it forward.

The thread does not end.

And here's the Kandi Trade — the vow.

- I will ask what it costs to leave before I decide to stay.
- I will find the people who left before I get on the bus.
- I will translate the vocabulary into plain English and read what the translation says.
- I will write the napkin when I know what the napkin says.
- I will pass it forward.
- I will maintain contact with the person inside, without ultimatums, without disappearing.
- I will do the Tuesday-by-Tuesday plan when I need to exit. One connection. One option. One thread at a time.
- I will remember that the door is always open and that “the door is always open” is the sentence that the door needs to prove itself on.
- The door proves itself by what it costs to walk through.

PROTECT THE
F*CKING MAGIC
