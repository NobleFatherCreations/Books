### TRACK 07 — THE LONG EXIT
*(staged departure protocols from coercive groups)*

Here's the behavior. The door is always open.

El says this.

This is a family. We don’t keep anyone. The door is always open.

The door is technically always open in the same way the Constitution is technically the law of the land — as an abstract principle that is functionally modified by an entire system of practice that makes the abstract principle very expensive to invoke.

The Long Exit is not a single dramatic departure. It is the understanding that leaving a coercive group requires a sequence of preparations that happen over time, mostly invisibly, before the physical departure becomes possible. It requires: external social connections (built quietly, not announced), alternative housing (secured before you need it), financial independence (accumulated separately from the group’s shared resources), the information about what you are leaving (the napkin, the exit interview, the pattern map), and the recognition that the departure will be narrated by the group in terms that you will not be able to control and may be costly in your social network.

Cara does not leave the bus in Colorado. She does not leave the bus at the next festival. She leaves the bus in Portland, in October, five months after Colorado, after five months of building the infrastructure for the exit while appearing, from the inside, to be exactly where she has always been, drinking only from sealed bottles, saying nothing about the napkin.

The Long Exit is not cowardice. The Long Exit is the rational response to a situation in which the immediate exit has costs that exceed the five-month wait for a prepared one.

Here's the read on it. If you are in a group where the door is always open but the cost of walking through it is everything — housing, transport, income, social network, identity — the door is not open in any functional sense. The door is open in the same way the bottom of a cliff is open to people standing at the top of it. Technically available. Practically a different conversation.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Tuesday-by-Tuesday Plan. **Week one:** identify one external social connection to maintain and strengthen. Not through the group. Independently. This week.

**Week two:** identify alternative housing. Not final housing. Possible housing. One person whose couch you could sleep on for a week if you needed to. One option. That is all week two requires.

**Week three:** map the financial situation. What is in the group’s shared resources versus what is yours. What would you need to build before you could leave without the group’s resources? Name the number.

**Week four:** one external contact who knows you are in the group and whom you trust with the full story. This person is the outside anchor — the one who knows the pattern, who can verify the Aged-Out Disappearance, who will be there when the exit happens.

**Week five and forward:** accumulate. One external connection per week. One more housing option. One more dollar toward independence. The Tuesday-by-Tuesday plan is not a sprint. It is the infrastructure for a departure that can happen when you are ready.

**The day you walk through the door:** you do not announce it in advance. You do not give El the opportunity to manage the narrative of your departure before the departure. You leave. You communicate with the people you want to communicate with directly and individually. You do not engage with the group’s characterization of your exit.

“I’m going.” Two words. Everything else is optional.

Quick vibe check. Some exits can be fast. Some groups have a genuine open door and the departure, while sad, is clean. The Long Exit is specifically for situations where the group’s control structure makes the immediate exit dangerous — physically, financially, emotionally, or in terms of your outside social network. Assess your situation honestly. If the immediate exit is available: take it. If it is not: build the infrastructure.

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever made it difficult for someone to leave a group, a relationship, a project, or an arrangement — not by blocking the door but by making the cost of walking through it very high? The Long Exit’s necessity is created by the group that made the exit expensive. If you have made exits expensive: examine how you did that and stop.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **Domestic Violence Lane:** The Long Exit has significant structural overlap with safety planning for domestic violence situations. ICSA (International Cultic Studies Association) and DV-specific organizations both have exit planning resources. The planning principles are similar because the structural dynamics are similar.

**LGBTQ+ Community Members in Coercive Groups:** If your social network, your housing, your safety, and your identity are all embedded in the group, the exit cost is especially high. The Tuesday-by-Tuesday plan applies specifically here. One external LGBTQ+-specific resource per week. The Trevor Project, the ICSA, local community centers — build the external network one thread at a time.

This runs in every direction. The Long Exit gets planned by the woman quietly rebuilding a bank account outside El’s system, by the collective member reconnecting with a sister she stopped calling, by the residential program alum reaching out to an old coworker, and by anyone in a group led by any gender or identity configuration. The planning is the same. The external resources vary. The first week is the same: identify one external connection and strengthen it.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. The job you cannot leave because your visa is sponsored by the employer, your housing is through the company, your professional network is entirely internal, and your savings account currently contains eleven hundred dollars. The Tuesday-by-Tuesday plan: one external professional contact per week. One month of living expenses saved before announcing departure. Two job leads before the resignation. The planning is not betrayal. The planning is exit infrastructure.
