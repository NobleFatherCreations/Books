### TRACK 06 — THE NAPKIN NETWORK
*(intra-group dissident communication — reciprocity hook)*

Here's the behavior. It happened to all of us. Don’t drink anything he hands you. We can’t leave yet but you still can. We’re sorry. — your sisters.

The Napkin Network is not a Wook Track.

The Napkin Network is the immune system.

It belongs in the Wook’s Setlist because it is the intra-group dissident communication that the coercive group cannot prevent without making its coercion visible — and which, when it runs, is the first crack in the control structure. It is in the Tracks section because understanding it is as important as understanding every other Track. Possibly more important.

The napkin exists because the older girl who wrote it could not leave yet but had information that was costing someone else something she knew the cost of. The napkin is two sentences of harm-reduction information and two sentences of community. We can’t leave yet but you still can. The information is the harm reduction. The we’re sorry is the community. Both in pencil. Both steady, from practice.

The napkin is also, three years later, the reason six women are on a Signal thread together. The napkin is why Cara, when she eventually leaves the bus in Portland in October, does not leave alone. She leaves into a network. A network that was started by a folded piece of paper dropped on a chest in Colorado between runs.

The Napkin Network is the chapter’s discernment hook turned inside out. The Wooks in this book depend on isolation. They depend on the person not knowing the pattern. They depend on the frame holding and the language not having a translation and the exits being managed and the witnesses being gone. The napkin breaks every one of those dependencies simultaneously. It says: you are not alone, we have been here before you, the frame is a frame, the history exists.

That is the whole immune system. Two sentences on a piece of paper from a notebook.

Here's the read on it. **If you are inside a coercive group:** look for the napkins. They exist. They are usually in the people who are positioned furthest from the center — the aged-out, the about-to-leave, the ones who have stopped using the full vocabulary. The napkin is in their face when they look at you. Sometimes it is in an actual piece of paper.

Look for it.

Pass it forward.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Napkin Protocol. If you are in a position to know what you know and cannot yet leave:

Step 1 — Find one person who is newer than you and being positioned for what you have been through. You know who this person is. You know.

Step 2 — Write the minimum necessary information. Plain language. “This happened. Don’t let X happen. You can leave. Here is how.” Specific, behavioral, actionable.

Step 3 — Deliver it without a conversation. The conversation creates risk. The napkin creates the information without creating the risk of the conversation.

**Step 4 — If you can leave:** the napkin is the bridge. It connects the information you carry to the person who needs it and it starts the chain.

**Step 5 — If you cannot leave yet:** the napkin is still the chapter. It is the immune system’s first move. Make it.

Quick vibe check. Not every piece of information passed between group members in private is a Napkin. Groups have private conversations that are not dissident. The Napkin is specifically: information about harm, passed from someone who has the information to someone who needs it, in a context where the information cannot be passed through the group’s normal communication channels.

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever been the person who knew and said nothing? Have you ever watched someone new arrive at a position you understood from the inside and not passed the information you had? The Napkin Network requires someone to write the first napkin. Sometimes that person is you. Sometimes you have been carrying the information for a long time and have not passed it yet.

Write the napkin.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **Survivor Lane:** If you were in a coercive group and you carried information about harm for years and did not or could not pass it: that is not a failure of courage. That is the control structure doing its job. The napkin is not about blame. It is about what happens when one thread of the immune system activates. If you have information: write it when you can. Pass it when it is safe to do. The thread does not expire.

This runs in every direction. The Napkin Network gets passed by the woman who’s been on the bus three years and knows what not to say out loud, by the newest member of the healing collective who overhears something and writes it down, by the friend who slips a folded note at the gas station, and by anyone in harm’s way passing warning to anyone about to be. It is the most democratic tool in this book. It requires only something to write with and someone to receive it.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. The whistleblower email that goes to one colleague before it goes to the regulatory body. The conversation in the break room that tells the new hire what the manager actually does versus what the handbook says. The text to the first-year resident from the second-year resident that says “don’t be alone with him.” The napkin runs in every institution that has harm it cannot afford to have named through official channels.
