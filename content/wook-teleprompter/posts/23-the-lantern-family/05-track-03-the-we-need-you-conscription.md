### TRACK 03 — THE “WE NEED YOU” CONSCRIPTION
*(duty-based labor and body extraction)*

Here's the behavior. We need you on the rail tonight.

This sentence is how the Kombucha lands. Not because the mason jar is in El’s hand — he has given her jars before — but because we need you activates the specific duty-response that three years of Family Frame conditioning has installed. The family needs her. The family’s need is her obligation. The rail is the expression of that obligation. The rail is where you prove your commitment to what the family is building by being visibly present in it.

The “We Need You” Conscription is the deployment of group duty as a body-extraction tool. It runs on the Family Frame’s installed obligation structure to produce physical presence, physical compliance, physical proximity to El, in circumstances where that proximity is necessary for what El is going to do next.

The conscription also runs for labor. Build weeks. Kitchen shifts. Van loading. The generator situation. The twenty-two people who need the portal toilet moved at three a.m. “We need you” is the sentence that makes refusal into dereliction. Not of a task — of the family. The family’s need is the argument. The argument is pre-installed.

Here's the read on it. Notice when “we need you” is used for things that require your physical presence specifically rather than your contribution generally. Notice when the need is time-sensitive and the time sensitivity closes your evaluation window. Notice when the need arrives accompanied by something that would affect your capacity — a jar, a cup, a “here, drink this first.”

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Duty Decoupler. **Step 1 — When “we need you” arrives:** pause. Name the ask in plain terms. What specifically is needed? From you specifically? Why you specifically?

**Step 2 — Ask:** what is the cost of saying no? A real need accepts a real no without emotional punishment. A conscription does not.

Step 3 — Decline once, cleanly. “I’m not able to do that tonight.” Watch what happens to the warmth in the thirty seconds after the decline.

**Step 4 — If the warmth cools:** the need was a conscription. The cooling is information. Document it.

Quick vibe check. Communities genuinely need contributions from their members. “We need you” can be a real statement about a real communal need. The test is whether the no is accepted warmly or whether it produces the cooling, the concern, the “everything okay? I just noticed you’ve been pulling away” that signals the need was duty rather than request.

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever used “we need you” to make someone’s presence feel obligatory when what you wanted was their compliance with a specific thing? The duty framing converts a request into a referendum on belonging. The distinction is whether “no” is acceptable.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **Activist and Movement Lane:** The “we need you” conscription runs in activist organizations with high stakes — “if you don’t show up, the movement suffers, the people we’re fighting for suffer.” The stakes are real. The duty-activation is still a manipulation. You are allowed to have limits even when the cause is real.

This runs in every direction. El runs the conscription on the women in his bus. The femme community leader runs it on her network. The queer collective lead runs it on chosen family. The camp lead runs it on first-timers who don’t yet know they can say no. The duty-activation is the Track, not the gender of the person running it.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. “I need you on this call.” Said by the manager at seven p.m. on a Thursday. The need is real. The sentence is also designed to activate the specific professional-duty response that makes declining feel like abandoning the team. The plain version is: “Can you be on this call?” The plain version allows a no. The conscription version does not.
