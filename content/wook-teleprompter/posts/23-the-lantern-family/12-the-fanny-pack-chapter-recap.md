## The Fanny Pack (chapter recap)

Chapter 23. The one you read before you get in the bus.

The Family Frame (pseudo-kinship as control structure): Ask what it costs to leave. If the answer is everything — the frame is a cage, not a family.

The Bus Hierarchy (age-based stratification): Map who is where and for how long. Talk to the people furthest from the center.

The “We Need You” Conscription (duty-based extraction): “We need you” that doesn’t accept “not tonight” is conscription, not a request.

**The Sister Word (linguistic capture):** Translate every group vocabulary term into plain English. If the translation makes a problem appear that the vocabulary hid — the vocabulary is the control.

The Aged-Out Disappearance (witness management): Find the people who left. Not the group’s story. Their story.

The Napkin Network (intra-group dissident communication): If you are inside one — look for the napkins. Write the napkin. Pass it forward.

**The Long Exit (staged departure protocol):** Tuesday-by-Tuesday. One external connection. One housing option. One more dollar. The door is open when the infrastructure is built.

“We’ve been waiting for someone like you” before he knows your name is the whole chapter in one sentence.

🔰 SAFETY APPENDIX — CHAPTER 23
- If you’re inside one, or someone you love is.
- IF YOU ARE INSIDE A HIGH-CONTROL GROUP
- The Tuesday-by-Tuesday Exit Plan:
- Week 1: Identify one external social contact to maintain independently of the group. Text or call them this week.
- Week 2: Identify one possible housing option outside the group. You do not need to commit. Just know it exists.
- Week 3: Map the financial situation. What is yours versus the group’s. What you need to build.
- Week 4: Tell one outside person the full story. Someone who knows the scene, knows the pattern, and can help you see clearly.
- Ongoing: one external connection per week. The infrastructure grows quietly. The exit happens when it is ready.
- When you leave: tell no one inside the group in advance. Leave. Communicate after. The narrative is theirs to manage. Your life is yours to live.
- IF SOMEONE YOU LOVE IS INSIDE ONE
- Do not issue ultimatums. Ultimatums accelerate the isolation the group is already building.
- Maintain contact. Every message you send is a thread of outside connection. Keep sending it. “Thinking of you. No pressure to respond.”
- Do not compete with the group’s warmth. You cannot win a warmth competition against a group that has engineered belonging as a retention tool.
- Be available. Not demanding. “I’m here whenever you want to talk. No agenda.”
- Name what you see, once, gently, without judgment: “I’ve noticed that when I ask about [specific person/behavior] you seem to change the subject. I’m not pushing. I just want you to know I see it.” Then drop it. The seed is planted. It germinates on its own time.
- Do not disappear. The group is counting on you disappearing.

- THE NAPKIN PROTOCOL
- If you are inside a coercive group and cannot yet leave:
- 1. Identify the person most recently arrived who is being positioned as you were positioned.
- 2. Write the minimum necessary information: what happened, what to avoid, how to leave, that they are not alone.
- 3. Deliver without a conversation. The delivery is the communication.
- 4. The act of writing the napkin is the act of preparing to leave. It is the first move.
