### TRACK 05 — THE AGED-OUT DISAPPEARANCE
*(involuntary exclusion of inconvenient witnesses)*

Here's the behavior. The older girls who left and now say things.

They are not in the circuit. Their names are not in the Lantern Family’s story. El says, in the fire-circle history that opens every run, “we have been touched by so many beautiful souls over the years, some of whom have gone on to their own journeys” — and that is the full accounting of every person who has ever left the bus.

Gone on to their own journeys.

**Not:** she left because this happened. Not: she left because we had a conversation about something. Not: she left because the older girl who came before her left because the older girl who came before her left — each of them, over nineteen years, “not ready for the work” or “going through something” or “on their own journey.”

The pattern is the evidence and the pattern is invisible because the pattern’s subjects are invisible, which is the function of the Aged-Out Disappearance: the witnesses to the fullest version of the bus’s history are managed out of the bus at the point at which they become most inconvenient, and their departure is explained in terms that discredit them before anyone can ask them what they know.

Here's the read on it. **Ask:** where are the people who were here before you? Not the official story. Their actual location and their actual story.

If the official story of why they left is consistent — consistently developmental, consistently the leaver’s insufficiency, consistently “the family couldn’t give them what they needed” rather than “they chose to go” — the official story is narrative management.

Ask one of them directly. Find one who left. Ask what happened. The conversation is the evidence.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Exit Interview Protocol. Step 1 — Before joining any group that has a significant history: identify two or three people who left. Not who the group identifies. People whose names you find independently.

Step 2 — Contact them directly. Not through the group’s network. Directly.

**Step 3 — Ask:** “Why did you leave? What was it like?” Listen without filtering for consistency with the group’s narrative.

Step 4 — If the stories of independent leavers are consistent with each other and inconsistent with the group’s narrative: the group’s narrative is management.

Step 5 — If the group discourages contact with former members: that discouragement is the most important finding of the exit interview protocol. It means the members have information the group cannot afford you to have.

Quick vibe check. Some former members leave with complicated feelings and say complicated things that are partly accurate and partly their own process. Not every critical former member is an objective witness. The test is pattern: do multiple independent former members describe similar experiences? One complicated ex is data. Five independent descriptions of the same specific behavior is a pattern.

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever managed someone’s departure from a group by preloading the narrative of their departure? Have you ever described someone’s exit as their insufficiency rather than as a response to your behavior? The Aged-Out Disappearance runs in every group that has exits it needs to explain without examining.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **Festival Circuit Regulars:** The Lantern Family travels a circuit. The circuit knows them. The circuit has heard El’s version. The circuit has also, if you ask the right people, heard the older girls’ version. The circuit whisper network contains the history. Ask the circuit.

This runs in every direction. The disappearance gets managed by El about the women who leave his bus, by the collective that never talks about who used to be here, by the residential program whose alumni simply stop being mentioned, and by any group that controls the narrative of its own exits. It runs equally in groups led by any gender or identity.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. The company where departing employees are required to sign NDAs and where the exit interview is conducted by the manager the employee is leaving. The company’s LinkedIn posts about its “culture of growth and development” do not include the dozen people who left in the last eighteen months. Their stories are their stories and the company’s story is the company’s story and the two versions live in entirely separate rooms that are specifically prevented from ever meeting.
