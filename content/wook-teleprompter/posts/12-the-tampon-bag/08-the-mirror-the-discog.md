## The Mirror + The Discog

HAVE YOU BEEN THE WOOK? Mirror Check At The Gate Line

The Borrowed Mule is uncomfortable to apply to yourself because the scenario seems extreme. But the architecture scales down.

Have you ever asked someone to hold something that was yours, for your safety, without fully explaining what you were asking them to hold? Have you ever used “it’s probably fine” as a way to activate someone’s yes without activating their assessment? Have you ever installed a narrative — about the relationship, about the situation, about who would know what — before an event where that narrative might be needed?

Have you ever been in a car with something in it and let someone else believe they knew what was in the car when they did not?

The scale of the consequence in this chapter is extreme. The architecture of the move is not. It runs in smaller configurations constantly. “Just hold this for a sec.” “Don’t mention to anyone that I—.” “If anyone asks, you just didn’t see anything.” Those are all the Rehearsed Sentence in smaller typeface. They are all the Borrowed Mule in an earlier stage.

Notice the architecture. Stop using it.

The Ride-Or-Die relationship is worth having. The Ride-Or-Die relationship requires two people running it. If you are the only one driving, look at the passenger seat.
