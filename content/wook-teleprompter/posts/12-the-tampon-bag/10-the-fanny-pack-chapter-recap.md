🔰 SAFETY APPENDIX — CHAPTER 12

- CONSTRUCTIVE POSSESSION — THE BASICS
- Constructive possession means you are legally responsible for contraband if:
- It is in a space you control (your car, your bag, your tent)
- You have knowledge it is there
- You have the ability to exercise control over it
- “I didn’t know it was there” is a defense — but you have to make it in court, not at the gate. At the gate, constructive possession is presumed if the contraband is in your vehicle and you are in the vehicle.
- YOUR RIGHTS AT THE FESTIVAL GATE
- Festival gates are on private property, but the Fourth Amendment applies to law enforcement (police, sheriff) conducting searches, not to private security doing bag checks per festival policy. Know the difference.
- For law enforcement:
- You can decline to consent to a search: “I don’t consent to a search.”
- Saying this is not probable cause.
- If they have probable cause (dog alert, visible contraband, warrant), they can search regardless of your consent.
- If you are detained: “Am I being detained? I want a lawyer.” Then silence.
- Do not explain. Do not elaborate. Do not say “it’s not mine.” Do not say “I didn’t know.” Those statements become evidence. Say nothing except “I want a lawyer.”
- For private security:
- They can require a bag check as a condition of entry.
- They can deny entry without probable cause.
- They cannot legally detain you beyond refusing entry.
- They cannot conduct a body search without your consent (except pat-down for weapons in some states by licensed security).
- WHY THE DOG HITS ON RESIDUE
- Drug detection dogs are trained to alert on odor, not quantity. A bag that contained cannabis two weeks ago and was wiped down may still trigger an alert. A jacket that was around someone else’s supply at the last event may trigger an alert. The alert creates probable cause for a search whether or not there is currently a controlled substance in the vehicle.
- This means: the claim “it’s clean, they tested negative” (if true) is not the same as “the dog won’t alert.”
- THE DUMP-IT-NOW PROTOCOL
- If you are in a vehicle that contains something you did not agree to carry and you are not yet at the gate:
- 1. Say: “Pull over before the gate.”
- 2. Do not touch the item.
- 3. Exit the vehicle.
- 4. Stand away from the vehicle while the driver disposes of the item.
- 5. Do not observe the disposal.
- 6. Re-enter the vehicle.
- 7. Document what happened immediately after: time, location, what was said. Send it to yourself in a text or email. This creates a timestamped record.
- If the driver refuses to pull over:
- “I need you to stop the car right now or I’m calling for help.”
- If they do not stop: do not exit a moving vehicle. Wait for the gate and exit at the gate, separately, without the vehicle.
- IF YOU HAVE BEEN CHARGED
- Contact a public defender or criminal defense attorney immediately.
- Do not speak to law enforcement without an attorney present.
