## The Fanny Pack (chapter recap)

Chapter 19. The inside pocket. The one with the emergency cash.

**The Geometry Trap (confined-space coercion):** All transactions happen in the open, with witnesses, at a distance you can walk away from without asking permission. “I’m good out here” is a complete transaction protocol.

The Friend-Of-A-Friend Chain (trust transitivity dilution): Map the chain. How many links? How old is the memory? “Solid” based on a fourteen-month-old festival vibe is not a reference. It is a story.

**The Witness Rule (third-party deterrent):** Bring someone. Every time. “Hold on, let me grab my friend” changes the math.

**The Camp Got Hit (opportunistic theft):** Lockbox. Body-carry. Camp rotation during main-stage windows. GoPro is in the bag. The lighter is a test.

The Crime With No Recourse (reporting suppression): Festival safety staff ≠ county law enforcement. Document immediately. Pass the information through the camp network and the scene’s informal channels.

The drawn curtains are information. The two-minute response time is information. Both arrived before the click that was not the door.

🔰 SAFETY APPENDIX — CHAPTER 19
- The practical content. In your pocket.
- LOCKBOXES THAT ACTUALLY WORK
- The $12 lockbox from Target does not work. A motivated thief with a screwdriver defeats it in forty seconds. The following work:
- Pacsafe Travelsafe — slash-resistant bag with combination lock. Fits over a tent stake or cooler hasp. Available at REI and online. ~$50.
- Pelican micro case with padlock hasp. Impact-resistant, weather-resistant, locks to a fixed object with a cable lock. ~$30–60.
- Steel cable lockbox (Vaultz or similar) — combination lock, cable for attachment. ~$25.
- What to put in the lockbox: medications, prescription glasses, car keys, passport/ID, significant cash, phone when charging, anything irreplaceable or high-value.
- What to leave out: things you need regular access to. The lockbox is for overnight and during-set storage, not a permanent depository.
- THE DEAL-IN-THE-OPEN PROTOCOL
- For any transaction with someone you don’t know well, regardless of the referral chain:
- 1. Location: public space with ambient foot traffic. Not an RV. Not a tent. Not a car. The edge of a food court, a main thoroughfare, anywhere you can call out and be heard.
- 2. Witness: one person from your camp or immediate social group, standing where they can see you. Brief them: “I’ve got a thing, just be visible.”
- 3. Timer: the witness has a fifteen-minute timer running from the moment you leave. If you don’t return: they come find you.
- 4. No inside: any offer to move the transaction inside is a decline: “I’m good out here.”
- 5. Note the details: the person’s approximate description, the number you texted, the time. If anything goes wrong, you have the starting information for a report.
- THE CAMPSITE SECURITY CHECKLIST
- Before any main-stage window (headliner, full-camp migration):
- Phones charging: in the lockbox
- Cash: lockbox or on a body
- Car keys: lockbox or deep pocket
- Prescriptions/medication: lockbox
- Laptop/tablets: in a vehicle or lockbox
- GoPro/camera: in a bag, in a vehicle
- One person staying at camp, or everything valuable locked
- WHAT REPORTING LOOKS LIKE AT FESTIVALS
- Festival safety staff at most events:
- Operate separately from county law enforcement
- Are empowered to document and respond to incidents
- Do not automatically refer to law enforcement
- Can remove people from the grounds
- County law enforcement at festivals:
- Present but typically not monitoring vendor transactions
- Filing a report with them involves explaining circumstances
- Harm-reduction volunteers:
- Non-law-enforcement
- Non-reporting
- Can sit with you, help you find your camp, connect you to resources
- The scene’s informal networks (Discord, subreddits, camp network):
- Can receive incident information without your name
- Can distribute safety warnings efficiently
- Are often the fastest-moving harm information channel at any event
- IF YOU HAVE BEEN ROBBED
- 1. Get to a safe, populated area.
- 2. Find your camp by walking — your feet still work.
- 3. Get to a phone as soon as you reach someone you know.
- 4. Document the description, location, time, and number you texted — before the memory degrades.
- 5. Report to festival safety if you choose. You do not have to explain the purpose of the meeting. “I was approached by individuals in a beige RV on row 14 who robbed me at gunpoint” is a complete report.
- 6. Activate the camp network. The information protects others.
