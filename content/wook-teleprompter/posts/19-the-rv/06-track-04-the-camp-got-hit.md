### TRACK 04 — THE CAMP GOT HIT
*(opportunistic theft and target-hardening)*

Here's the behavior. While everyone is at the main stage for the headliner, the camp has a ghost population of exactly zero and a portable speaker, three phones charging from a power bank, someone’s wallet in a mesh pocket of a bag they left on a chair, a laptop in a day bag with the zipper facing outward, a cash stack under a rolled sleeping bag, and a GoPro clipped to a camp table as if it is an ambient fixture rather than a three-hundred-dollar camera that is cordially inviting any passing raccoon — literal or figurative — to take it.

The camp-hit scenario is not a sophisticated crime. It requires exactly the following assets: the ability to walk, peripheral awareness, and the two minutes that a gap in ambient camp presence provides. The perpetrator does not need a master plan. They need a scan of an unattended camp, a pocket large enough for a phone, and the confidence that a crowded festival with a headliner on the main stage is not going to produce a witness within the relevant time window.

The relevant time window is about ninety seconds.

Everything that is not secured or on a body is available in ninety seconds.

Here's the read on it. **The camp-hit is predictable in its timing:** headliner set, full-camp migration to main stage, transition windows between stages. These are the windows when camp population drops to zero and the camp’s material contents are ambient and accessible.

The read is not about identifying the predator. The read is about identifying the window and building the target-hardening before the window opens.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Camp Hardening Protocol. **Step 1 — Before the event:** identify what is in the camp that cannot be replaced (medication, prescription glasses, keys, passport, ID, significant cash, irreplaceable personal items). Those go in a lockbox or on a body. No exceptions.

**Step 2 — Phones charging:** in a lockbox. Not on a camp table. Not in an open pocket. A lockbox bolted to a tent stake or a cooler hasp.

**Step 3 — Cash:** body-carried in the smallest amount needed for the session. The rest in the lockbox. The lockbox is not a hotel safe — it is a physical deterrent that raises the cost of the theft above what most opportunistic thieves are prepared to spend. Most opportunistic thieves will skip the lockbox for the next camp that doesn’t have one.

**Step 4 — Camp rotation:** if the camp has more than four people, someone stays. Not as a punishment rotation. As a standing policy. One person at camp during every main-stage window. They get their phone charged and their dinner eaten and their introvert time logged and the camp does not lose three hundred dollars of gear.

**Step 5 — GoPro:** in the bag. In the locked car. Not on the table. The table is not a display case. It is a festival table. It belongs to nobody and everything on it belongs to whoever walks by with the right pocket size.

Quick vibe check. Festival theft is statistically uncommon relative to the size of the crowds. Most people are not there to steal. The target-hardening is not about paranoia. It is about making the camp a less attractive target than the camp next door, which is the whole game. Opportunistic theft is a math problem. Change the math.

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever taken something from a camp that was not yours because it seemed unattended and you needed it? A lighter. A bottle of water. The charger that you returned but took for three hours first. Small thefts are the Studio Debut version of the Camp Got Hit. Notice the shape.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **Vendors:** Your booth at a festival is a camp-got-hit target of the first order. Cashbox protocols, card reader security, and booth-sitting coverage during adjacent-stage transitions are not paranoid. They are professional practice.

**Artists with Gear:** Your equipment at a festival is the most valuable thing in the camp and the least secured thing in the camp because you trusted the vibe of the space and the vibe of the space has, historically, not been a cable lock. Cable lock everything that can be cable-locked.

This runs in every direction. Whoever hits the unguarded camp isn’t a specific archetype — the opportunistic vendor row neighbor, the festie passing through at 4 a.m., the person who’s been casing tents since Thursday, the stranger who just noticed nobody locked the trailer. They come in every gender, identity, and presentation. The target-hardening works regardless.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. The office where nobody locks their computers because “we’re all colleagues here.” The bike locked with a cable that requires one pair of wire cutters and twenty seconds. The apartment building with a lobby door that doesn’t fully latch. The target-hardening is not paranoia. It is the cost comparison between a cable lock and a replacement bike.
