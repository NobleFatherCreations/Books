## The Fanny Pack (chapter recap)

Chapter 10. The front pocket. The one with the folded copy of the incident report.

**The Ticket Wage (compensation in admission):** Divide by the hours. The ticket is only worth face value to someone who wasn’t coming anyway, and everyone on that crew was coming anyway. Say the number out loud, then decide.

The Family Payroll (kinship framing as wage suppression): Family framing that runs in both directions is a culture. Family framing that runs downward only, from paid people to unpaid people, is a payroll instrument with a hug on it.

**The Uninsured Ladder (liability laundering):** Ask in April, not in the shade with a splint. “If I’m hurt doing this, who pays?” A real answer has a noun in it. “We’d take care of you” has no noun in it.

The Promotion That Isn’t (title inflation): A real promotion moves at least three of hours, authority, budget, liability, pay. This one moves two and hands you a word for the rest.

**The Off-Season Problem (exit cost in friendships):** If you stopped tomorrow, who’s still in your life in a year? The person with somewhere else to stand can ask a question at a production meeting. That’s what keeps an org honest.

The mechanism here does not require a predator. It requires a budget, a deadline, and a room full of people who love the thing enough to absorb the gap.

The gap is always absorbed by somebody.

**If you are hurt:** get treated, say “this happened on site, on shift” to a named person, photograph everything, ask for the report in writing, and do not accept “let’s call it personal.” Say it once, kindly, then stop talking. You don’t have to win. You only have to not agree.

And do not apologize. I know. Do it anyway.

💬 POCKET SCRIPTS — SAY THESE

**The April question:** “One boring question — if someone gets hurt during build, how does that work? Just want to know what I’m covered by.”

**The payroll question, asked warmly, at intake:** “Totally happy to be volunteer — I just like knowing how it’s structured. Who’s on payroll, and roughly what’s the split?”

**The eleven words:** “This happened on site. I’m on shift. Who’s taking the report?”

**Declining the reclassification:** “I understand, but it happened at work, so I need it recorded that way.”

**Getting the paper:** “Can you email me a copy of the report for my records?”

**Taking a title without taking the whole year:** “That means a lot. What does it change on my end — hours, decisions, anything attached to it?”

**Insisting on subtraction:** “Happy to take it on — I’ll need to hand off the shuttle schedule then.”

**Asking the money question without apology:** “Is there a stipend attached at this level, or is this still volunteer?”

**Holding a rate as a professional:** “That’s below my rate — I’d love to do it, and I can do it at [number]. Totally understand if the budget isn’t there this year.”
