### TRACK 02 — THE “HOLDING SPACE” HOSTAGE SITUATION
*(sacred-duty manipulation, emotional version)*

Here's the behavior. You try to leave the conversation.

“I just need you to hold space for me right now.”

You try to say no.

“Wow, that’s a really activated response. Can you regulate before we continue?”

You try to disagree.

“I feel like you’re not honoring my truth.”

You try to set a limit.

“Are you familiar with attachment theory? Because that sounds avoidant.”

You try to leave the camp.

“I think you’re abandoning me.”

You try to be honest.

“Your tone is harmful.”

This is not therapy. Therapy has a trained practitioner, a contract, a frame, a duration, and a fee that makes both parties aware they are in a bounded professional relationship. This is a conversation in the dust where one person has memorized the vocabulary of therapeutic practice and is using it as a perimeter fence around their own behavior. The fence is labeled “this is a healing space.” What it actually says is: “you are not allowed to leave.”

The specific corruption of “holding space” deserves its own paragraph. Real holding space is quiet, non-coercive presence with someone in pain. You do not have to speak. You do not have to agree. You do not have to stay in the conversation indefinitely. You do not have to sleep with someone to “complete the energetic loop.” Real holding space is more like standing near a fire while someone warms themselves — you are present, they are warming, neither of you is obligated by the warmth to do something else.

**In the Wook version, holding space means:** you are not allowed to leave, and if you leave, you are not a loving person.

Here's the read on it. The test is not vocabulary. The test is whether the vocabulary expands your options or removes them. “I need a boundary” as a sentence that gives you more room — that is the language doing its job. “Your boundary is a trauma response” as a sentence that closes the door on your no — that is the language as the door. Ask: after this person uses a clinical term in conversation with me, do I have more options or fewer? If fewer: the word is a cage.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Plain English Translation. Every therapeutic vocabulary hostage has a plain English version. Find it. Use it.

**Step 1 — Translate the hostage sentence:** “I just need you to hold space for me” → “I want you to stay in this conversation.” Answer the plain version. “I’m not available to stay in this conversation right now.”

Step 2 — Do not defend your psychological interpretation of yourself. “Are you familiar with attachment theory?” does not require you to explain your attachment style. It requires a redirect: “I’m not doing an attachment analysis. I’m ending this conversation.”

**Step 3 — Decline the diagnostic:** “I notice you’re framing my limit as a symptom. I’m not accepting that framing.”

**Step 4 — Use plain English about your exit:** “I’m leaving now.” Not “I need to honor my nervous system right now.” Leaving now. Two words. Full stop.

Step 5 — If they escalate — “I can’t believe you’re doing this” — that is not a reason to stay. “I’m going to find my crew.” Move toward people and light.

Quick vibe check. Some people are genuinely trying to communicate their emotional needs in the best vocabulary they have. The therapy language can be an attempt at clarity rather than a weapon. The test is not the vocabulary. The test is what happens after you say no. Does the vocabulary get softer and more ordinary, or does it escalate into more clinical, more pathologizing, more diagnostic? The escalation is the tell.

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever called someone “avoidant” because they would not give you what you wanted? Have you ever invoked “attachment theory” to make your request sound like a clinical finding? Have you ever labeled someone’s normal disagreement as harm? If yes: you have been the workshop wearing a person. Take off the workshop. Put on a normal coat. Ask for what you want in plain English. Accept the plain no.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **Camp Leads:** If one camper consistently invokes therapy vocabulary to override normal camp limits — to avoid labor, to extend conflict, to make accountability conversations about their nervous system rather than about the behavior — this is a culture issue, not a vibe issue. Name it in camp governance language, not therapeutic language. “At this camp, people are expected to participate in labor and accountability processes. Emotional language does not substitute for participation.”

**Ceremony and Integration Lane:** Real integration facilitators do not corner participants after ceremony and deploy therapeutic vocabulary to extend access. They refer out. “It sounds like this needs more support than I can offer here — let me connect you with someone who can actually help.”

This runs in every direction. The hostage holder can be femme, masc, nonbinary, sober, sponsored, twenty-five years deep, brand new, or the person you have been vaguely uncomfortable with since 2019 but could not name why. The vocabulary is available to everyone. Fluency in therapeutic language is not gendered. Neither is its misuse.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. “I feel like you’re not creating a safe space for me to share my concerns.” Said at a family dinner where the concern is that you brought up something that actually happened. The vocabulary redefined the conversation as a trauma response instead of the accountability conversation it was. The plain version of the sentence is: “I don’t like what you’re saying.” Answer the plain version.
