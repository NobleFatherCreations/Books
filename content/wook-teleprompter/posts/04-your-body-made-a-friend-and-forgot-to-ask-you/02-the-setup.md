🎛️ THE TAPERS’ SECTION

Bowlby · van der Kolk · Walker

Short. Then back to the dust.

**John Bowlby gave us attachment theory:** humans are wired to bond with whoever provides safety during threat or distress. The bond is not a choice. It is a biological system. In festival language: the person who holds your head during a bad trip gets filed in the same neurological drawer as the person who held your hand when you were four and scared. The drawer doesn’t check credentials. It checks: were they there? Did it help?

Bessel van der Kolk showed us that the body keeps its own record — that nervous system states, especially those formed during high-intensity experiences, encode differently than ordinary memories. The bass festival sunrise is not stored the same way a Tuesday meeting is stored. It is stored in the body itself: in muscle, breath pattern, heart rate response. When Kai’s name appears on the screen three weeks later, the body remembers the speaker stack. The brain is late to the meeting.

**Pete Walker gave us the fourth F:** the fawn response. Fight, flight, freeze — and fawn. The fawn response is the nervous system’s social survival strategy: appease, accommodate, be helpful, be agreeable, smooth the thing over, don’t cause friction, especially when the person causing discomfort is the person you are also bonded to. In scene language: “I didn’t want him to touch me right then but I didn’t want to kill the vibe” is not a failure of courage. It is a nervous system running a survival script it learned long before the festival.
