## Tales From The Camp Network — The Save

Festie POV. Same scenario. Counter-Drops live. Named protagonist. Watermelon Moment present.

Devi Raman has been coming to this festival for three years. She is twenty-six. She does not distribute substances. She does take them, carefully, from tested sources, which is a distinction she worked out in her head during her first year in the scene after a night that did not go badly but that could have, easily, if the person she was with had been less careful.

The person she is currently buying from is a guy named Sam. Sam is known in her camp network. Sam has been at this festival for four years. Sam is the person her friend Dani described as “solid, he tests everything, show him your strips and he’ll compare results” — which is the specific quality reference Devi has learned to listen for.

She finds Sam on Thursday evening and runs the protocol she has been running since year two.

**She says:** “Can I see the test result?”

Sam does not flinch. He has his phone out. He has a photo of the test result, timestamped Thursday afternoon. Marquis, purple-black. Fentanyl strip, two lines — negative. He turns the phone so she can see clearly.

She has her own strips. She tests one unit herself, in front of him. Purple-black. Two lines. She keeps the strip. She photographs it. She buys four caps.

This is the watermelon moment. Not in the emotional register of some other chapters — smaller, more ordinary. Standing at the edge of the water station, phone out, photographing a test strip that has produced the result that means “not today, not this.” The feeling in her chest when the strip develops correctly is not relief in the dramatic sense. It is the small, specific satisfaction of a system working the way systems are supposed to work — of having built the protocol and run it and received the correct output.

She puts the strip photo in a folder on her phone labeled “testing.” The folder has photographs going back two years. Every test she has run. Every result. Every festival.

On Saturday afternoon, her group chat lights up.

The message says what the messages on Saturday afternoon at this festival always say, the message that the scene dreads and that eventually arrives at every major event: a batch is bad, people are in the medical tent, do not take anything from anyone you didn’t vet yourself.

Devi reads the message. She looks at the folder on her phone. She looks at the test results from Thursday.

**She texts Dani:** “Sam’s stuff tested clean. I have the results. I’ve also stopped taking anything else today until this clears.”

She goes to find the people in her camp who might need information. She is not an authority. She is a person who has strips and results and the specific calm of someone whose protocol has already run.

She finds two people who were thinking about taking something from someone else — a different source, someone they know less well, someone who said “my guy is solid” in roughly those words.

She shows them the Saturday messages. She says: “I’d wait. Not because your guy isn’t, but because we don’t know yet where the bad batch is coming from and until we do, the test is the only answer.”

They wait.

The Saturday messages continue. The bad batch is identified by the Bunk Police booth by early evening. The source is not Sam. The source is someone whose name Devi does not know, whose eighty caps are now somewhere in the festival’s population, and whose testing protocol apparently ran on the same Thursday logic as the man in the good tent who is by now sitting with two Rangers and a Bunk Police volunteer with a kind face and tired eyes.

Devi’s folder has Thursday’s results. Her two campmates did not take the unvetted source’s supply.

The watermelon moment was the strip developing correctly.

The rest was the protocol doing its job.
