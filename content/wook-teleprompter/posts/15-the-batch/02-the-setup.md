🎛️ THE TAPERS’ SECTION

Harm Reduction · Supply Chain Accountability · Moral Licensing

Quick theory. Then the practical content.

Supply chain accountability is the principle that everyone in the distribution chain of a substance bears partial accountability for the harm downstream. The source, the mid-level, the person who fronts a few for friends — all of them are nodes in the chain, and the chain produces outcomes at the end of it regardless of how the nodes feel about their role. This is not a legal analysis. It is a harm-reduction framework. In harm-reduction terms: you are accountable for what you distribute, regardless of what you call yourself.

Moral licensing is the psychological phenomenon where one positive self-perception licenses a subsequent failure of care. I am generous, therefore my product is good. I have been doing this for two years without incident, therefore this instance will also be without incident. The good history becomes the permission slip for the next decision. The license is not valid. The license is also very convincing to the person holding it.

The test-kit imperative is not a suggestion in harm-reduction practice. It is the baseline. No batch, no matter how solid the source, no matter how long the relationship, no matter how inconvenient the moment, is safe to distribute without testing. Not because sources lie — because chemistry is unpredictable, because the supply chain above your source has unknown links, because fentanyl contamination has reached substrates that were previously considered clean. Because the test takes three minutes. Because the girl in the Santa hat takes the rest of your life.

You are in.
