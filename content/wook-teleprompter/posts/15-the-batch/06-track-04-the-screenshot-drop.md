### TRACK 04 — THE SCREENSHOT DROP
*(reputation cascade and the no-recall problem)*

Here's the behavior. His name is in a screenshot.

The screenshot is in a group chat of eighty people. Each of those eighty people is in other group chats. The screenshot will be in those group chats within the hour. The content of the screenshot is his name and the information that his batch is involved in a medical emergency. By the time he sees the screenshot, the screenshot is already downstream of the original send. By the time he finishes reading the screenshot, his name is probably in forty more group chats.

He cannot recall the screenshot.

This is the no-recall problem. Information distributed through informal social networks cannot be centrally removed. Every attempt to delete it from one location does not remove it from the locations it has already reached. Every send multiplies the locations it has reached. The cascade is exponential and it is irreversible and the only variables that were available to affect it were the variables that existed before the batch was distributed.

The Screenshot Drop is not a punishment imposed by the scene. It is a function of how information travels in peer networks. It is also, in this specific instance, the correct function. The information “don’t take anything from [name], the batch is involved in a medical emergency” is harm-reduction information of the highest urgency. The people sending the screenshot are doing the right thing. The problem is that they are doing the right thing with his name in it, and his name is there because he did not do the right thing first.

Here's the read on it. The Screenshot Drop is not something you can manage after the fact. It can only be prevented by the test that was not run. But it can be partially addressed by the recall play — the set of actions available in the first ten minutes after the information that the batch is bad.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Recall Play. If you have distributed a batch and receive information that the batch is causing harm:

Step 1 — Contact every buyer immediately. Every single one. “Stop. Don’t take anything you got from me. There may be something wrong with the batch.” Text, call, find in person. Every one. Within ten minutes.

Step 2 — Stop distribution immediately. Cap the container. Do not sell or give another unit.

Step 3 — Contact the medical tent or harm-reduction team directly. Before they find you. This is critical. Before they find you. “I think I may have distributed something that is causing harm. I want to help. What do you need from me?” This call, made proactively, before the Rangers arrive at the tent, is the difference between cooperation and apprehension.

Step 4 — Destroy the remaining supply. Dispose of it in a way that cannot be recovered.

Step 5 — Give the medical team everything you know. Your source. The form of the substance. Where it was distributed. When. To whom. All of it. “We’re not cops. We just need to know what was in it” is the sentence you are going to hear. Answer it completely. The girl in the medical tent is more important than your source’s identity.

Quick vibe check. The recall play does not undo the harm. There is no counter-drop that undoes the harm. What the recall play does is limit the additional harm — the caps that have not yet been taken, the people who have not yet received the information, the medical team that does not yet know what they are treating.

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever received information that something you distributed was causing harm and not immediately acted on it? Have you ever waited — for more information, for certainty, for the situation to clarify — while the clock on the recall window was running? The ten minutes is not approximate. The ten minutes is the interval between the batch’s first harm and the point at which the additional harm is preventable.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **Harm-Reduction Volunteers:** When someone contacts you proactively with the recall information, that person deserves protection, not prosecution. Build a culture where the proactive call is possible and rewarded. The call saves lives. The fear of consequences for making the call costs lives.

This runs in every direction. The Screenshot Drop runs equally against the guy who bought from a stranger, the woman who bought from her regular, the nonbinary festie who bought from a friend of a friend, and everyone across the distribution spectrum who assumed they’d remember exactly what it looked like. The no-recall problem is a physics problem. It has no social bias.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. The product recall issued two weeks after the company received the first complaint report. The ten-day gap between first complaint and public recall is the Skipped Recall Play in default-world manufacturing. People were harmed in those ten days who would not have been harmed if the recall had run immediately.
