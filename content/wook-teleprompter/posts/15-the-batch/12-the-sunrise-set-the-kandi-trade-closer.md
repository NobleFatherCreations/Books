## The Sunrise Set + The Kandi Trade (closer)

Here's the Sunrise Set. Zero slang — just the true thing. The batch chapter is the hardest chapter in the book to end gently.

There is a nineteen-year-old who did not come home.

There is a mother in Fremont who drove to a hospital in the middle of the night.

There is a man sitting in a good tent whose hands stopped shaking by Sunday but who will be answering a different question for a very long time: what would have happened if I had spent twenty minutes in the Bunk Police line on Thursday afternoon?

**The answer is:** we would not be in this chapter.

This is not the chapter that says substances are the enemy. They are not. They are chemistry. Chemistry is what it is, and what it is changes depending on what is in the substance and what you know about what is in the substance and whether you have the tools to find out.

The tools exist. The kits cost forty dollars. The line was twenty minutes long.

**The chapter is here to say:** twenty minutes of inconvenience is not comparable to any of the other outcomes in evidence.

Test the batch.

Every time.

For the girl in the Santa hat.

For the people who are going to take what you brought to the next one.

For the thirty seconds at sunrise when you get to say: I did everything that could be done, and everything that could be done was done.

- I will not call myself “not a dealer” to avoid the responsibilities that come with distribution.

- I will remember that the test takes three minutes and that three minutes is the smallest possible version of what we owe each other.
