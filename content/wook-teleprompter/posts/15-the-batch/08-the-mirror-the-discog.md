## The Mirror + The Discog

HAVE YOU BEEN THE WOOK? Mirror Check At The Tent Flap

This chapter’s mirror is the chapter’s whole argument.

The most relatable Wook in the book is the one who is not trying to hurt anyone. The one who has built a self-image that is largely accurate — generous, connected, knowledgeable, a good person to have in the scene — and who has used that self-image to construct a series of reasoning errors that accumulated into a batch that killed a nineteen-year-old he met at Christmas.

He is not a monster. He is a person who thought he was not a dealer, thought his guy was solid, thought he would test it Sunday, thought forty a cap was fair, and thought none of these thoughts were the same as the thought: I am going to distribute an untested substance to people who trust me and call it friendship.

He didn’t think that thought. He thought a series of other thoughts that pointed in the same direction.

Have you ever distributed something without testing it? Have you ever used your source’s track record as a substitute for the test result? Have you ever marked up a substance and told yourself the markup was doing the accountability work? Have you ever been the person whose name is in the screenshot?

The reckoning is not the chapter’s question. The reckoning is not punitive. The reckoning is: what do you do at the next event? Do you test? Do you change the framing? Do you stop distributing entirely? Do you build the recall plan?

The girl in the Santa hat deserves the answer to be: yes, I test. Every time.

That is the only honor this chapter can offer her.

Here's the Wook Discog for this chapter. Studio Debut — The Baby Wook learning the moves

Twenty-one, first festival, fronting two hundred dollars to bring enough for their close group of six people. Has not yet heard the phrase “good source for two years.” Has a source they found through a mutual friend at a house show. The source seems fine. They bring enough for six. They test zero caps because testing feels like it implies doubt and they do not want to doubt their friend’s friend. Nobody gets hurt. This is the Studio Debut: the version where the skips don’t produce consequences yet, which teaches the brain that the skips are fine, which is the lesson that makes the Live Album possible.

Live Album — The Seasoned Operator

Him. Twenty-seven. Good tent. Two years. Eighty caps. Forty a cap. The Bunk Police line at twenty minutes. I’ll test it Sunday. The Live Album sounds fine for two years and then it doesn’t and the sound is specific and terrible and it is the sound of a phone buzzing in a good tent at 3:14 p.m. with his name in a screenshot going around a group chat of eighty people. The Live Album ends there. Everything after is silence and accounting.

Greatest Hits Compilation — The Institutional Scaled-Up Version

The supply chain. The pressing operation two levels above the source that does not test for fentanyl cross-contamination because testing costs money and the batch ships on a schedule and the distributor at the bottom of the chain has a two-year relationship with the person above them and trusts the relationship as a quality guarantee. The pressing operation that is responsible for the batch that produced the Santa hat. The pressing operation that has been solid for two years and will be solid for two more and will produce one more bad batch and that batch will also have names in it. The Greatest Hits Compilation is not a person at a festival. It is the infrastructure that makes the My Guy Is Solid a fiction at the bottom of a supply chain that has never introduced itself.
