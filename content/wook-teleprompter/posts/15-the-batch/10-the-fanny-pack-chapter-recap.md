**The My Guy Is Solid (trust transitivity):** Your source’s two-year track record is evidence about your source. It is not evidence about the batch. The test is the evidence about the batch.

🔰 SAFETY APPENDIX — CHAPTER 15

- MARQUIS + FENTANYL STRIP PROTOCOL — EVERY BATCH, EVERY TIME
- Marquis Reagent (MDMA/MDA/MDEA)
- Procedure:
- 1. Place a tiny amount (a few milligrams — the size of a match head) on the white test surface of the kit.
- 2. Add one to two drops of Marquis reagent.
- 3. Observe the color change over ninety seconds.
- Results:
- Purple to black: consistent with MDMA/MDA/MDEA. Not a guarantee of purity or dose, but indicates the primary compound is likely present.
- Orange to brown: likely amphetamine or methamphetamine.
- No reaction or unexpected color: unknown compound. Do not distribute.
- Fentanyl Test Strips (every substance, every time)
- Procedure:
- 1. Dissolve a small amount of the substance in water (approximately one-quarter teaspoon per cap or equivalent).
- 2. Dip the strip in the water for fifteen seconds.
- 3. Lay flat and read at two to five minutes.
- Results:
- ONE line: fentanyl detected. Do not distribute or consume. Destroy the batch.
- TWO lines: fentanyl not detected at the strip’s sensitivity threshold. Note: does not rule out all fentanyl analogs.
- IMPORTANT: A negative fentanyl test does not mean the substance is safe. It means fentanyl was not detected at this strip’s sensitivity level. Run the Marquis and the fentanyl strip together, every time.
- THE RECALL PLAY — IF THE BATCH GOES BAD
- The ten-minute window:
- Minutes 0–2: Contact every person who received supply from you. Text, call, find in person. “Stop. Don’t take anything you got from me. There may be something wrong with the batch. Go to the medical tent or harm-reduction tent if you feel anything unusual.”
- Minutes 2–5: Stop all distribution. Do not sell or give another unit regardless of prior commitments.
- Minutes 5–7: Contact the medical tent or harm-reduction team directly. “I think I may have distributed something causing harm. I want to help.” Give them everything: substance form, estimated dose, time of distribution, how many units.
- Minutes 7–10: Destroy the remaining supply.
- After 10 minutes: Cooperate fully with medical and harm-reduction personnel. Give your source’s contact information. Give buyer contact information. The priority is the people in the medical tent.
- WHY “GOOD SOURCE FOR TWO YEARS” IS NOT A TEST
- Supply chains for controlled substances pass through multiple hands between synthesis and sale. At any step in that chain, cross-contamination with fentanyl or other adulterants can occur without the person above or below knowing. A source who has been reliable for two years has a two-year track record on their portion of the chain. They have no visibility into what happened above them.
- The fentanyl contamination wave that began in earnest in the early 2020s has reached every class of illegal substance. There is no substrate that has not had contaminated batches documented. There is no source track record long enough to substitute for a test.
- Test the batch your source gave you. Not because they lied. Because you don’t know what was above them.
