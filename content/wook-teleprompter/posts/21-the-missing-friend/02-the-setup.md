## The Setup

Here's the soundboard quote for this chapter. “Every person in that group loved Jess. They would have done anything for her in a crisis. They just hadn’t done the fifteen minutes before the crisis that would have made the crisis four hours shorter. That is the specific tragedy of this chapter. Not malice. Not neglect. Just: the fifteen minutes that felt like overkill at three p.m. on a Thursday, when they were excited and the playlist was good and they were going to a festival.” — Lourdes “Sister Lou” Mantilla, fifty-eight, founding member of the sober crew protocol, certified Wilderness First Responder, veteran of fourteen festival seasons and what she describes as “an embarrassing number of unnecessary four-hour searches that all could have been prevented with the same fifteen-minute conversation.” Runs a pre-event crew briefing at every festival she attends. Has run it for twelve years. Has never once had a missing-friend situation in the twelve years since. Recorded at the gate of a regional in Northern California while simultaneously eating a granola bar, reviewing her briefing checklist, and judging, with visible professional sadness, the group of six people who just walked past her saying “we’ll find each other, it’s fine.” She did not say anything to them. She will not say anything to them. She will be there at 3 a.m. if they need her.

Here's what this chapter is actually about. This chapter is not about a single Wook doing a single bad thing. It is about a collective vulnerability — the gap between the warmth of the scene and the operational infrastructure that would protect the scene’s people inside the warmth. The five Tracks are the five failure points that produced the four hours at 1:47 a.m.: the “we’ll find each other” plan that wasn’t, the real-name vacuum that left them searching for Jess Something, the dead phone that took the group’s only connection point offline, the four-hour threshold that nobody knew about, and the briefing they skipped in the car because they were excited and it would be fine. The chapter then gives you the briefing. It is fifteen minutes long. Sister Lou has been running it for eleven years without a missing-friend incident.

🎛️ THE TAPERS’ SECTION

Coordination Failure · Search and Rescue Protocol · The Planning Fallacy

Fast theory. Then the checklist that actually matters.

Coordination failure is the term for situations where the collective outcome is worse than any individual’s intentions — where everyone loves Jess and nobody has her last name. It is produced not by neglect but by the assumption that prior successful coordination predicts future coordination, without accounting for the specific new conditions under which the future coordination will be attempted. In festival language: we always find each other is a true statement about past events. It is not a plan for future events. It is especially not a plan for a future event in which Jess’s phone is dead and the description of the last person who saw her is “tall, beard.”

Wilderness search and rescue protocols exist because losing track of people in large outdoor environments has a solved set of initial response steps that dramatically improve outcomes. The four-hour escalation protocol in this chapter’s Safety Appendix is derived from those protocols, adapted for festival-specific contexts where the difference between “Jess is asleep in a stranger’s tent” and “Jess is in the medical tent with a number she cannot give you” can hinge entirely on the information you have at 1:47 a.m.

The planning fallacy — which we met in Chapter 3 — is the bias toward underestimating the time, cost, and complexity of future events while overestimating the reliability of best-case assumptions. In festival language: “We’ll find each other” is the planning fallacy’s Shakedown row t-shirt. It is optimistic and scene-coded and has been printed on a shirt that nobody remembers wearing when the find-each-other plan fails.

You are in.

And here's the roster — the fronts in this chapter. Five failure modes in the missing-friend chapter. No classic Wook runs them. The scene’s own optimism runs them, with an assist from the specific social pressure that makes preparation feel uncool:

The “We’ll Find Each Other” Lie · The Real-Name Vacuum · The Dead Phone Cascade · The Four-Hour Mark · The Briefing You Skipped.

None of these are new problems. All of them are fixable in fifteen minutes. The fifteen minutes is the whole chapter.
