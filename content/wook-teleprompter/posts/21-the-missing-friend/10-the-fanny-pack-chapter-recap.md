🔰 SAFETY APPENDIX — CHAPTER 21
- Screenshot this. Do the fifteen minutes.
- THE SISTER LOU BRIEFING — COMPLETE VERSION
- Fifteen minutes. Before the gate. Everyone in the car or the camp.
- Beat 1 — Real Names (2 minutes)
- Everyone says their legal name aloud. The person to their left repeats it. Goes around the full group. No playa names.
- Beat 2 — ICE Contacts (3 minutes)
- Everyone opens their phone:
- ICE-FIRST: name and number
- ICE-SECOND: name and number
- Lock screen: set to medical ID (Settings → Health → Medical ID on iPhone; lock screen emergency info on Android)
- Beat 3 — Meet-Up Spot (1 minute)
- Name one physical location visible from multiple parts of the festival without cell service. Describe it in words specific enough to be found from memory. Everyone confirms they can picture it.
- Beat 4 — Check-In Interval (30 seconds)
- Four hours, or shorter if: first-timer in the group, known medical condition, high-dose night planned, or previous missing-friend incidents. Name the interval. Everyone repeats it back.
- Beat 5 — Code Word (30 seconds)
- One ordinary-sounding phrase that means “come get me now, no questions.” Something only the group knows the meaning of. Everyone says it aloud once.
- Beat 6 — Escalation Threshold (1 minute)
- Name the time after which a missing crew member triggers formal escalation: medical tent, lost and found, festival safety desk, in that order. “If [person] isn’t at the spot and hasn’t texted after four hours, [first person] goes to the medical tent.” Named. Assigned. Not negotiated in real time.
- Beat 7 — Wristband Photos (2 minutes)
- Every crew member photographs their wristband number. Photos shared to the group chat. Group chat confirms receipt.
- Beat 8 — Buddy Assignment (2 minutes)
- Explicit buddies. Said aloud. “[Name], you’re with [Name].” The buddy knows the other buddy’s ICE contact and the escalation threshold.
- Total: 15 minutes.
- THE FOUR-HOUR ESCALATION SEQUENCE
- Four hours since last contact and not at the meet-up spot:
- Step 1 — Medical Tent
- Ask if the person is a patient. You need: legal name, approximate age, physical description, wristband number. They may or may not be able to confirm due to privacy. Give them the legal name and the wristband number — these are the two identifiers that produce a match in a festival medical system.
- Step 2 — Lost and Found
- Items are logged here. If the person lost their belongings before losing contact, they may have come here. The person’s legal name may be on an ID found with their belongings.
- Step 3 — Festival Safety Desk
- File a missing person report. You need: legal name, age, last known location, last known companion or time of last contact, physical description, wristband number. This is why Beat 7 exists.
- After six hours with no result: Contact county emergency services. This is the hardest step to take. It is the right step.
- THE PHONE-CHARGING STRATEGY
- The minimum: one ten-thousand-milliamp-hour portable battery per two people, fully charged before the festival.
- The sober anchor’s phone: must be kept above fifty percent at all times during the event. The anchor’s phone is infrastructure. Charge it before fun.
- Physical charging stations: every medium-to-large festival has them. Walk the grounds on arrival and note the location. This takes four minutes and saves the Dead Phone Cascade.
- THE ICE-ON-THE-LOCK-SCREEN RULE
- iPhone: Settings → Health → Medical ID → enable “Show When Locked”
- Android: varies by manufacturer, but most have an emergency information section in the lock screen settings.
- This allows first responders to access your emergency contact and basic medical information without your phone passcode.
- Takes forty-five seconds.
- Do it before you leave the car.
