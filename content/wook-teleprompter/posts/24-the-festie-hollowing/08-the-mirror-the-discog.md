## The Mirror + The Discog

HAVE YOU BEEN THE WOOK? Mirror Check At The Camera Roll

This chapter’s mirror is the gentlest one in the book and also the most specific.

Open your camera roll.

Scroll back to two years before you found the scene. Find a photo of yourself. Look at that person.

**Now ask:** what did they want? Not the version of what they wanted that the scene later supplied the language for — the genuine, pre-scene wanting. The wants that were forming before the circuit became the frame.

How many of those wants have been pursued? How many have been translated into their scene equivalents and counted as addressed? How many have been quietly put in a box in the corner of the apartment and not visited since 2020?

This is not an accusation. This is an audit.

The scene gave you real things. It gave you community and beauty and peak experience and the specific kind of knowing that comes from surviving a shared event together. Those are real.

What did it cost?

Not dramatically. Not as a crisis. Just: what did you trade for it, and was the trade something you made consciously, or something that happened while you were at the festival?

The audit is not the exit. The audit is the information you need to make the next choice an actual choice.

Here's the Wook Discog for this chapter. Studio Debut — The Baby Wook learning the moves

Twenty-three. First festival. The contrast with the default world is the sharpest it will ever be, because the default world at twenty-three is still forming and the festival is extraordinary and the difference between the two states is enormous and entirely in the festival’s favor. The Indoctrination does not require a teacher at twenty-three. It runs on the contrast alone. The Studio Debut is not running a Wook move. The Studio Debut is in love. The love is real. The love is also, if not tended carefully, the beginning of the architecture.

Live Album — The Seasoned Operator

Eight years in. The circuit is the job, the social life, the identity, the vocabulary, and the frame for evaluating all choices. The Live Album sounds exactly like what it describes itself as: a life fully lived in the scene, generous, present, known, creative, and real. What it sounds like from the outside — from the sister’s end of the infrequent phone call — is a person who is in a room with very good lighting that has no windows. The Live Album is Remi. Remi is not a villain. Remi is not the Wook of this chapter. Remi is someone who loved something so completely that she forgot to also be something else.

Greatest Hits Compilation — The Institutional Scaled-Up Version

The scene itself. Not any particular event or camp or person — the accumulated aesthetic and social reward structure of the festival community, which has built, over decades, an environment that produces total immersion as its peak expression of belonging and which has never, as an institution, asked its members whether the total immersion is serving them. The Greatest Hits Compilation is the scene’s own success: a community that created conditions so good, so beautiful, so rewarding of deep commitment, that the people who love it most are the people at highest risk of the Hollowing. The scene did not intend this. The scene is not a person. The scene does not know what it is asking. That is still the outcome. The chapter is not an indictment. It is a maintenance manual.
