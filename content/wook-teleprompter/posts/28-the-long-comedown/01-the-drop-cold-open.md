## THE DROP (Cold Open)

*📍 FESTIE POV*

It is 3:14 a.m. on a Wednesday and she has been awake for forty-one minutes.

This is not unusual. The forty-one minutes is the specific window between when her nervous system decides to run the encore and when it gets tired enough to let her sleep again. The encore runs every night now, sometimes at three, sometimes at four-seventeen, occasionally at the really ambitious hour of five-thirty a.m. when the light is already going gray and sleep becomes more of a philosophical position than a physiological event.

The encore is a voice.

The voice is the problem, and the problem with naming it is that it sounds like her voice. Not like his voice — she knows what his voice sounds like from the outside, the particular warm register of it, the way he could say her name in a way that made her feel like the name was specifically his to say, like he had a deed on it. That voice she has blocked in every medium. That voice is at a festival somewhere in a different state, doing what it does, because the scene does not run out of people for that voice to talk to and the state he moved to has several events on the calendar and his wristband is already purchased.

No, the voice that runs the encore is the installed version. The version that learned his interpretive framework and now applies it to her from the inside. The version that says: you needed too much. You overreacted to what was honestly a beautiful experience for both of you. You are the kind of person who cannot hold space for complexity. You are the kind of person who turns intensity into injury. You are the kind of person who—

She opens her eyes.

The ceiling is the ceiling.

The apartment is an apartment.

The apartment is gray because the city outside it is gray because it is three-fourteen in the morning in a city that was, for a long time, entirely adequate as an apartment city and which has, since the festival and the bus ride home and the week of texts she did not respond to and the week after that of not texting and the week after that of the encore starting, become a space she is living in at arm’s length.

The festival was four weeks ago.

He is gone — left the scene, blocked everywhere, moved cities with the efficiency of someone who has done this before, which he has. Blocked is not the same as gone. Blocked means the direct channel is closed. The installed voice does not require the direct channel.

Her body aches in places that are not the dance.

In her jaw, specifically, which she has been clenching in sleep. In her sternum, where the holding has been happening — the specific held quality of a body that is waiting for something to be resolved and has been waiting for four weeks and has started expressing the waiting through the sternum, which is apparently a location where unresolved things accumulate. In the band of muscle between her shoulder blades that holds the shape of being touched without consent, which is not a metaphor. The muscle holds the shape. She learned this in a body scan during a meditation class she attended three weeks ago partly because the meditation class was free and partly because the alternative was being in the apartment with the encore and the gray ceiling.

Her therapist’s earliest available appointment was in eleven days when she called.

That was nine days ago.

In two days she will have the appointment.

Two days feels extremely far away at three-fourteen in the morning.

The friend group is splintering.

This is the word she uses. Not broken — splintering, which is more accurate to the process, which is not a clean break but a gradual separation along lines of social pressure. Two of the six have sided with him in the slow quiet way of people who have decided that his version of events is the version they can afford to hold, possibly because he controls their access to certain camps and certain shows and possibly because his version is easier to hold than her version, which requires them to have been in proximity to something they did not see and should have seen.

The two who stayed have been checking in. One sends a voice note every three days — not asking for a report, not asking her to explain anything, just: “Hey, I’ve been thinking about you. I hope you ate something good today. I don’t need you to respond.” The other shows up physically, which is the act that costs the most and means the most, with soup sometimes and sometimes just her presence, sitting in the apartment that has the gray ceiling.

The soup is very good. It is cream of tomato. It comes from a can but she heats it properly, with milk, not water, which is the sign of someone who takes soup seriously.

She is eating the soup at three twenty-six in the morning, having abandoned the project of sleeping, sitting on the couch with the bowl in her lap, and she is thinking about what it means that “she heated the soup with milk, not water” is one of the more comforting facts she has available right now.

Not the only comforting fact. Just: one of them.

She has been doing the things.

**The things are:** texting the crisis line three times since the festival, which connects her to someone who is calm and unhurried in the way of people specifically trained to be calm and unhurried at three a.m. and which has, each time, helped the encore run down faster. Finding the RAINN hotline number and putting it in her phone as a contact under “resources” in the same note as the Zendo number and the SMART Recovery number and the number for the local DV advocacy organization and the NAMI helpline. Not because she is certain any of them are right for her situation specifically, but because having them in the phone at three a.m. means the research was done at noon and noon-brain is a better researcher than three-a.m.-brain.

She has been doing the re-entry wobble work, which is not a clinical term for anything, just the name she has given to the specific practice of being in the gray apartment without making the gray apartment a verdict on her whole life. The gray apartment is a gray apartment. It is not evidence of failure. It is where she lives right now while the other thing resolves. The other thing resolving has a timeline that is not three weeks. She knows this.

She knows this because she found a blog post by someone who had been in a similar situation who said: “The timeline for the nervous system to recalibrate after a high-control relationship that operated through your scene identity is longer than three weeks. It is longer than three months. There is no shame in this. It is the neurology of what happened, not the measure of your resilience.”

She saved the blog post.

She re-reads it at three a.m. sometimes.

The soup is warm.

The ceiling is the ceiling.

The encore is not the truth.

She is going to go back to sleep.
