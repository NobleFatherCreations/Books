## The Mirror + The Discog

HAVE YOU BEEN THE WOOK? Mirror Check At The Cereal Aisle

This chapter’s mirror is specific and quiet.

Have you ever left an installation running in someone’s head and taken no responsibility for having left it? Have you ever moved cities with the efficiency of someone who has done this before, leaving the Encore running on someone else’s hardware while you packed the Sprinter?

Have you ever exploited the Decom window — the text at four-thirty a.m. when you knew the crash was happening?

Have you ever contributed to the Friend Group Triage by quietly aligning yourself with the Wook because the alignment was less expensive than the disclosure’s cost?

Have you ever dismissed someone’s body-held memory as insufficient evidence for their own experience?

Most of these questions are about smaller moves than El or Wedge or the man with the eight-ball. They are about the ordinary costs of being a person in a community where harm can travel through ambient social structures, not just through individual acts.

The Encore running in a stranger’s head is partly made of every person who agreed with the Wook’s framing of the situation even once. The Triage is partly made of every provisional alignment. The Spillover contains the songs that played at events where someone helped normalize what should not have been normalized.

The mirror is not a verdict.

It is an invitation to the smaller accountabilities that the community asks of everyone, not just the chapter’s villains.

You know what yours are.

Here's the Wook Discog for this chapter. Studio Debut — The Baby Wook learning the moves

The friend group member at year one who sided with the Wook in the Triage because the cost of not siding was too high and the full information was not available. Did not know they were contributing to the Encore. Did not know their provisional alignment would show up in the chapter. Didn’t know the chapter existed. The Studio Debut is not the Wook of this chapter. The Studio Debut is every person who made the social cost calculation in week four and chose the less expensive path and carried that choice quietly. Some of them will shift as the information becomes available. Some will not. The Studio Debut has enough information now to choose differently.

Live Album — The Seasoned Operator

The person who moved cities. Who left the installed framework running. Who is at a festival in a different state right now, wristband already purchased, warm and competent and doing what they do. The Live Album is not here for this chapter. That is the point. The Live Album does not have a three-fourteen a.m. The Live Album is the thing the chapter is written about, not the chapter’s reader. The chapter’s reader is on the couch with the soup. The Live Album is elsewhere, and that is the Live Album’s crime.

Greatest Hits Compilation — The Institutional Scaled-Up Version

The festival circuit and the scene culture that has not yet built the infrastructure to reduce the distance between the incident and the resource. The scene that has RAINN in its harm-reduction materials but has not integrated crisis support for the long tail. The community that holds someone well for the first seventy-two hours and then does not know what to do at week four. The community that has good information about acute harm and limited information about the Encore and the Triage and the Spillover in the cereal aisle. The Greatest Hits Compilation is the scene’s gap. The chapter is the scene building toward closing it.
