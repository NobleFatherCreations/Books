### TRACK 05 — THE RE-ENTRY WOBBLE
*(reintegration distress)*

Here's the behavior. The default world feels wrong.

Not slightly off. Wrong in the specific way of a frequency mismatch — like being a subwoofer in a room that was built for a different kind of sound. The fluorescent lights. The grocery store music. The specific quality of being in a body that is at a desk in an office when the festival is over and the body is still trying to run the festival’s operating system on the default world’s hardware.

The Re-Entry Wobble is the reintegration distress that follows any high-intensity experience. It has a normal version that passes in two weeks. It has a complicated version that is part of the aftermath of harm — where the high-intensity experience was not only the festival but also the situation, and the default world has become the place where the Encore runs and the Triage is happening and the body holds shapes and the wobble is not just “I miss the festival” but “I do not know who I am in this room.”

The Wobble is also the specific disorientation of having had your interpretive framework actively managed for a period of time and now being in a context where the management has stopped but the installed framework is still running. The default world is not labeled by the installed framework. The installed framework tries to label it anyway. The mismatch is the Wobble.

Here's the read on it. Name the Wobble when it is happening. “I am in the Re-Entry Wobble. This is a known state. It has a trajectory.” The naming does not make it comfortable. It makes it temporary.

**Ask:** “Is the Wobble worse than it was last week?” The trajectory should be gradual improvement. If it is consistently worsening: that is information about the state of the nervous system that the therapist appointment should address.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Ground-Laying Protocol. Step 1 — One ordinary thing per day. Not festival-adjacent. Not extraordinary. Something that is specifically the default world in its least remarkable expression: walking to the grocery store, cooking something, watching television, calling a family member. One ordinary thing. Accumulated, the ordinary things build the floor.

Step 2 — The body first. The wobble is partly neurological — the nervous system running on the wrong platform. Physical regulation before cognitive processing: sleep, food, hydration, sunlight, movement that is not dancing in the dark. Boring. Essential.

Step 3 — One week’s horizon. Not “when will I be better.” One week. What can I do in one week that is small and consistent and builds the floor?

Step 4 — Reduce the comparison. “This is so much worse than the festival” is the Wobble’s interpretive frame. The festival was the festival. This is the Wednesday after. Both are real. The comparison is not helping the Wednesday.

Step 5 — The therapist appointment. The eleven days. The two days. All of this is temporary. The therapist appointment is a resource. The eleven days to get to it is the timeline, not the verdict.

Quick vibe check. The Re-Entry Wobble has a normal version that most festies experience after most festivals — the “Sunday sadness,” the “why is my apartment so quiet” decompression. That version resolves in a week. The chapter’s version involves the Wook aftermath and is longer. Both are real. The chapter’s version requires more support.

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever been a person whose departure from someone’s life contributed to their Re-Entry Wobble? Have you left quickly, efficiently, moved cities, left the installed framework running with no resources for managing it? The exit was yours to make. The residue of the installation is still your responsibility to acknowledge.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **First-timers:** First post-harm re-entry is the most disorienting because there is no previous experience to reference. The Wobble feels permanent because you have not been through it before to know it is temporary. It is temporary. This is known. Many people have been through it. The trajectory resolves.

**Recovery Lane:** Re-entry after a harm-adjacent event can activate patterns and states that overlap with early recovery. If you are in recovery: contact your sponsor, your therapist, your peer support. Not instead of the recovery community — alongside it. Both are happening.

This runs in every direction. The Wobble hits the first-timer a week after her first big weekend, the veteran two decades into the scene, the sober mentor who thought he’d built immunity to it, and anyone who experienced harm in a high-intensity context. It is universal. It is not gendered. It is the nervous system doing what the nervous system does after something significant. The trajectory is known. The landing is on the other side.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. The week after a significant job loss. The week after a long relationship ends. The week after any high-intensity situation resolves and the ordinary world reasserts itself and the ordinary world is unreasonably ordinary. The Wobble runs in every context. The trajectory resolves in every context. It just takes longer than the Friday morning brain wants it to.
