### TRACK 06 — THE DECOM SPIRAL
*(post-event depressive crash)*

Here's the behavior. The neurochemistry of the festival — the dopamine, the serotonin, the oxytocin, the specific cocktail of peak experience and altered states and community and music and touch — leaves the building on Sunday afternoon. What it leaves behind is the baseline. The baseline, in contrast to what just left, feels like failure.

The Decom Spiral is when the contrast becomes a loop: the default world is gray, the gray reinforces the absence of the festival’s chemical state, the absence of the chemical state is interpreted as evidence that the default world is a lesser place, the interpretation produces more gray, the more gray makes the next event feel more necessary, and the necessity makes the gap between events feel more like deprivation and less like ordinary time.

In the aftermath of harm, the Decom Spiral is amplified. The return to baseline is not just “the festival is over” — it is “the festival is over AND the thing happened AND the friend group is splintering AND the Encore is running AND I have to go to work tomorrow.” The baseline, under these conditions, is not just ordinary. It is the context in which the full weight of what happened becomes available without the festival’s noise to buffer it.

The cereal in the coffee mug at four-thirty in the morning is not depression in the clinical sense necessarily. It is the Decom Spiral: the body and the mind catching up to the events in the only quiet window available, which is the window that is specifically not built for catching up but is the only one the Wednesday provides.

Here's the read on it. Distinguish “the Decom Spiral” from “the way things actually are.” The gray apartment is the same apartment it was six months ago before the festival. The gray is not a permanent new property of the apartment. It is the contrast between peak and baseline, amplified by the aftermath.

**Ask:** “Is what I am feeling about this situation, or is it the neurochemical contrast of this situation landing in a depleted nervous system?” Both can be true simultaneously. The situation is real. The neurochemical context it is landing in is also real. Both need to be addressed. Neither can be addressed effectively from inside the spiral.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Spiral Interruption Protocol. Step 1 — Eat something. Hot if possible. The specific body intervention that is most effective on the spiral is not meditation or journaling or calling a friend. It is food. Warm food specifically activates the parasympathetic nervous system in ways that are not mystical, just physiology.

Step 2 — Do not make decisions from inside the spiral. The Decom Spiral produces a specific quality of certainty — “this is how things are, this is how they will always be” — that is not accurate but feels accurate. Wait. The certainty will soften.

Step 3 — Sleep, if it can be had. If it cannot: rest horizontally without the phone. The phone at four-thirty a.m. is specifically bad because the phone provides stimulation that prevents the nervous system from settling, and the content of the phone at four-thirty a.m. is specifically calibrated by the algorithm for the engaged brain, not the depleted one.

Step 4 — One task. When daylight arrives: one task. Not “fix the whole situation.” One thing. The dishes. The shower. The email. The walk. One task.

Step 5 — The spiral has a floor. It does not go indefinitely down. The floor is: you are here, you are breathing, you ate something, it is four-thirty a.m. and in four hours it will be eight-thirty a.m. and in eight hours it will be noon. Noon is a different time signature than four-thirty.

Quick vibe check. The Decom Spiral can be a sign of clinical depression that predates the festival and the situation, in which case it requires its own specific treatment separate from the aftermath processing. If the spiral is persistent and worsening over weeks rather than gradually improving: the therapist appointment is the assessment for this, not the chapter.

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever timed your contact with someone specifically for the Decom window — the text at the four-thirty a.m. Tuesday crash that you knew would land differently there? That is the Comedown Glue from Chapter 4. That is also a Decom exploitation. The timing is the manipulation, whether the contact is otherwise genuine.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **Sober and Recovery Lane:** The Decom Spiral’s specific flavor in recovery can be the “everything is gray, why not” thought pattern. If you are in recovery and the spiral is producing those specific thoughts: contact your sponsor and your therapist before the thought develops further.

**ND Festies:** The contrast between peak festival stimulation and default-world sensory environment can be especially severe for ND nervous systems, and the Decom Spiral may be more intense and last longer. This is not a character flaw. It is sensory contrast. The spiral interruption protocol applies with adjustments for your specific nervous system.

This runs in every direction. The Decom Spiral runs through the production crew coming off a six-day build, the touring artist coming off a run, the first-timer coming off her first festival ever, and anyone returning from a high-intensity environment to a lower-stimulation one. It is universal, universal, universal. It does not indicate something wrong with you. The protocol is food and sleep and time and the floor.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. The week after a major project completes, the production wraps, the wedding weekend ends, the conference concludes. The specific gray of the week after something excellent. The specific contrast. The specific certainty, from inside it, that everything is now permanently less excellent. It isn’t. The contrast is the whole explanation.
