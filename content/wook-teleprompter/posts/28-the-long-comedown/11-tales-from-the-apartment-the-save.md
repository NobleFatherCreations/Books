## Tales From The Apartment — The Save

Festie POV. Same scenario. Counter-Drops live. Named protagonist. Watermelon Moment present.

She has been doing the things.

Her name is Simone and she has been doing the things for four weeks and some of the things are working and some of them are the kind of work that doesn’t feel like working yet but that the information says is working.

The voice note from her friend Junie comes every three days: “Hey. No need to respond. Thinking of you.” The no need to respond has been, every single time, the sentence that makes her respond, because the removal of the pressure is the thing that makes the reaching back feel possible.

She has the RAINN number in her phone. She has the crisis text line in her phone. She has not needed either at the clinical level — she has been managing — but having them there is the difference between the three a.m. Encore producing anxiety about having no options and the three a.m. Encore happening in an apartment that contains options.

She found a trauma-informed therapist who does not specialize in festival harm specifically but who specializes in coercive control and has a session yesterday at ten a.m. It was the first session. She cried for the first twenty minutes and then described the full setlist and the therapist wrote things down and at the end said: “This is going to take some time. You’re in the right place.”

“This is going to take some time” from someone who has the training to know is not a sentence she has found comforting from uninformed sources. From someone with the training, it was the most specific relief she has felt in four weeks. The timeline is known. It is longer than she wants. It is survivable.

**The watermelon moment:** she is in the cereal aisle at seven p.m. on a Tuesday and a song starts playing — not from the bad night but from the first night, the night before, the night she danced until three a.m. and felt exactly like herself and the sky was doing the thing over the desert and everything was exactly right. The song is just playing in the cereal aisle and her sternum goes and her jaw goes and for about forty seconds she is back in the desert.

She does the five-sense ground.

**Five things I can see:** the green of the cereal box, the woman with the cart, the fluorescent light, the yellow tag on the sale display, the tile floor.

**Four things I can touch:** the basket handle, the wool of her sweater, the floor through her shoes, her own left hand pressed against her sternum.

**Three things I can hear:** the song ending, the checkout beep, the refrigerator hum.

**Two things I can smell:** the store air, faintly, something baking from the bakery section.

**One thing I can taste:** the toothpaste from this morning.

The sternum settles.

The song ends.

The cereal aisle is the cereal aisle.

She buys the cereal. She goes home. She eats soup — cream of tomato, with milk, not water. She texts Junie: “I did the five-sense ground today at the grocery store. It worked.”

**Junie texts back:** “YES. That’s it. That’s the whole thing.”

She puts the phone down.

She is not over it.

She is doing the things.

The things are working.

The timeline is longer than she wants.

She will be okay.
