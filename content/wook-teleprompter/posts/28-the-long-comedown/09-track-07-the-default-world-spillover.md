### TRACK 07 — THE DEFAULT-WORLD SPILLOVER
*(PTSD activation in non-event contexts)*

Here's the behavior. It is not at a festival when it happens.

It is in the cereal aisle of the grocery store when a song starts playing from the store’s ambient playlist. The song was on a set from that weekend. The song does not know it was on that set. The song is just playing in the grocery store because an algorithm decided it was the right tempo for Tuesday afternoon shopping. But the body — the body that encoded what happened in somatic fragments and sensory impressions and emotional tones — the body receives the song before the brain has made any decisions about the song.

The sternum goes. The jaw goes. The band of muscle between the shoulder blades does the thing. The body is back at the festival before the mind has finished reading the cereal box.

This is the Default-World Spillover. It is the nervous system’s encoded material being activated by an associative trigger in a context where the activation is not expected and not prepared for. It is the body’s record refusing to stay in the past where the mind has filed it.

The Spillover is not a sign that you are not healing. It is a sign that you are healing — that the nervous system is processing the encoded material, testing its responses, checking whether the trigger is still dangerous. The check produces the response. The response is information.

Here's the read on it. **When the Spillover happens:** notice it. Name it. “This is the Spillover. My body is responding to an associative trigger. I am in the grocery store. The song does not know what it is.”

Do not fight the response. The fight extends the response. The naming usually begins to settle it within sixty seconds.

Do not leave the situation unless you need to. Leaving teaches the nervous system that the trigger is genuinely dangerous and requires avoidance. Staying — with the grounding techniques below — teaches it that the trigger activated a response and the response was not a crisis.

Here's your counter-drop — run this. &rarr; The Spillover Ground. The five-sense grounding technique. Available at any time, in any location, requires only your senses.

Name five things you can see. Specifically. “The green of this cereal box. The woman with the cart in front of me. The fluorescent light above the yogurt section.” Five specific visible things.

Name four things you can touch. Without moving if you need to stay still. “The handle of the basket I’m holding. The fabric of my jacket sleeve. The floor through the soles of my shoes.”

Name three things you can hear. “The song. The hum of the refrigerated display. The checkout beep from somewhere to my right.”

Name two things you can smell.

Name one thing you can taste.

The sequence brings sensory attention to the present context. The present context is not the festival. The song is in the cereal aisle. The cereal aisle is safe. The naming confirms the safety to the nervous system in the only language the nervous system actually speaks, which is sensory.

**After the grounding:** buy the cereal. Leave normally. Do not give the moment more weight than it requires.

Quick vibe check. The Spillover will decrease in frequency and intensity over time. The first month it will happen several times a week. The sixth month it will happen occasionally. The second year it will happen rarely and with significantly reduced intensity. The trajectory is real and documented. It is not permanent.

Turn it around — the mirror set. Have you ever been aware that a song, a smell, a setting, a tone of voice was in someone’s nervous system because of something you were part of? Have you ever known that the trigger existed and chosen not to change your behavior in spaces where you might activate it? You are not required to sterilize the world of your presence. You are, perhaps, required to hold the fact that the world contains your trigger now.

Refractions — this lands differently depending on who you are. **Recovery Lane:** The Spillover can be a high-risk moment for people in recovery when the trigger is associated with a substance. The five-sense grounding applies. The sponsor call applies. The protocol is: name it, ground it, call if you need to.

**Scene professionals:** If your work requires you to be in the scene — DJing, harm reduction, vending, performing — and the Spillover is activated by scene-specific triggers that are unavoidable in your professional context: trauma-informed therapy specifically for occupational trigger exposure is available. Ask for it specifically.

This runs in every direction. The Spillover runs in the survivor who flinches at a specific bassline in a grocery store, the veteran who can’t be in a crowded room the same way anymore, the first-timer startled by a stranger’s cologne, and survivors of every kind of harm in every context. It is universal. The specific triggers are scene-specific. The mechanism is human.

And Sober Tuesday — the morning-after truth. The commute on the subway when the train passes the station near the old office and the stomach goes before the brain catches up. The Spillover runs in every context where the body encoded something significant. The cereal aisle is just the festival version of the subway.
