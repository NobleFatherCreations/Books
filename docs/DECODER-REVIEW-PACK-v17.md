## 1. Product, reader and job

- **What:** a free guide to **310 manipulation and control tactics** in plain words. Each one covers what it is, what it
  can sound like, how to tell honestly whether it fits (both ways), and what a person can do.
- **Reader:** someone hurting and unsure, often on a phone, sometimes in secret or on a shared device, who may not know
  what to search for. They may be tired, frightened or dysregulated.
- **Job:** help them put a name to what is happening, check it fairly, and find a safe next step. It is **a reading aid,
  never a diagnosis, a verdict, legal advice or therapy**. That framing is in the content, the UI copy and the footer.
- **Owner:** Noble Father Creations. It is one of about 20 projects proxied under one domain, and each project carries a
  shared "House" menu (the NF button, bottom right).

## 2. Hard constraints (non-negotiable)

1. **Self-contained.** No frameworks, no CDN, no external requests. The one sanctioned exception is the Cloudflare Web
   Analytics beacon, which runs on every site. Fonts, icons and data are inlined or self-hosted.
2. **Content is frozen for design reviews.** The 310 entries, their fields, the safety framing and the validation logic
   are not in scope. Comment on how content is *presented*, not what it says. Content issues can be flagged separately.
3. **Built, never hand-edited.** Output is generated by `design/build-decoder-v15.py` behind a byte-for-byte gate.
4. **No gamification, scores, streaks or verdicts.** The "tick what fits" lists count nothing and keep nothing.
5. **Every motion is caused by the reader**, and all of it obeys reduced motion and the in-app Motion setting.
6. **A commit is not a deploy.** Live is checked byte-for-byte against the repo (`scripts/verify-deployed.mjs`).

**The app inside index.html:**
- A start-up index of 310 records (name, slug, line, settings, subtitle, search words, safety flag, aliases, related,
  popularity, kind, map position, plus three short fields) is parsed at start. It is about 479 KB.
- Each line's full entries sit in their own `<script type="application/json" id="L-<line>">` block at the end of the
  file and are parsed the first time a station on that line opens. This lazy parse cut the old 836 ms start-up task to
  about 121 ms.
- Routing uses hash routes (`#/entry/gaslighting`). Back, forward and bookmarks work.
- Rendering is string templates into `#main`. The home view is server-rendered into the HTML, so the first paint needs
  no script.

## 7. The entry (station) page in detail

In reading order:
1. **Back sign** ("← Guilt, pressure and fear").
2. **Station sign:** an 8px band in the line colour, a 48px line bullet, the name (Overpass 800, up to 52px), and an
   optional subtitle.
3. **"On the G line, Guilt, pressure and fear"** plus a code plate `G 0 1`.
4. **Safety first** (only on the 105 safety-sensitive entries): a **red band with a triangle and tactile-paving dots**,
   then the safety note. It always comes *before* the answer.
5. **The answer:** the plain-language summary (first sentence bold), up to two "it can sound like" quotes with a
   line-coloured quote mark, and "Also called …" for merged names. The answer panel's top edge takes the line colour.
6. **Actions:** Save · Listen (on-device voices only; warns first; reads the safety note first) · Text size · Share link ·
   Print.
7. **Strip map:** previous and next stations on the line, "Station n of N".
8. **Four steps** (tabs, swipeable, keys 1 to 4, deep-linkable):
   1. **Is this it?** Signs it might be this; signs it is probably something else; questions to ask yourself; a fold
      with "more worrying if", "what shows up over time" and "what else it could be" (what it is not, ordinary
      explanations, often mistaken for this). Ticking is just for the reader: nothing is counted or kept.
   2. **Understand it.** What it is, why it matters, how it shows up, more things people say.
   3. **What you can do.** A script ("Words you could use", "Words to say to yourself", or "A check to run before you
      share or act") with Copy; five ways to respond (ask gently, set a clear limit, step back, get support, at work or
      school or with services); "Is it changing?". On sensitive entries the hint says to skip the wording if speaking
      up could make things worse.
   4. **Go deeper.** How it differs from related tactics, using this label fairly, patterns it is part of.
9. **Side** (desktop, from 1120px): Show on the map; **Change here for** (up to 6 look-alikes, each with Compare); where it
   happens; patterns.
10. **Previous / next station** cards.
11. **Print footer:** "A reading aid, not a diagnosis…".

Phone extras: a slim step dock above the sign bar once the tabs scroll away, back to top (only while scrolling up), a
one-time swipe tip, and a reading-progress line under the top bar (CSS scroll-driven; absent where unsupported).

## 9. Search engine

`search(q)` in app.js:
1. **Normalise:** lowercase, expand contractions, strip punctuation, cap input at 400 characters.
2. **Phrase pass** (2 or more characters): substring match across name, aliases, subtitle, search words, short
   definition, summary and "sounds like". Ranked: exact name, name prefix, name contains, search words, sounds-like,
   anything else. Queries under 4 characters with more than 40 hits return nothing, to avoid noise.
3. **Fuzzy pass** (multi-word queries with fewer than 5 hits):
   - Stems words and maps everyday synonyms (boyfriend, wife, ex → partner; boss → manager; text, DM, email → message;
     ignore → silent; and so on). Stop words are removed.
   - Scores each token: name hit 4, search words 3.5, sounds-like 3, elsewhere 1. Two- and three-word phrases add 8 when
     found in the search words and 6 when found in the sounds-like lines.
   - Needs at least half the tokens to hit (at most 3). Keeps results at 50% of the top score or more; returns up to 12.
4. **Station codes:** `T9`, `t09` and `T 09` jump to that station.
5. Rested lines are excluded everywhere.

The index is built in idle slices (no slice over about 25 ms on a slow phone). Snippets show which words matched.

Measured: "they blamed me" gives Blame-Shifting; "they told everyone I was lying" gives Smear Campaign; "manufactured
urgency" gives Urgency Manufacturing.

## 10. Help me figure it out (the guide)

Scoring:
- Each picked feeling contributes its ranked list from the feelings index (`narrow`): 10 points for the first, falling
  by half a point per rank, floor 5.5.
- Plus 3 points if the tactic happens in the chosen setting.
- Results are deduplicated through aliases, rested lines are excluded, and the top 15 are kept with their reasons.

There are 6 places and 50 feelings.

## 11. Privacy, storage and the Leave button

- **Saving is off by default.** Nothing is written to the device: no localStorage, no sessionStorage, no cookies. The
  suite verifies this after browsing 25 stations and a search.
- **When saving is on:**
  - One `localStorage` entry, `ui-state`, scrambled with XOR plus base64, **and honestly labelled "scrambled, not
    secret"**.
  - It holds only numbers: saved and recent tactics as FNV hashes of their slugs, rested lines as indexes, settings as
    short codes. **No timestamps and no tactic words.**
  - Turning saving off deletes it. Old v14 `fg_*` keys are only ever imported or deleted.
- **Since v16, addresses and tab titles name the tactic**, whatever the saving setting. Settings and the safety page say
  the browser keeps these in its history, and that a private window is safest.
- **Leave** (green, top right, every screen; also Escape twice):
  - Hides the page, clears in-memory state and stops read-aloud.
  - Steps back over every page of this visit (the depth is kept in `history.state`), then `location.replace()`s to the
    chosen destination: weather search, Wikipedia, or a blank page; a blank page when offline.
  - No referrer is sent. On the plain pages, Leave is a simpler replace to the weather search.
- **Rest a line:** the reader can hide whole lines they are not ready for. They are removed from search, matches, lists,
  the map, codes, Saved, Continue and routes. At least one line stays visible.
- **Analytics:** only the Cloudflare beacon counts anonymous visits. Nothing typed or read is sent anywhere.

**Colour rules:**
- Gold means "press this / selected".
- Red means danger and is always paired with a triangle.
- Green means leave.
- Line colours are identity and always come with their letter.
- Colour never carries meaning alone.

**Contrast:** 1,344 text runs were measured in both themes and all pass at ≥4.5:1 (≥3:1 for large text). Gold on void
is 11.7:1; safety fill with ink is 5.4:1.

**Modes:**
- **Quiet colours** shrinks the line fills to small marks and turns off glows and the stripe.
- `prefers-contrast: more` lifts secondary text and adds 2px outlines.
- `forced-colors` uses system colours with borders.
- Print is greyscale: no fills, no glows, chrome hidden.

## 13. Design system: type, space, shape, elevation, components

- **Type.** Two faces, both self-hosted and inlined.
  - **Overpass 600 to 800** is used for everything scanned: signs, headings, labels, buttons, numbers.
  - **Atkinson Next 400 to 700 plus italic** (a low-vision legibility face) is used for everything read.
  - Scale:
    - display: clamp 2.25 to 3.75rem, line height 1.02
    - station name: 2 to 3.25rem
    - headline: 1.875 to 2.75rem
    - title: 1.375rem
    - sign: 1.1875rem
    - **body: 1.125rem at line height 1.6**
    - label: 0.8125 to 0.9375rem; nothing smaller than 0.8125rem
  - Measure is about 42 to 44rem. Text size can be raised to 112.5% or 125%; a roomy spacing mode sets line height 1.8.
    Everything is in rem.
  - Uppercase tracked text appears in exactly two places.
- **Space.** An 8px ladder (8/16/24/32/48/64), with a few 4, 6, 10 and 14px micro-gaps inside components. **Every tap
  target is at least 44px**; blades are 72px. Safe-area insets are honoured on every fixed edge.
- **Shape (meaning by shape).** Circle = line or station. Rectangle with 0 radius = sign or control. Triangle = safety.
  The only soft exceptions are the active door pill and the sheet grab handle.
- **Elevation.** Flat and tonal. **No drop shadows.** Separation comes from tone steps, hairlines and inset rings. Focus
  is a 3px `fg` outline with a 3px offset on every control.
- **Components:**
  - blades (primary / quiet / safety)
  - buttons and segments (invert to gold when selected)
  - Leave
  - line bullet and emblem
  - code plate
  - strip map
  - rows (departures board)
  - four-step tabs with a progress bar
  - answer panel
  - safety plate and exit plate
  - search field
  - network map and station sheet
  - toast
  - glossary term (dotted underline with an inline definition)
  - breath panel
  - The shared House menu (`#nf-chrome`) is outside this system.
- **CSS.** Layers in order: reset, tokens, base, layout, components, pages, motion, print. About 50 KB, with 7
  `!important` (all justified in DESIGN.md).

## 14. Motion budget

Everything is reader-caused, transform/opacity only, and off under `prefers-reduced-motion` or the Motion setting:
1. Press-scale to 0.97 (0.09s).
2. Step panel slide (24px, 0.22s).
3. Interchange-line draw on the map (0.32s).
4. Bottom sheet rise (0.28s; fade on desktop).
5. View-transition cross-fade between pages (0.12 to 0.18s), with the station name morphing from the tapped row (0.28s).
6. The station band sweep after a tap (0.3s).
7. Toast fade and progress-bar scale.
8. The reading-progress line, which moves only when the reader scrolls.

**Nothing loops, parallaxes or starts by itself.** Grain, meteors, pointer light, magnet, blur reveals and the moving sky
from v14 were cut on purpose. Tap buzz is opt-in and never on Leave.

## 17. Findability (SEO and AI crawlers) — v16

- **App `<head>`:**
  - Title "Pattern Decoder: 310 Manipulation and Control Tactics, Explained in Plain Words", description, canonical,
    Open Graph and Twitter tags.
  - JSON-LD `DefinedTermSet` plus `WebApplication`.
  - A `<noscript>` index linking to all line pages and the tactics index.
- **Per route,** `seo()` sets the title, description, canonical, og:* and a `DefinedTerm` (with `termCode` such as
  `G01`). The canonical for a station is its plain page.
- **Plain pages** are what crawlers read, because hash routes are one URL to search engines and most AI crawlers do not
  run JavaScript:
  - Each tactic page has the full reading order of section 7 in static HTML (safety first; the answer; a gold "Check it
    step by step in the guide" link into the app; signs both ways; questions; what it is; why it matters; how it shows
    up; what you can do; using the label fairly; look-alikes with links; previous/next on the line).
  - Its JSON-LD graph is `DefinedTerm` + `DefinedTermSet` + `WebPage` + `BreadcrumbList`.
  - Line pages are `CollectionPage` + `ItemList`. The tactics index is a `CollectionPage`.
  - There are 322 unique titles and 322 unique descriptions, with no dead internal links.
- **Title and description formats:** `<Tactic>: Signs, Examples and What to Do · Pattern Decoder` and
  `What is <Tactic>? <short definition>` (clipped at 160 characters).
- **robots.txt** (root) allows everything and names GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, ClaudeBot, Claude-SearchBot,
  Claude-User, PerplexityBot, Perplexity-User, Google-Extended, Applebot-Extended and CCBot. It lists both sitemaps.
- **Routing detail:** Netlify serves `/playbook/gaslighting` from `gaslighting.html` through the proxy with no redirect.
  `x/index.html` must never be used, because a trailing-slash redirect would escape the `/playbook` prefix.
- **Not done:** submitting the sitemaps to Google Search Console and Bing (this needs the owner's account); Open Graph
  images; FAQ/HowTo markup (deliberately skipped, since Google restricted FAQ rich results); `hreflang` (English only).

## 19. Known gaps, risks and open decisions

**Risks a reviewer should weigh:**
1. **Privacy regression is by design.** Tactic names appear in history, tabs and shared links. The mitigation is wording
   plus Leave. Is the in-app disclosure prominent enough? It lives in Settings and Use this safely, not on first visit.
2. **Two kinds of safety red.** The red safety band (#F04438) and the orange People line (#FF8A4C) are now distinct in
   hue but both warm. Check this for colour-vision deficiencies. The triangle and the letter carry the meaning either way.
3. **Gold and the line colours.** The gold primary blade sits beside a solid tile in each line's colour on Home. Is gold
   still clearly "the thing to press"?
4. **Plain pages differ from the app.** No tick lists, no tabs, all sections open at once, and system-level behaviour
   only (no Settings). The plain page's Leave is simpler: it does not unwind multi-page history.
5. **The app is 3.34 MB.** It is fine on Wi-Fi and measured fast with CPU throttling, but not tested on a slow 3G
   network.
6. **Real devices and screen readers are untested.**
7. **No clinical review** of the medical symptom lists and safeguarding notes.

**Debt:**
- The `--amber*` token names now mean red.
- A stale comment at `app.js:124` ("names when saving, numbers when private") ships in the live file; fix it on the next
  deploy.
- The DESIGN.md frontmatter still lists v15 colour values, with a v17 addendum below.
- The build script is still named `build-decoder-v15.py`.
- The House menu has its own unlayered CSS.

**Open decisions for the owner:**
- **13 gates:** what the design was meant to show, and whether it is a new layer above the 10 lines, a sequence (for
  example stages a reader passes through), or something else.
- Vivid or Quiet colours by default.
- Search Console submission.
- Whether the first visit should state the privacy trade-off up front.

## 20. Review brief: what to check and how to answer

Please review the live site (https://noblefathercreations.com/playbook, plus for example `/playbook/gaslighting`,
`/playbook/line-truth` and `/playbook/tactics`) against this pack. Use it on a phone-sized and a desktop-sized window.

1. **Completeness:** is anything a reader would expect missing (a screen, a state, an error, an empty state, a way
   back)? Is anything in this pack not true of the live site?
2. **Task tests** (count taps and note any hesitation):
   1. "My partner keeps saying things never happened", with no idea what to search.
   2. Find and read "Silent Treatment" fully.
   3. Compare two look-alikes.
   4. Leave instantly.
   5. Arrive from Google on a plain page and continue in the app.
3. **Visual system:** does Midnight Matte hold together? Is gold unambiguous as "press this", is red unambiguous as
   danger, and is there anything that reads as generic or templated?
4. **Safety and trauma-informed design:** choice, control, predictability, no pressure, easy exit. Given section 3,
   is the privacy disclosure adequate? Is there any surprise?
5. **Accessibility** beyond the automated checks: cognitive load, reading order, focus order, and colour-vision issues
   (section 19 item 2).
6. **Findability:** titles, descriptions, structured data, internal linking, and crawl paths. Anything that would stop
   Google or an AI assistant from citing a tactic page correctly?
7. **13 gates:** given the 10 lines, propose two or three concrete ways "13 gates" could be shown without inventing
   content, and say what each would need from the owner.

**Answer format:**
- A 10-line verdict.
- Then a findings table: severity (blocker / major / minor), area, evidence (URL plus what you saw), fix, effort.
- Then the 13-gates options.
- Mark anything you could not verify. Do not propose React, a CDN or external services (section 2). Do not change the
  content itself.
