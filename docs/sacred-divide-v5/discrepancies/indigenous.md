Format: **[Location]** what is wrong → what it should be (proposed wording) → why it matters. Nothing below has been changed in the text unless it says "Fixed in IND-…"; every item touches the frozen layer (numbers, receipts, grades, source entries, case tags) or asserts a claim the wording pass may not weaken.

## P1 · Critical (factually exposed, or breaks the codex's own rules)

1. **[§12 grades for 13–26, and the rationales now under them]** The rationales now state what each grade rests on (IND-R013 … R026). For every entry from 13 to 25 the basis is an external record (residential schools, bans on ceremony, retreat extraction, withheld records), while the bullets describe internal behaviour by elders, healers or families that no document on the page records. The grades are unchanged:
   - **13 Normalization**: Documented on the residential-school systems; bullets describe reconnecting people normalized into elder authority. Points to **Cultural**.
   - **14 Isolation, 15 Triangulation**: Documented on the bans on ceremony; bullets describe elders, secrecy and spirit fear. Points to **Cultural** for the bullets, or rewrite the entries to describe the external isolation.
   - **16, 18–25 (Flying Monkeys, Stonewalling, Manufactured Consent, Trauma Bonding, Learned Helplessness, Benevolent Control, Infantilization, Identity Erosion, Spiritual Bypassing)**: all Documented on one shared basis, the external extraction (retreat tourism, appropriated ceremony, unconsented research). The bullets describe internal conduct. Points to **Cultural**.
   - **17 Smear Campaign**: Documented on withheld records; bullets describe smearing of internal critics. Points to **Cultural**.
   - **26 Financial Control**: Documented on retreat extraction; bullets describe offerings and initiation fees, which are not documented as coercive. The basis fits the external half only.
   - **2 and 30 carry "(sourced)"**, but no source on the page is tied to them; 2 is Contested and 30 is Cultural. Remove the marker or name the source.
   If 13–25 move to Cultural the §1 tally becomes Documented 1, Cultural 26, Contested 3, and "6 of 30 sourced" changes.
   → The grade is the page's strongest signal, and thirteen Documented chips currently sit on entries about internal control that the evidence cannot reach.
2. **[§7 "Individual ceremonial figures" row; §10 card 3; §11 Bodies and Children; §16; §17; §12 passim]** Control and abuse by elders, healers and ceremonial figures is stated as documented ("Abuse by ceremonial leaders is documented", "the one internal harm this page must name") with the receipt FORMER MEMBER TESTIMONY and no named case, people or nation. The section's cites ([10] WHO FGM, [11] UNICEF witchcraft, [12] MMIWG) do not concern it (F19). Proposed wording: "Abuse by ceremonial leaders has been reported in some communities; this page cites no case, and the claim is an allegation, not a finding." Or add a named, sourced case. → An unsourced attribution of abuse to the traditions' leaders is the line a hostile reader attacks first, and the volume's whole second argument stands on it.
3. **[§12 techniques 1–30 as a set; §7 Participation; §11 Bodies "arranged or early marriage, menstrual restriction"; §1 Leaving row]** The entries attribute coercive patterns to "Indigenous, folk, and ancestral systems", to "elders, shamans, healers", and in §11 to arranged marriage and menstrual restriction, without naming one people, nation or organization and without a source. Per this volume's rule, a claim that treats Indigenous traditions as one body with shared clergy and practices needs a named people and a source. Proposed: name the community and source each pattern ("In [named nation], [source] reports …") or label the entries as a framework applied to hypothetical communities. → The page's own §6 says these are thousands of distinct nations; §12 does not.
4. **[§5 timeline, row 1876–1996]** "mass graves later identified" → "potential unmarked graves identified since 2021, most not yet excavated" (F4). The §5 card says "potential unmarked graves" correctly; the timeline row overstates and cites the TRC (2015), which predates the graves reporting. → Denialist sources attack this word; it must match the card.
5. **[§11 Bodies, last bullet]** "Missing and murdered Indigenous women is the overwhelming gendered harm here, and its source is external." The inquiry [12] roots the violence in colonial and patriarchal policy and finds genocide, but it also records interpersonal violence, and it does not call the source external; the harm is not religious in the sense used elsewhere on the page (F16). Proposed: "The National Inquiry into Missing and Murdered Indigenous Women and Girls found that violence against Indigenous women and girls has colonial and patriarchal roots and amounts to genocide [12]." → An attribution of a harm to a source that the cited report does not state.
6. **[§6 Africa row; §11 Children; §16; §17; §23 question 4]** FGM is listed as a harm of "ancestral and folk religions" and "traditional contexts defended in cultural terms". WHO [10] says no religious scripts prescribe it and that religious leaders differ (F14). Proposed: "FGM is a social practice found in communities of several religions and none; it is recorded here only where it is defended as tradition [10]." → Presenting a cross-religious practice as an Indigenous-religion harm misleads the reader about its cause.
7. **[§23 "In closing"]** "Your traditions are the only ones in this codex that were nearly exterminated by another religion's institutions." This is a superlative across 30+ volumes with no source, and "your traditions" treats many peoples as one. Proposed: "Many Indigenous nations were targeted by the institutions of other religions, and the accounting for that is nowhere near complete." → A single counter-example in another volume (for instance a persecuted minority) breaks the sentence.

## P2 · Structural (inconsistent, incomplete, or weakens comparison)

8. **[§3 lede; §3 "strongest objection"; §23]** "your traditions" and "your family" address the reader as one Indigenous community, and "Almost everything done to your traditions was done from outside" is a sweeping claim. Proposed: "Much of the documented harm to Indigenous peoples in Canada, the United States and Australia was done from outside." → Aligns the thesis with the three countries the sources cover.
9. **[§5 card heading "1883–1951"; §5 timeline; §7; §8; §12 t14, t15]** The Potlatch ban was statutory in Canada (1884 amendment, in force 1885, repealed 1951; Sun Dance 1895); the US Code of Indian Offenses (1883) was never passed by Congress and the US ban ran to 1978 per [5]. "By statute" and "a century of statutory bans" are accurate for neither country as written, and "1883–1951" joins the two (F11, F12). Proposed: "Canada banned the Potlatch (1885–1951) and the Sun Dance (from 1895) by law; the US Interior Department's 1883 Code of Indian Offenses banned ceremonies without an act of Congress, and the ban lasted until 1978 [5]." → Hostile readers cite the 1934 policy change and the Canadian dates.
10. **[§5 card 1996 and 2015; §8; §9]** "documenting more than 3,200" (§5) and "about 3,200" (§8); the 4,037 figure for 2019 is unverified (F1, F2). Proposed: "at least 3,200 recorded deaths" in both places; delete or source 4,037. State that the register records deaths, which include illness, not only abuse. → Allegation, finding and count must stay distinct.
11. **[§9 Money in numbers]** "the church said dioceses and orders had paid $50 million" → "had provided more than $50 million in cash and in-kind contributions" (F8). → "Paid" drops "in-kind" and "more than".
