## P1 · Critical (factually exposed, or breaks the codex's own rules)

1. **[§20 first row: "Rape convictions of major godmen, upheld on appeal"]** Only Asaram's conviction is recorded as upheld (Rajasthan High Court, May 2026). Ram Rahim's appeal against his rape conviction is admitted and pending (F1, F6, F7). → "The rape convictions of Asaram (upheld on appeal in 2026) and Gurmeet Ram Rahim Singh (appeal pending)." → It is the first row of the precedent table and a hostile reader checks it against the case list in §19, which says only that the sentence "stands".
2. **[§7 "Convicted godmen" row, "murder convictions in one case were overturned on appeal (2024, 2026)"]** §5, §9 and §19 record two murder convictions in two cases (Ranjit Singh, overturned 2024; Chhatrapati, overturned 2026) (F3). → "murder convictions in two cases were overturned on appeal (2024, 2026)". → An in-page count that contradicts three other sections.
3. **[§3 strongest objection: "Kabir's, Basava's, and Ambedkar's, all of which predate any Western reading"]** Ambedkar (1891–1956) does not predate Western readings of Hinduism (F21). → "The anti-caste critique here is Kabir's and Basava's, which predate any Western reading, and Ambedkar's, made from inside India." → The sentence is the page's answer to the objection that criticism is foreign; a false premise hands the objection back its force. (The gloss added in HIN-G003 describes the three names and does not repeat the claim as fact.)
4. **[§14 say-do row 4; §23 question 3]** "Two leaders of movements with tens of millions of followers" and "Two godmen with tens of millions of followers": no follower figure exists in the volume or its sources (F5). → "Two leaders of movements with mass followings". → The claim sits in the say-do table and in a question the reader is told to ask aloud.
5. **[§12 grade basis, decide each; grades unchanged]** After the rationales were rewritten (HIN-R001 onward), these grades rest on facts the entry does not describe:
   - **22 Benevolent Control: Documented.** The note cites the Sabarimala judgment on menstrual exclusion; the entry describes family and guru control framed as love. Points to **Cultural**.
   - **26 Financial Control: Codified.** State endowment acts codify temple administration, but the entry's bullets are ritual fees, guru offerings, pilgrimage costs and family finances, and "hereditary ritual monopolies are customary law" is not codification. Points to **Cultural**, or **Codified** for a narrowed entry.
   - **12 DARVO: Documented.** The basis is the godmen's rape convictions (section 19); the bullets describe labeling critics anti-Hindu, which is customary. The grade holds for the guru half only.
   - **30 Plausible Deniability: Cultural**, but §19 tags the Ram Rahim and Asaram cases to it. **17 Smear Campaign: Cultural**, but §19 tags the Dabholkar case to it. Decide whether the cases or the grades change.
   - **15 Triangulation** is a Cultural entry sourced to the Sabarimala case tag only; the case text does not show intermediaries.
   → Any regrade changes the §1 tally (Cultural 21, Taught 6, Documented 2, Codified 1) and the "7 of 30 sourced" count.
6. **[§12 evidence lines marked "(sourced)" against the §12 cites line]** Seven entries (12, 15, 19, 22, 25, 26, 28) are marked sourced, but the section's cites line lists only [5] and [8]. The convictions ([1], [3], [4]) and the dowry, temple and caste sources are not attached to them (F23). → Add the matching citation markers to each "(sourced)" line, or to the §12 cites line. → "Sourced to a named document" is the §1 claim; the reader cannot follow it.
