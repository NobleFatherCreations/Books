# Comparator blockers — structural divergences between volumes

## The headline
- **The seven newer volumes are built differently.** Ahmadiyya, Anglicanism, Dawoodi Bohra, Oriental Orthodoxy, Plymouth Brethren, Soka Gakkai and Unification Church differ in §3, §5, §7 and, most of all, §12, where the 30 techniques are one compact table instead of thirty full entries (about 850–1,000 words against a median of 5,868). Set side by side, they will look far better evidenced or far less examined than their siblings, depending on the reader. This is the largest single blocker, and an asymmetry-honesty problem as well as a layout one.
- **§6 Branches has 18 different table shapes** and **§8 Law & state has 10.** These are the sections a reader is most likely to compare.
- **§4 What healthy looks like is short everywhere** (median 63 words against 5,868 for §12). The Method says no tradition is reduced to its worst documented moments; the template does exactly that. This is a template flaw, not a flaw in any one volume.


When the same section sits side by side in two volumes, any difference in shape (different table columns, a table in one and cards in the other, different subsections) makes the comparison unfair or unreadable. This lists every section where the 34 volumes do not share one shape. Fixing these improves the PDFs too.
