# Steelman of the "Who made this" bio (version 4)

2026-11-23. Three hostile but fair readers. Each lists what works, what breaks trust, and the exact fix.

---

## 1. Psychologist specialising in brand identity and trust

**What works**
- **The opening flips a lifelong insult** ("too much") within two lines. Strong recognition, instantly personal.
- **"I know, because it was used on me"** is authority from a shared wound: the most credible source this audience accepts.
- **Costly, checkable signals:** two years, free, no tracking. These are the best trust assets on the page.
- **The tip-link aside** is self-deprecating and specific, so it reads human. Moments like this lower defences honestly.

**What breaks trust**
1. **Too many slogan lines in a row:** "That's the trick. It always was." "You were just easy to aim at." "It became something I was *for*." One-line punches in quick succession are the cadence of online gurus and sales pages. That's the opposite of the page's claim ("no funnel"). Congruence is everything here: the style has to match the promise. **Fix:** keep the three strongest punches, and let the rest run as ordinary sentences.
2. **Mind-reading overused:** "You already know the feeling," "If any of this felt like I was describing you, I was." Once is resonance. Repeated, it starts to feel like a cold reading. **Fix:** keep the shrink list (it *shows*, rather than claims), and cut "You already know the feeling."
3. **No concrete anchor.** There are feelings but no facts. A brand earns trust when emotion is backed by something countable. **Fix:** one plain line of numbers: fourteen books, 335 tactics, every source named.

## 2. Someone in the thick of it who distrusts most content

**What works**
- **The shrink list is the moment I felt seen:** checking my memory, explaining for the tenth time, apologising for things I didn't do, 3am. That's the part I'd screenshot.
- **"I'm not going to tell you what happened."** Good. I'm tired of people selling their trauma.
- **Free with no email.** That's the first thing I check, and it passes.

**What sets off my alarms**
1. **"You were never too much."** That's what the person who hurt me said in the beginning. Instant warmth from a stranger is how it starts. **Fix:** earn it. Open with what *happened to me*, then say it.
2. **"The big-hearted ones… the very thing that makes you good."** Flattery. I've learned to flinch at flattery. **Fix:** say it as a fact about tactics, not a compliment about me.
3. **"Welcome."** "You're not on your own anymore." "When I say the people I love, I mean everyone. Yes. You too." That's belonging offered to a stranger, which is exactly what groups use to pull people in. **Fix:** "I don't know you. I don't need anything from you." Then the love line, grounded.
4. **Faith appears, and my guard goes up.** I've been hurt by religious people. Am I about to be recruited? **Fix:** one line straight after: "I'm not here to convert anyone. You don't need to believe what I believe."
5. **The catch arrives too late.** "No price" is two-thirds down. **Fix:** a short, early signal that nothing is wanted from me.

## 3. Domestic-abuse advocate who vets resources for survivors

**What works**
- **No details of the abuse,** and a clear statement of no credentials. Honest, and appropriate.
- **"Where the research exists, I've named it."** I can check that.
- **Free, no tracking, a Leave button.** Survivor-safe by design. I can recommend that.

**What I'd need changed before sharing it with clients**
1. **"You were just easy to aim at."** This places the cause in the survivor's traits. Victim-blaming language, even unintended, is the first thing I screen for. **Fix:** "You were aimed at. And they aimed at what's good in you." The responsibility sits with the person doing it.
2. **The book list leaves out men and people who have caused harm.** The author is a man, and *The Silence* exists for men. *The Repair* exists for people who hurt someone and want to stop. Including both signals fairness, which is rare, and makes it safer to recommend. **Fix:** add two lines.
3. **The women's joke.** "Apparently I like to live dangerously" is charming, but some clients will read a man joking about writing for women as flippant. **Fix:** keep the humour, and make the respect land in the same sentence.
4. **"In churches."** Fine, and honest, given *The Sacred Divide*. Keep it.

## Changes applied (version 5)
- The opening now starts with what happened to him, before speaking to the reader. "Too much" stays, but it's earned.
- "Easy to aim at" became "aimed at… what's good in you".
- Fewer slogan lines; one plain line of numbers added.
- "You already know the feeling" cut.
- An early "nothing wanted from you" line.
- "Welcome" and "not on your own" removed. The love line is grounded with "I don't know you. I don't need anything from you."
- A "not here to convert anyone" line straight after faith appears.
- Men (*The Silence*) and people who've caused harm (*The Repair*) added to the list.
- The women's line keeps the joke, with the respect in the same breath.
