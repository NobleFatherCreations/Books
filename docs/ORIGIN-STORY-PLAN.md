# "Who made this": the origin story plan and draft

Written 2026-11-23. **Owner's brief:**
- One person in a real situation with high stakes wrote the first book to get through it safely.
- The other books came from wanting no one else to face that without a map.
- It is a work of love, given away free, built every day for almost two years.
- The credential is a lifetime of living through this and healing from it.
- It must earn trust through honesty.

**Status:** draft for the owner to edit. Nothing is published.

---

## 1. What a reader needs to know (in the order they'll wonder)
1. **Who made this?** A real person, named.
2. **Why does it exist?** A real situation, not a business plan.
3. **Can I trust it?** What it rests on, and what it isn't.
4. **What do they want from me?** Nothing. That has to be visibly true.
5. **Is this for me?** If you're here, you're in the right place.

The page answers all five in that order. Each answer is something a reader can check for themselves.

## 2. The line we don't cross
These books teach people to recognise manipulation, so the page can't use any of it. A reader who has just learned the tactics will spot them instantly, and the trust is gone. Every lever below is a *true* signal, made easy to see.

**Never used:**
- urgency ("before it's too late")
- guilt ("if this helped, you owe…")
- invented testimonials or numbers
- inflated credentials
- graphic detail used to win sympathy
- a donation or email ask in the story
- pseudo-intimacy ("you and I are the same")
- telling the reader what they feel

## 3. The trust levers, all honest, each with how the page uses it
| Lever (what the research says builds trust) | How the page uses it |
|---|---|
| **Costly signal:** effort that only makes sense if it's sincere | "Every day for almost two years." "Free, no ads, no tracking, no sign-up." All of it can be checked, and it costs you. |
| **Admitting limits** (people trust those who name their own limits) | "I'm not a therapist or a lawyer. My credential is a life spent inside this." Plus what the books are not, and where to go instead. |
| **Specific over general** | Real numbers that can be counted: 14 books, 335 tactics, 263 sources on The Long After. Never "thousands of people helped". |
| **Vulnerability with a boundary** | The *shape* of what happened ("I was in a situation where getting it wrong had real consequences"), not the details. The reader feels it's real without being used. |
| **Story arc** (stories are trusted more than claims) | No map, then building one to survive, then realising others had none, then two years of daily work, then giving it away. One person, one turn, one choice. |
| **Consistency** | The same voice as the books. The same promises the books make (no tracking, a Leave button). The page keeps the books' own rules. |
| **Transparency of method** | Link to "How this guide is made" (sources checked, labels, fair-reading sections). Say plainly what tools helped, according to the AI-disclosure decision (§9). |
| **Giving with nothing asked** | No ask anywhere on the page. The only links are to the books, help lines, and how to share. |
| **Belonging and relief** | One line for the reader in it right now: "If something feels wrong and nobody around you can name it, that's exactly who this is for." |
| **Autonomy** | "Take what helps. Leave the rest." The Leave button stays. |
| **Ease of reading** (people trust what's easy to read) | Short paragraphs, plain words, generous type, readable at 2am on a phone. |
| **Accountability** | A way to report an error, a "last updated" date, and a changelog. Someone answers for this. |

## 4. The story's shape (the full page)
1. **The moment there was no map.** One or two sentences. High stakes, no detail.
2. **What I did.** Started writing it down, to understand it and get through it safely. That became the first book.
3. **The turn.** Realising how many people are inside something like that with no words for it.
4. **The work.** Every day, almost two years. 14 books, the Decoder, the sources checked one by one.
5. **Why free.** Nobody deserves to have their mind toyed with, knowingly or not. A map shouldn't cost anything.
6. **The credential, honestly.** No degree in this. A lifetime of living through it and healing from it in every part of life. Sources wherever research exists. Limits named.
7. **What you can trust, and how to check.** Sources, method page, the privacy promises, corrections welcome.
8. **For you, right now.** Belonging, permission, and where to start (three doors).
9. **Signature.** Name, the brand mark, a date.

## 5. Three lengths, used in different places

- **Short** (homepage section and book colophons, about 90 words): see §6A.

### 6B. Full page: "Who made this, and why"
> **There was no map.**
>
> A while ago I found myself in a situation where the stakes were real: my safety, my peace, and people I love. Something was wrong, and I couldn't name it. The more I tried to explain it, the less sense I seemed to make, even to myself.
>
> So I started writing it down. Not to publish anything, just to see it clearly. I wrote down what was said and what was done, then what it was doing to me. I looked for the patterns and the names other people had given them. Page by page it became a guide I could hold onto, and it got me through safely. That guide was [first book].
>
> **Then I looked up.**
>
> Once I could see the patterns, I saw them everywhere: in friends' stories, in workplaces, in families, in courtrooms, in feeds. So many people were living inside something like what I'd lived through, with no words for it and no one to tell them they weren't imagining it.
>
> I didn't want anyone to have to go through that the way I did, with no map and no understanding of what was going on. So I kept building.
>
> **Every day for almost two years.**
>
> That's how long this has been my work, every single day. One book became fourteen, covering:
> - leaving and the time after
> - work
> - later life
> - festivals
> - faith
> - the bigger systems
> - kids learning to spot tricky people
>
> The Pattern Decoder grew to 335 tactics in plain words. Each has signs it fits, *and* signs it doesn't, because a label used carelessly can do harm of its own. The Long After alone lists 263 sources, checked one by one.
>
> **Why it's free.**
>
> Because nobody deserves to have their mind toyed with, whether the person doing it knows it or not. And because the people who need this most often have the least: money is controlled, time isn't theirs, phones are watched. So there's no price, no sign-up, no ads, and nothing that tracks you. There's a Leave button on every page and a one-page edition you can read offline. This is a work of love, and it's given away.
>
> **What I am, and what I'm not.**
>
> I don't have a degree in this. I'm not a therapist or a lawyer, and nothing here replaces one. What I have is a lifetime of living through manipulation and control, and healing from it, in every part of my life. That, and a stubborn need for it to mean something.
>
> Where research exists, I've used it and named it, so you can check it. Where it doesn't, the books say so. If I've got something wrong, I want to know: corrections make the books better for the next person.
>
> **If you're here right now.**
>
> If something feels wrong and nobody around you can name it, that's exactly who this is for. You don't have to read it all. Start where you are:
> - **If you're not safe right now:** [helplines]
> - **If you want to name what's happening:** [Pattern Decoder]
> - **If you've left and it hasn't stopped:** [The Long After]
>
> Take what helps. Leave the rest. Nothing here asks anything of you.

## 8. Design (no stock look, built for this page)
- **Hero:** your brand header image, full width, lettering and figure as made.
- **Name:** the extracted wordmark (`design/brand/wordmark-white.png`), never a font imitation.
- **Type:**
  - Display: **Gloock**, the face you picked for the homepage.
  - Body: **Newsreader**, a warm reading serif.
  - Small labels: **Space Mono**.
  - All self-hosted.
- **Rhythm:** each of the 8 beats in §4 is its own short section with lots of space around it. One pull line: "Nobody deserves to have their mind toyed with." No other decoration.
- **Facts strip** (honest numbers): 14 books, 335 tactics, almost 2 years, free forever.
- **The three doors** (§6B end) as large, calm cards.
- **Mechanics:**
  - Leave button, reduced-motion version, readable at 375px, AA contrast.
  - No tracking beyond the site-wide beacon. Nothing stored on the reader's device.
- **Built** by a small script (`scripts/seo/build_about.py`) that reads the text from `content/about.md`, so you can edit words without touching code. The homepage rule still applies: built by script, never hand-edited.
