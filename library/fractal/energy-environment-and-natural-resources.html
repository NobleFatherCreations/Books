The Narrative — from the inside

The earth didn't need you. That is not an insult. It is the most important fact the civilization forgot. The earth was here for four and a half billion years before you arrived. It built mountains and dissolved them. It froze entire continents and thawed them. It grew forests so vast they changed the composition of the atmosphere and then it burned them down and grew different ones. It produced every element your body is made of — the calcium in your bones was forged in a dying star, the iron in your blood was forged in another one, the water in your cells has been cycling through the planet's body since before life existed — and the earth did all of this without a single meeting, without a single quarterly report, without a single cost-benefit analysis, and without asking permission from anyone. The earth is not a resource. The earth is the body you live inside. Not metaphorically. Your body is made of the earth. Your breath is the earth's atmosphere passing through your lungs. Your food is the earth's soil converted into something your stomach can process. Your water is the earth's water, borrowed for the span of your life and returned when you're done. You are not on the earth. You are of the earth. The distinction is the most expensive confusion the civilization has ever produced. Because the moment the species decided it was on the earth rather than of it — the moment the relationship shifted from communion to extraction, from reciprocity to ownership, from belonging to dominion — the pattern entered. The same pattern. The same false god. The same cycle. But applied not to a person or an institution or a population. Applied to the living system that sustains all persons, all institutions, and all populations. The earth became the ultimate target of the cycle — the resource that could not say no, could not leave, could not file a complaint, could not hire a lawyer, could not vote, could not protest, could not tell its story in a language the civilization understood. And the civilization — the same civilization that ran the cycle on its own citizens — ran the cycle on the planet with the same precision and the same indifference and the same result: idealize the resource, extract the value, discard the husk, replace with the next deposit. And the earth absorbed it. For centuries. The way a body absorbs abuse — silently, incrementally, converting the damage into symptoms that the abuser ignores because the symptoms are slow and the profit is fast. The glacier melts a little. Stage 1: Idealize

Targeting

01 · Love Bombing

Warmth delivered faster than trust can be earned.

Individual. The energy company welcomes you with convenience — the flick of a switch, the turn of a key, the warmth that arrives without effort, the light that appears without fire — and the convenience is so total and so invisible that the energy behind it disappears, and the disappearance is the point, because a consumer who never sees the coal mine, the oil rig, the pipeline, the refinery, the power plant, the transmission line, the waste pit, or the community that lives next to any of them is a consumer who will never question what powers the light.

Institutional. The fossil fuel industry positioned itself as the engine of prosperity — "energy is freedom," "power for progress," "fueling the American dream" — and the positioning wraps the extraction of finite resources from the body of the earth in the emotional language of human advancement, which means questioning the extraction sounds like questioning advancement, and no one wants to be against progress.

Civilizational. The Industrial Revolution is idealized as humanity's greatest leap — the moment the species harnessed the earth's stored energy and converted it into material abundance at a scale never before imagined — and the idealizing is so deep and so culturally reinforced that questioning whether the leap has landed well sounds like nostalgia for the cave, when what it actually is, is a question about whether a species that burns its own nest can continue to call the burning intelligent.

02 · Weaponized Generosity

A gift you can’t refuse, recorded on a ledger you never signed.

Individual. Cheap energy is the most effective form of weaponized generosity in this sector — gasoline priced below its actual cost because the externalities are subsidized, electricity priced below its environmental damage because the damage is deferred, heating priced below its atmospheric impact because the atmosphere can't send a bill — and the cheapness creates a dependency so total that the population cannot imagine life without the energy and therefore cannot challenge the system that provides it.

Institutional. Fossil fuel companies donate to communities, fund schools, sponsor sports teams, and provide charitable giving in the regions where they extract — and the generosity is genuine enough to generate goodwill and strategic enough to purchase silence, because the community whose school was funded by the drilling company is the community that cannot oppose the drilling without defunding the school.

Civilizational. The entire infrastructure of modern life — the roads, the grids, the supply chains, the communication networks, the heating systems, the cooling systems — was built on the generosity of cheap fossil energy, and the generosity created a dependency so structural that the civilization cannot transition away from fossil fuels without rebuilding the infrastructure, and the rebuilding would require a commitment of resources that the civilization has never made for anything except war.

Stage 2: Hook

Entrapment through investment

03 · Future Faking

A future vivid enough to keep you waiting, designed never to arrive.

Individual. "Clean coal" is a future fake — a phrase that describes a technology that has never been deployed at commercial scale, cited for decades as the reason coal can continue to be burned, and the phrase exists not as a technology but as a delay tactic, a promise of a future that justifies the continuation of the present.

Institutional. The fossil promise of a future that justifies the continuation of the present. The fossil fuel industry promises "net zero by 2050" — a target that is far enough away to defer action, vague enough to avoid accountability, and dependent on carbon capture technologies that do not yet exist at the scale required — and the promise functions as a permission slip for continued extraction.

Civilizational. "Technology will save us" is the energy sector's civilizational future fake — the belief that the same species that created the problem will innovate its way out of the problem without changing the behavior that created it, and the belief has been sustained for fifty years during which the problem has worsened because the belief was being used as a substitute for the behavioral change it was supposed to precede.

04 · Hoovering

The pull back in — nostalgia, guilt, and manufactured urgency about what you’ll lose.

Individual. The gas price drops and the consumer who was considering an electric vehicle returns to the pump — the price was the hoover, the savings was the hook, and the return to fossil fuel consumption was the industry's recapture of a customer who was about to leave.

Institutional. Every time a nation moves toward renewable energy policy, the fossil fuel industry re-engages with lobbying, campaign contributions, and economic threat narratives — "energy prices will spike," "jobs will be lost," "reliability will suffer" — and the re-engagement pulls the policy back toward the extraction.

Civilizational. Every global climate agreement is followed by a period of re-engagement with fossil fuels — the Paris Agreement was followed by continued growth in fossil fuel investment, COP meetings are sponsored by fossil fuel companies, and the cycle of commitment and withdrawal is the civilizational hoover.

Stage 3: Devalue

Transfer of authority over your worth

05 · Devaluation

The praise that hooked you, slowly converted into the criticism that keeps you striving.

Individual. The person who conserves energy, who drives less, who turns down the heat, who chooses the bicycle over the car — is socially devalued as eccentric, impractical, or virtue-signaling, and the devaluation ensures that the behavior that would reduce extraction is the behavior that carries a social cost.

Institutional. Renewable energy companies and clean technology innovators are devalued by an investment landscape that still favors fossil fuel returns — the venture capital that funds a new oil extraction technique flows faster than the capital that funds a new solar storage system, because the extraction has a proven revenue model and the alternative doesn't have decades of lobbying infrastructure supporting it.

Civilizational. Indigenous communities whose relationship with the land represents the original frequency — reciprocal, regenerative, sustainable over millennia — are systematically devalued as primitive, as standing in the way of progress, as obstacles to development, and the devaluation ensures that the only model of human-earth relationship the civilization takes seriously is the model that is destroying both.

06 · Gaslighting

Making you doubt your own memory until the system becomes your only source of truth.

Individual. The energy company says "we're committed to sustainability" while investing ninety-five percent of its capital expenditure in new fossil fuel development — and the sustainability commitment exists in the annual report and in the advertising and in the press release and nowhere in the actual budget allocation, and the gap between the stated commitment and the actual investment is the gaslighting.

Institutional. The fossil fuel industry funded climate denial for half a century — not because its own scientists didn't know the climate was changing, they knew by the 1970s, but because acknowledging the change would threaten the revenue — and the denial was not a mistake or an oversight but a deliberate, documented, strategic disinformation campaign that delayed global action by decades, during which the warming accelerated, and the delay was profitable, and the profit funded more denial.

Civilizational. The narrative that "the science is uncertain" was the most effective gaslighting campaign in modern environmental history — manufactured by the same public relations firms that manufactured doubt about tobacco's link to cancer, using the same playbook, funded by the same class of actors — and the campaign worked, not because the science was actually uncertain but because the appearance of uncertainty was sufficient to justify inaction, and the inaction was sufficient to protect the revenue, and the revenue was sufficient to fund the next cycle of doubt.

07 · Double Bind

Every available choice routed to the same punishment.

Individual. The consumer who wants to reduce their carbon footprint faces a structural double bind — drive the car and contribute to climate change, don't drive the car and lose the job because there's no public transit — and the bind was engineered by the same industries that dismantled public transit, lobbied against fuel efficiency standards, and designed the suburbs around the automobile.

Institutional. Developing nations face a devastating double bind — industrialize using cheap fossil fuels and accelerate the warming, or refuse to industrialize and remain in poverty while the nations that already industrialized using fossil fuels lecture them about clean energy — and the hypocrisy is the bind.

Civilizational. The entire population faces a civilizational double bind with energy — continue using fossil fuels and guarantee climate catastrophe, or transition to renewables and face the economic disruption that the fossil fuel industry has spent decades ensuring would be as painful as possible by preventing the transition infrastructure from being built — and the bind was designed by the entities that benefit from both horns of it.

Stage 4: Confuse

Destruction of your inner compass

08 · Intermittent Reinforcement

Reward and punishment on an unpredictable schedule — the chemistry of addiction.

Individual. The weather cooperates and then it doesn't — the summer is mild and you forget the crisis, the winter is brutal and you remember it, the spring is beautiful and you forget again — and the intermittent experience of climate impacts prevents the sustained urgency that action requires.

Institutional. Environmental regulation is enacted and then rolled back, funded and then defunded, enforced and then ignored — the inconsistency of governance producing the same learned helplessness that intermittent reinforcement always produces.

Civilizational. The planet delivers its signals intermittently — a devastating hurricane followed by a calm season, a record-breaking fire followed by a wet year, a drought followed by a flood — and the intermittency prevents the population from maintaining the alarm that the data justifies, because the data says the trend is catastrophic but the experience says some days are fine, and the fine days are the reinforcement that keeps the population disengaged.

09 · Moving the Goalposts

A finish line that moves the instant you reach it, because it doesn’t exist.

Individual. The personal actions that were supposed to matter keep being revised — recycle, then recycle better, then actually recycling doesn't matter because the system doesn't actually recycle, then compost, then reduce, then offset — and the goalposts move in the direction of individual responsibility while the systemic responsibility never arrives.

Institutional. The emission reduction targets are revised with each failure — the target that was supposed to be met in 2020 was moved to 2030, the 2030 target will be moved to 2040 — and the moving of the goalpost is presented as updated ambition rather than compounded failure.

Civilizational. The temperature target has moved — two degrees was the limit, then 1.5 was the aspiration, and now 1.5 appears unachievable and two degrees appears optimistic and three degrees is being modeled and four degrees is being discussed — and the goalpost has moved in the direction of catastrophe because the entities that benefit from the extraction controlled the speed at which the goalpost could be approached.

10 · Strategic Ambiguity

Language engineered to mean whatever the listener needs it to mean.

Individual. The product label says "sustainably sourced" — a phrase that has no standardized definition, no regulatory enforcement, and no verification mechanism, and that means whatever the company needs it to mean on the day the consumer reads it.

Institutional. Corporate environmental commitments use language like "aspire to," "work toward," "in line with" — phrases that gesture toward action without committing to it, and the ambiguity is the legal strategy.

Civilizational. "Clean energy transition" is the most strategically ambiguous phrase in energy policy — it sounds like a plan and contains no timeline, no mechanism, no funding source, and no accountability structure — and the phrase has been repeated at every international climate conference for thirty years while global fossil fuel consumption has increased.

11 · Projection

The system’s own behavior, pinned to your chest.

Individual. The corporation that produces more carbon in a single day than the individual will produce in a lifetime tells the individual to "reduce your carbon footprint" — the phrase "carbon footprint" was literally invented by BP's advertising agency to shift responsibility from the producer to the consumer, and the shift is the projection.

Institutional. The fossil fuel industry that funded climate denial for decades now blames governments for not acting sooner — "we need better policy, we need a level playing field, we support sensible regulation" — the industry that spent billions preventing the regulation now projects the responsibility for the absence of regulation onto the government it captured.

Civilizational. The industrialized nations that produced the majority of historical carbon emissions project the responsibility for climate action onto developing nations — "China and India need to reduce their emissions" — while the historical emissions that caused the warming were produced by the nations now demanding that others solve the problem, and the projection converts the consequence of Western industrialization into the obligation of the Global South.

12 · DARVO

Deny it happened, attack the one who named it, swap victim and offender.

Individual. The activist who blocks a pipeline is arrested for trespassing — the person who is trying to protect the water supply is charged with a crime while the corporation that is threatening the water supply is protected by the law, and the protector is prosecuted and the polluter is permitted.

Institutional. Indigenous communities that assert treaty rights to protect their land from extraction are characterized as obstructing economic development — the communities whose land was stolen and whose treaties were violated are reframed as the obstacle to the progress that is being built on their land without their consent.

Civilizational. Scientists who warn about climate change are accused of being alarmist, politically motivated, and enemies of the economy — the people who are trying to save the civilization are characterized as the people who are threatening it, and the industry that is actually threatening it is characterized as the engine that sustains it.

13 · Normalization

Harm raised in increments too small to trigger alarm, until it’s the background hum.

Individual. The first time you saw a photograph of an oil-soaked pelican it broke something in you — the image was intolerable, the suffering was visible, the violation was clear — and by the hundredth oil spill the image generates a share rather than a scream, and the normalization of ecological destruction is the energy sector's most important desensitization.

Institutional. Environmental damage that would have caused riots a generation ago now generates press releases — "the company is cooperating with cleanup efforts," "we take our environmental responsibility seriously" — and the language of responsibility has been so normalized that the population accepts the statement as the response rather than demanding the accountability the statement was designed to replace.

Civilizational. The normalization of climate change itself is the sector's defining desensitization — the phrase "the new normal" applied to temperatures, storms, fires, and floods that are not normal at all, that are the symptoms of a planetary system being driven past its operating parameters by a civilization that has normalized the driving.

Stage 5: Isolate

Elimination of comparison

14 · Isolation

Cutting the lines to everyone who could tell you this isn’t normal.

Individual. The consumer is isolated from the source of their energy — the coal plant is far away, the pipeline is underground, the oil rig is offshore, the refinery is in someone else's neighborhood — and the isolation is geographic and perceptual, because a person who never sees the extraction cannot feel the extraction, and the inability to feel is what makes the continuation possible.

Institutional. Environmental science has been isolated from policy-making — the scientists produce the data, the politicians receive the data, and the space between the production and the reception is filled with lobbyists who interpret the data for the politicians, and the interpretation serves the lobbyist's client rather than the planet's future.

Civilizational. The human species has been isolated from the biosphere — living inside climate-controlled buildings, eating food from supply chains, drinking water from treatment plants — and the insulation from the natural world is so complete that the average person experiences the environment not as a living system they depend on but as a backdrop they drive past, and the disconnection is the isolation, and the isolation is what allows the destruction to continue without producing the grief that would stop it.

15 · Triangulation

A third party inserted to carry the message the system wanted carried.

Individual. The energy industry triangulates between the consumer and the environmentalist — "they want to take away your car," "they want to raise your energy prices," "they want you to live in the dark" — and the triangulation converts the person who is trying to protect the consumer's future into the consumer's enemy, and the conversion ensures that the consumer's anger is directed at the advocate rather than at the industry.

Institutional. Fossil fuel companies play nations against each other — "if we can't extract here, we'll extract there" — the threat of capital flight used as leverage against environmental regulation, ensuring that the nation that regulates loses the revenue and the nation that doesn't gains it, and the competition between nations becomes the race to the bottom that the industry designed.

Civilizational. The energy industry triangulates between the present and the future — "we need this energy now, we'll figure out clean energy later" — and the triangulation positions the present as a crisis that justifies the extraction and the future as a someday that never arrives, and the gap between the present's urgency and the future's deferral is where the profit lives.

Stage 6: Extract

Harvesting

16 · Flying Monkeys

Recruiting other people to enforce the system while it keeps clean hands.

Individual. Your neighbor who works at the refinery defends the industry — "it puts food on my table, it's not perfect but what is?" — and the defense is sincere and the sincerity is the flying monkey's power, because the person whose livelihood depends on the extraction is the person most invested in defending it.

Institutional. Think tanks funded by fossil fuel companies produce research that supports fossil fuel interests — the Heritage Foundation, the Heartland Institute, the Global Climate Coalition — and the research is cited by politicians, published by media outlets, and consumed by the public as independent analysis when it is industry product.

Civilizational. Petrostate governments function as the fossil fuel industry's flying monkeys — entire nations whose economies depend on oil and gas revenue defend the industry in international forums, block climate agreements, and use their geopolitical influence to prevent the transition that would reduce their revenue.

17 · Smear Campaign

Poisoning your reputation in advance, so your testimony arrives pre-discredited.

Individual. The person who installs solar panels is called naive — "you'll never recoup the investment," "the grid is more reliable," "you're just trying to be trendy" — and the smearing of the individual who is modeling the alternative protects the system that the alternative threatens.

Institutional. Climate scientists are smeared with precision — their emails hacked and selectively quoted, their funding scrutinized, their motives questioned, their competence challenged — and the smearing is funded by the industry that the scientists' findings threaten.

Civilizational. The environmental movement itself is smeared as anti-human — "they care more about trees than people," "they want to destroy the economy," "they're extremists" — and the smearing ensures that the population's instinct to protect the planet is redirected toward protecting the economy that is destroying it.

18 · Silent Treatment

Silence weaponized — the refusal to engage, dressed as discipline.

Individual. The energy company that contaminated the aquifer does not respond to the community's complaints — the complaint is filed, the number is given, the hold music plays, the transfer connects to voicemail, and the voicemail is never returned, and the silence is the response.

Institutional. Requests for disclosure — what chemicals are in the fracking fluid, what the emissions data shows, what the internal memos say about climate risk — are met with claims of proprietary information, trade secrets, and competitive advantage, and the stonewalling is legal because the regulations that would require disclosure were prevented by the lobbying that the revenue funded.

Civilizational. The silence around the connection between the fossil fuel industry and climate change was maintained for decades — the industry knew, the industry concealed, the industry funded the concealment, and the silence was the most profitable silence in the history of the species, and the profit was measured in trillions and the cost will be measured in everything else.

19 · Manufactured Consent

A vote whose outcome was decided before the question was asked.

Individual. You "consent" to the energy system by using electricity — the consent manufactured by the absence of an alternative, because the grid is the only grid and the energy on the grid is the energy the utility decided to purchase and the utility's decision was shaped by the regulatory framework that the energy industry lobbied into existence.

Institutional. Environmental impact assessments manufacture the appearance of scientific rigor — the assessment conducted by a firm hired by the company seeking the permit, the assessment finding that the impact is manageable, and the permit granted on the basis of the assessment the applicant funded.

Civilizational. Public consent for the fossil fuel economy was manufactured over a century through the systematic dismantling of alternatives — public transit defunded, renewable research underfunded, nuclear energy stigmatized, and the population left with no choice but the choice the industry provided, and the choosing of the only available option was recorded as preference.

20 · Trauma Bonding

Pain and relief alternating until the source of the wound becomes the only cure.

Individual. The community that lives next to the refinery is bonded to the refinery through dependency — the refinery provides the jobs that the community needs and the pollution that the community endures, and the bond is maintained by the dependency, because the community that depends on the refinery for survival cannot challenge the refinery without threatening its own survival.

Institutional. Entire nations are bonded to fossil fuel economies through resource dependency — the petrostates whose identity, revenue, and geopolitical power are derived from oil and gas, the nations that cannot imagine themselves without the resource, the bond between the nation and the extraction maintained by the absence of any other identity the nation has been allowed to develop.

Civilizational. The entire global economy is trauma-bonded to fossil fuels — the energy that powers the extraction is the energy that powers the civilization, and the civilization cannot stop the extraction without stopping itself, and the inability to stop is the bond, and the bond is maintained by the infrastructure that was built around the extraction and that now requires the extraction to continue in order to function.

21 · Learned Helplessness

Punishing every attempt to act until the body stops trying and calls it peace.

Individual. "I'm just one person, what can I do?" is the learned helplessness that the energy industry has installed in the population — the belief that individual action is meaningless against systemic extraction, spoken by people who were never told that their helplessness was designed by the system that benefits from it.

Institutional. Environmental organizations that have been fighting the industry for decades with incrementally decreasing success have developed a professional helplessness — "we can't beat the fossil fuel lobby," "the political will isn't there," "we have to be pragmatic" — and the pragmatism is the helplessness wearing a professional suit.

Civilizational. "It's too late" is the civilizational learned helplessness that the fossil fuel industry has been working toward for fifty years — because a population that believes it is too late will not demand the changes that would reduce the industry's revenue, and the belief that it is too late is the industry's most valuable product after the fuel itself.

22 · Benevolent Control

Restriction, surveillance, and command — all framed as care you’d be ungrateful to refuse.

Individual. The utility company says "we're providing reliable, affordable energy to keep your family safe and comfortable" — and the provision is real and the framing converts a monopoly into a service, and the service cannot be refused because the monopoly has no competitor.

Institutional. Environmental regulation is framed as the government protecting the public — and much of it does protect — but the regulatory agencies are also the gatekeepers that determine which energy sources are allowed, and the determination is influenced by the industry being regulated, and the protection and the capture exist in the same agency.

Civilizational. The energy industry frames its dominance as civilization's essential infrastructure — "without us, the lights go off, the hospitals close, the economy stops" — and the framing is accurate enough to be terrifying and strategic enough to prevent the transition, because the industry that controls the infrastructure controls the narrative about whether the infrastructure can be changed, and the answer is always "not yet."

23 · Infantilization

Keeping you perpetually ‘not ready’ so your own discernment is never trusted.

Individual. The consumer is treated as incapable of understanding energy policy — "it's too complex," "leave it to the experts," "you don't understand how the grid works" — and the infantilization ensures that the population that pays the bill and breathes the air is excluded from the decisions about what generates the energy that produces the bill and the air.

Institutional. Developing nations are infantilized by the international energy community — "they need our guidance to develop responsibly," "they can't manage a clean energy transition without our technology" — the same paternalism that characterized colonialism now characterizing the energy transition, the formerly colonized nations told they cannot power their own development without the permission and the products of the nations that industrialized using the cheapest and dirtiest energy available.

Civilizational. The population is infantilized by the very scale of the problem — "climate change is so big that only governments and corporations can address it" — and the framing removes the citizen from the response, which is precisely where the fossil fuel industry needs the citizen to be: disempowered, dependent, and waiting for someone else to act.

24 · Identity Erosion

Replacing who you were with a system-approved version of who you should be.

Individual. The human relationship with the natural world has been eroded to the point where "nature" is something you visit on vacation rather than something you are — the parks are the nature, the rest is infrastructure, and the distinction ensures that the destruction of the non-park landscape generates no grief because it was never experienced as nature in the first place.

Institutional. Communities whose identity was formed by their relationship with the land — fishing communities, farming communities, ranching communities, indigenous communities — have had that identity eroded by extractive industries that changed the land, contaminated the water, altered the climate, and eliminated the species that the identity was built around.

Civilizational. The human identity as an animal, as a member of the biosphere, as a creature dependent on the health of the living system — that identity has been so thoroughly eroded that the statement "you are an animal" is received as an insult rather than as a fact, and the erasure of the animal identity is what allows the species to destroy the biosphere without experiencing it as self-harm.

25 · Spiritual Bypassing

Money and debt repurposed as a leash that makes leaving unaffordable.

Individual. "The earth will heal itself" bypasses the urgency of the crisis — a statement that may be true on a geological timescale and that is meaningless on a human one, because the earth may heal in ten thousand years but the civilization will collapse in the next hundred if the trajectory continues.

Institutional. "We're stewards of the earth" is the energy industry's spiritual bypass — the language of stewardship applied to an industry that has extracted, contaminated, and destabilized more of the earth's systems than any other human activity, and the stewardship is the framing that prevents the activity from being called what it is.

Civilizational. "God gave us dominion over the earth" is the civilizational spiritual bypass that underwrites the extraction — the theological mandate to subdue and have dominion reinterpreted as permission to extract without limit, and the interpretation persists because the interpretation serves the economy, and the economy funds the institutions that teach the interpretation.

26 · Financial Control

Spiritual or therapeutic language used to bypass your pain and dodge accountability.

Individual. The energy bill is a non-negotiable monthly extraction — the price set by a utility you did not choose, for a service you cannot refuse, at a rate determined by a regulatory process you were not invited to participate in — and the bill arrives whether or not you approve of how the energy was generated.

Institutional. The fossil fuel industry receives more government subsidy than any other industry on earth — $7 trillion annually according to the IMF when accounting for implicit subsidies — and the subsidy is public money used to make the extraction cheaper, which makes the alternative more expensive by comparison, which makes the transition harder, which is the purpose of the subsidy.

Civilizational. The global financial system is entangled with the fossil fuel economy to the point where a rapid transition would destabilize the banking system — fossil fuel assets on balance sheets, pension funds invested in oil and gas, sovereign wealth built on petroleum revenue — and the entanglement is the financial control, because a civilization whose retirement funds depend on the continued burning of fossil fuels is a civilization that has a financial incentive to continue the burning even as the burning destroys the climate that the retirees will live in.

Stage 7: Discard

Disposal

27 · Manufactured Crisis

A permanent emergency — too urgent for you to stop, think, and ask if it’s true.

Individual. The "energy crisis" is manufactured when prices spike — the spike caused by supply manipulation, by refinery shutdowns, by geopolitical maneuvering — and the crisis is amplified to justify expanded drilling, relaxed regulation, and the deferral of the clean energy transition.

Institutional. "Energy security" is the manufactured crisis that justifies domestic extraction — the fear that dependence on foreign energy makes the nation vulnerable, used to justify fracking, offshore drilling, and pipeline construction that serves the industry's expansion more than the nation's security.

Civilizational. The climate crisis itself is being leveraged as a manufactured opportunity — carbon markets that allow corporations to continue polluting while purchasing offsets, green bonds that fund projects of questionable impact, "net zero" commitments that defer action while generating positive press — and the crisis that should produce transformation is instead producing a new revenue stream for the same entities that caused it.

28 · Discard

Released the moment maintaining you costs more than you produce.

Individual. The community that hosted the extraction is discarded when the resource runs out — the boom town becomes the ghost town, the mine closes, the refinery shuts down, and the workers who gave their bodies to the extraction are left with the contaminated water, the elevated cancer rates, and the unemployment, and the company that profited has moved to the next deposit.

Institutional. Entire ecosystems are discarded — the forest logged and left, the mountaintop removed and left, the ocean floor dredged and left, the aquifer drained and left — and the leaving is performed with the same administrative indifference the pattern applies in every sector.

Civilizational. The earth itself is being discarded by a civilization that has begun planning for Mars — the billionaires who profited most from the extraction now funding space programs that position other planets as the replacement for the one they helped destroy, and the replacement is the final stage of the cycle applied at planetary scale.

Stage 8: Replace

Proof

29 · Replacement

Your seat filled before it cools — proof the warmth was for the position, not for you.

Individual. One energy source replaces another without changing the extraction model — coal replaced by natural gas, gas supplemented by fracking, fracking supplemented by deep-water drilling — each replacement presented as cleaner, each one extracting from a different part of the same earth, none of them changing the fundamental relationship between the civilization and the planet.

Institutional.
