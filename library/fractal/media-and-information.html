The Narrative — from the inside

You open the app because you're bored. Or lonely. Or because the silence in the room got too loud and you needed something to fill it. And the screen lights up and there it is — a feed built just for you. Not for anyone else. For you. It knows what you like. It knows what you fear. It knows what makes you angry enough to stay and what makes you hopeful enough to scroll one more time. And you didn't ask for any of it but it arrived perfectly and you think to yourself "this is convenient" without ever asking who it's convenient for. And at first it feels like connection. Like the world is closer now. Like you matter because someone liked your post and shared your thought and a stranger in another country agreed with you at 2 a.m. and for a moment the loneliness lifted. So you come back. You come back every morning before your feet touch the floor. You come back every night after you've said goodnight to the people who actually live in your house. You come back in the bathroom and in the car and in the line at the grocery store and during conversations you used to be present for. And you don't notice when the feed changes. When the content that used to make you feel connected starts making you feel afraid. When the news that used to inform you starts inflaming you. When the algorithm that used to show you what you wanted starts showing you what keeps you watching — and those are not the same thing. And now you're angry but you don't know why. Or you're anxious but you can't point to a cause. Or you're comparing yourself to people who don't exist in the form the screen presents them. And the thing you opened to fill the silence has become louder than the silence ever was. But you can't close it. Not because you're weak. Because it was engineered by people who understand your nervous system better than you do. People who A/B tested the color of the notification dot and the timing of the push alert and the exact ratio of reward to frustration that keeps a human thumb moving across glass. And when someone says "just put the phone down" they don't understand that you're not holding the phone. The phone is holding you. It has your memories. Your contacts. Your identity. Your business. Your photos of your dead grandmother. It has everything. And that wasn't an accident. That was a strategy. Make the product hold everything so the person can never leave. And when you finally look up — hours gone, neck aching, eyes dry, heart racing from something you read that may or may not be true — you realize you have been sitting in a room full of people you love and you have no idea what any of them said. And the app doesn't care. It got what it came for. Your attention. Your time. Your data. Your emotional state mapped and packaged and sold to someone whose name you will never know for a purpose you will never be told. And tomorrow morning before your feet touch the floor, you will open it again. Because the alternative is the silence. And you forgot how to sit in that.

Stage 1: Idealize

Targeting

01 · Love Bombing

Warmth delivered faster than trust can be earned.

Individual. A journalist flatters a source to build trust and gain access — warmth deployed as an extraction tool, intimacy manufactured for information.

Institutional. New platforms launch with utopian promises — connecting the world, democratizing information, giving everyone a voice, leveling the playing field — language that sounds like liberation and functions as onboarding.

Civilizational. Mass media positions itself as the sacred guardian of truth and democracy — the idealized fourth estate, the noble watchdog, the institution without which civilization would collapse — and the population grants it authority based on what it claims to be rather than what it has proven itself to be.

02 · Weaponized Generosity

A gift you can’t refuse, recorded on a ledger you never signed.

Individual. The platform gives you everything for free — free storage, free communication, free entertainment, free tools, free connection — and the generosity is so total and so frictionless that you never pause to calculate what you're paying with, because you're paying with attention and data and behavioral patterns and emotional states and the ability to sit in a quiet room without reaching for a screen, and the payment is invisible because the currency is invisible, and the currency is invisible because the platform designed it that way.

Institutional. Media outlets offer free content — free news, free analysis, free opinion, free streaming — and the generosity builds a dependency that makes the audience unable to function without the feed, and the dependency is the product, because an audience that depends on you for its understanding of reality is an audience that cannot challenge your version of it.

Civilizational. The entire internet was positioned as a gift to humanity — free information, free connection, free expression, free access to the sum of human knowledge — and the gift was real and the gift was also the most sophisticated onboarding strategy ever deployed, because a civilization that moved its communication, its commerce, its relationships, its politics, and its identity onto a platform it doesn't own has given the platform something it can never get back, and the platform knows this, and the platform's generosity was never about giving, it was about making the taking invisible.

Stage 2: Hook

Entrapment through investment

03 · Future Faking

A future vivid enough to keep you waiting, designed never to arrive.

Individual. A paywall promises "subscribe and you'll get the full story" — and the full story either never arrives or turns out to be the same surface-level analysis with a premium price tag.

Institutional. Platforms promise fair creator monetization that never materializes — the terms change, the algorithm shifts, the revenue share shrinks, and the promise of a sustainable living remains permanently around the next corner.

Civilizational. Media has promised an informed citizenry for over a century while consistently delivering entertainment disguised as journalism, outrage disguised as analysis, and spectacle disguised as truth — the promise of an enlightened public renewed every generation and fulfilled in none of them.

04 · Hoovering

The pull back in — nostalgia, guilt, and manufactured urgency about what you’ll lose.

Individual. The notification reads "you have 47 unread messages" or "your friend just posted for the first time in a while" — emotional hooks designed to pull you back into the feed after you tried to leave.

Institutional. Outrage cycles are engineered to re-engage audiences who attempted to disconnect — the story is so alarming, so infuriating, so personally relevant that stepping away feels irresponsible.

Civilizational. Every generation personally relevant that stepping away feels irresponsible. Every generation is promised "this is the platform that will finally get it right" — this is the technology that will fix the problems the last technology created — and the population re-enters the attention economy with renewed faith that this time it will be different.

Stage 3: Devalue

Transfer of authority over your worth

05 · Devaluation

The praise that hooked you, slowly converted into the criticism that keeps you striving.

Individual. The source is discarded the moment their usefulness expires — calls stop being returned, access is revoked, the person who was courted for information is abandoned once the story is filed.

Institutional. Independent journalists and small outlets are labeled as unreliable, partisan, and dangerous — their credibility attacked not because their reporting is inaccurate but because their existence threatens the monopoly on narrative.

Civilizational. Public trust itself is systematically eroded — citizens told they cannot interpret reality without institutional guidance, that their own perception is unreliable, that without an approved mediator between them and the world they will be lost in misinformation, which is another way of saying they will be free.

06 · Gaslighting

Making you doubt your own memory until the system becomes your only source of truth.

Individual. A quote is taken out of context and when the subject protests they are told they are overreacting — the distortion is presented as the truth and the truth is presented as defensiveness.

Institutional. Events witnessed by millions are reframed within hours — "what you saw isn't what happened," official narratives override collective experience, and the population learns to distrust its own eyes.

Civilizational. Decades of manufactured consensus replace organic understanding — populations are trained through repetition to accept narratives that contradict their lived experience, and the gap between what they feel and what they are told becomes so normalized that they stop noticing it entirely.

07 · Double Bind

Every available choice routed to the same punishment.

Individual. The user is told to "protect your privacy" by the same platform that requires your data to function — opt out and lose the service, opt in and lose the privacy — and the choice is presented as freedom when both options serve the platform and neither option serves you.

Institutional. Journalists face a structural double bind — publish what's true and risk losing access to the sources that provide the truth, maintain access by softening the truth and become complicit in the distortion — and the bind ensures that the reporting that reaches the public is always filtered through the journalist's calculation of what they can afford to say, which is never the same as what they know.

Civilizational. The entire information ecosystem places the population in a double bind — engage with media and be manipulated, disengage from media and be uninformed — and neither position produces a citizen capable of genuine self-governance, which is the point, because a population that is either manipulated or ignorant is a population that can be managed, and management is what the information system was built to enable.

Stage 4: Confuse

Destruction of your inner compass

08 · Intermittent Reinforcement

Reward and punishment on an unpredictable schedule — the chemistry of addiction.

Individual. Notification systems deliver dopamine on an unpredictable schedule — sometimes the red dot brings love, sometimes it brings nothing, sometimes it brings conflict — and the inconsistency is precisely what makes it impossible to ignore.

Institutional. News cycles alternate between terror and relief — catastrophe followed by heartwarming stories, crisis followed by distraction — a rhythm that keeps the nervous system in a state of perpetual dysregulation disguised as staying informed.

Civilizational. Decades of "things are getting better" alternate with decades of "everything is falling apart" — hope and fear traded back and forth across generations in a pattern designed to prevent the population from ever reaching the emotional equilibrium required to see clearly.

09 · Moving the Goalposts

A finish line that moves the instant you reach it, because it doesn’t exist.

Individual. Algorithm changes force creators into constant adaptation — the rules for visibility shift without warning, what worked yesterday fails today, and the platform offers no explanation because the instability is the strategy.

Institutional. Community guidelines are rewritten to justify retroactive enforcement — content that was compliant when posted becomes a violation when the policy changes, and the creator is punished for following rules that no longer exist.

Civilizational. The definition of truth itself shifts based on institutional convenience — what is misinformation today was accepted science yesterday, what is fact today was conspiracy last year, and the population is expected to update its understanding on command without ever questioning who is commanding the update or why.

10 · Strategic Ambiguity

Language engineered to mean whatever the listener needs it to mean.

Individual. The platform's community guidelines are written in language broad enough to justify any enforcement action — "harmful content," "misleading information," "inauthentic behavior" — terms that sound specific but are defined by the platform in real time based on criteria that are never published, which means the creator cannot know whether their content complies because compliance is determined after publication by an entity that benefits from the uncertainty.

Institutional. Editorial standards at major outlets use phrases like "sources say," "officials familiar with the matter," and "according to people who spoke on condition of anonymity" — language that sounds like transparency but functions as a shield, because the source cannot be verified, the claim cannot be challenged, and the outlet's credibility becomes the only evidence offered.

Civilizational. The phrase "misinformation" has become the most strategically ambiguous term in the modern information landscape — applied to everything from demonstrable falsehood to inconvenient truth to premature accuracy to unsanctioned opinion — and the ambiguity of the term is its power, because a label that can mean anything can be applied to anyone, and a population that accepts the label without demanding a definition has given the labeler the authority to decide what is true.

11 · Projection

The system’s own behavior, pinned to your chest.

Individual. Outlets actively spreading misinformation accuse independent voices of spreading misinformation — the label deployed not as description but as weapon, and whoever uses it first wins.

Institutional. Platforms that harvest user data at unprecedented scale accuse users of not protecting their own privacy — the entity that built the surveillance system blames the people inside it for being surveilled.

Civilizational. Propaganda machines with decades of documented manipulation label independent thought as propaganda — the word itself colonized by the very thing it was invented to describe.

12 · DARVO

Deny it happened, attack the one who named it, swap victim and offender.

Individual. A journalist exposed for fabrication attacks the credibility of the person who caught them — the correction becomes the controversy and the lie disappears behind the fight.

Institutional. Networks caught broadcasting false information sue the fact-checkers who identified the falsehood — the entity that lied becomes the plaintiff and the entity that told the truth becomes the defendant.

Civilizational. Institutions that manufactured consent for wars, normalized surveillance, and suppressed inconvenient truths for decades claim to be the victims of public distrust — as if the distrust is an attack rather than a natural consequence of being lied to repeatedly.

13 · Normalization

Harm raised in increments too small to trigger alarm, until it’s the background hum.

Individual. The first time you saw a violent image in your feed it disturbed you — your body contracted, your breath changed, your nervous system registered threat — but the algorithm showed you another one and another one and by the hundredth exposure the contraction stopped, not because you became stronger but because your threshold was raised, and the raising was not resilience but damage, the body's last resort when it cannot escape a stimulus it cannot process.

Institutional. The normalization of surveillance, data harvesting, and algorithmic manipulation has been so gradual and so consistent that practices that would have triggered mass protest twenty years ago are now accepted as the cost of using a search engine — the population surrendered its privacy not in a single dramatic moment but in a million small terms-of-service agreements that each took one more inch of territory.

Civilizational. The normalization of propaganda itself is the media system's final achievement — the population knows it is being manipulated, discusses the manipulation openly, shares articles about the manipulation, and then opens the app and consumes the manipulation anyway, because the knowledge of the pattern no longer produces the behavioral change the knowledge should produce, and the gap between knowing and acting is where normalization lives.

Stage 5: Isolate

Elimination of comparison

14 · Isolation

Cutting the lines to everyone who could tell you this isn’t normal.

Individual. Algorithmic echo chambers ensure that each person occupies a personalized reality — you see what confirms your beliefs, you never encounter genuine disagreement, and you mistake your feed for the world.

Institutional. Deplatforming and shadow-banning isolate dissenting voices from their audiences — the person still speaks but nobody can hear them, which is a silence more complete than censorship because the speaker doesn't even know they've been muted.

Civilizational. Information silos create parallel civilizations occupying the same geography but inhabiting completely different realities — populations that cannot communicate across ideological lines because they no longer share a common set of facts, or even a common understanding of what a fact is.

15 · Triangulation

A third party inserted to carry the message the system wanted carried.

Individual. A journalist plays two sources against each other for a better story — each one told what the other said, each one provoked into revealing more than they intended, and the journalist positioned between them as the neutral observer when they are in fact the architect of the conflict.

Institutional. Networks pit left against right while corporate ownership profits from both — the argument is the product, the division is the revenue model, and the resolution of the argument would be the end of the business.

Civilizational. Global media empires owned by the same entities fund opposing narratives simultaneously — the illusion of ideological diversity maintained by a concentration of ownership so severe that the entire spectrum of debate occurs within a range defined by a handful of people the audience has never met.

Stage 6: Extract

Harvesting

16 · Flying Monkeys

Recruiting other people to enforce the system while it keeps clean hands.

Individual. Troll farms and coordinated inauthentic accounts are deployed to attack specific people on behalf of undisclosed interests — the harassment appears organic, the fingerprints are invisible, and the target has no one to confront.

Institutional. Fact-checking organizations funded by the entities they should be investigating perform credibility laundering — the check itself becomes the weapon, and the checked have no recourse because questioning a fact-checker makes you sound like you oppose facts.

Civilizational. Intelligence agencies use media outlets as distribution channels for approved narratives — the story originates in a classified briefing, passes through a trusted journalist, and arrives in public consciousness as reporting rather than placement.

17 · Smear Campaign

Poisoning your reputation in advance, so your testimony arrives pre-discredited.

Individual. Coordinated hit pieces destroy a person's reputation in twenty-four hours — multiple outlets publishing the same angle simultaneously, creating the appearance of independent corroboration when the source is singular and the timing is orchestrated.

Institutional. Entire movements are delegitimized through selective coverage and strategic framing — showing the worst elements, ignoring the core message, and repeating the most dismissive label until it becomes the only word the public associates with the movement.

Civilizational. Entire nations are demonized through decades of media portrayal — a country's culture reduced to its worst stereotypes, its people flattened into caricatures, its complexity erased so completely that when intervention comes the public feels it is justified because they have been trained to see monsters rather than humans.

18 · Silent Treatment

Silence weaponized — the refusal to engage, dressed as discipline.

Individual. Shadow-banning creates a uniquely modern silence — your voice exists, your posts are published, your account is active, but the algorithm has placed you behind a wall no one can see and your words reach no one, which means you scream into a room you believe is full but is actually empty.

Institutional. Stories that threaten power simply never get covered — there is no denial, no retraction, no counter-narrative, just absence, and the absence is so total that the event ceases to exist in public consciousness because if it wasn't reported it didn't happen.

Civilizational. Entire histories are erased through omission — generations grow up without knowing what was done in their name, without access to information that would change how they understand their own country, their own institutions, their own identity — and the silence is so vast and so normalized that most people don't even know there is something they don't know.

19 · Manufactured Consent

A vote whose outcome was decided before the question was asked.

Individual. The platform presents you with a "choice" — accept the cookies or leave the website, agree to the terms or don't use the service — and the choice is technically free but structurally coerced, because the service has made itself essential and the alternative to agreeing is exile from the infrastructure of modern life.

Institutional. Public opinion is manufactured through coordinated media saturation — the same talking point appears on six networks in the same hour, the same framing appears in twelve publications in the same day, and the repetition creates the appearance of consensus that the audience mistakes for organic agreement when it is in fact coordinated messaging.

Civilizational. Noam Chomsky named this mechanism decades ago and the naming changed nothing because the mechanism is so deeply embedded in the information architecture that recognizing it does not disable it — the population consents to wars it didn't choose, policies it didn't evaluate, and narratives it didn't verify because the consent was manufactured before the question was asked, and a population that believes it arrived at its own conclusion will defend that conclusion more fiercely than one that knows it was told what to think.

20 · Trauma Bonding

Pain and relief alternating until the source of the wound becomes the only cure.

Individual. Doomscrolling is the clearest expression of the bond — you cannot stop consuming content that harms you, the anxiety it produces becomes chemically familiar, and the platform that delivers the pain becomes the only place that feels like something.

Institutional. Audiences are bonded to specific outlets through shared outrage — the network's anger becomes your anger, their enemy becomes your enemy, and the nightly broadcast becomes a ritual of collective dysregulation that feels like community because everyone is hurting in the same direction.

Civilizational. Entire populations are addicted to the news cycle as a stress response — the nervous system has been trained to scan for threat through a screen, and the absence of information feels more dangerous than the presence of misinformation, which means the population cannot stop consuming the very thing that is destabilizing it.

21 · Learned Helplessness

Punishing every attempt to act until the body stops trying and calls it peace.

Individual. The creator who was shadow-banned, demonetized, algorithmically suppressed, and deplatformed without explanation eventually stops creating — not because they ran out of things to say but because every attempt to speak was met with an invisible consequence they could not predict, appeal, or understand, and the body learned that expression leads to punishment, and the silence that follows is not peace but surrender.

Institutional. Entire communities of independent journalists, researchers, and truth-tellers have learned that challenging mainstream narratives results in professional destruction — funding pulled, access revoked, reputation attacked, audience algorithmically removed — and the helplessness is collective and contagious, because each person who is destroyed serves as a lesson to everyone watching.

Civilizational. The phrase "you can't fight the algorithm" is the sound of civilizational learned helplessness — the collective acceptance that an invisible, unaccountable, opaque system determines what billions of people see, think, and believe, and the acceptance is not agreement but exhaustion, and the exhaustion is the product.

22 · Benevolent Control

Restriction, surveillance, and command — all framed as care you’d be ungrateful to refuse.

Individual. The platform says "we removed this content to protect you from harmful information" — and the protection sounds like care and functions as censorship, and the user who lost access to the removed content has no way to evaluate whether the removal served their safety or the platform's interest, because the content is gone and the evaluation requires the content's presence.

Institutional. "Content moderation" is the language of benevolent control — the platform positions itself as the protector of public discourse while simultaneously controlling what discourse is allowed, and the control is performed in the name of safety and the safety is defined by the controller.

Civilizational. The entire concept of "trusted sources" functions as benevolent control at scale — certain outlets are elevated as reliable and others suppressed as dangerous, certain voices are amplified as authoritative and others silenced as fringe — and the curation is presented as quality control when it is actually narrative control, and the population that accepts the curation has outsourced its discernment to an entity that has a financial interest in what the population believes.

23 · Infantilization

Keeping you perpetually ‘not ready’ so your own discernment is never trusted.

Individual. The platform assumes you cannot evaluate information for yourself — warning labels, fact-check overlays, "this content may be misleading" banners — each one positioning the platform as the adult and the user as the child who needs to be told what is safe to read, and the positioning may sometimes be appropriate and is always paternalistic, and the cumulative effect is a population that increasingly expects to be told what is true rather than developing the capacity to determine it.

Institutional. Media literacy is discussed endlessly while the structural conditions that require it are never addressed — the solution is always to educate the consumer rather than regulate the producer, which means the population is treated as the problem and the system that produces the problem is treated as the educator, and the educator has no incentive to produce a student that no longer needs its guidance.

Civilizational. The infantilization of the public by the media system is so complete that a significant portion of the population cannot distinguish between a news report and an opinion column, between a sponsored post and an organic one, between a documentary and a docudrama — and the inability is not innate but cultivated by a system that profits from confusion while claiming to combat it.

24 · Identity Erosion

Replacing who you were with a system-approved version of who you should be.

Individual. Your online identity replaces your embodied identity — you are your profile, your bio, your follower count, your engagement rate, and the person you are when the screen is off becomes less real than the person you perform when it is on.

Institutional. Nuanced, complex, contradictory human beings are flattened into takes and hot takes and engagement metrics — a person's entire intellectual and emotional life reduced to a position on an issue, and that position reduced to a reaction, and that reaction measured not by its depth but by how many people clicked on it.

Civilizational. Collective identity is no longer formed through shared experience but through algorithmic sorting — humanity categorized into consumer segments, political blocs, and behavioral profiles by systems that do not care who you are but care enormously about what you will click on next.

25 · Spiritual Bypassing

Money and debt repurposed as a leash that makes leaving unaffordable.

Individual. The platform encourages "positivity" and "good vibes" while suppressing content that addresses systemic injustice, institutional harm, or uncomfortable truth — the algorithm rewards the inspirational and buries the structural, which means the user's experience of reality is filtered through a lens that makes the world look more beautiful and less broken than it actually is, and the beauty is a bypass.

Institutional. Media outlets convert systemic failures into feel-good stories — the child who raises money for their own surgery framed as inspiring rather than indicting, the community that rebuilds after a disaster framed as resilient rather than abandoned — and the framing bypasses the political reality by routing the audience's emotion through a narrative that feels like hope but functions as anesthesia.

Civilizational. The phrase "the marketplace of ideas" functions as a spiritual bypass for information inequality — the belief that truth will naturally rise to the surface in a free information environment ignores the fact that the environment is not free, that the marketplace is rigged, that the loudest voices are the best-funded voices, and that the population's exposure to ideas is curated by algorithms that optimize for engagement rather than truth — and the metaphor of the marketplace makes the rigging invisible by attributing the outcome to a natural process rather than a designed one.

26 · Financial Control

Spiritual or therapeutic language used to bypass your pain and dodge accountability.

Individual. A creator's entire income depends on platform favor — one algorithm change can destroy a livelihood built over years, and the creator has no contract, no recourse, no union, no protection.

Institutional. Advertising revenue determines which stories get told and which get buried — money edits the news before it reaches the editor, and the stories that survive are the stories that don't threaten the advertisers.

Civilizational. Six corporations control approximately ninety percent of everything the Western world reads, watches, and hears — the flow of information through an entire civilization controlled by a boardroom small enough to fit in a single restaurant, making decisions about what billions of people will believe over appetizers.

Stage 7: Discard

Disposal

27 · Manufactured Crisis

A permanent emergency — too urgent for you to stop, think, and ask if it’s true.

Individual. Clickbait takes a minor event and frames it as catastrophe — the headline is designed to activate your amygdala and your thumb simultaneously, and by the time you realize the story is nothing your attention has already been sold.

Institutional. Networks amplify division during election cycles, crisis periods, and cultural flashpoints to boost ratings — the crisis is real but the amplification is strategic, and the volume is controlled by people who profit from the noise.

Civilizational. A perpetual state of informational emergency is maintained across decades — the public is never allowed to rest, never allowed to process, never given a week without a new threat, a new scandal, a new emergency that demands their attention immediately and then is replaced by the next one before it can be resolved.

28 · Discard

Released the moment maintaining you costs more than you produce.

Individual. Creators are deplatformed overnight with no explanation, no appeal process, and no human contact — years of content, community, and income evaporated in a single automated decision.

Institutional. Journalists who challenge editorial direction are quietly reassigned, sidelined, or let go — their byline disappears and no one publishes an explanation.

Civilizational. Entire media formats are abandoned when they stop generating profit — local newspapers shuttered, radio stations consolidated, platforms closed — and the communities that were built on them are left without a voice, without a record, without proof that they existed.

Stage 8: Replace

Proof

29 · Replacement

Your seat filled before it cools — proof the warmth was for the position, not for you.

Individual. A new influencer is promoted into the exact same niche by the exact same algorithm — same aesthetic, same talking points, same audience, different face — and the platform doesn't skip a beat because the person was never the point, the attention was the point.

Institutional. A new platform replaces the old platform with the same business model wearing different clothes — the interface changes, the name changes, the onboarding experience feels fresh, but the extraction mechanism underneath is identical.

Civilizational. One propaganda framework replaces another across decades — the medium shifts from print to radio to television to social media to whatever comes next, but the function never changes because the function was never about the medium. It was about controlling what a population believes. And belief doesn't care what screen it's delivered on. It only cares that the screen stays on.

30 · Plausible Deniability

The harm structured so it can never be proven intentional. ‘It was God’s will.’

Individual. The platform that suppressed your content says "the algorithm is automated, we don't make individual decisions" — and the automation becomes the shield, because you cannot hold a person accountable when the person points to a machine, and you cannot hold a machine accountable because a machine has no conscience, and the space between the person and the machine is where the deniability lives.

Institutional. Media outlets that amplify division, extremism, and misinformation point to editorial independence, algorithmic neutrality, and the First Amendment — each one a legitimate principle that is being used as a shield for outcomes the institution could prevent but chooses not to because preventing them would reduce engagement and engagement is revenue.

Civilizational. The entire attention economy is structured around plausible deniability — "we don't control what people see, the algorithm does," "we don't create the content, the users do," "we don't cause the harm, the technology is neutral" — and every layer of deniability is technically accurate and structurally dishonest, because the algorithm was designed by people, the platform was built to amplify what harms, and the neutrality was a design choice made by an entity that profits from the specific kind of non-neutrality the neutral system produces.
