APPENDIX B

LIBERATION LOGISTICS (Safety, Exit, Documentation, Resources)

Mission

This appendix gives you a practical, step-by-step logistics playbook for leaving or limiting access-especially in volatile, coercive, or high-control situations.

The goal is not to “win the conversation.”

The goal is to get you safe, stabilize your life, and preserve your future options.

Safety First: Some tactics (especially disengagement strategies) can increase risk in volatile or violent situations.

If you fear physical harm, prioritize safety planning and support over communication strategies.

Quick-Start (If you have 15 minutes)

  1. 1. Choose one safe person to tell: “I need help planning safely.”
  2. 2. Create one safe channel (new email + strong password + 2FA).
  3. 3. Start one evidence log (date / what happened / screenshots).
  4. 4. Identify one exit constraint (money, housing, custody, immigration, disability needs) and one first step to reduce it.

THE LIBERATION CHECKLIST (ONE STEP AT A TIME)

1)

Safety Triage (do this first)

High-risk indicators include threats of violence/self-harm, stalking/tracking/surveillance, strangulation history, weapons access, sexual coercion, escalation after boundaries, isolation + financial control, post-separation harassment.

If any apply:

Do not announce plans.

Do not “expose” them.

Do not negotiate in person.

Shift to quiet planning + allies + documentation + professional support.

2)

Your Safe Channel (the “master key” move)

If someone can access your phone or email, they can often access everything.

Minimum digital safety stack:

New secure email (not logged into shared devices)

Strong unique password

Two-factor authentication (2FA)

Recovery codes stored offline (paper, not photos on a shared phone)

Rule: secure your email first (it’s the password reset hub).

3)

Documents (build your “identity folder”)

Collect originals or copies.

Photograph if you can’t take them yet.

Core docs (adapt to your situation):

ID (passport, driver’s license, state ID)

Birth certificate / social security card (or local equivalents)

Health insurance cards, medical records you may need

Marriage/divorce/custody orders; restraining/ protective orders if any

Lease/mortgage, car title/registration, recent bills (proof of residence)

Pay stubs, tax returns, bank statements

Any evidence of threats/abuse/harassment (printed where possible)

Store: one physical copy (safe location) + one encrypted digital backup.

4)

Money (freedom fund + separation)

Financial control is one of the most common constraint engines in coercive environments.

Minimum moves:

  • “Freedom fund” (even small)

Separate bank account if safe

Cash stash for emergencies (transport, food, hotel)

Cancel/replace shared cards if needed (only when it won’t escalate danger)

Quietly gather employment records and references if leaving a job is part of the exit

5)

The Go-Bag (leave-ready kit)

Pack and store somewhere safe (friend’s house, trunk, locker).

Keep it boring and practical.

Go-bag basics:

Documents (or copies)

Keys (house/car), spare phone charger

Cash, debit card, meds, glasses/contacts

A few days of clothes, hygiene

A list of critical numbers (written)

For kids: essentials + comfort item

For pets: carrier/leash + food + vet info

6)

Evidence + Documentation (your credibility armor)

If systems get weaponized (HR, legal threats, false reports), you need proof, not paragraphs.

Evidence protocol:

Keep a running timeline: dates, incidents, witnesses, outcomes

Screenshot texts/emails/DMs (include timestamps)

Save voicemails and call logs

Photograph damage or injuries (if applicable)

Keep copies of filings, reports, or notices

Rule: document facts, not just feelings (feelings matter- facts travel farther in systems).

7)

Support Network (reduce isolation first)

Manipulation thrives in isolation; safety planning works faster with witnesses and allies.

Your minimum team:

One safe witness (friend/family)

One professional resource (advocate, counselor, attorney, caseworker-where appropriate)

One practical ally (ride, spare room, childcare, storage)

If you’re not ready to disclose everything, start small: “I’m dealing with something and I need backup.”

8)

Communication Rules (post-separation containment)

If contact continues (shared kids, workplace overlap, family systems), use structure.

Contact protocol:

One channel only (preferably written)

One response window (not immediate)

One scope (only logistics)

No “tone” debates, no character trials, no late-night spirals

This reduces nervous-system drain and limits bait opportunities.

9)

Children / Custody / School Safety (if relevant)

Because threats to children are explicitly listed as high- risk indicators.

Practical moves:

Keep copies of custody orders and emergency contacts

Inform school/daycare about authorized pickup lists

Use written communication and keep records

Consult local professional support for safety planning when custody is involved (especially if retaliation escalates)

10)

The Exit Moment (timing + transport + destination)

If risk is high, your plan should include:

  • safest time to leave (when they’re away/asleep-only if safe and legal where you are)
  • transport options (friend, rideshare, public transit, vehicle)
  • first destination (friend, hotel, shelter, family)
  • backup destination
  • code word with allies

11)

After Exit (stabilization)

First 72 hours priorities:

Sleep, food, hydration (your nervous system is injured, not weak)

Re-secure accounts and devices

Update locks and privacy settings if needed

Notify only trusted people; avoid broadcasting location

Continue documentation if harassment continues

IV. DECISION TREES (THE “WHAT DO I DO NOW?” SECTION)

Decision Tree 1: Manipulation vs Human Friction

Run this when you’re unsure.

  • 1. Pattern Test: Is it repeated / escalating / retaliatory after boundaries?
  • If yes → treat as likely manipulation pattern.
  • If no → proceed to Boundary Test.
  • 2. Boundary Test: Set a small, clear boundary and enforce it once.
  • If they respect it → human friction / repair possible.
  • If they punish it → manipulation/control likely.
  • 3. Repair Test: Do they show ownership + empathy + behavior change over time?
  • If yes → repair lane.
  • If no → limit or exit lane.

Decision Tree 2: Choose the Lane (Repair vs Limit vs Exit)

This corresponds to the Harm Ladder logic in Chapter 7.

Lane A - REPAIR (attempt repair)

Choose Repair if all are true:

  • they can hear impact without punishment
  • they can own behavior
  • they change behavior consistently
  • your nervous system improves over time

Go to: 40 (Repair Templates), 41 (Playbook)

Lane B - LIMIT ACCESS (reduce exposure)

Choose Limit if:

  • you can’t fully exit (kids, work, shared systems)
  • behavior is mixed or inconsistent
  • direct repair isn’t available, but safety requires structure

Go to: 41 (Gray Rock + Exit Ladders), 25 (admin structure), Appendix A (regulation)

Lane C - EXIT (end the dynamic / prioritize safety)

Choose Exit if:

  • boundaries trigger retaliation
  • contempt, threats, coercion, stalking, or surveillance are present
  • your health is deteriorating
  • the system requires your self-erasure

Go to: Appendix B (Liberation Logistics), 34 (Post- Separation Control), 32 (High-Control Cage)

Decision Tree 3: Safety First (When strategy is not the move)

If any high-risk markers are present, prioritize safety planning over communication tactics.

The book explicitly lists these as red flags.

High-risk indicators include (examples from the Safety First section):

  • threats of violence/self-harm
  • stalking/tracking/surveillance
  • strangulation history
  • weapons access
  • sexual or reproductive coercion
  • escalation after boundaries

Action: start with Part 0 + Appendix B.
