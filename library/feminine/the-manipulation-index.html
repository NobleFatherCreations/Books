APPENDIX D

THE SCRIPT LIBRARY (All Scripts, Sorted and Searchable)

Mission

This appendix gives you ready-to-use sentences for the moments when your brain goes blank and your body goes into threat response.

Under pressure, you don’t need perfect language-you need retrievable language (the book calls these “memory keys”).

Safety First: If you fear physical harm, prioritize safety planning/support over communication tactics.

Some disengagement strategies can increase risk in volatile situations.

How to use this library (fast)

  1. 1. Pick the category (Frame, Boundary, Fog, Work, Family, Digital, Medical, Post-Separation, Social Siege).
  2. 2. Choose your intensity level: Soft / Firm / Final.
  3. 3. Say one sentence, then stop talking.
  4. 4. If it repeats: enforce (end call, leave, reduce access, move to writing).

The Memory Keys (repeatable anchors)

  • “My feelings are valid data.”
  • “Other people’s perceptions are information—not identity.”
  • “I will not argue inside a false frame.”
  • “A boundary without follow-through is a suggestion.”
  • “The pattern is the proof.”
  • “I can be compassionate without being compliant.”
  • “Love without self-abandonment is the standard.”

A) UNIVERSAL SCRIPTS (WORK ANYWHERE)

A1.

Frame-Holding (when the topic is being hijacked)

Soft

  • “Let’s stay on the issue.”
  • “I want to address what happened.”

Firm

  • “We’re discussing behavior and impact-not my tone.”
  • “That’s not the topic.”
  • “Answer the question I asked.”

Final

  • “This conversation isn’t productive.

I’m ending it.”

  • “We can revisit later when it’s respectful.”

A2.

Boundary Protocol (statement → consequence → follow-through)

Template

  • “Do not .

If you, I will .”

Examples

  • “Do not raise your voice.

If you keep yelling, I will end the call.”

  • “Do not insult me.

If it happens again, I’m leaving.”

A3.

One-Line Exit (when you feel yourself spiraling)

  • “I’m not available for this conversation.”
  • “I’m going to take time and respond later.”
  • “I’m going to step away now.”

B) ANTI-FOG / ANTI-GUILT SCRIPTS (FEAR-OBLIGATION- GUILT)

B1.

The “AND”

Statement (love + boundary)

  • “I care about you, and my answer is no.”
  • “I understand you’re upset, and I’m still choosing what works for me.”
  • “I’m grateful, and I’m not available for that.”

B2.

When they say: “After everything I’ve done for you...”

  • “I appreciate what you’ve done.

I’m still saying no.”

  • “Gratitude doesn’t erase my boundary.”

B3.

When they say: “You’ve changed.”

  • “Yes.

I’m choosing what’s healthier for me.”

  • “Change is not an apology.”

B4.

When they say: “Be the bigger person.”

  • “I’m being the adult by being direct.”
  • “I’m not available to absorb harm to keep peace.”

(“Calm down.”

  • “You’re too emotional.”)

APPENDIX E

THE LEXICON TRANSLATOR (Plain Language ?

HR / Medical / Legal Language)

Mission

This appendix translates your lived experience into the languages that systems reliably respond to: process, documentation, and measurable impact.

It helps you speak truth without getting trapped in tone court, and it helps you request what you actually need: clear standards, written records, accountability, and protection.

Important disclaimer: this is educational language support-not medical, legal, or financial advice.

Quick-Start (60 seconds)

If you’re about to send a message while flooded:

  1. 1. Write one sentence of plain truth.
  2. 2. Translate it into system language (HR/medical/ legal).
  3. 3. Remove all extra emotion words and keep facts + impact + request + timeline.
  4. 4. Send once.

Then stop.

Memory keys that apply here: “The pattern is the proof.”

  • “I will not argue inside a false frame.”

PART 1 - PLAIN LANGUAGE

? HR LANGUAGE (WORK / HR

/ INSTITUTIONS)

A)

Credibility Theft / Tone Policing

Plain: “He dismissed me as emotional so he didn’t have to address the issue.”

HR translation: “During [meeting/date], my concern was redirected from the substantive issue to commentary about my ’tone/emotions,’ which prevented resolution and undermined my ability to participate.”

Plain: “They’re calling me ’difficult’ for setting boundaries.”

HR translation: “After I set a boundary/asked for clarity, I received character-based feedback (e.g., ’difficult/ intense’) instead of behavior-specific guidance.

I’m requesting specific examples and performance expectations in writing.”

B)

Exclusion / Withholding Information

Plain: “I’m being left out and then blamed.”

HR translation: “I was excluded from [meetings/emails/ systems] required to complete [deliverable].

As a result, timelines and outcomes were impacted.

Please clarify access, decision-making process, and expectations.”

C)

Retaliation After Reporting

Plain: “I reported a problem and now they’re punishing me.”

HR translation: “After raising a concern on [date], I experienced [change in assignments/negative review/PIP/ exclusion].

I’m requesting a review for potential retaliation and a clear plan for fair process going forward.”

D) “Vibes”

Feedback → Request Specifics

Plain: “They keep saying ’culture fit’ but won’t say what I did.”

HR translation (template):

  • “Thank you.

To respond accurately, please provide (1) the specific behavior(s) of concern, (2) dates/examples, (3) the applicable policy/expectation, and (4) what success looks like moving forward.”

Post-Separation Contact Protocol

D)

Evidence Language (what to collect / how to describe)

  • “On [date/time], [person] [action/words].”
  • “Witnesses: [names].”
  • “Artifacts: [screenshots/emails/voicemails/photos].”
  • “Impact: [missed work, safety change, financial cost, fear response].”
  • “Boundary stated: [exact words].”
  • “Response: [what happened next].”

PART 4 - REVERSE TRANSLATOR (SYSTEM SPEAK → PLAIN ENGLISH)

HR / Workplace

  • “Concerns were raised” → Someone complained (maybe vaguely), and we’re protecting the process.
  • “Not a culture fit” → You don’t conform / someone with power dislikes your boundaries / politics are in play.
  • “Opportunities for growth” → You’re being warned; get specifics in writing.
  • “Communication issues” → Often code for tone policing unless examples are provided.
  • “Confidential feedback” → You may be denied the data needed to respond; insist on specifics.

Medical

  • “Normal labs” → This test didn’t show the cause; symptoms still need explanation.
  • “Just anxiety/stress” → May be true or may be dismissal; ask what has been ruled out and what comes next.
  • “It’s common” → Common doesn’t mean acceptable; ask about treatment and function impact.

Legal / Administrative

  • “We recommend you resolve it privately” → We may not want to act without stronger documentation.
  • “No evidence” → They mean no admissible/recorded evidence; build a timeline and preserve artifacts.
  • “Civil matter” → You may need a different channel; continue documenting and seek appropriate guidance.

PART 5 - TEMPLATES YOU CAN COPY/PASTE

A) “Confirming in Writing” (work or any institution)

  • “Confirming our conversation on [date].

We discussed [topic].

Next steps are [X].

Success criteria are [Y].

Timeline is [Z].

Please reply if anything here is inaccurate.”

B)

Request for Specifics (anti-fog)

  • “To respond accurately, please provide specific examples (dates/quotes/outputs), the expectation/policy involved, and what change is requested.”

C)

Boundary + Consequence (clean, non- emotional)

  • “Do not [behavior].

If it happens again, I will [action].”

D)

Documentation Boundary (when you suspect distortion)

  • “For clarity, I will communicate in writing going forward.”

PART 6 - COMMON MISTAKES (AND HOW TO FIX THEM)

  1. 1. Over-explaining to prove you’re reasonable Fix: one paragraph max; request specifics; set next step.
  2. 2. Writing while flooded

Fix: ground first (Appendix A); send later; keep it factual.

  1. 3. Arguing character instead of process

Fix: stop defending “I’m not difficult.”

Ask: “What are the specific examples and expectations?”

  1. 4. Using therapy language in institutions

Fix: keep it behavioral and measurable. (Systems don’t litigate “gaslighting”; they litigate actions, dates, impacts.)
