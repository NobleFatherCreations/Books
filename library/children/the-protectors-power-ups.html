THE PROTECTOR’S POWER-UPS

Your Complete Defense System, Fully Assembled

In this mission:ShaelaZill

For grown-ups, also known as: coping skills, assertiveness for kids, what to say to a bully

In plain words

All your Power-Ups in one place: the things you can say and do when a trick happens. Practice them, so they are ready when you need them.

By Shae Stovell · Updated November 8, 2026 · Free to read

For kids and the grown-ups reading with them; not a substitute for professional, medical, legal or mental-health advice. If a child is in danger, call emergency services. Childhelp: 1-800-422-4453. Full notice.

PICTURE THIS

It’s December. Shaela is sitting in the library with her journal open, and she’s doing something she’s been meaning to do for months.

She’s making the list.

Not the list of tricks — she knows those. She’s making the list of everything she knows how to do now that she didn’t know how to do in September. The list of Power-Ups, assembled in one place, with notes on when to use each one.

She’s been building this all year without fully realizing it. Each chapter was a tool. Each tool was a response to a specific kind of pressure. And now, looking at the full list together, she can see something she couldn’t see before:

The Power-Ups aren’t just individual responses to individual tricks. They’re a system. They connect. They support each other. Some are for the external world. Some are for the internal one. Some are for the first move in a situation. Some are for when everything else has been tried. Some are about protection. Some are about relationship.

Together, they make something she’s decided to call — because Zill dared her to give it a name — the COMPLETE DEFENSE ARRAY.

He also pointed out that the COMPLETE DEFENSE ARRAY sounds like something from a video game, which is accurate, and also the point.

THE WAIT A MINUTE FEELING

This chapter’s feeling is different from all the others. It’s not a feeling produced by something going wrong — it’s the feeling of something going right. Of standing on ground that used to feel unstable and feeling it hold.

The Feeling Word: GROUNDED

🧠 This chapter’s Feeling Word: GROUNDED

Grounded is what happens when all the skills you’ve been building settle into your body — not in theory, not as memory, but as actual knowing. You don’t have to think about it. You just have it.

WHAT THIS CHAPTER IS

This chapter is not another trick to learn. It’s a synthesis — the full Power-Up system assembled together so you can see it as a whole, carry it as a whole, and use it as a whole.

There are three layers:

Layer One: THE PERCEPTION LAYER — Power-Ups for noticing.

What’s happening? Is this a trick? Which kind? How serious?

Layer Two: THE RESPONSE LAYER — Power-Ups for acting.

What do I do? What do I say? How do I protect myself in this moment?

Layer Three: THE RECOVERY LAYER — Power-Ups for healing.

What do I do after? How do I rebuild? How do I stay whole?

THE COMPLETE DEFENSE ARRAY

LAYER ONE: PERCEPTION

⚡ TRUTH LOCK (Chapter 1)

What it does: Keeps you connected to what you actually know when someone tries to make you doubt your memory.

When to use it: Anytime someone says “that didn’t happen” or “you’re imagining things.”

Activation: “I remember it differently. I trust what I know.”

⚡ PATTERN SCANNER ELITE (Chapters 7 & 17)

What it does: Tracks repeated behavior over time so you can see the pattern instead of just the single incident.

When to use it: When something has happened before and you’re not sure if it’s a coincidence or a system.

Activation: Write it down. Date it. Count it. Three times is a pattern.

⚡ GENUINE RADAR (Chapter 13)

What it does: Runs a quick check on whether kindness is genuine or done for a reason.

When to use it: When a compliment arrives right before a request, or disappears right after a refusal.

Activation: “Would this compliment still exist if I said no?”

⚡ SIGNAL SCRAMBLER (Chapter 12)

What it does: Disrupts tactical mirroring to check if fake comfort is present.

When to use it: When something feels artificially easy to trust before trust has been earned.

Activation: Change your posture. Change your gesture. Watch for the mirror.

⚡ IDENTITY CHECK PROTOCOL (Chapter 20)

What it does: Verifies the real identity of an online connection before trust escalates.

When to use it: Before sharing personal information or moving to private channels with someone you’ve only met online.

Activation: “Can we video call? Can I verify you through a shared friend?”

LAYER TWO: RESPONSE

⚡ INNER GPS (Chapter 11)

What it does: Navigates by your own values when external pressure is loud.

When to use it: When “everyone is doing it” and something feels wrong anyway.

Activation: “This doesn’t feel right to me. I’m going my own way.”

⚡ FREE WILL FORCE (Chapter 2)

What it does: Separates your choices from other people’s feelings about your choices.

When to use it: When someone says “you made me do this” or tries to put their choices on you.

Activation: “Your feelings are real. Your choices are still yours. These are different things.”

⚡ GUILT-FREE ZONE (Chapter 3)

What it does: Separates caring about someone from complying with their demands.

When to use it: When guilt is being used as the entry point for a request.

Activation: “I care about you. And I can’t do this. Both are true.”

⚡ SIGNAL BOOST ULTRA (Chapters 6 & 16)

What it does: Goes directly to the source to bypass whisper system.

When to use it: When information is coming through third parties and group opinion is shifting without visible cause.

Activation: Go to the person directly. State your truth clearly. Do not operate through middle person.

⚡ UNSHAKEABLE MODE (Chapter 18)

What it does: Maintains your internal temperature when someone needs your reaction to fuel their next move.

When to use it: When a Level 3 player is running the Good Guy / Bad Guy Switch.

Activation: Take one breath. Slow down. Answer at half the speed you want to. Say less than you want to.

⚡ BROADCAST MODE (Chapters 16 & 21)

What it does: Makes your truth visible to more than one person simultaneously.

When to use it: When rumors are running, when group opinion has shifted, when something wrong is happening in a public space.

Activation: Say the thing out loud, clearly, once, to the people who need to hear it.

⚡ HERO WALK (Chapter 11)

What it does: Exits a group or situation that is charging the wrong price for belonging.

When to use it: When belonging requires you to compromise your values.

Activation: Turn. Walk. Steadily. No drama. Head up.

⚡ SOVEREIGNTY FIELD (Chapter 19)

What it does: Asserts that your thoughts, information access, and choices outside a group belong to you.

When to use it: When a group’s rules begin to control your thinking or outside relationships.

Activation: “My thoughts and my choices outside this group are mine.”

⚡ PERSONAL FORCE FIELD (Chapter 12)

What it does: Asserts and maintains your physical space.

When to use it: Any time someone is invading your personal space and not stopping.

Activation: “I need more space, please.” Said once, clearly, with no apology.

LAYER THREE: RECOVERY

⚡ FRIEND MODE ACTIVATED (Chapter 22)

What it does: Extends to yourself the compassion you would naturally give to a friend.

When to use it: When the Mean Voice is running. When self-criticism has turned harsh.

Activation: “What would I say to Zill in this situation? Okay. Say that to myself.”

⚡ FULL RECEIVE (Chapter 22)

What it does: Allows a good moment to be fully experienced before the Voice can redirect it.

When to use it: After a win, a success, a moment of genuine joy.

Activation: Stop. Breathe. Let it land. Thirty seconds without “yeah, but.”

⚡ NOT MY DATA (Chapter 18)

What it does: Refuses to accept an inaccurate description of yourself.

When to use it: When someone projects their behavior onto you, or describes you in a way that isn’t true.

Activation: “That description is not accurate. I don’t have to carry it.”

⚡ CLEAN EXIT PROTOCOL (Chapter 19)

What it does: Exits an unhealthy group without drama, argument, or manifesto.

When to use it: When a group has stopped serving you and has started costing you.

Activation: “I’ve decided this isn’t the right fit for me right now.” Then: out.

⚡ EMERGENCY EJECT (Chapters 18 & 20)

What it does: Removes you from a situation that Power-Ups cannot fix.

When to use it: Level 3 situations where engagement is producing harm. Online situations that have crossed safety lines.

Activation: Stop engaging. Limit contact to necessary only. Tell a trusted adult. Document if needed.

THE THREE-MOVE EMERGENCY SEQUENCE

When something happens and you’re not sure which Power-Up to use:

Step One: SLOW DOWN. Before any response — take one breath. Count to three. Let the first wave of reaction pass.

Step Two: NAME IT. What kind of trick is this? Memory Mixer-Upper? Blame Game? Group-Up? Naming it, even just in your head, immediately reduces its power.

Step Three: PICK ONE POWER-UP. You don’t need the perfect one. You need one. Any one. A response that names your experience and protects your ground is better than a perfect response you couldn’t find in time.

📖 STORY TIME
SHAELA’S STORY

She showed Zill the list.

He read through it carefully, in that methodical way he had, running his finger down each one.

“You know what this is?” he said.

“What?”

“This is everything we’ve figured out this year. All of it. Written down.”

“I know.”

“So even if you forget the name of the Power-Up in the moment — even if you can’t remember which chapter it came from—”

“I remember the feeling of using it,” Shaela said. “It’s in me now. Not just in the book.”

Zill nodded slowly. “My grandmother says: a skill you’ve actually used lives in your hands, not just your head. You can’t read your way to knowing how to ride a bike. You have to fall off a few times.”

Shaela thought about the year — about Furbie and the Truth Lock, about Jumby and the Courage Surge, about Amy and Ground Control, about Bryant and not-my-data, about the group chat and Broadcast Mode Online.

She had fallen off. Many times.

And here she was: grounded.

TRAFFIC LIGHT

The Traffic Light for this chapter is different — not a situation-specific guide but a self-assessment.

🟢 Grounded: You can name at least five Power-Ups and know when to use them. You’ve used at least two of them in real situations this year.

🟡 Building: You understand the Power-Ups in theory but haven’t had to use many of them yet. Keep reading. Keep the list. Your time will come — and when it does, you’ll be more ready than you think.

🔴 Struggling: You’re in a situation right now that feels bigger than your Power-Ups can handle. That’s not a failure — it’s information. Tell a trusted adult. Some situations need more than a twelve-year-old’s Power-Ups. Knowing when to call for backup is the most advanced skill in the whole book.

GROWN-UP CORNER

This chapter’s synthesis serves a specific psychological function: it consolidates scattered skills into an accessible internal framework. Research on what psychologists call “schema consolidation” shows that discrete skills taught in sequence are significantly more deployable when the learner can see their relationship to each other — when they can see the whole system.

The COMPLETE DEFENSE ARRAY isn’t just a list. It’s a cognitive map. When a child has a map, they can navigate. When they’re navigating by feel alone, even well-learned skills can be unavailable under pressure.

We suggest you read through the Power-Up list with your child, letting them explain each one in their own words. Anything they can explain, they own. Anything they can’t quite explain — that’s the skill worth revisiting.

Ask your child: “Which Power-Up have you used the most this year? Which one do you feel most ready to use? Which one feels hardest? Is there one you’ve never had to use — and do you hope you don’t, or do you feel ready?”

YOUR QUEST!

Build your Personal COMPLETE DEFENSE ARRAY.

Not the whole list — your list. The Power-Ups that feel most like yours. The ones you’ve actually used. The ones you think you’ll need most.

Write them on one page in your own words — not the official names if you don’t want to use them, just what they mean to you and when you’d use them.

That page lives in your journal forever. It’s your system. Take it everywhere.

LEVEL UP TOGETHER

Parent and child: Read through the Power-Up list together. For each one, your child explains what it is. For each one, you share whether you could have used it at some point in your own life — and what happened instead. This conversation is the whole point.
