THE FINAL LEVEL

A Celebration of How Far You Have Come

In this mission:ShaelaZill

For grown-ups, also known as: book review for kids, recap

In plain words

You made it to the end! This is a look back at everything you learned, and all the ways you can keep being a Playground Protector.

By Shae Stovell · Updated November 8, 2026 · Free to read

For kids and the grown-ups reading with them; not a substitute for professional, medical, legal or mental-health advice. If a child is in danger, call emergency services. Childhelp: 1-800-422-4453. Full notice.

PICTURE THIS

It is May. The school year is almost over.

Shaela is at her locker — the same locker she was standing at in September when Furbie told her the pen never happened and the floor had shifted under her feet — and she is thinking about the list.

Not the Power-Up list, though that is part of it. Not the Trickster list, though that is part of it too. The other list. The one she made in January in the library when she was trying to understand what she knew now that she had not known in September.

She knows what the Memory Mixer-Upper is, and she knows that the confused, floor-shifting feeling it makes is the trick working — not evidence that she is wrong.

She knows how to separate someone’s feelings from someone’s choices, and she knows that the Blame Game puts both in the same pile and tries to hand them to her.

She knows how guilt is installed and how to distinguish the real kind — the useful kind, the kind that says I did something wrong and I want to fix it — from the fake obligation kind that arrives pre-packaged from someone else’s need.

She knows the difference between a friendship that floods with intensity to bypass her judgment and a friendship that builds in layers because it actually has something to build.

She knows the SAFE feeling from the inside now. Not just as a concept. As a felt thing. As something she can locate in her chest when she is at the table with Zill and Remy and Maya and things are as they should be.

She knows how to go direct, broadcast truth, scramble a signal, and perform a clean exit. She knows how to stay on the ground when someone tries to put her on a pedestal. She knows how to play boring against someone who needs her reaction as fuel.

She knows that the Mean Voice in her head is installed content, not original content, and that it can be argued with using receipts. She knows how to forgive without forgetting and how to put down carry weight when she is ready to put it down.

And she knows — maybe most importantly — what the chain reaction of kindness feels like when it is running. The warm-chest CONNECTED feeling of having given something freely and watched it travel without needing to follow it.

She closes the locker.

She is ready for whatever comes next.

THE WAIT A MINUTE FEELING

This chapter’s feeling is the most complete one in the book. It is not produced by a single event. It is produced by the building up of everything — all the chapters, all the Power-Ups, all the conversations, all the hard moments that turned into skills.

The word for it is WHOLE.

Not finished. Not perfect. Not past difficulty. Just: more complete than you were. In possession of something that belongs to you now. The vocabulary, the tools, the map, the feeling of safe from the inside — these live in you now in a way they did not in September.

🧠 This chapter’s Feeling Word: WHOLE

Whole does not mean fixed or finished. It means more complete than you were. In possession of something you did not have before.

WHAT’S THE GOAL?

This chapter is not a lesson. It is a ceremony.

You made it through twenty-eight chapters of tricks and Power-Ups and Trickster Levels and stories and Traffic Lights and quests and conversations with the people who love you. That is not nothing. That is a year of learning that most people do not get until they are much older — and some people never get at all.

Let us take stock of what you now have:

You have 28 Power-Ups. You know when to use each one. You have practiced at least some of them in real situations, which means they live in your hands now and not just your head.

You have a Feeling Vocabulary with 28 words for 28 specific emotional experiences. The next time something happens, you can name it within seconds instead of carrying an unnamed thing in your stomach for days.

You have the Traffic Light system — so you know when to handle something yourself, when to loop in an adult, and when to get help immediately. That decision framework, practiced over 28 chapters, is now automatic.

You have the Grown-Up Corner relationship with the adult who read this with you — which means you have at least one person who has been reading the same book, building the same vocabulary, learning the same names for the same things. That shared language is one of the most valuable things this book created.

And you have the four building blocks for real friendship: Kindness, Trust, Celebration, and Apology. Which means you know how to build something worth having and you know what it feels like from the inside when you have found it.

What does a Playground Protector actually do?

They notice. They name what they notice. They respond with the right tool. They stay steady when things are uncertain. They go direct when information is being filtered. They protect their own memory, their own feelings, their own choices. They give kindness without a receipt. They say something when something is wrong. They build friendships with all four bricks. They put down what they are ready to put down and hold on to what they are not yet ready to release. They trust their INNER GPS. They run COURAGE SURGE when the right thing is scary.

And when they get confused — which still happens, because it always happens to everyone — they take one breath, name what is happening, and pick one Power-Up. Not the perfect one. Just one. Any one. And they go from there.

That is the whole job.

You have got it.

THEIR MOVES & YOUR POWER-UPS

Reflection Move: Checking in with your complete system.

POWER-UP!
⚡ THE FULL INVENTORY

THE FULL INVENTORY is a practice, not a one-time event. Once a season — or whenever you feel like you have drifted from your grounding — go through your Power-Up Master Guide in the back of this book and check in.

Which Power-Ups have you used? Which ones do you feel most ready for? Which ones do you hope you never need? Which ones do you currently have a situation where you need them?

The inventory is not a test. It is a check-in with yourself. The map does not expire. It just updates.

Teaching Move: Passing it on.

POWER-UP!
⚡ TEACH IT FORWARD

TEACH IT FORWARD is one of the most effective ways to know you have actually learned something: explain it to someone else. Find one person — a younger sibling, a friend, a cousin, someone new to your school — who could use one Power-Up you have learned.

Do not lecture. Have a conversation. Start with: “Something happened to me once, and here is what I figured out.”

Teaching something is the deepest way to know you have it. If you can say it in your own words to someone who needs it, it is yours.

Commitment Move: The Playground Protectors Pledge.

POWER-UP!
⚡ THE PLEDGE

See the full Playground Protectors Pledge in the back of this book.

Read it out loud. Sign it. You can do it alone or with someone. Either way, the saying of it makes it real in a way the reading of it does not quite. Saying something out loud is the oldest form of commitment we have. Use it.

TRICKSTER LEVELS

This chapter’s levels are about where you are right now, at the end of the journey.

Level One — You Have the Map:

You have read the book and understood the concepts. You have the vocabulary. You have the Power-Up names. You have the framework. You are ready to start applying it in real situations. The map is in your hands. Now you start walking with it.

Level Two — You Are Using the System:

You have used several Power-Ups in real situations this year. You have made some of them yours — they come naturally now, without having to look them up. You have had at least one conversation with a trusted adult using the shared vocabulary this book built. You are not just a reader. You are a practitioner.

Level Three — You Are Teaching It:

You have used the system, taught something from it to at least one other person, and had real conversations — with a parent, with a friend, with anyone — where the vocabulary made something speakable that was not speakable before. At Level Three you are not just protecting yourself. You are expanding the circle of people who have the tools.

📖 STORY TIME
SHAELA’S STORY

Zill found her at the locker.

“Last week,” he said.

“Last week,” she confirmed.

They walked toward homeroom in the comfortable rhythm of people who have been walking the same direction together for a long time.

“What is the thing you are taking with you?” Zill asked.

It was the kind of question he asked — the one that seemed simple and was not. The one that required actual thought rather than a fast answer.

She thought about it seriously. Not about what she was supposed to say. About what was actually true.

“I trust what I actually know,” she said finally. “That is the main one. Before this year I was so easy to confuse. Someone would tell me I remembered something wrong and I would start doubting. Someone would manufacture intensity and I would mistake it for depth. Someone would use guilt and I would carry it as if it were mine.” She paused. “I trust what I actually know now. That is the core thing. Everything else is built on top of it.”

Zill nodded. “For me it is the Pattern Scanner. Not panicking at one incident. Looking for the pattern. Because the pattern is the truth, not the incident.”

“Your grandmother taught you that?”

“She taught me a version of it. You would be surprised how much of what I have said this year is basically her, translated slightly.”

“Tell her I said thank you.”

“She already knows about you,” Zill said. “She said: the girl who holds her ground sounds like someone who has been doing the work.”

Shaela smiled. “She would say that about a lot of people.”

“She would,” Zill agreed. “But she said it specifically about you.”

They turned the corner toward homeroom.

Shaela thought about the year — Furbie and the pen, Jumby and the chips, Amy and the Super-Fast Best Friend, Bryant and all his careful engineering, Fran and her partial secrets, the group chat and the single sentence that shifted the temperature, Remy at the cafeteria entrance, Kai and the door held open for three people in a row.

All of it had been real. All of it had cost something. And all of it had produced something — tools she had not had before, skills that lived in her hands now, a friend who asked the real questions and waited for the real answers.

“Hey,” she said at the homeroom door. “Next year — same table at lunch?”

Zill looked at her with the mild exasperation of someone being asked whether water is wet.

“Obviously,” he said. “Same table.”

She walked in.

She was ready.

TRAFFIC LIGHT

Green You have the tools. You have the vocabulary. You have the map. You have the SAFE feeling as a reference point. You have at least one person who read this book with you and built the shared language. Go use all of it. You are ready.
Yellow You have the tools but something big is happening right now that feels larger than the Power-Ups can handle alone. That is okay — that is what the yellow level is for. Tell a trusted adult what is happening. Use the vocabulary you built together. You do not have to solve everything yourself. Knowing when to ask for backup is the most advanced skill in the whole book.
Red Something is happening right now that is not safe — to you or someone you know. Tell an adult today. The Playground Protectors Pledge is for real life, and in real life, Playground Protectors know when to call for backup. That is not a failure. That is the system working correctly.

GROWN-UP CORNER

You have now read twenty-eight chapters of a children’s book about psychological manipulation and relational protection. Some of it — perhaps more than you expected — was about your own life too.

This chapter is an invitation: carry the conversation forward. The vocabulary you built together — the Power-Ups, the Trickster Levels, the Feeling Words, the Traffic Light — does not belong only to the book. It belongs to your relationship with your child now. Use it. When something happens at school, ask: “Which Power-Up would you use?” When you see your child navigating something difficult, name what you see: “That looks like the Pattern Scanner in action.” When you see them do something brave — especially the small moves, the one-sentence moves, the quiet BRAVE VOICE moments — name it: “That was BRAVE VOICE. I saw it.”

And when you encounter the same patterns in your own adult life — which you will, because these patterns do not stop at graduation — tell your child about it. Tell them you ran into a Blame Game situation at work. Tell them you recognized the Hot-and-Cold cycle in an adult relationship and used Steady Sunshine. Tell them you used COURAGE SURGE when something needed to be said and the scared thing was very loud.

Your transparency normalizes theirs. Your using the vocabulary keeps it alive. Your honesty about the moments when you did not have the tools — and what happened then — is the most powerful teaching you can offer.

The book ends here. The work does not.

Ask your child: “What is the thing you are taking from this year — from this book — that you will still have when you are my age? What do you want to carry forward? And what do you want to put down?” Then tell them what you are taking forward too — and what you are putting down.

YOUR FINAL QUEST!

Your final quest has three parts. All three matter.

Part One: Your Personal Power-Up Inventory.

Write down the five Power-Ups you feel most ready to use. Not the ones you think you should feel ready for — the ones that feel genuinely yours now. In your own words: what they are, when you would use them, and what the script sounds like when you say it.

Part Two: Teach It Forward.

Find one person who could use one Power-Up you have learned. Not a lecture — a conversation that starts: “Something happened to me once, and here is what I figured out.” See what happens.

Part Three: Take the Pledge.

Turn to the Playground Protectors Pledge in the back of this book. Read it out loud. Both the child version and — if your grown-up is there — the parent version together. Then sign it.

You have earned this. Go take it.

LEVEL UP TOGETHER

Parent and child: Do the Playground Protectors Pledge together. Out loud. Both of you. Read every word. Then: put this book down and look at each other. Tell each other one thing you learned. Tell each other one thing you want to do differently. Tell each other one thing you are proud of in the other person.

That conversation is the whole point.

That conversation is what the book was for.
