THE HOT-AND-COLD GAME

A Guide to the “Why Are They So Moody?” Trick

In this mission:ShaelaFurbie

For grown-ups, also known as: hot and cold behavior, intermittent reinforcement, moody friend

In plain words

Sometimes a friend is super nice one day and mean the next. That mix-up can make you try harder and harder. A good friend is kind most of the time.

By Shae Stovell · Updated November 8, 2026 · Free to read

For kids and the grown-ups reading with them; not a substitute for professional, medical, legal or mental-health advice. If a child is in danger, call emergency services. Childhelp: 1-800-422-4453. Full notice.

PICTURE THIS

Here is Shaela’s last three weeks with Furbie, summarized:

Week One: Furbie sits with Shaela and Zill at lunch every day. He shares his chips. He laughs at her jokes. On Friday he says, “This was the best week — you guys are the best.”

Week Two: Furbie doesn’t come to lunch on Monday. Tuesday he’s there but barely talks. Wednesday he’s cold — short answers, no eye contact. Thursday she walks past him in the hall and he doesn’t notice her. Friday she asks if he’s okay and he says “fine” in the exact tone that means not fine.

Week Three: Monday, Furbie is back. Full warmth. “Where have you been? I feel like we haven’t hung out.” He acts like the cold week never happened. By Wednesday, he’s saving her a seat again.

Shaela is not sure what she did wrong in Week Two. She thinks about it constantly. By Week Three she is so relieved to have Warm Furbie back that she doesn’t even bring up Cold Furbie. She just enjoys it.

Which is exactly the problem.

THE WAIT A MINUTE FEELING

The Hot-and-Cold Game makes a feeling that’s hard to name because it alternates: it’s relief during the warm parts and anxiety during the cold parts. Over time the whole thing starts to feel like a roller coaster — dizzy.

Dizzy is the right word. Because you can never get steady. Because just when things feel good, you’re waiting for them to go cold. And just when they’ve gone cold, there’s the possibility that they’ll go warm again. So you stay on the ride instead of getting off.

The warmth feels so good partly because the cold is so cold. That contrast is not an accident.

🧠 This chapter’s Feeling Word: DIZZY

Dizzy is what happens when someone keeps you off-balance on purpose — warm one week, cold the next, so you’re always focused on the temperature instead of anything else.

What’s the trick?

The Hot-and-Cold Game works on the same principle as a slot machine: unpredictable rewards are more addictive than predictable ones. If you pull the lever and nothing happens, you stop pulling. If you pull the lever and something happens every single time, you pull it, get your reward, and stop being very excited about it. But if you pull the lever and sometimes nothing happens and sometimes something wonderful happens — and you can’t tell which it’ll be — you pull it forever.

The Cold Shoulder removes warmth — suddenly, without explanation, often for no clear reason. It doesn’t always require words. A distant look. No response to a text. A shorter-than-usual conversation. Just enough cool that you notice.

The Sudden Thaw brings it back — full warmth, like the cold never happened. No acknowledgment. No explanation. Just: here I am, your favorite version of this person.

And because the warm version is so good, you don’t bring up the cold version. You don’t want to mess it up. So the cold version never gets addressed. And it can happen again whenever it needs to.

THEIR MOVES & YOUR POWER-UPS

TRICKY MOVEMove #1: The Cold Shoulder — unexplained distance, one-word answers, sudden unavailability.
POWER-UP!
⚡ STEADY SUNSHINE

Steady Sunshine is an internal temperature you maintain regardless of what someone else is doing. When someone goes cold, your job is not to turn your heat up to compensate — chasing, over-texting, trying harder to get the warmth back. Your job is to stay at your own steady temperature.

Say this

“I notice things feel different between us. I’m here if you want to talk. Otherwise I’ll be doing my thing.”

Then you actually do your thing. You don’t monitor. You don’t anxiously check. You be warm with everyone else and let them find their way back to the table on their own.

TRICKY MOVEMove #2: The Sudden Thaw — the warm return with no acknowledgment of the cold period.
POWER-UP!
⚡ THE PATTERN SCANNER

The PATTERN SCANNER is a mental note — or an actual note — of the cycle. How many times has this happened? What was the pattern? The warmth feels real in the moment, but the pattern is the truth.

Say this

(an honest conversation, if and when you choose to have it): “I’ve noticed that things go warm and then cold sometimes without me knowing why. I’m not upset — I just want to understand what’s happening so I know what to expect.”

This isn’t an accusation. It’s a request for honesty. How the person responds to that request tells you everything you need to know.

TRICKSTER LEVELS

🌱
Level 1 — Accidental

Someone has genuinely hard weeks where they pull away from everyone — not just you. They’re dealing with something and don’t have the communication skills to explain it. They come back with apology and noticing.

🔥
Level 2 — Occasional

Someone’s moods affect how they treat people. They’re not deliberately running a play — they’re just different all the time, and their inconsistency happens to hook you in. Still worth addressing, but not necessarily malicious.

💀
Level 3 — The Full Trick

The cycle is deliberate and controlled. The cold is deployed when they want leverage — when you’ve done something they disapprove of, when they want you to work harder for their attention, when they want to test your loyalty. The warm is deployed when they need something from you. If you trace the pattern, the timing is never random.

📖 STORY TIME
SHAELA’S STORY

“I keep trying to figure out what I did,” Shaela told Zill. “Like what triggers the cold week.”

Zill had been listening carefully. “Does it happen after you spend time with someone else?”

Shaela thought about it. “…Sometimes.”

“Does it happen after you don’t respond to a message fast enough?”

Another pause. “Maybe.”

“So the cold might not be random.”

Shaela hadn’t thought about it from that angle. She’d been so focused on what she did that she hadn’t looked at the pattern from outside.

“You mean he’s doing it on purpose?”

“I mean,” Zill said carefully, “that it might not be about his mood at all. It might be about what gets the most response from you. Have you noticed that when he goes cold, you try harder?”

Shaela was quiet for a long moment. “…Yes.”

“So the cold might be working.”

She tried Steady Sunshine for two weeks. When Furbie went cool, she said hi once and then turned her attention to other things. She didn’t monitor. She didn’t text extra. She stayed at her own temperature.

The cold week was shorter than usual. Like without the pull of her reaction, it had less fuel.

Furbie still ran the cycle sometimes. But Shaela had gotten off the roller coaster. She could see the ride from the outside now, which made it a lot less dizzying.

TRAFFIC LIGHT

Green Someone is moody but the pattern doesn’t feel intentional and they’re generally kind. Use Steady Sunshine and the PATTERN SCANNER. Don’t chase.
Yellow The cycle is the same every time and you find yourself organizing your behavior around someone else’s temperature — being careful, monitoring, adjusting — to avoid the cold. That’s more than a friendship quirk.
Red The “cold” includes cruelty — deliberately excluding you, turning others against you, or saying mean things — and the “warm” is used to get you to forgive and forget. That is a cycle of harm and needs support.

GROWN-UP CORNER

Intermittent reinforcement — what this chapter calls the Hot-and-Cold Game — is the psychological mechanism behind some of the most difficult-to-leave relationships in adulthood, including those involving emotional abuse. The unpredictability of reward and punishment creates a neurological pull that consistent good treatment does not. It is the same mechanism behind gambling addiction.

Children who learn to identify this pattern early — and who learn that steady, consistent warmth is the correct baseline to expect in a friendship, not a rare treat — develop a fundamental understanding of healthy relating that many adults never gained. Helping your child understand that “the warmth feeling so good” might be partly a product of how cold the cold was is a nuanced conversation, but one worth having.

Ask your child: “Is there anyone in your life who makes you feel really good sometimes and then pulls away? How does it feel during the good times? How does it feel during the cold times? Do you find yourself working harder to get back to the good times?”

YOUR QUEST!

This week, draw a Friendship Temperature Chart.

Think of your most important friendships. For each one, draw a simple line: flat and steady, or up and down?

The flattest line is usually the safest friendship. The biggest ups and downs might feel exciting, but ask yourself: does that friendship make you feel steady or dizzy overall?

Keep the chart. Look at it again in a month.

LEVEL UP TOGETHER

Parent and child: Have you ever been friends with — or in a relationship with — someone whose warmth felt really special partly because they weren’t warm all the time? What did that feel like? How did you figure out the pattern?
