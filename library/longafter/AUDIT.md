## AUDIT 2 · THE STEELMAN PASS

**Ch2 (why it often gets worse first):** *Steelman —* "Telling someone who has just left that things typically get worse first could be read as discouraging, potentially destabilizing someone in a fragile early period, or even implicitly suggesting they should have anticipated and avoided this by not leaving." *Response:* Checked the actual text — it opens with an explicit warning box stating this is not a reason to stay and is written for people who have already left, and closes by stating plainly this is not evidence the reader did anything wrong. Judged the framing correct: informed is better than blindsided for this specific population.

**Ch14 (when it stops):** *Steelman —* "Listing factors that 'correlate with it stopping' (new relationship for the ex, consistent boundaries) risks reading as a checklist the reader is failing if they've done everything right and it still hasn't stopped, adding self-blame to an already difficult situation." *Response:* Real risk. Checked the chapter's actual hedging — it explicitly states these are "not a formula you can reliably execute to guarantee an end date" and "patterns observed... not promises about yours specifically." Judged the hedge sufficiently strong, but this is the closest call in the audit — noting it here in case a future revision wants to strengthen it further.

**Ch18 (family court, honestly):** *Steelman —* "Presenting genuine uncertainty about whether a reader's jurisdiction will recognize coercive control could discourage someone from pursuing a legitimate custody protection they'd actually secure, out of pessimism the book itself introduced." *Response:* Structurally identical to the jurisdiction-variance steelman in *At Will* and *The Slow Take*. Checked the chapter: it explicitly declines both a universally optimistic and universally pessimistic framing and directs to jurisdiction-specific specialist advice rather than either encouraging or discouraging action. Judged correct — no change needed.

**Ch42 (if you go back):** *Steelman —* "By normalizing 'going back' as common and not a moral failure, this chapter could inadvertently function as permission or reduced urgency around leaving well the first time, for a reader who hasn't yet left for good." *Response:* Checked the chapter's explicit disclaimer: "not encouragement to go back and 'get more information.'" The chapter is addressed to readers processing a past return, not counseling a current one. Judged the framing holds, and this is a necessary chapter — the alternative (silence on this common pattern) would leave readers who have gone back carrying unwarranted additional shame, which chapter 42 exists specifically to prevent.
