The short answer

You don’t have to read this book in order. Pick the line below that sounds most like today, and it opens the three chapters that matter most for what to do after leaving an abusive relationship. Nothing is stored, and tomorrow you can come back and pick a different line.

Information, not legal, medical or therapeutic advice. If you are in danger, contact your local emergency services. Full notice.

Wondering what to do after leaving an abusive relationship? You probably didn’t arrive here calmly, with a cup of tea and a free afternoon. A message just landed. A court letter came. It’s 2am and the house is too quiet. So this book starts with you, not with chapter one.

Find the line that sounds like today. Next week it might be a different one, and that’s fine.

I left in the last few days

Start with The first 30 days, a plain list for the first month. Then read A safety plan for after you’ve left, because the weeks after leaving can be the most dangerous. Then Leaving was the middle, for why it doesn’t feel like the ending you were promised.

They keep contacting me

Hoovering helps you spot the pattern. Words you can use has replies you can copy and send. Messages you don’t have to answer helps you sort the ones that need a reply from the ones that can wait forever.

We have children together

Start with Co-parenting or parallel parenting?. Then Your children, and what to say. Then Children as the channel, for the ways children get pulled into the middle, and what you can do about it.

There’s a court case

Start with What to document and how, because the record does a lot of the heavy lifting. Then Family court, honestly. Then Your day in court.

I don’t feel safe

Go to If you’re not safe right now first. Then read A safety plan for after you’ve left and Warning signs to take seriously.

I can’t stop thinking about them

Read The trauma bond. It explains why your head keeps going back there. Then What you’re allowed to feel about someone you left. Then Checking their profile, for the thumb that keeps opening the app.

I can’t sleep, or I’m not okay

Read What your nervous system is doing and Sleep. If it’s more than a hard week, go straight to When it’s more than aftermath.

They left me

Start with If they left you. Then Hoovering, because people who leave often come back. Then The grief nobody will let you have.

We still live together

Read Still under the same roof. Then A safety plan for after you’ve left. Don’t let the title put you off. It works before the move too.

I’ve been accused of something

Read When they say you’re the abuser. Then What to document and how. Then Legal help with little or no money.

I’m thinking of going back

Read If you go back. There’s no judgment in it, promise. Then The trauma bond and Hoovering.

I’m under 18

You matter just as much as any adult reading this. Read If your story doesn’t look like the usual one, which lists help made for young people. Then If you’re not safe right now.

They follow me or turn up

Read Stalking and harassment after a breakup first. Then Warning signs to take seriously, because following and turning up are among the signs that matter most. Then What to document and how, so every time counts.

Money is the problem

If they control what you get, start with Money as the channel. If you’re starting again with nothing, read Money from zero. For help with benefits or a place to live, go to Housing and benefits.

I’m pregnant or have a new baby

Read Pregnant or with a new baby. Pregnancy can raise the risk, so read A safety plan for after you’ve left as well. And tell your midwife or doctor what’s happening. They’ve heard it before, and they can help.

They have a new partner

Read The new partner. It covers the comparing, the warning you may want to give, and why a new relationship rarely changes the pattern. If it’s pulling you under, Checking their profile helps with the urge to look.

My family or friends are on their side

Read Your family and mutual friends and The friends who didn’t believe you. If the story your ex is telling is the problem, add The narrative campaign.

It was years ago and it still hurts

That’s allowed, and it doesn’t mean you’ve gone backwards. Read The anniversary effect and Memory, and doubting yourself. What “recovered” means offers a way to think about it that isn’t a finish line.

I’m thinking about dating again

Read Dating, when you’re ready and when you’re not. Then Trust as a practice, which is about trusting yourself as much as anyone new.

I’m helping someone

Thank you. Read Helping someone in year one. It’s short and written to be forwarded. Then What post-separation abuse is.

If you only have five minutes

Whatever brought you here, these three pages do the most in the least time:

  1. If you’re not safe right now: the numbers, and how to use this book on a phone someone else might check.
  2. The record: four lines you can write tonight that make every later step easier.
  3. Messages you don’t have to answer: how to tell what needs a reply.

Then put the phone down. The rest will still be here tomorrow.
