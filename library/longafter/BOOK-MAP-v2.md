# THE LONG AFTER, second edition: the full outline (proposal, 2026-11-21)

**Status: outline for the owner's approval. Nothing in the book has changed yet.**

**Owner's brief (2026-11-21):**
- a full voice rewrite and the full expansion, all three tiers
- a complete resource guide, unique in its approach
- built around the words people search, without reading as if it is
- chapters expanded to *complete information*, not to a word count
- easy to follow, easy to find the section you need, easy to understand

Title and subtitle stay: **The Long After: What happens once you've left.** Every one of the 45 existing chapters survives, rewritten and moved to where a reader would look for it (see the mapping in section 9).

## 1. What makes this edition different (the approach)

Most books on leaving abuse are written in the order an author thinks. This one is organised around **the moment the reader is in**, and written in **the words they type into a search bar at 2am**.

1. **It starts with a question, not a contents page.** "Where are you right now?" Eight plain situations (they keep contacting me; we have children; there's a court case; I can't sleep; I miss them; I'm scared; I'm thinking of going back; I'm helping someone) each open the three chapters that matter for that situation. No sign-up and nothing stored: it is just navigation.
2. **Every chapter answers first.** Under the title sits *the short answer*: two to four plain sentences that answer the question the reader came with. Everything after it is depth. This is what a frightened reader needs, and it is also what Google and AI assistants quote.
3. **Headings are the reader's own questions.** "Does it ever end?", "Will they come back?", "Is this a crime?", "How long does a trauma bond last?" These are the questions people really ask, taken from Google's autocomplete data (section 7). They sound like a friend asking, and they match the searches exactly.
4. **It meets the reader's words, then gives better ones.** People search "narcissist", "flying monkeys", "trauma bond". The book uses those words where the reader would, says honestly what they do and don't mean, and then gives the pattern language that is more useful in a courtroom or a therapist's office. One chapter, "Is my ex a narcissist?", exists for exactly this.
5. **It gives you things to use, not just things to know:**
   - words to send
   - a four-line record
   - a safety plan
   - a court-day checklist
   - a device audit
   - a pocket card

   All of them are printable and all of them work offline.
6. **It stays honest about the world.** Laws and services differ by country. Every legal chapter teaches what the thing *is*, gives a short sourced note for the main English-speaking countries, and ends with the exact words to search to find the local version.
7. **It never sets a timeline and never tells you what to feel.** That promise from the first edition is kept word for word in spirit, even where people search "how long does it last". Those questions get an honest answer about ranges and what makes the difference.

## 2. The voice: "the friend who sat through the hearing"

Someone who has been there, or sat beside someone who was: in the car park before a handover, in the court corridor, at the kitchen table at 3am. They are not a therapist or a lawyer, and not a cheerleader.

**How it sounds:**
- **Short sentences. Second person. Present tense.** "The message comes on your birthday. It is warm. It is the first warm thing in months. That is why it was sent today."
- **One concrete picture per section:** the phone face down on the table, the envelope with the court stamp, the child's backpack coming home with a note inside.
- **Says the hard thing once, plainly**, without softening it into mush or wrapping it in three caveats. Caution goes where it matters (children, legal variance, safety) and nowhere else.
- **Never clinical, never cute.** It doesn't say "trauma response cascade", and it doesn't say "you've got this, warrior".
- **It trusts the reader:** it gives the information and lets them choose. It does not say "you must".

**Words and habits the rewrite removes** (measured in the first edition, see `DESIGN-STATE.md` §2.4):
- genuinely, actually, specifically, really, it's important to note, it's worth noting
- chains of "rather than"
- "not X, but Y" reveals
- lists of three adjectives for rhythm
- "both are true at once" used as a refrain
- dashes over 3 per 1,000 words
- template headings ("What actually helps") repeated across chapters
- cross-references written as "chapter twenty-seven covers…" (they become links: "Finding the right therapist")

**Three registers, one voice:**

| Where | How it sounds | Example (direction, not final text) |
|---|---|---|
| Explaining | patient, concrete, one idea per paragraph | "When you left, they lost most of their reach in one day. The court case, the late payments, the messages through your sister: that is the reach coming back by other roads." |
| Doing | numbered, direct, scripts in quotation marks | "Reply once, in writing: 'I've received your message. I'll respond about the school pickup by Friday.' Then stop." |
| Crisis (chapter 49, warning signs, safety plan) | slowest, plainest, no metaphors, help first | "If you are thinking about ending your life, call your local crisis line or emergency number now. You do not need to be sure it's serious enough." |

**How the search words go in without showing:**
- **Where they go:** the searched phrase appears in the chapter's browser title, in the first sentence of the short answer and in one heading phrased as the question. In the body it appears only where a person would naturally say it.
- **Natural variety:** the book uses the phrases people really use ("your ex", "the other parent", "the person you left"), not the same keyword ten times.
- **No keyword lists:** related terms live in the A to Z glossary (tool T8) and the questions page (T9). Both are useful to readers and both are strong pages for search.
- **No diagnosing:** the book never calls a specific person a narcissist and never diagnoses the reader. It explains the word and moves to patterns.

## 3. How the new book is organised

**Ten parts plus a front door and a toolbox.** The parts follow the questions a reader has, roughly in the order they arrive. Every chapter also stands alone, because most readers will land in the middle from a search.

| | Part | What it answers | Chapters |
|---|---|---|---|
| — | **Start here** | Where are you right now? Are you safe? How does this book work? | S1–S3 |
| I | **What is happening** | Why it didn't end when I left; what post-separation abuse is | 1–7 |
| II | **Staying safe after you leave** | How to stay safe now that they can't reach me directly | 8–12 |
| III | **How it continues** | The channels: children, money, the story, friends, the new partner, hoovering | 13–19 |
| IV | **Contact** | How much contact, how, and what to say | 20–23 |
| V | **Courts and the systems** | Records, legal help, family court, protective orders, police, housing | 24–32 |
| VI | **Children** | Co-parenting or parallel parenting, handovers, what to tell them | 33–37 |
| VII | **Body and mind** | Trauma bond, grief, nervous system, sleep, memory, anger, guilt, therapy, crisis | 38–49 |
| VIII | **The people around you** | Friends, family, new people, small communities | 50–55 |
| IX | **Rebuilding** | Money, work, home, pets, holidays, trust, dating, who you are now | 56–65 |
| X | **The ledger** | Going back, helping someone, what this book might have wrong, the long view | 66–69 |
| — | **Toolbox** | Printable tools, glossary A to Z, questions people ask | T1–T9 |

**69 chapters**: the 45 existing chapters rewritten and expanded, plus 24 new ones. With 3 start pages and 9 tools, that is **81 pages**.

### Every chapter follows one frame (only the top and bottom are fixed)
1. **Title** (book voice), with the reader's question under it in smaller type.
2. **The short answer**: 2–4 sentences. It is the one boxed element in a chapter.
3. **The body**, shaped by the chapter's needs: some chapters are steps, some are explanation, some are scripts. No repeated template headings.
4. Where relevant:
   - **"Words you can use"** for scripts
   - **"Keep a record"** linking the record tool
   - a **"Safety"** line in heavy chapters
5. **Sources** (collapsible), so every claim is checkable.
6. **"Next, if this is you"**: two or three chapters for the likely next question, plus the matching Pattern Decoder entries.

### Finding the section you need (four ways in)
- **Where are you right now?** The situation router (S1), also reachable from the top bar.
- **Search**: an in-page search that runs offline and sends nothing anywhere. It also matches the reader's words, so "flying monkeys" finds chapter 16 and "restraining order" finds chapter 29.
- **Contents**: ten parts on a horizon line, each chapter listed as a plain numbered line with its question.
- **A to Z** (T8) and **Questions people ask** (T9).

## 5. Layout: how a reader moves through it (design direction A, "Dusk to first light")

- **Opening:** the cover; one line ("For the part after you leave"); the safety strip (Leave, help); then **Where are you right now?** as eight large buttons; then search; then the contents.
- **Colour:** the ten parts run from deep dusk (Part I) to first light (Part X), using the cover's palette. The page colour itself shows where you are, with no progress bar.
- **Contents:** a horizon line with ten points. Each part opens to plain numbered lines (number, title, the question in small type). No cards.
- **Chapter page:**
  - a thin location line (part · chapter N of 69)
  - the title, then the question
  - the short answer, as the one box
  - open text at 18 px or more in full-contrast ink
  - scripts set as small "say it like this" cards
  - checklists that print cleanly
  - sources in a fold-out section
  - "Next, if this is you"
- **Always there:** Leave (the largest button), help, search, contents, text size. All at least 44 px, one-thumb reachable. The NF seal moves into the bar.
- **Dark by default** at night, following the phone setting, in two type families.
- **Motion:** only a slow change of light between parts; none under reduced motion.
- **Print:** every chapter and tool prints without menus.

## 11. Steelman round 1 (2026-11-21): what the outline misses

Method:
- **Eight hostile readers** attacked the outline. The reader in crisis outranks the others.
- **Search data** was checked again (autocomplete phrases the outline did not cover).
- **Overlap** with the rest of the library was measured: The Sovereign Divine Feminine already uses "safety plan" 27 times, "grey rock" 9 times and "hoovering" 8 times; the Decoder has one page each on trauma bonding and hoovering.

### 11.1 The biggest blind spot: the outline assumes one kind of reader

The outline was written for someone who *chose* to leave, has *already moved out*, and is *the target* of the abuse. Five large groups fall outside that, and each one searches in large numbers:

| Reader the outline misses | Evidence (searches) | Fix |
|---|---|---|

| Was left (discarded) | "after a narcissist breaks up with you", "hoovering after discard", "when a narcissist leaves you" | **New: If they left you** |
