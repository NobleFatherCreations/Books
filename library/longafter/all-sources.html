Where does the information in this book come from?

The short answer

This page lists every source this guide cites as domestic abuse research sources, 263 in all: research studies and books, laws and official guidance, and the services and organisations named in the chapters. Each one shows which chapters use it, with a link where one could be confirmed.

Information, not legal, medical or therapeutic advice. If you are in danger, contact your local emergency services. Full notice.

Every chapter of this book ends with its own list of sources. This page brings them together, so you, an advocate or a researcher can see the whole base the book stands on. It’s here for checking, not for reading straight through, and it doubles as a list of domestic abuse research sources for anyone studying the subject.

How are the sources chosen?

The book uses published research where it exists, the laws and official guidance that apply, and the national services that people can call. Where research is thin or disputed, the chapter says so. What this book might have wrong explains the weak spots, and For professionals explains how to cite the book.

How do the links work?

Research articles link to their DOI, a permanent address for the article, where one could be matched with confidence. Services link to their own websites. Laws in the UK link to a search on the official legislation site. 104 of 263 sources have a link. Laws, helpline numbers and services change, so check the current position where you live. The A to Z explains the terms.
