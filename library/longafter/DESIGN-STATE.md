## 1. Summary

The Long After is **safe, careful and accurate, and it is thin, hedged and visually generic.**

- **Content.** It covers the right ground in the right order, and its safety work (chapter 28, no timelines, no prescribed feelings) is excellent. But chapters average **211 words**: about one minute of reading on subjects like family court, protective orders and hoovering. Readers arriving from search need ten times that.
- **Voice.** The brief promises "the most patient voice, short paragraphs, opened at 2am". The prose instead runs long, abstract and padded with reassurance words. Examples: "specific" appears 65 times, "actually" 47, "genuinely" 46 and "rather than" 43; the average sentence is 22 words, and 41 sentences run past 35.
- **Design.** The book wears the same skeleton as The Silence, The Repair, At Will and The Slow Take: the same typewriter top bar, the same mono labels, the same white cards. The only distinctive thing on the page is the new cover. It opens in a light lavender theme, which is wrong for a book read at 2am.
- **Search.** "Hoovering" alone is searched **60,500 times a month** in the US, with low difficulty (31/100). "Post separation abuse" is searched 1,000 a month and rising, from 720 in July to 1,000 in October. The book has a chapter for each, at 261 and about 200 words. This is the biggest search opportunity in the library outside the Decoder.

**Recommended order:** voice and content first, then design, then the search layer. Expanded chapters change every layout, and search titles should describe the final text.

### 2.4 Voice

**The brief:**
- the most patient voice in the library
- short paragraphs
- read slowly
- often re-read
- opened at 2am
- never prescriptive

**What the text does, measured across 9,504 words:**

| Habit | Count | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| "specific / specifically" | 65 | padding; reads as a model hedging |
| "actually / actual" | 47 | implies the reader was wrong before |
| "genuine / genuinely" | 46 | reassurance that weakens what it touches |
| "rather than" | 43 | every sentence corrects an imagined misreading |
| "chapter twenty-seven" style cross-references | 40 | the book keeps pointing away from itself |
| em and en dashes | 57 (6.0 per 1,000 words) | the house limit is 3 per 1,000 |
| "both are true at once" | 15 | a good idea, said so often it becomes a tic |
| "What actually helps" style headings | 10 | the same template heading on every chapter |
| average sentence | 21.6 words; 41 over 35 | not "short paragraphs at 2am" |

These are AI-writing tells. The book was drafted in a pipeline, and the reader can feel it: every chapter has the same three-part shape (claim, pull quote, "What actually helps") and the same careful, abstract register.

**Proposed voice: "the friend who sat through the hearing".**
- Second person, present tense, short sentences.
- **One concrete picture per section:** the car park at handover, the phone face down on the table, the envelope from the court, the 3am kitchen.
- Says the hard thing once, plainly, and does not apologise for it.
- Keeps every safety line and every "no timeline" promise exactly as strong.
- **Never uses:** "genuinely", "actually", "specifically", "it's important to note", "rather than" chains, "not X, but Y" reveals, triplet lists for rhythm, or more than 3 dashes per 1,000 words.
- Chapter headings in the reader's own words ("When they text on your birthday") instead of template headings ("What actually helps").
- **Cross-references become links.** "Chapter sixteen covers documentation" becomes "Keep a record of it."

**Example: one passage from chapter 8, shown only to illustrate the direction. It is not applied.**

> Today: "Support payments that arrive late, incomplete, or require repeated chasing, not due to genuine hardship, but as a way of maintaining a foothold of control and disruption in your life."
>
> Direction: "The payment is late again. It is always late the week you have something good happening. That pattern is the point. Write down the date it was due and the date it came."

**Two ways to do it (owner picks):**
- **A. Light edit.** Remove the tics, shorten the sentences and keep each chapter's structure. Low risk and fast.
- **B. Full voice pass with the expansion.** Each chapter is rewritten in the new voice as it grows, and the owner approves each one. This is the only way the book gets a voice of its own. **Recommended.**

### 2.5 Sources (missing today)
- **The book cites nothing.** Chapter 43 says "well evidenced" and chapter 2 says "the domestic-abuse literature", with no references.
- **Proposal: a short sources list per chapter**, on the model of The Fracture: government guidance, major charities' published guidance and peer-reviewed reviews.
- **Why:** it builds reader trust and makes the claims checkable. It also strengthens how Google and AI assistants judge the book (expertise and trust signals).

## 3. Visual design audit

### 3.1 Machine checks (`design-gates.mjs`)
No failing checks. Four warnings:
- **Tiny text:** the vow chips are 11.8 px.
- **Tap targets under 40 px:** A−, A+, Leave and Dark are 30 px tall. The Leave button is the safety exit and should be the easiest thing to hit.
- **Four font families:** BookSerif, BookDisplay, BookMono and system-ui. The house rule is at most two.
- **Low focal point:** screens lack one clear main element.

### 3.2 What the screenshots show (ranked)
1. **It opens in light lavender.** It ignores the phone's dark setting (the phone-dark profile is identical to light), so readers at 2am get a bright screen. Dark exists only behind a button.
2. **The NF seal covers reading text on phones.** In chapter 16 it sits over "messages, calls, an…" at the right edge.
3. **The chapters are boxes inside boxes.** Every section of every chapter sits in a white card with a squiggle icon. It reads as an app settings screen, not a book (playbook failure B3/B5).
4. **The body text is small and grey:** 16.5 px in the muted ink, where the playbook asks for at least 17 px in full ink for sustained reading.
5. **The contents are 45 identical white cards** with coral "heavy" tags. Nothing shows the shape of the book or the seven movements at a glance (B5).
6. **The top bar and labels are typewriter mono**, identical to the four sibling books (B1: same template, colours swapped). The truncated safety line "If this has be…" is hard to read on phones.
7. **The movement breaks are almost invisible.** The documented "horizon that fades out at both ends" is a faint hairline.
8. **The end of the book is a block of mono text** (colophon and updates). There is no closing moment for a book whose last chapter is about the long view.
9. **The new cover is the one distinctive asset**, and nothing else on the page takes from it: not its colours, its horizon, its path or its warm dusk light.

**Verdict: competent but generic.** Swap the cover and the colour and it could be any of five books.

### 3.3 Three directions to sketch (the owner picks by picture; nothing built yet)

All three keep:
- the Leave button and Esc
- the `#/help` route and the safety line
- the chapter-28 route
- THE HOUSE menu
- every chapter address and anchor
- chapter numbers and movement labels
- no tracking of any kind

**A. "Dusk to first light" (recommended).** The cover's world becomes the book.
- **Signature move:** each of the seven movements is a time of night. Movement I opens at deep dusk and movement VII at first light. The colour of the page lightens a step per movement, so the reader feels where they are without a progress bar.
- **Type:** the existing Newsreader (two families only: Newsreader for text, one display cut for titles). The typewriter mono is dropped.
- **Contents:** a horizon line with the seven movements as points along it, with chapters listed under each as plain numbered lines, not cards.
- **Chapters:** open text with no boxes, a real horizon rule between sections and pull quotes set large in the margin on desktop.
- **Dark by default**, following the phone's setting.

**B. "The kept notebook".**
- The book's strongest practical idea, the four-line record, becomes its look: a private notebook with ruled lines, hand-set numbering, paper texture in light mode and an ink-blue night page in dark.
- **Signature move:** the record template and checklists look like real pages you would keep.
- **Risk:** it is less about time and healing and more about evidence.

**C. "One lamp".**
- The quietest option: near-black page, warm off-white text and a single pool of warm light at the top of each chapter.
- Almost no decoration. Typography does everything.
- **Signature move:** stillness, with minimal chrome at night.
- **Risk:** it is the closest to The Silence's subtraction argument.

**Budget for whichever wins:**
- one lead style
- one signature move
- one accent: the cover's coral dusk
- at most two type families
- at least 70% of screens quiet, since this is a long reading book
- motion only at movement openings, gentle, and off under reduced motion

### 3.4 Fixes that apply to any direction
- **Dark mode:** default to the phone's dark setting.
- **Tap targets:** at least 44 px, with Leave the largest control.
- **The NF seal:** never over text. Move it into the top bar on phones.
- **Body text:** at least 17.5 px in full-contrast ink, with a measure of 60–68 characters.
- **Fonts:** two families. Drop mono and system-ui.
- **Chips:** at least 14 px for the vow chips (or remove them).
- **A closing screen** for chapter 45.
- **A printable record page.**
