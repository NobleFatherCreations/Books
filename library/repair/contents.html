The Repair

I. Before you decide what you are

  • You looked it upAlmost nobody does. That counts — and for less than you want it to.
  • This book will not call you a monsterIt also will not tell you you’re fine. Both are exits, and both are more comfortable than the third option.
  • The two performancesTotal confession and total denial both end the conversation. The first is worse, because it looks like progress.
  • The honesty floorThree admissions. Without all three, nothing after this chapter functions.
  • Who this is not forFour reasons to be reading this that make it useless or harmful — and what each should do instead.
  • What changes and what doesn’tThe past is fixed. What is available is the next ten years, which is less and more than you came here for.

II. What you actually did

  • The inventoryA written list of observable acts. No motive, no context, no explanation attached.
  • The ones you knew about at the timeAnd the sentence you said to yourself that made it permissible. Its speed is the evidence.
  • The ones you didn’tNot seeing it is a fact about your attention. It is not a defence.
  • The pattern you’d name instantly in someone elseYour own standard, applied to your own record. The resistance to doing it is the finding.
  • Your own fileFrequency, duration, escalation, and what happened when it was named. The fourth is the one that matters.
  • Who has stopped raising things with youThe most diagnostic document in this book, and you can build it without asking anyone.
  • What your apologies have actually been forEnding the subject, or addressing the act? The weeks afterwards answer it.
  • The cost you can name, and the cost you can’tThe visible damage is a fraction. The rest is capacity removed from a person’s life.

III. Why — causes, not excuses

  • The difference, and why it’s practicalA cause that leads nowhere is functioning as an excuse, however accurate it is.
  • What you were taughtExplains a great deal, settles nothing. Everyone in that house had the same information.
  • FearThe most common engine and the least admitted, because it does not fit the picture of someone in control.
  • What you were gettingNobody does this for nothing. The reluctance to name the payoff is proportional to its accuracy.
  • Power you didn’t notice holdingYour bad mood was weather to you and climate to them.
  • The story you told yourself while doing itEvery sustained pattern is narrated. The narration is a mechanism, not a memory.
  • Substance, exhaustion, and the thing you were avoidingReal contributors. They change what to do next and change nothing about the record.
  • Whether you have a conditionA diagnosis explains, sometimes helps, and is not an answer. Self-diagnosis from content is its own trap.

IV. The people you did it to

  • What it was like on the other sideReconstructed from what you already know. Asking them to explain it is a further imposition.
  • Why they stayedExplained by mechanisms you operated. It is not evidence that it wasn’t happening.
  • What you took that isn’t obviousTheir ability to read their own life. That is the durable damage.
  • The children who watchedThey saw more than you think, and what they concluded was about themselves.
  • The people you isolated them fromA specific list of relationships ended, and you were the reason. Restoring them is not yours to do.
  • What they owe youNothing. Not forgiveness, not a hearing, not an account of what it was like.
  • What contact costs themYour need to explain is not a reason to appear.

V. Stopping

  • Stopping is not repairIt comes first, completely. Amends attempted before stopping are a second harm.
  • Removing the conditionsEnvironment beats willpower, and most of this has conditions you can remove today.
  • The moment beforeThere is a window. It is short, it is physical first, and it can be learned.
  • What to do with the urgeNot satisfaction, not suppression. A rehearsed move that takes you out of the room.
  • When you slipA lapse and a return are different events. Treating the first as proof of futility produces the second.
  • Who has to knowOne person outside. Concealment is a condition of the pattern, and privacy here is maintenance.
  • Finding real helpSpecialist help exists. General therapy can make this worse. Knowing the difference is on you.
  • If you are dangerousIf there is a risk of serious harm today, everything else in this book is suspended.

VI. Amends, properly

  • What an apology is forIt is an instrument for their benefit. If it produced relief in you, it was for you.
  • When amends would harm themSometimes the correct amends is absence. Every version of this work omits this chapter.
  • The letter you don’t sendIt does most of the work an apology would have done, without imposing on anyone.
  • Living amendsChanged behaviour over years, most of it unobserved. The absence of an audience is the point.
  • If they never forgive youThe likely outcome. A life has to be built that does not require it.
  • If they doThe more dangerous of the two, because it removes the pressure that was doing the work.

VII. The rest of your life

  • Being wrong in publicThe record is permanent and some people will not update. You pay it anyway.
  • New relationships, honestlyTwo errors: concealing it, and using the disclosure as absolution.
  • Not becoming your worst monthBoth identities come back years later. Both still stop the work.
  • The people who use this book as a shieldYou might be one. There is a test, and it is not administered by you.
  • What you can still beNot redemption. Not nothing. A life measured by what stops happening to the people near you.
