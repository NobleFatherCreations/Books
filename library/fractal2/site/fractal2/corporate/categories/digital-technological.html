Digital & Technological Composites

Corporate & Consulting4 chains

This category does not arrive as an event. It runs in the background of everything else. A data room, a shared drive, a workspace channel, a cap table platform, a CRM, a client portal, a meeting transcription tool that quietly joins every call. Each one was adopted for a good reason: speed, security, convenience, institutional polish. And somewhere along the way the question of who holds the data, who can see it, and who can switch it off stops being asked. The mechanism is not a conversation you can point to. It is infrastructure.

Read the full introduction

In this sector, the infrastructure carries the relationship. The advisor’s platform holds your financial model, your investor contacts, your board materials, your deliverables and the history of what was agreed. The fund administrator’s portal holds your capital account. The employer’s systems hold your scores, your messages and your equity ledger. When all of that sits inside a system one party controls, leaving gets expensive in a new way: your files, your access, your history and your working relationships may all be tied to a login you do not own. The sector dresses this up as efficiency, security, collaboration and “one source of truth.” Often it really is those things. Disclosed monitoring of work systems and access controls on a data room are ordinary risk management, and a long set of terms and conditions is not evidence of a plan.

Concern rises when monitoring introduced for security or efficiency quietly widens, when the people affected by the data cannot see or export it, when access is changed without notice, when what you said in a tool is quoted back to you later, and when the terms are too long, too late or too tangled to review before you depend on them. Recording, monitoring and privacy rules differ by jurisdiction and by agreement, so do not assume what is allowed on either side.

The defense is mostly practical and mostly done before you adopt anything. Ask what is collected, who can see it, how long it is kept and how you get your own data out. Read the clauses that cover ownership, export, termination and access on exit. Keep copies of your own work, lawfully held, on accounts and devices you control. Keep your own record of who has administrative rights. Do not reach into systems or files you are not authorized to open, even when you suspect something is hidden there. If you believe access has been used improperly, take that to counsel or the appropriate compliance body. The one composite in this category, the Platform Lock-In, is a good reminder that the strongest hold is rarely a threat. It is a login.
