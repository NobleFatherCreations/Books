Before you sign

The patterns that live in contracts and money

Patterns tagged with signing, contracts, money or royalties. The ten that matter most come first.

Read this first

If anyone is in danger, contact emergency services. If a child is involved, contact child protection.

77 patterns

Two tests and one phrase from each pattern, in the pattern's own words. Law and contracts vary by place: have an independent adviser read anything before you sign it.

  1. 01 The Debt Prison

    Exit Prevention & Entrapment

    • The Ledger Test. "Can I reproduce what I owe, from which income, and when it could clear?" Concern rises if: "No, and the answer is always 'it's complicated.'"
    • The Clause Test. "Does my balance follow me from this record to the next?" Concern rises if: "Yes, and I was told it was a technicality."

    You could say: "Could you send me an itemized recoupment statement with each category listed?"

  2. 02 The Phantom Equity

    Exit Prevention & Entrapment

    • The One-Page Test. "Can we put what I get, what I give, and when it ends on one page this month?" Concern rises if: the answer is a reason to wait, or an explanation instead of a page.
    • The Trigger Test. "What exact event releases the paperwork, and by what date must it happen?" Concern rises if: the event has already changed once and no date is offered.

    You could say: "Before I start the spring run, can we put the founding-contributor terms on one page, even roughly?"

  3. 03 The Erased Contributor

    Institutional & Systemic

    • The Draft Test. "Does the earliest dated file show my contribution before it appeared under another name?" Concern rises if: the dated file exists and nobody wants to look at it.
    • The Hand-off Test. "At which step in the chain did my name disappear, and who made that change?" Concern rises if: no one can say, and each person points at the next.

    You could say: "I'd like to finish my point on the second verse, and then I'm happy to hear yours."

  4. 07 The Economics Obfuscation Layer

    Accountability Evasion

    • The One-Page Test. "Can someone show me income, every deduction and my net on one page?" Concern rises if: no one can, or the answer is a portal that shows only one entity.
    • The Ownership Test. "Which of these companies are related, and who owns each?" Concern rises if: the answer is vague, or you learn of the link from the paperwork itself.

    You could say: "Before I sign, could you send everything related to this agreement, from every entity, together?"

  5. 08 The NDA Silencer

    Accountability Evasion

    • The Scope Test. "Does 'confidential information' cover what I saw and how people were treated, or only unreleased work and business terms?" Concern rises if: it covers behavior, relationships and conditions.
    • The Carve-Out Test. "Does the agreement say I can still speak to authorities, doctors, therapists and my own lawyer?" Concern rises if: it says nothing, or says the opposite.

    You could say: "I'd like more time to review this with my own lawyer before I respond."

  6. 09 The Soft Sell

    Capture & Attachment

    • The Total Test. "What will I have paid by the end of the first year, with every add-on?" Concern rises if: the page cannot show one clear number before you click.
    • The Exit Test. "Can I end this in as few steps as it took to start it?" Concern rises if: cancelling needs more screens, a call or a "Are you sure?" loop.

    You could say: "Before I sign up, can you tell me the total I'll be charged after the trial?"

  7. 10 The Memory Hole

    Reality Distortion & Perception Control

    • The Draft Test. "Where is the later version that replaced this one, and who signed it?" Concern rises if: no document can be shown.
    • The Purge Test. "Will the purge pause for material that is currently questioned?" Concern rises if: it will not, or nobody can say.

    You could say: "A figure from those shows is in question. Please confirm in writing that the related files will be retained."

  8. The "Both Sides" Equalizer

    Accountability Evasion

    • The Direction Test. "Does anything in this document say who did what, or only that tension existed?" Concern rises if: no one's conduct is described, and the grievance that started it is absent.
    • The Chair Test. "Who chose the facilitator, who is paying them, and who do they work for the rest of the year?" Concern rises if: the answer is the manager or the party with more influence.

    You could say: "Could I take the draft home and have my lawyer read it before I sign?"

  9. The Accountability Black Hole

    Accountability Evasion

    • The Name Test. "Can anyone tell me who submitted this registration, and on what date?" Concern rises if: every party names another party and none names a person.
    • The Restart Test. "How many times have I had to explain this from the beginning?" Concern rises if: new contacts routinely have no record of earlier conversations.

    You could say: "Could you confirm who submitted the registration that lists five writers, and on what date?"

  10. The Accountability Firewall

    Accountability Evasion

    • The Same Question Test. "If I ask the same question twice, do I get the same answer twice?" Concern rises if: the answer changes shape, subject or speaker each time.
    • The Name Test. "Who decided that this rate would apply, and can I have their name or role in writing?" Concern rises if: the answer is a system, a vendor or a department.

    You could say: "I'd like to take one question at a time. On the statement for [period], the rate shown is lower than the schedule at page [N]. Which clause accounts for the difference?"

  11. The Afterlife

    Exit Prevention & Entrapment

    • The Knowledge Test. "How does he know where I am, and who told him?" Concern rises if: no one did, and a sharing setting or device list may explain it.
    • The Name-on-the-Account Test. "Whose name is on my number, my distributor, my website and my mailing list?" Concern rises if: the answer is his, and moving any of them needs his consent.

    You could say: "Please send any requests about the ensemble's assets through my attorney."

  12. The Alliance Fracture

    Social Warfare

    • The Door Test. "Was this offered to the group or to one person?" Concern rises if: the door opened only for the most moderate member.
    • The Scope Test. "What does this agreement forbid me from saying, and does it include that I was ever there?" Concern rises if: it covers the existence of the meetings.

    You could say: "Thank you for the invitation. I'd like to share it with the steering committee before responding."

  13. The Allocation Scarcity Engine

    Capture & Attachment

    • The Rival Test. "Can anyone show me, in writing, how many others have actually confirmed?" Concern rises if: the answer is a promise of confidentiality and a reminder of the clock.
    • The Attendee Test. "Can I speak to, or get written confirmation from, the person who is meant to attend?" Concern rises if: the person is "keen," "being confirmed," or only reachable through the sender.

    You could say: "Thank you. I'll respond within five working days of reviewing the full terms. Please send the agreement and refund policy in writing."

  14. Bait and Switch

    Capture & Attachment

    • The Audience Test. "Who exactly will be in the room, and can you confirm it in writing?" Concern rises if: the answers are general and nobody will name anyone.
    • The Invoice Test. "Why am I being asked to pay for a room that is meant to discover me?" Concern rises if: the explanation is that this is how everyone gets seen.

    You could say: "Before I commit, can you tell me in writing who has confirmed attendance, and in what role?"

  15. The Big Ask

    Conditioning & Behavioral Shaping

    • The Distance Test. "Is this draft fair on its own terms, or only fair compared with the first one?" Concern rises if: the second draft feels good mainly because of the first.
    • The First-Yes Test. "Did I say anything that could be quoted back as agreement, and was that my intention?" Concern rises if: a casual reply is now described as acceptance in principle.

    You could say: "I've delivered the theme as a courtesy. I need the agreement signed before I send more."

  16. The Blame Reversal Engine

    Accountability Evasion

    • The Direction Test. "Is the conversation now about my question, or about me?" Concern rises if: the meeting spends more time on how, to whom and when I asked than on whether the fee was paid.
    • The Calendar Test. "Did any of this criticism exist in writing before I raised my concern?" Concern rises if: every item in the review is dated after, or refers to events no one mentioned at the time.

    You could say: "Thank you for the feedback. Before we go further, can we confirm which version of the January fee and the draft credit is correct?"

  17. Bureaucratic Violence

    Institutional & Systemic

    • The Closure Test. "What would I have to send for this matter to be closed?" Concern rises if: nobody will say, or the list grows each time.
    • The Specifics Test. "Which practice, which date and which clause?" Concern rises if: the reply restates the concern without naming any of them.

    You could say: "Could you list the specific items still outstanding, so that I can close this out?"

  18. The Captured Referee

    Institutional & Systemic

    • The Appointment Test. "Who chose the people on this panel, and who pays for it?" Concern rises if: the answer is the party it oversees.
    • The Complaint Test. "Can an artist reach this body without going through the organizer?" Concern rises if: no, or the route is only a liaison inside the organization.

    You could say: "Could you send me the charter and the process for appointing panel members?"

  19. The Circular Referral Loop

    Accountability Evasion

    • The Owner Test. "Who, by name and in writing, is responsible for resolving this?" Concern rises if: each party names someone else.
    • The Window Test. "What is the objection or audit window in my agreement, and how much of it is left?" Concern rises if: nobody has mentioned it and it is nearly gone.

    You could say: "Thank you. Before this goes anywhere else, who is responsible for the outcome of this query?"

  20. Coercive Control

    Institutional & Systemic

    • The Switch-Off Test. "Can I turn off tracking or refuse to share my location without a penalty?" Concern rises if: I cannot, or the answer is a conversation that frightens me.
    • The Ledger Test. "Can I see every statement, budget and contract about my own career?" Concern rises if: I am told no, or that it is too boring.

    You could say: "I'd like to have a copy of my royalty statements and the tour budget for my own files."

  21. The Collective Punishment Deflector

    Accountability Evasion

    • The Itemization Test. "What exactly did the delay cost, and how does that figure become one day for each person?" Concern rises if: no figure exists, or asking for one is treated as the problem.
    • The Name Test. "Has the person actually involved been spoken to directly, and is that on record?" Concern rises if: the notice stays general because naming anyone would require documenting a decision.

    You could say: "Could you help me understand how the cost was worked out and how it became one day for each of us?"

  22. The Confusion Protocol

    Reality Distortion & Perception Control

    • The Loop Test. "Can you tell me this without using a defined term?" Concern rises if: the answer sends you to the term you asked about.
    • The Scope Test. "What exactly does 'deemed' include, and what does it leave out?" Concern rises if: no one will state the scope in writing.

    You could say: "Could you tell me in one plain sentence what this changes about the term of my older works?"

  23. The Crisis Covenant

    Capture & Attachment

    • The Terms Test. "Is the deferral in writing, with an end date and a way to be repaid?" Concern rises if: it is open-ended and described as a matter of trust.
    • The Who-Is-Owed Test. "Who is being kept from knowing this, and are they owed it?" Concern rises if: the answer is artists or partners who rely on accurate information.

    You could say: "Thank you for telling me. Could you send me a short summary of the position, with figures?"

  24. The Dependency Web

    Exit Prevention & Entrapment

    • The Statement Test. "Has the performer seen what has been earned, held and spent?" Concern rises if: "No, and asking is discouraged."
    • The Door Test. "How many separate people could the performer talk to about this, besides the gatekeeper?" Concern rises if: "None, and the others have faded."

    You could say: (To a parent:) "I'd like us to have an independent trustee for the account, so the work stays about the music."

  25. Divide and Conquer

    Social Warfare

    • The Two-Packet Test. "If I laid both groups' documents next to each other, would they describe the same proposal the same way?" Concern rises if: figures, dates or framing differ, and nobody has explained why.
    • The Hub Test. "Do messages between us only travel through one person?" Concern rises if: attempts to talk directly are discouraged or postponed.

    You could say: "Could I see what the other group received, so we're all working from the same facts?"

  26. The Double Bind Trap

    Reality Distortion & Perception Control

    • The Both-Clauses Test. "Is there any action that satisfies both clauses at once?" Concern rises if: nobody can name one.
    • The Ruling Test. "Will you confirm in writing which clause governs?" Concern rises if: the answer is "we'll be reasonable."

    You could say: "Clause 7 asks me to promote and clause 11 restricts announcements. Could you confirm which governs?"

  27. Emotional Debt Spiral

    Capture & Attachment

    • The Tab Test. "If someone is counting hours or favors, why am I hearing about it now?" Concern rises if: the tally appears when you assert independence.
    • The Exit Test. "What happens when I say I'm working with someone else?" Concern rises if: the reaction is hurt, silence or a list of what has been given.

    You could say: "Thank you for the time. Before we go further, can we agree in writing how studio hours are handled?"

  28. The Exception Script

    Specialized Exploitation

    • The Exception Test. "Would I be given the same message and the same timeline if I were any other artist?" Concern rises if: the answer is that I am special and therefore it doesn't apply.
    • The Paper Test. "Does the draft say, in writing, what I was promised in conversation?" Concern rises if: the vivid future appears nowhere in the document, or only as discretionary.

    You could say: "Thank you, I'm honored. I'd like a week to review this with my own attorney."

  29. Exit Prevention Architecture

    Exit Prevention & Entrapment

    • The Staple Test. "Are my option, my housing and my debt being delivered as one package, and could I price each one separately?" Concern rises if: "No one can show me each on its own, and asking feels like betrayal."
    • The Doom Test. "Who is predicting what happens if I leave, and what are they basing it on?" Concern rises if: "The predictions are vast, unsourced and only appear when I mention leaving."

    You could say: "I want to understand the option notice. Can you send me the full clause and the dates in writing?"

  30. The Expert Validation Shield

    Accountability Evasion

    • The Timeline Test. "When was this framework first drafted, relative to the date the consultant was engaged?" Concern rises if: nobody will say, or the framework came first.
    • The Brief Test. "Who wrote the consultant's scope, and which alternatives were modeled?" Concern rises if: the scope has not been shared and only one model was analyzed.

    You could say: "When was the preferred framework first drafted, and when was the consultant engaged?"

  31. Forced Dependency

    Exit Prevention & Entrapment

    • The Column Test. "Are my health and the tour's recoupment treated as the same line?" Concern rises if: "Rest is described as a cost to others, not as a health matter."
    • The Paper Test. "Can I see the cancellation terms, insurance and routing that affect me?" Concern rises if: "Someone else holds them and tells me not to worry."

    You could say: "I need to see a specialist, and I'd like to understand what the schedule allows."

  32. The Frog in Boiling Water

    Conditioning & Behavioral Shaping

    • The Baseline Test. "What was I hired to do, and what am I doing now?" Concern rises if: the difference is large, unwritten and entirely in one direction.
    • The Staircase Test. "If these changes had been listed together on day one, would I have agreed?" Concern rises if: the answer is no.

    You could say: "Could you confirm in writing what my duties and day rate are for the rest of the run?"

  33. The Gamification Trap

    Digital & Technological

    • The Missed-Day Test. "What happens if I take a day off?" Concern rises if: the count resets, and I feel dread.
    • The Reason Test. "If the badges disappeared tomorrow, would I still be here?" Concern rises if: no one can answer, or the answer is no.

    You could say: (To the community team:) "Before I say yes, can you tell me what the moderator role involves and how many hours a week?"

  34. The Gaslighting Machine

    Reality Distortion & Perception Control

    • The Noodles Test. "If I send a short written summary of what was said, will the other person confirm or correct it?" Concern rises if: they prefer to talk it through by phone and will not engage with the written version.
    • The Size Test. "Who is calling this small, and what do they gain if I agree?" Concern rises if: the person shrinking the matter is the one who would pay, and will not write the number down.

    You could say: "My understanding from our conversation is that mixing points would be set out in a side letter. Can you confirm that in writing?"

  35. The Golden Cage

    Exit Prevention & Entrapment

    • The Replacement Test. "If this engagement ended, what would it cost me to replace housing, a car and health coverage?" Concern rises if: "I don't know, and finding out feels disloyal."
    • The Cash Test. "Is my pay competitive without the perks, or do the perks make up the difference?" Concern rises if: "The perks are doing the work that pay should do."

    You could say: "I'm grateful for the housing and the plan, and I'd like to review the renewal terms before we talk."

  36. Golden Handcuffs

    Exit Prevention & Entrapment

    • The Draw Test. "Is my monthly payment a salary or a loan against my future income?" Concern rises if: "It is recoupable, and I cannot see how the balance clears."
    • The Portable Test. "If I left, what could I take with me: songs, relationships, reputation?" Concern rises if: "Almost nothing, and I'm told that's normal."

    You could say: "I'd like the draw statement broken out so I can see draws, credited income, reserves and deductions."

  37. Grooming

    Capture & Attachment

    • The Packet Test. "Can we take the full enrollment packet home to an independent lawyer before deciding?" Concern rises if: "This weekend's rate is only for families who sign before they leave."
    • The Secret Test. "Is my child being asked to keep anything from me or from the other participants?" Concern rises if: yes, even when it is framed as a compliment or a special selection.

    You could say: "This sounds exciting. We review every agreement with our own lawyer, so we'll take it home tonight."

  38. The Hostage Scenario

    Exit Prevention & Entrapment

    • The Same-Breath Test. "Was my relative mentioned in the same conversation as what I was asked to sign or keep quiet about?" Concern rises if: the two were linked, even gently.
    • The Closed-Door Test. "Was the remark made where no one else could hear?" Concern rises if: the setting was chosen so that there would be no witness.

    You could say: "I'll sign once my attorney has reviewed it. I'll send it back as soon as that's done."

  39. The Identity Lock

    Exit Prevention & Entrapment

    • The Third-Person Test. "Do people in the room speak about my persona as if it were separate from me, and as if they own it?" Concern rises if: the persona is discussed as an asset and your own words for yourself are never asked.
    • The Merits Test. "Has anyone listened to the acoustic songs and said what they think of the music?" Concern rises if: every response is about the brand, and none is about the songs.

    You could say: "I'd like to understand what would make an acoustic release approvable. Could you send me the criteria in writing?"

  40. Knowledge Weaponization

    Institutional & Systemic

    • The Paper Test. "Can you show me this on paper?" Concern rises if: the answer is an explanation, a credential or a reassurance instead of a document.
    • The Worked Example Test. "Can you walk me through one actual payment, from source to my account?" Concern rises if: the explanation stays in terms and never reaches a number.

    You could say: "Could you send me the underlying statements for the last three years? I'd like to read them myself."

  41. The Learned Helplessness Machine

    Conditioning & Behavioral Shaping

    • The Owner Test. "Can anyone tell me the name of the person or role who decides my claim?" Concern rises if: "No, it's always 'the team' or 'the policy.'"
    • The Same-Input Test. "When I do exactly what was asked, does anything change?" Concern rises if: "No, and a new requirement appears."

    You could say: "Who is the decision-maker on this claim, and how can I reach them?"

  42. The Long Con

    Digital & Technological

    • The Familiar Stranger Test. "How does this person know so much about me, and can I check how?" Concern rises if: the details came from public posts you did not connect.
    • The Door Test. "Why does this conversation need to move off the public page to a private app?" Concern rises if: the reason is quiet, privacy or trust, and the app is the contact's suggestion.

    You could say: "I enjoy our conversations. I don't use other apps for business. If you'd like to keep talking, we can do it here."

  43. The Loyalty Test

    Conditioning & Behavioral Shaping

    • The Clock Test. "What is the actual reason this answer is needed by this exact time, and who set it?" Concern rises if: the reason is vague or the time moves when you ask.
    • The Third Door Test. "Besides everything or nothing, what else is possible: a later start, a shorter set, a new date?" Concern rises if: the question is treated as lack of commitment.

    You could say: "I want to decide this properly. What time exactly do you need an answer, and why that time?"

  44. Manufactured Consensus

    Social Warfare

    • The Mechanism Test. "What specific activities contributed to this position?" Concern rises if: the answer is "we worked it" with no specifics.
    • The Voice Test. "Who are the people saying this, and are they independent of the campaign?" Concern rises if: the voices are new, similar or linked to the team.

    You could say: "I'm thrilled about the position. Can you walk me through what contributed to it?"

  45. The Meaning Hijack

    Reality Distortion & Perception Control

    • The Dictionary Test. "Where in the document is this word defined, and does the definition match how it is used at dinner?" Concern rises if: the definition is much narrower.
    • The Net Test. "What goes into 'net,' line by line?" Concern rises if: no schedule exists, or the answer is "after costs."

    You could say: "Could you point me to where 'ownership' is defined, and tell me in plain words what it covers?"

  46. The Mirror Hook

    Capture & Attachment

    • The Name Test. "Who exactly is this, which company do they work for, and how can I check?" Concern rises if: the answer is delayed, vague, or framed as confidential.
    • The Pace Test. "Who is setting the speed of this, and has it picked up since we started?" Concern rises if: contact is intensifying and each step arrives with a warm reason.

    You could say: "I enjoy talking with you. Before we go further, could you tell me the name of the company and a colleague I can contact?"

  47. The Mood Weather

    Conditioning & Behavioral Shaping

    • The Proportion Test. "Is the size of the reaction related to the size of what triggered it?" Concern rises if: a small note brings a large reaction, repeatedly.
    • The Pre-Edit Test. "Am I changing what I say, write or log before anyone has reacted?" Concern rises if: I delete a note or soften a correction because of how I expect it to land.

    You could say: "I'd like to give feedback that actually helps. Can we agree on a time to review notes after each session?"

  48. Obedience Training

    Conditioning & Behavioral Shaping

    • The Silence Test. "What happens when I don't answer or say no?" Concern rises if: coldness, delay or lost opportunity follows, and warmth returns only after you give in.
    • The Proportion Test. "Is the response as big as what I actually did?" Concern rises if: a small decline produces days of consequence.

    You could say: "I'm available for calls between nine and eight. If something is urgent outside that, I'll respond first thing."

  49. The Obligation Web

    Exit Prevention & Entrapment

    • The Ledger Test. "Can anyone tell me what these favors are worth and what I owe?" Concern rises if: "The ledger is never stated, but it is always referenced."
    • The Thread Test. "If I pull one thread, which others tighten?" Concern rises if: "Booking, accounting, legal and my family all move."

    You could say: "I'm so grateful for everything. I'd like my own attorney to read the renewal, as I would for any contract."

  50. The Paper Cage

    Exit Prevention & Entrapment

    • The Balance Test. "Does my balance fall each month by what I was told it would?" Concern rises if: it rises, or new lines keep appearing, without a written reason.
    • The Passport Test. "If I asked for my passport today, could I have it today?" Concern rises if: the answer is later, never, or "why do you want it?"

    You could say: "Could you send me the full breakdown of how my balance is calculated, in writing?"

  51. The Parent Silencer

    Specialized Exploitation

    • The Purpose Test. "What is this identification needed for, and is there a written explanation?" Concern rises if: the answer is only "standard" or "policy," with nothing in writing.
    • The Copy Test. "Who will see a copy of this, and how long is it kept?" Concern rises if: no one can answer and there is no posted policy.

    You could say: "Could you send me in writing which documents are required and why each one is needed?"

  52. Pavlovian Conditioning

    Conditioning & Behavioral Shaping

    • The Criteria Test. "What would a good night look like on paper, and where is that written?" Concern rises if: nobody will name the criteria, and the answer is that you will know it when you see it.
    • The Relief Test. "When my status was restored, what had I changed, and was it about the show?" Concern rises if: the restoration followed a change in your deference or mood, not your performance.

    You could say: "To plan my sets well, could you tell me how the slot rotation works for the support acts?"

  53. Performance Extraction

    Conditioning & Behavioral Shaping

    • The Currency Test. "Am I being paid in money, in a written percentage, or in a promise?" Concern rises if: the payment is only "credit," "exposure" or "next time."
    • The Paper-First Test. "Is there a signed split or agreement before I send anything more?" Concern rises if: it is always "after the label signs."

    You could say: "I'd love to work on this. Before I send anything more, can we confirm the fee, credit and split in writing?"

  54. The Performative Ally

    Specialized Exploitation

    • The Fee Test. "What is the fee for this panel, in writing, and how does it compare to other stages?" Concern rises if: the answer is exposure, wristbands or worry about my wellbeing.
    • The Lineup Test. "Does the booking, not the banner, reflect the pledge?" Concern rises if: the proportions have not changed and nobody can explain why.

    You could say: "I'm glad to speak. Could you confirm the fee and what is included, in writing?"

  55. The Perspective Eraser

    Identity & Psychological Destruction

    • The Question Test. "Was my question about the figure answered?" Concern rises if: the reply was entirely about how I seem.
    • The Minutes Test. "Did both of us see and agree the notes before they became the record?" Concern rises if: one person writes and keeps them, and corrections are met with surprise.

    You could say: "I would like to check I have the decisions right. Here is what I wrote down."

  56. The Platform Lock-In

    Digital & Technological

    • The Export Test. "Can I download my full history today, in a form another service could use?" Concern rises if: not completely, or only for a fee.
    • The Door Test. "Is it as easy to leave as it was to join?" Concern rises if: the exit steps are hidden, and the warnings are long.

    You could say: "Before I upload, can you point me to the takedown and export terms?"

  57. The Positivity Muzzle

    Conditioning & Behavioral Shaping

    • The Answer Test. "Did I get a figure or a date, or did I get a mood?" Concern rises if: the reply is reassurance with no number.
    • The Nickname Test. "When did that nickname appear, and what had I just asked?" Concern rises if: it followed a pay or safety question.

    You could say: "I am glad we have a good team. I would also like a clear answer on the four festival dates."

  58. The Praise Leash

    Conditioning & Behavioral Shaping

    • The Usefulness Test. "Does the praise I receive follow the quality of my work or my availability?" Concern rises if: the warmest praise comes at the hours or times I'm most useful.
    • The Confidence Test. "Has anything I've told in confidence been brought up in a work disagreement?" Concern rises if: yes, even gently, even as concern.

    You could say: "I want to keep doing this well, so I'm going to be offline after midnight unless it's urgent."

  59. Psychological Identity Theft

    Identity & Psychological Destruction

    • The Mirror Test. "Does the person in the marketing materials look, sound and speak like the person who walked in on the first day?" Concern rises if: the changes began after signing, nobody can say who asked for them, and you did not.
    • The Word Test. "Are the labels in the brief describing a plan for presenting me, or describing me?" Concern rises if: the unapproved list contains the qualities that first drew the label to you.

    You could say: "Before I respond to the Persona Brief, could I have a copy and a week to read it?"

  60. The Recursive Trap

    Specialized Exploitation

    • The Both-At-Once Test. "Can I actually do both of these instructions at the same time?" Concern rises if: no, and I have been told to proceed in line with both.
    • The Subject Test. "After I raised the problem, is the conversation about the problem or about me?" Concern rises if: the topic has become my style, my tone, or my pattern.

    You could say: "Instruction A and instruction B appear to conflict for Friday. Which should take priority?"

  61. The Reputation Assassination

    Social Warfare

    • The Specifics Test. "Can someone tell me exactly what the concern is, in writing?" Concern rises if: the answer is "just general," and the note remains.
    • The Timing Test. "Did any doubt about me exist before this decision came into view?" Concern rises if: the first negative sign appears only days before the decision.

    You could say: "Could you share the specific concerns so I can address them before Friday?"

  62. The Reputation Hostage

    Exit Prevention & Entrapment

    • The Shared Name Test. "If I speak up, who is the argument really protecting?" Concern rises if: the reason given is that it would hurt both of us.
    • The Date Test. "On what exact date will the artist be paid?" Concern rises if: there is a promise, but no date, or a date that has already moved.

    You could say: "The duo's guarantee is overdue. Please confirm in writing the amount and the date it will be paid."

  63. The Resource Extraction Pipeline

    Institutional & Systemic

    • The Missing Line Test. "Where in the budget is the cost of the thing I am being thanked for?" Concern rises if: there is no such line, or the line is for in-kind contributions.
    • The Total Test. "What is the total of my own spending and hours this season?" Concern rises if: nobody has counted, including me.

    You could say: "I'd love to do this, and I'd like us to put a number on it. Can we add a line to the budget for scores and materials?"

  64. The Rigged Game

    Conditioning & Behavioral Shaping

    • The Definition Test. "What exactly does a four look like?" Concern rises if: no one can say in writing.
    • The Credit Test. "Who is named as lead on the work I did?" Concern rises if: a more senior person is named and the record of your contribution is hard to find.

    You could say: "Could we agree on what each rating means, so I know what to aim for?"

  65. The Savior Trap

    Capture & Attachment

    • The Rate Test. "Have we said what this costs, or what it is?" Concern rises if: the answer is "we'll sort that out later" for the third time.
    • The Ledger Test. "Can I show how many hours I have given and when?" Concern rises if: nothing is written down and the work keeps growing.

    You could say: "I can give you this weekend at my day rate. I'll send a confirmation email tonight."

  66. The Social Proof Trap

    Social Warfare

    • The Silence Test. "Did anyone actually say yes, or did the room just not say no?" Concern rises if: the record calls silence agreement.
    • The First-Voices Test. "Who spoke first, and had they been told beforehand?" Concern rises if: the opening supporters were briefed and the most affected were asked last.

    You could say: "Thanks for the update. Could you share the old and new rates and how horn invitations will be decided?"

  67. The Sunk Cost Prison

    Exit Prevention & Entrapment

    • The Definition Test. "What exactly does 'commercially satisfactory' mean, and who decides?" Concern rises if: no one will put it in writing, or the answer changes with the person asked.
    • The Direction Test. "Are the latest notes refining the last version, or pointing somewhere new?" Concern rises if: each round redirects the record and the previous round's work is discarded.

    You could say: "Could you confirm in writing what 'commercially satisfactory' means for this delivery and who makes the call?"

  68. Surveillance Architecture

    Digital & Technological

    • The Page Eleven Test. "Could I find, today, the exact sentence in my paperwork that covers recording of my calls?" Concern rises if: it is short, vague and nobody drew my attention to it.
    • The Recipient Test. "Who, by name or role, can hear or read what I say in these sessions?" Concern rises if: nobody will answer in writing.

    You could say: "Could you point me to the part of the packet that covers recording of my calls?"

  69. Systemic Entrapment

    Institutional & Systemic

    • The Missing Schedule Test. "Does every schedule that sets the price of leaving appear in my copy of the agreement?" Concern rises if: one is missing, and the answer is that it is available on request.
    • The Exit Quote Test. "Can the company give me a written figure for leaving today, with a date it is valid?" Concern rises if: the figure is "to be confirmed" and no one is named.

    You could say: "Could you send me the full agreement including Schedule 4?"

  70. The Trauma Bond Engine

    Capture & Attachment

    • The Body Test. "How does my body respond when I picture being back in that room, under those conditions?" Concern rises if: the answer is dread that the offer's warmth and money are being asked to talk me out of.
    • The Timing Test. "When did the warmth arrive, and what was the request that followed it?" Concern rises if: relief repeatedly arrives just before a request and goes cold after a no.

    You could say: "Thank you for reaching out. I'll respond in writing by the end of the week."

  71. The Unwinnable Conversation

    Reality Distortion & Perception Control

    • The One-Claim Test. "Can we take one claim at a time and look at the document for it?" Concern rises if: the answer is that this is nitpicking, or the list simply continues.
    • The Premise Test. "Where is that written, so I can read it?" Concern rises if: the premise appears in no agreement, registration or statement and is just asserted as obvious.

    You could say: "I would like to look at one item today: the writing credit on the fiddle tune."

  72. The Velvet Threat

    Accountability Evasion

    • The Timing Test. "Did the remark about the licence, the file or the renewal come soon after I raised a concern?" Concern rises if: it did, and was not raised with anyone else.
    • The Quote Test. "Can I repeat the exact words without them sounding harmless?" Concern rises if: no, and yet my body reacted.

    You could say: "Thanks for mentioning the renewal. Is there anything in it that involves me, or that I can help with?"

  73. The Voluntary Departure Rewrite

    Accountability Evasion

    • The Strength Test. "Is the 'misalignment' the same thing my results were praised for last year?" Concern rises if: the strength has become the problem.
    • The Third Door Test. "Is staying, mediation or reassignment on the table?" Concern rises if: both options lead out.

    You could say: "I'd like the specific objectives and the criteria behind the 'alignment' feedback, in writing."

  74. The Wall

    Accountability Evasion

    • The Name Test. "Who is the person who can decide this invoice?" Concern rises if: no one can be named, or the name changes with every call.
    • The Repeat Test. "Have I been asked for the same document more than once?" Concern rises if: yes, and each time it was already in their possession.

    You could say: "Could you tell me who owns this invoice and when I can expect a decision?"

  75. The Weaponized Gift Economy

    Specialized Exploitation

    • The Label Test. "Is this a gift, an advance, or a loan, and where is that written?" Concern rises if: nobody will say, and the answer changes later.
    • The Receipt Test. "What happens when I ask for the paperwork?" Concern rises if: the response is hurt rather than a document.

    You could say: "Thank you, this is generous. Could you confirm in writing whether it is a gift, an advance, or a loan?"

  76. Weaponized Therapy Language

    Identity & Psychological Destruction

    • The Separation Test. "Would this same observation have been made if I had agreed with the clause?" Concern rises if: the comment about my state appears only when I object.
    • The Notes Test. "Who reads what is written about me in these sessions?" Concern rises if: management or business affairs can see it and I did not agree to that specifically.

    You could say: "I appreciate the support. I'd like to keep my sessions separate from the contract discussion."

  77. The Whistleblower Inversion

    Accountability Evasion

    • The Calendar Test. "What changed in how I am treated, and on what date, compared with the date of my filing?" Concern rises if: the first new scrutiny, document or rating change falls within days or weeks after it.
    • The Source Test. "Who raised this concern, when, and about which specific event?" Concern rises if: the answer is "a colleague" or "the team," with nothing you can respond to.

    You could say: "I'd like to confirm in writing what happened to the account mapping issue I reported."

Universal Evidence To Preserve

In this sector the paper that matters is often spread across inboxes, DMs, shared drives, dashboards and a manager's phone, and much of it is created in a hurry around sessions, showcases and signings. Keep what you lawfully hold, in its original form where possible, with dates visible.

  • Agreements and their history: signed and unsigned drafts, redlines, term sheets, offer emails and any "final" version that differs from the one discussed; recording, publishing, co-publishing, administration, management, producer, booking, merchandise and brand or likeness agreements; option and delivery clauses; NDAs and non-disparagement clauses; minor performer contracts and guardian arrangements.
  • Creative and credit records: split sheets and writer credit confirmations (with the date they were signed relative to the session), work-for-hire and session releases, beat licenses, sample clearance records, voice memos and demos with timestamps, session logs, studio invoices and booking confirmations.
  • Money records: advance and draw payments, recoupment statements, royalty statements and accounting summaries, distribution and streaming dashboard exports, tour budgets, settlement sheets, merch settlement records, payment records for showcases or program fees, commission invoices, and bank records showing what was actually paid and received.
  • Registration and metadata: ISRC and work registrations, metadata screenshots, platform and distributor account records, policy notices and playlist pitch records.
  • Scheduling and obligation records: call sheets, itineraries, calendar invites, content calendars and posting requirements, riders and technical riders, delivery notices and default letters, work permits and guardian consents where minors are involved, and medical or wellness accommodation requests.
  • Communications: emails, DMs and texts with labels, managers, producers, agents, publicists and assistants, especially around session and signing dates; written summaries of verbal promises ("per our call, my understanding is..."); audit requests and responses; union or guild correspondence; second legal opinion notes.
  • People: names of those present at signings, sessions and meetings, and who recommended which lawyer, accountant or manager.
Preserve records lawfully. Do not secretly record where it is illegal. Do not access systems, files, accounts, or documents you are not authorized to access.
