The Drop

“The DanceSafe Pin”

The cold open

📍 WOOK POV

The DanceSafe pin is on my snapback.

Not ironically. I want to be clear about that because the word ironically will occur to some people and I need them to understand that the word does not apply. I support DanceSafe. I read their materials. I update my Telegram harm-reduction FAQ after every festival based on what I observe at the medical tents, which is more field research than most of the people at DanceSafe’s informational table have ever done, with respect to those people, who are doing genuinely important work. I have Narcan in the Sprinter. Three units, not one. The Narcan is real. The pin is real. I am, by any meaningful operational metric, running one of the most harm-reduction-aware supply operations in the western US festival circuit.

My name is Wedge.

I am forty-seven. I have been doing this for nineteen years. My Sprinter is a 2022 high-roof cargo, custom interior, two locking compartments, refrigeration unit I installed myself because temperature stability matters for pressed pills and also because I bring very good cheese and the cheese deserves the same respect as the product. The Sprinter is clean in every sense of the word. It does not smell like what you would expect it to smell like. It smells like sandalwood car air freshener and the specific clean-chemical smell of the food-safe containers I use for storage, which I ordered from a restaurant supply company and which are, incidentally, NSF-certified, which is a certification that applies to food safety and which I find provides a certain aesthetic coherence to the operation.

It is 3:12 p.m. on Friday at a 40,000-person bass weekend in the Mojave.

My Telegram has been firing since eleven a.m.

The accounting, as of 3:12 p.m.:

Sixty-eight grams pressed molly. MDMA content verified via Marquis, fentanyl negative, pressed at a trusted lab on the West Coast, consistent yield across the batch. I test everything I move. I test it with the proper kits, I photograph the results, I keep the photos in a folder organized by batch number and date. If something were to go wrong — and in nineteen years, my product has not been the cause of a serious adverse event, which is a record I protect with genuine professional pride — I would have documentation. The documentation is part of the operation. The documentation is part of being one of the good ones.

Twenty-two grams clean K. Same protocol. Tested. Verified. I communicate the harm-reduction guidance with this one specifically because ketamine has dose-dependency concerns that MDMA does not, and I provide accurate dosing information per transaction. Not because I am legally required to. Because it is right, and because customers who are not in the medical tent are return customers, and return customers are good business, and good business and harm reduction are, in my operation, the same thing. People think these values are in tension. In my operation they are identical.

Fourteen grams cut K.

This is the one I do not discuss in the FAQ.

The word for it in the trade is stepping on it. You step on a thing to make it go further. Everyone in the supply chain above me steps on everything, which is the entire reason my testing protocol exists in the first place, and there is a version of that sentence where that fact does moral work on my behalf.

I have used that version of the sentence. I am using it right now.

This is the one going to four specific returning customers whose tolerance has climbed over the past two cycles to a point where the clean product is producing diminishing returns on their satisfaction, and where the cut product — it is cut with a common dissociative adulterant at a ratio that I have personally calibrated to produce approximately eighty percent of the subjective experience at sixty-five percent of the active compound — will, in my assessment, meet their needs at a margin that allows me to maintain pricing parity while managing my supply costs.

They do not know it is cut.

I know it is cut.

The ratio is safe, in my assessment, based on my nineteen years of experience and my specific knowledge of these four customers’ histories. I am not guessing. I have data. I have used this ratio before with customers at similar tolerance profiles. Nothing bad has happened.

I have not disclosed this to them because disclosure would require an explanation that would damage the relationship, and the relationship is what the long game runs on, and the long game is why I am still here at forty-seven with a perfect adverse event record and an operation that has survived nineteen years of the festival circuit. Disclosure would be, in a practical sense, self-defeating. The four customers would be upset not because of harm — the ratio is safe — but because of the framing. The framing would damage trust. The trust is the product, as much as the K.

I have made a professional calculation.

I make professional calculations every day.

This is one of them.

At 4:08 p.m. I take a smoke break outside the Sprinter.

I do not smoke. I take smoke breaks anyway because the smoke-break posture — outside, cigarette or vape in hand, slightly looking at nothing — is the universal signal for I am not conducting business right now, I am just a person standing outside a van. The smoke break is the visible inactivity that makes the invisible activity easier. I have a vape that I use for this purpose. It produces a vapor that smells like mango and contains zero nicotine. I am one of the good ones in this respect also.

Two women in their mid-twenties are sitting on beanbags fifteen feet from me. They are talking about someone they encountered last night. The description they are giving, in the specific warm-indignant tone of people recounting something that did not reach the level of reportable harm but that left a residue, is of a man who offered them something, would not take their first no, kept moving closer, suggested he had a “private spot” where they could “really talk.”

This is not me. My operation does not involve proximity pressure. If someone says no to what I’m offering, I note it as a preference and move on. I do not push. Pushing is amateur. Pushing is how you get flagged.

The description continues. The man was last seen near the art installations on the east side, they say. He has a specific look — they describe the look. I know the look. I know the person. He is one of three freelance operators I track as displacement risk in the Mojave circuit. He is, of all the freelance operators I track, the most visible and the most problematic. He operates without the discretion that nineteen years of professional practice produces. He makes scenes. He leaves residue. He is the reason harm-reduction organizations associate supply with predation, which creates a cultural environment that is bad for everyone including me.

I take out my phone.

I have a photo of him from an event last summer, taken when he did not know I was taking it, which I have been keeping in a folder labeled “competitors - general” for exactly this kind of situation. I have the parking location of his vehicle. I have the plate number. I have, because I maintain a reasonably thorough operational intelligence function, the number of a festival safety contact I have cultivated over three years of mutual professional courtesy — I tip them when I see something, they do not look too carefully at the Sprinter.

I compose a message. I am not reporting a competitor. I am not protecting market share. I am providing safety information about a documented predatory actor to festival safety personnel who have the authority and the means to act on it. These two things are simultaneous and they are not in conflict. They are, in my accounting of this moment, the same thing.

I hit send.

By Saturday afternoon, he will be removed from the festival.

The women on the beanbags, and every other woman at this festival who would have encountered him between now and his Sunday departure, will be safe from whatever he would have done.

The man who tipped security will be, in the festival safety team’s internal record, a concerned member of the supply community who helps keep the festival safe.

That concerned member is me.

I am one of the good ones.

I am a very good one.

I am, in the specific ledger I maintain in the part of my brain where the accounting happens, one of the best ones at being one of the good ones.

Here is what the chapter needs to say about Wedge before it says anything else:

Everything in his ledger is true.

The Narcan is real. The testing is real. The documentation is real. The harm-reduction FAQ is real and it is actually good and several people have cited it in their own event materials. The competitor he reported is genuinely predatory and is genuinely better off removed from this festival. The nineteen-year adverse-event-free record is real. The professional pride he takes in the operation is genuine. By the standards of most supply operations in most festival contexts, Wedge is running a materially safer, more harm-reduction-conscious, more operationally disciplined business than the alternatives.

None of that is the point.

The point is: he is downgrading four customers’ K without telling them.

The point is: the people who become dependent on him become dependent on him specifically because he has engineered the dependency. The “I got you” character he performs is not separate from the supply. The supply is the delivery mechanism for the character, and the character is the delivery mechanism for the dependency, and the dependency is what makes people return, and what makes people return is what keeps the margins working.

The point is: the warm, competent, DanceSafe-pin-wearing, Narcan-carrying, harm-reduction-FAQ-writing supply operator is the hardest version of this chapter to see because the 95% that is genuinely good makes the 5% that is predatory invisible. And the 5% that is predatory is not smaller in impact because it is 5%. It is larger, because the cover is the safety community’s own infrastructure.

You cannot test for the cut K with a Marquis kit. Marquis tests for the presence of the active compound, not the ratio. You can only find out the ratio is wrong when you buy from someone else and the same dose lands differently.

Or when Wedge decides you’re a high-trust returning customer whose tolerance has climbed to a point where the professional calculation says: cut it.

This is the discernment hook.

Not “is the plug good or bad” — the categories collapse. This plug is both. The discernment hook is: “what is this person’s relationship to my dependency?”

A real vendor wants you informed, capable, and free to buy elsewhere. A Wook plug wants you dependent, trusting, and unable to evaluate what you are actually receiving because the relationship has been specifically engineered to make that evaluation socially costly.

The DanceSafe pin is on the snapback.

The snapback is on Wedge.

Wedge is one of the good ones.

And he is building you a cage.

What this chapter is actually about

This chapter is the last of the safety-primary chapters and the hardest one, because its subject — the operator who is mostly good and uses the mostly-good as cover for the predatory 5% — is the Wook the scene least wants to name. Naming the freelance-operator-in-the-beige-approach-RV is easy. The Tapers’ Section

Dependency Economics · The Coverage Problem · Harm Reduction As Cover

Fast theory. Then the six Tracks.

Dependency economics is not unique to drug supply relationships. Any relationship in which one party controls access to something the other party wants and cannot easily obtain elsewhere produces structural dependency, and structural dependency produces leverage. The supply relationship crystallizes this because the thing being supplied is both physically addictive in some cases and deeply socially embedded in festival culture in all cases. Wedge’s dependency is not chemically constructed in most cases. It is socially constructed: through warmth, reliability, competitive pricing, the sense of being known and cared for, and the specific social cost of switching to an unknown vendor in a world where unknown vendors carry risk.

The coverage problem is why harm reduction has not made the Wedge invisible. Harm reduction covers legitimate safety practices — testing, dosing, information, crisis support. It does not cover the supply relationship’s internal dynamics: who knows what about the substance, who is managing the dependency, what is being disclosed versus withheld. A perfectly harm-reduction-compliant supply relationship can still be exploitative if the disclosure is incomplete and the dependency is engineered. Wedge passes every publicly visible harm-reduction standard. The coverage problem is that the standard is visible and the dependency architecture is not.

Harm reduction as cover is the chapter’s central dark irony: the safety vocabulary that exists to protect people is also the most effective camouflage available to a sophisticated operator, because the operator who uses the vocabulary correctly is functionally indistinguishable from the operator who uses it genuinely, until you are in the test-kit gap or the dependency and cannot get out.

🎫 THE TRACKS

Track 01: The Free First Bump Funnel

(reciprocity hook applied to substances)

The Behavior

It’s free.

Not a sample — a gift. The first one is always framed as a gift because a gift creates a different social register than a sample. A sample is a commercial act. A gift is a personal one. A gift establishes the relationship as a friendship-adjacent thing before any transaction occurs, which means by the time the first transaction occurs, the relationship-before-the-transaction is doing structural work.

The gift is not random. The gift is targeted. Wedge does not offer the free first bump to everyone. He offers it to people who have the profile — regular festival attendance, the right camp associations, the social standing that makes a return customer valuable. Not the broke first-timer. Not the one-off. The person who will come back, who will tell friends, who will become a node in the supply network’s social graph. The free first bump is a marketing expense with a specific target demographic.

It is also — and this is the thing the chapter needs to sit with — usually genuinely good. The first bump is the tested, undiluted, accurately represented product. Wedge gives you the best version first because the best version is what you are going to remember when you come back. The first bump is the advertisement and the benchmark. When you come back and it’s “almost as good” — which it will be, eventually, as the relationship deepens and the professional calculations compound — you will attribute the slight difference to your tolerance rather than to Wedge’s margin management.

The Read

Ask: why is this free?

Not cynically. Factually. Every business decision has a logic. The free first bump’s logic is: dependency is more profitable than a single sale. If the logic of “free first” is “I make more money when you come back repeatedly than I make on this transaction,” you are being enrolled in a dependency architecture before the first transaction. That is not friendship. That is customer acquisition.

Counter-Drop — Run This: → The Own-Source Discipline

Step 1 — Own-source discipline means: you build and maintain at least two verified supply relationships simultaneously, neither of which knows the other exists in your supply network.

Step 2 — Accepting a free first bump does not obligate a second transaction with the same source. The free thing is free. It does not create a preference or a loyalty.

Step 3 — After the free first bump, evaluate the source using the same criteria you would use for any first transaction: tested and documented, disclosed contents, accurate dosing information, no pressure tactics, transparent pricing, no access-for-relationship exchange.

Step 4 — If the source passes: they are one of two sources. Not the only source. Not the preferred source. One of two.

Step 5 — Maintain the two-source discipline permanently. The source that knows they are one of two has different leverage than the source that knows they are the only one.

Vibe Check

Scene generosity is real. People offer free things because they are generous. Not every free first bump is a funnel. The test is what comes after: does the source treat the free thing as a no-strings gift that closes when it closes, or does it become the first entry in a relationship ledger?

Mirror Set

Have you ever given someone a free first thing specifically because you wanted them to come back? Have you ever framed a commercial calculation as generosity in a way that you did not disclose? The free first bump runs at many scales. The gift that was really a deposit is not unique to supply relationships.

Refractions

Touring Artists: The music industry’s version of the free first bump is the feature, the support slot, the “let me put you on this showcase.” Free exposure for a long-term relationship that benefits the person offering exposure. Same architecture.

Camp Leads: If your camp runs on first-timers being warmly welcomed and deeply appreciated and then asked to take on significant labor in year two — that is the free first bump applied to community membership. The welcome was free. The labor was the second transaction.

Runs in Every Direction

The free first bump gets offered by Wedge, by the femme operator two camps over, by the nonbinary distributor everyone trusts, by the camp lead who fronts for newcomers, and by the booking agent who’s figured out that giving first produces a return. The funnel is not gendered. The architecture is.

Sober Tuesday

The software company that gives you a free trial, specifically configured to be maximally useful during the trial period and to create as much integration into your workflow as possible, so that the transition away from it after the trial ends feels more costly than the subscription fee. Free first. Dependency second. Billing third. The funnel is a business model.

Track 02: The “I Got You” Dependency Build

(supplier-as-caregiver role merge)

The Behavior

Wedge knows your preferred form. He knows your tolerance. He knows which products work well together for you and which combinations he has seen create problems for people with your profile. He texts back fast. He is discreet. He comes to you rather than making you navigate to him. He remembers your camp name without being reminded. He asks how you are doing and means it in the specific way of someone who has a professional interest in your wellbeing that is genuine and also not disinterested.

“I got you” is his most-used phrase. It means something precise in his usage: I am your person for this. You do not need to look elsewhere. You do not need to navigate the uncertainty of a new source. You do not need to do the four-questions protocol from Chapter 11 because I have already answered all four questions for you and the answers are good and I am the person who stands between you and a world where the answers might not be good.

The “I got you” is the warmth. The warmth is real. The warmth is also the cage: a warm place whose walls only become visible when you try to leave it.

Dependency builds over time in the specific way that any relationship deepens — through positive interactions, through reliable service, through the accumulation of being-known. The difference is that Wedge is actively engineering the accumulation. The “I got you” is not the spontaneous expression of care. It is the positioning statement of a supply relationship optimized for retention.

The Read

Does your source want you informed, capable, and free to verify what they tell you? Or does your source position themselves as the person whose job it is to have already done the verification for you, so that your verification instinct points toward them rather than toward independent confirmation?

A real vendor gives you the tools. A Wook plug gives you the conclusions.

Counter-Drop — Run This: → The Regular Source Audit

Every three months: audit the source relationship.

Step 1 — Can I currently buy what I need from a second source, independently, without significant friction? If no: the dependency has formed. Build the second source now.

Step 2 — Has the quality or quantity varied without disclosure in the last cycle? If yes: the trust asymmetry is being used against you.

Step 3 — Would I tell my source I’m doing this audit? Would they respond well? If the audit feels like a betrayal: the relationship has been engineered to feel that way.

Step 4 — Test the source’s reaction to the two-source discipline. Tell them you’re diversifying. A real vendor says fine. A Wook plug makes you feel disloyal.

Vibe Check

Some sources genuinely are the best available and the loyalty is earned through consistently excellent product and service. Loyalty that is earned through performance is legitimate. Loyalty that is engineered through dependency architecture is not. The distinction is: what happens when you threaten to leave? Relief that you found what you needed elsewhere is a clean source. Anything else is not.

Mirror Set

Have you ever positioned yourself as the person whose job it is to handle the verification for someone else, so that their instinct to verify points toward you rather than away from you? Have you ever made someone feel like diversifying their support network was a betrayal? The “I got you” is also the sentence that sober mentors use, that camp leads use, that romantic partners use. It carries the same leverage regardless of the domain.

Refractions

Recovery Lane: The sponsor relationship has a version of the “I got you” dependency build built into its legitimate structure. Real sponsors support the development of independent sobriety tools. Sponsors who make themselves irreplaceable — the only person you can call, the only person who understands, the only safe person in the scene — have converted a legitimate care role into a structural dependency that serves them.

Solo Festies: The Wedge dependency builds fastest on people who don’t have an existing supply network to compare against. Solo festie with no reference class is the ideal target for the “I got you” positioning. You don’t know what else is available. You don’t know what the comparison looks like. You have one data point and the data point is performing very well.

Runs in Every Direction

The dependency gets built by the plug who remembers your order, the camp connect who always has extra, the touring supplier who checks in between runs, and anyone whose genuine care produces structural dependency whether they meant it to or not. It runs across every supply relationship dynamic regardless of the supplier’s gender or the customer’s.

Sober Tuesday

The HOA board member who is the only one with the key to the clubhouse, the pool schedule, and the neighborhood Facebook group password — so the whole community’s dependency on him becomes total and the role becomes immune to question. “I got you” as a volunteer-committee control mechanism. Works the same way.

Track 03: The Dose-and-Decide

(incapacitation prior to consent moments)

The Behavior

The timing of the ask is not accidental.

The offer that requires a significant decision — more supply on credit, an upgrade to a larger quantity, the accommodation that seems convenient and turns out to require access, the favor that seems small and turns out to be a choke point — arrives after the product has been administered and before full cognitive function has returned.

“Hey, while I’m here — if you need more before Sunday I can front you and you settle up Monday.” Said at the precise moment when: the product is active, the person is warm and open and not running the full credit-risk calculation, and the relationship’s warmth has been recently reinforced by the positive experience of the product itself.

The Dose-And-Decide is not necessarily violent or coercive. It is timing. The timing is the manipulation. The ask that would produce “let me think about that” at noon on a Tuesday produces “yeah, sure, that’d be great” at eleven p.m. after the second cap is properly seated.

This runs especially in the access-for-access direction: “I can get you into the green room/secret set/backstage/afters/the camp that’s hard to get into if you want to connect on Sunday.” The offer is real. The timing is chosen.

The Read

Notice when significant offers arrive relative to your capacity state. Ask: would this offer have been made at noon on Thursday? If not — the timing is strategic. The offer may be real and the timing may still be strategic.

Counter-Drop — Run This: → The Sober Monday Rule

Any offer that arrives while you are under the influence of any substance that meaningfully affects your decision-making:

“That sounds potentially interesting. I’ll check in with you Monday when I can think about it clearly.”

Full sentence. Warm. Non-committal. Does not require you to evaluate the offer now.

If the offer evaporates because you won’t decide on altered time: the offer was the timing and not the thing.

Real offers survive Monday.

Vibe Check

Some timing is coincidental. Wedge is present at the festival when you are present at the festival. His offers arrive when you are both there. Not all of it is strategic. The test is: does the offer require a fast decision, and does the fast decision benefit Wedge specifically?

Mirror Set

Have you ever timed an ask for when you knew the other person’s capacity was most open to it? Have you ever introduced a proposal at a dinner party rather than a Tuesday meeting because the dinner party’s social warmth makes refusal harder? The timing is the leverage. The leverage runs at all scales.

Refractions

Ceremony Lane: The post-ceremony period is the highest-dependency, lowest-capacity window in any psychedelic facilitation context. Integration discussions, housing arrangements, financial commitments, and ongoing relationship structures that get established in the post-ceremony window carry the full vulnerability of the Dose-And-Decide. Real facilitators do not use the post-ceremony window for logistics. Wook facilitators do.

Runs in Every Direction

The Dose-And-Decide gets run by the plug who waits until you’re already in it, the camp connect who frames the ask as casual, the touring supplier whose timing is never accidental, and anyone whose capacity to negotiate depends on someone else’s capacity to wait. It runs across every supply relationship and every context where one person’s capacity is affected and the other person’s timing is strategic. It is not gendered. It has a very good close rate.

Sober Tuesday

The job offer with a forty-eight-hour deadline, timed to arrive on a Friday afternoon when your weekend plans are falling through and your current job has just had a bad week. The timing is selected. The offer may be real. The timing is still selected.

Track 04: The Withdrawal Threat

(supply-as-coercion in dependency)

The Behavior

It does not have to be spoken.

The withdrawal threat is rarely explicit — “if you don’t do this I’ll cut you off.” That version exists but it is the amateur version, the version that comes out in the moments when Wedge loses patience, which he almost never does because nineteen years of professional practice has taught him that explicit threats are worse for business than the ambient threat that the relationship manages on its own.

The ambient version: a slight cooling after a complicated interaction. A slower response time. A “might not be able to make it work this weekend” delivered with the specific cadence of someone whose “might not” is conditional on something that has not been spoken. The dependency does the rest. The dependent person fills the gap with the calculation they have already been trained to run: what did I do wrong, what do I need to do differently, what will make the supply stable again.

The withdrawal threat is the leverage the dependency produces. Once the dependency exists, the threat does not need to be spoken. It lives in the relationship’s weather. The supply cools when the dependent person creates friction. The supply warms when the friction stops. The lesson is written in temperature, not words.

The Read

Track the temperature gradient in your supply relationship relative to your behavior. Does the warmth cool when you ask questions? Does access become complicated when you raise concerns? Does the “might not be able to make it work” arrive in proximity to moments when you have been less compliant, less grateful, less available?

If yes: the dependency has become leverage.

Counter-Drop — Run This: → The Friction Test

Step 1 — Introduce one small friction into the supply relationship. Ask for the lot number. Ask for the independent test results. Ask what’s in the cut. Note the temperature response.

Step 2 — If the temperature drops in response to normal due-diligence questions: the relationship has been engineered to treat due diligence as disloyalty. That is the leverage structure.

Step 3 — If you are currently in a dependent position with no alternative source: build the second source before introducing the friction. The second source is the counterweight that makes the friction test safe to run.

Step 4 — The test is not whether Wedge responds perfectly. The test is whether asking reasonable questions is treated as a reasonable thing to do. It should be.

Vibe Check

Sources can have genuinely bad days. A slower response time is not always leverage. A “might not make it work” is not always threat. The pattern over time is the test, not the individual instance.

Mirror Set

Have you ever used the slight cooling as a management tool in a relationship where you had access the other person wanted? Have you ever let the implied threat do work without saying it? The withdrawal threat runs in every relationship where one person has something the other person wants and uses the having of it as ambient pressure. Supply relationships are the most obvious version. They are not the only version.

Refractions

Touring Lane: The booking agent who becomes slightly less responsive after you raise a contract question. The management structure that becomes less enthusiastic about your career after you hire a lawyer. The withdrawal threat runs professionally.

Domestic and Romantic Contexts: The partner who becomes cold after the “we need to talk” and warm again after you back down. The same structure. The supply is intimacy rather than K. The leverage is identical.

Runs in Every Direction

The threat gets made by the plug who goes cold the moment you push back, the camp connect whose warmth turns conditional, the touring supplier who reminds you what you’d be losing, and anyone with leverage and a dependency. Its sophistication is not gendered. Its effectiveness is universal.

Sober Tuesday

The landlord who is very responsive to maintenance requests until you ask about the lease terms. The manager whose approval is warm until you submit PTO for the day they needed you. The temperature is the tell.

Track 05: The Plug Romance

(sex-for-supply / coercive intimacy)

The Behavior

The warm supply relationship becomes warmer.

The texts arrive later in the evening. The “I got you” starts to have a quality of attention that the supply transaction does not require. The free extra appears alongside a compliment that has nothing to do with the product. The access escalates: not just the supply, but the green room, the private camp, the “I’d love for you to come by later and we could—”

The Plug Romance is the supply relationship repurposed as romantic leverage, where the supply line and the intimacy line run in parallel and close to each other and in proximity to each other and occasionally through the same door. Sometimes the romance is genuine and the supply relationship simply coexists with it and neither contaminates the other, and those relationships exist and they are not this Track.

The Track is when the supply is the leverage. When the warmer nights come after the warmer access. When the colder response follows the declined invitation. When the question “is this person interested in me or in what I can provide them” has the answer “both, and the both is structural.”

The Plug Romance is the Covert Facilitator from Chapter 1 applied to a supply relationship: the help that is real and that turns into a claim, where the claim is not money but intimacy. The intimacy is a form of the supply. The supply is a form of the intimacy. The chapter needs to say clearly that access to substances is not a form of consent to a romantic relationship, and that using supply access to create the conditions for romantic pursuit is coercive regardless of how warmly it is executed.

The Read

Ask whether the romantic interest existed before the supply relationship. Ask whether the access would change if the romantic interest were declined. If the answer to the second question is “probably yes” — the access and the interest are entangled, and the entanglement is the coercion.

Counter-Drop — Run This: → The Lane-Clear Protocol

Step 1 — If a supply relationship becomes romantically charged: separate the lanes explicitly. “I value this [supply relationship / friendship]. I want to be clear that the two are separate for me and I’d like to keep them that way.”

Step 2 — Observe whether the supply relationship remains unchanged after the lane-clear. If yes: clean. If it cools, becomes complicated, or becomes suddenly less available: the lanes were never separate.

Step 3 — If you are in a supply-dependent position when the romantic pressure arrives: build the alternative source first. The alternative source is the only thing that makes the lane-clear carry no cost.

Step 4 — If you have already declined and the access has changed: document the change. The temperature change is evidence. Evidence is what you have when the relationship eventually requires addressing through a camp network or a festival safety team.

Vibe Check

People in supply-adjacent relationships can develop genuine romantic interest in each other, and that interest can be mutual and consensual and healthy. The test is not “did romantic interest form.” The test is “what happens to the supply relationship when the romantic interest is declined.” A clean romantic interest that is declined produces no supply change. The Plug Romance produces a supply change.

Mirror Set

Have you ever used something you could provide as ambient leverage in a romantic context — not explicitly, but in the sense that your warmth and your access were entangled in a way that made declining the romantic interest feel like declining the access? The Plug Romance runs at every scale where access and attraction coexist.

Refractions

BIPOC Festies: In supply relationships in predominantly white scenes, the Plug Romance can carry additional power dynamics that compound with the racial dynamics of the space. If the person controlling your supply access also controls other forms of your belonging in the scene, the coercion is structural even when the romantic interest is presented as personal.

Queer Scene: The Plug Romance in small queer communities runs through the same supply-access leverage in a more socially dense environment. The small scene means the temperature change after a declined romance is visible to more people and has more social consequences. Both the leverage and the consequence are amplified.

Runs in Every Direction

The Plug Romance runs through the male dealer who starts calling it something else, the femme distributor whose customers become something else, the nonbinary supplier whose access and affection blur, and anyone whose supply relationship and romantic interest have been allowed to become entangled. The gender distribution of who runs it and who receives it varies. The structural architecture is gender-neutral.

Sober Tuesday

The manager whose “management style” involves taking the high-potential employees to dinner, where the dinner’s purpose oscillates between professional mentorship and personal interest, and where declining the personal interest produces a slightly less interesting management experience going forward. The supply is career advancement. The leverage is identical.

Track 06: The Tour-Bus Captivity

(mid-leg supply chain dependency)

The Behavior

You are two events into a five-event run.

You are in someone else’s vehicle. Your supply for the next three events is in Wedge’s Sprinter. Your financial arrangement with Wedge involves a credit structure that makes sense at event one and becomes significantly more complicated by event three when the credit has accumulated to a level that the event-four cash flow will not cleanly cover. You are in a city two hundred miles from your default home base. Your alternative sources have not been cultivated because the first two events with Wedge were good and the first two events with Wedge were the only data you thought you needed.

This is the Tour-Bus Captivity. It is the Sunk Cost Swamp applied to logistics, run at the specific scale of a multi-day touring scenario where the dependency becomes load-bearing before you realize it is load-bearing. The captivity does not require a locked door. It requires: being two events in, having supply in someone else’s vehicle, owing money that is easier to settle by continuing the relationship than by disrupting it, and having no alternative source cultivated.

By event three, the tour is running Wedge’s route.

Not yours.

The Read

Before any multi-event run: map the supply chain independence for the full run. Not just the first event. If you cannot answer the question “if this source falls through at event three, what is my alternative” before event one — you have already begun building the captivity.

Counter-Drop — Run This: → The Run Independence Plan

Step 1 — Before a multi-event run: identify the full run’s supply needs. Total quantity. Total type.

Step 2 — Establish at minimum two verified independent sources capable of covering the full run, either individually or jointly.

Step 3 — No credit arrangements that compound across events. Each event’s financial arrangement closes completely before the next event begins. A credit arrangement that accumulates across events is a dependency instrument.

Step 4 — Your supply does not travel in someone else’s vehicle unless you have verified, documented access to it at all times, at your discretion, without requiring their cooperation.

Step 5 — The Van Rule: if your supply is in their van and they are driving, you do not have your supply. What you have is access to your supply contingent on their cooperation. That is not supply independence. That is the captivity wearing a logistics arrangement.

Vibe Check

Multi-event runs with a single trusted source are common and work well. The test is not whether you work with one source across a run. The test is whether you could leave the arrangement at any point without significant logistical cost. If you can: not captivity. If you can’t: captivity, regardless of how comfortable and warm the arrangement has been.

Mirror Set

Have you ever structured a supply arrangement, housing arrangement, transportation arrangement, or professional arrangement in a way that made it difficult for the other person to exit mid-run without significant cost? Have you ever let someone’s credit accumulate across events in a way that created dependency rather than resolved it? The Tour-Bus Captivity runs in every domain where someone else’s logistics have become the infrastructure of your movement.

Refractions

Touring Artists: The tour-bus captivity runs literally in the touring music industry. The band on a management-controlled tour with a management-controlled supply chain in a management-controlled vehicle on a management-controlled route, with a credit arrangement that makes leaving the management before the run ends financially catastrophic. This is the Track at its institutional scale. Build independence into the touring infrastructure before you need it.

New Vendors: The vendor who gets a discounted booth spot at a festival circuit in exchange for a multi-event commitment that includes supply arrangements that benefit the booth operator who gave them the spot. The discount is the free first bump. The multi-event commitment is the Captivity beginning.

Runs in Every Direction

The Captivity runs through the supply arrangement that’s costly to walk away from, the touring crew position that’s hard to quit mid-run, the camp housing that’s tied to staying loyal, and any relationship where the cost of leaving has outgrown the value of staying. It runs in every context where the mid-leg exit is economically or logistically costly.

Sober Tuesday

The subscription that is trivial to start and requires a phone call during business hours and a thirty-day notice period to cancel. The gym membership that required no commitment to join and requires a certified letter to exit. The Tour-Bus Captivity is the default business model of every service that has decided captive customers are more profitable than satisfied ones.

He texts a person he met at event two whose supply reputation he has heard good things about. He does not explain why he is reaching out. He says he is looking to diversify his sources and heard they might be able to help.

The person texts back and the supply relationship begins.

He has a second source.

Wedge texts back immediately, warm, no temperature drop: “Of course. Smart to have options. I appreciate the heads up. Let me know if you need anything this weekend.”

This is actually a green flag. The warm response to the diversification is the clean source telling on itself. Kai notes it. The source that accepts the diversification without cooling is the source that was never running the dependency architecture.

Over the next two events, Kai rotates between sources. He runs his own test every transaction. He does not extend credit that accumulates. He has no supply in any vehicle that is not his own vehicle.

He is not perfectly safe. There is no perfectly safe. But he has removed the single-source dependency and the accumulated credit and the testing gap, which are the three specific vulnerabilities that the dependency architecture requires.

Wedge remains a good source. The relationship continues. The relationship is now one of two.

The cage’s walls are not gone. But the door is open.

The Supply Independence Drill

Before your next event. Sober. Seven questions. Honest answers.

1. Name your current supply source. Name your second supply source. If you cannot name two: you are single-source. This is the only finding that matters for the rest of the drill.

2. When did you last run your own independent test of the product from each source? If longer than one year for either: test before the next event.

3. Do you have any credit outstanding with any supply source that spans more than one event? If yes: settle before the next event begins.

4. Is any of your supply currently in someone else’s vehicle, storage, or custody? If yes: can you access it right now, unilaterally, without their cooperation? If no: The Van Rule applies.

Seven questions. Ten minutes. Every event.
