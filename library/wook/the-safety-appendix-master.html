FROM CHAPTER 11 — BAD TRIP RESPONSE

The Recovery Position

Turn on left side. Upper knee forward for stability. Head back to open airway. Stay with them. Do not leave them on their back.

When 911 Is Non-Negotiable

Loss of consciousness · gray/blue lips · labored or absent breathing · seizure · unresponsive to voice or touch · extreme temperature · chest pain · significant blood loss. Stop calculating. Call. Move.

Good Samaritan Amnesty

All fifty US states have some form as of 2026. Most protect the caller and the person in crisis from possession prosecution when calling in good faith. State-specific: DanceSafe.org/good-samaritan-laws.

The Four Words

“We don’t know what.” Every time you arrive at any medical resource. Gives the medic the correct clinical posture without inaccurate information.

Test Kit Basics

Marquis: MDMA purple-black. Orange = amphetamines. No reaction/unexpected color = unknown, do not distribute.

Fentanyl strips: One line = positive. Two lines = negative. Positive = destroy the batch. Do not take anything positive for fentanyl.

Source: DanceSafe.org, Bunk Police (bfrp.com).

The Pre-Event Crew Briefing

Real names out loud → ICE contacts entered → physical meeting spot → four-hour check-in interval → extraction code word → sober anchor designated → medical tent location confirmed → fifteen minutes, every event.

FROM CHAPTER 12 — GATE RIGHTS

Constructive Possession Basics

Legal responsibility for contraband if: in a space you control + you have knowledge it’s there + you can exercise control. “I didn’t know” is a court defense, not a gate conversation.

Your Rights at the Festival Gate

Law enforcement gate search: “I don’t consent to a search.” If detained: “Am I being detained? I want a lawyer.” Then nothing. Not “it’s not mine.” Not “we’ve been having problems.” Nothing.

The Dump-It-Now Protocol

If contraband is in your vehicle before the gate: Pull over. Do not touch the item. Exit. Stand clear. Let the driver handle it. Do not observe. Re-enter. Text yourself a timestamped record.

FROM CHAPTER 13 — ROAD RIGHTS

Your Rights During a Traffic Stop

You do not have to consent to a search. “I don’t consent to a search.” Say it clearly. Politely. Once. It is not an invitation to negotiate.

The Four Sentences

1. “Can you tell me what you stopped us for?”

2. “I don’t consent to a search.”

3. “Are we free to go once you’ve written the ticket?”

4. “I want to speak with an attorney.” [Then nothing.]

The Co-Passenger Rule

One person speaks. The driver handles all communication. Passengers: “I’ll let the driver handle this.” Practiced before the drive. Not improvised at the window.

The Pre-Drive Inventory Conversation

Everyone in the vehicle knows what is in the vehicle. This is not optional. Four people in the car = four people in the legal situation. The information belongs to everyone it affects.

Know Your Rights Resources

NORML: norml.org/legal/know-your-rights

ACLU traffic stop rights: aclu.org

DanceSafe: dancesafe.org

FROM CHAPTER 15 — TESTING AND RECALL

Marquis + Fentanyl Strip Protocol

Marquis: tiny sample on white test surface + one to two drops reagent + ninety seconds. Purple-black for MDMA. Anything else: stop.

Fentanyl strip: dissolve small amount in quarter-teaspoon water + strip in fifteen seconds + read at two to five minutes. One line = fentanyl. Stop. Destroy. Do not distribute.

The Recall Play

Minutes 0–2: Contact every buyer. “Stop. Don’t take anything from me. Go to harm reduction if anything feels wrong.”

Minutes 2–5: Stop all distribution.

Minutes 5–7: Call medical/harm reduction proactively. “I may have distributed something causing harm. I want to help.”

Minutes 7–10: Destroy the remaining supply.

After 10 minutes: full cooperation. Give source contact. Give buyer list. Give everything.

Why “Good Source For Two Years” Is Not A Test

Supply chains have unknown links. Fentanyl contamination has been documented in every substance class. The two-year record covers your source’s portion of the chain. It covers nothing above them. Test the batch.

FROM CHAPTER 16 — GIFTED SUBSTANCES

The Seal Protocol

You drink from things you sealed yourself or watched sealed in front of you. This is not paranoia. This is how consent works with substances. A container that was already open when it reached you is a container you cannot verify.

“What’s in here?” Is a Complete Question

Vagueness is a no. “A little bit of magic” is vagueness. A complete answer names what it contains. You are allowed to require a complete answer before accepting anything you will put in your body.

The Ledger Check

Count the free things. When it starts to feel heavy — you owe something. You are not obligated to discharge a ledger you did not agree to open.

If Something Happened

RAINN: 1-800-656-4673 or rainn.org

Crisis Text Line: Text HOME to 741741

Zendo Project: zendoproject.org

Mara Hot Water (or the nearest harm-reduction volunteer): they have paper, they have time, and the word you are looking for is already on the form.

FROM CHAPTER 18 — RIGHTS AND THE FESTIE CODE

Your Rights If Approached

You do not have to answer questions about your camp, your contents, your activities. “Am I being detained?” If no: “I’m free to go.” If yes: “I want a lawyer.” Silence.

The Festie Code

We do not sell, trade, give, or transfer controlled substances to people we have not met before this event, regardless of claimed mutual connections, expressed distress, or offered price. Standing policy. Not negotiated in the moment.

The Verify-The-Mutual-Friend Rule

“Can you text them right now?” Wait for the text to be sent in your presence. Wait for the reply. Read the reply. Real friends reply fast.

FROM CHAPTER 19 — PHYSICAL SECURITY

Lockboxes That Work

Pacsafe Travelsafe (~$50) · Pelican micro case with padlock hasp (~$30–60) · Vaultz combination lockbox with cable (~$25). In the lockbox: medications, prescription glasses, car keys, ID, significant cash, charging phones, anything irreplaceable.

The Deal-In-The-Open Protocol

Location: public space with ambient foot traffic. Witness: one person from your crew with a sight line. Timer: the witness has fifteen minutes running. No inside: any “come inside” offer declines the transaction. Note the details before you go.

Campsite Security Checklist

Phones charging: lockbox · ☐ Cash: lockbox or body · ☐ Car keys: lockbox · ☐ Prescriptions: lockbox · ☐ Laptop/tablet: vehicle or lockbox · ☐ GoPro/camera: in a bag · ☐ One person staying at camp during main-stage windows OR everything locked.

If You Have Been Robbed

Get to a safe area. Get to a phone. Document before the memory degrades. Report to festival safety (not county if you prefer — they are different). Activate the camp network. The information protects others.

FROM CHAPTER 21 — MISSING PERSON

The Sister Lou Briefing (Full)

Government names → ICE contacts and lock screen → physical meet-up spot → four-hour interval → code word → escalation threshold → wristband photos → buddy assignment. Fifteen minutes. Before the gate. Every event.

Four-Hour Escalation Sequence

1. Medical tent (legal name + wristband number)

2. Lost and found

3. Festival safety desk

After six hours with no result: county emergency services.

ICE-On-The-Lock-Screen

iPhone: Settings → Health → Medical ID → enable Show When Locked.

Android: varies, but most have lock screen emergency information in settings. Takes forty-five seconds. Do it before you leave the car.

FROM CHAPTER 22 — SUPPLY INDEPENDENCE

Own-Source Discipline

Two verified, independent supply sources at all times. Neither knows the other exists in your network. Test both independently at minimum once per year. Maintain the second source even when the first is performing well. Single-source test: if your first source became unavailable tomorrow, how long would it take to have supply? If “start from scratch”: build the second source this week.

The Van Rule

Your supply does not travel in anyone else’s vehicle unless: you have explicit documented access to it at any time without their cooperation + it is inventoried before and after each move + financial arrangements are entirely separate + you can exit the vehicle at any point and take it with you.

Applies equally to: housing, equipment, transportation, any resource you cannot access without the other person’s cooperation.

Two-Person Bump Rule

Never use a new substance in an environment where you are the only person monitoring yourself. One other person, not under the same substance, knows what you’ve taken and when, and has your emergency contact.

Phone Camera Audit

After any blackout or significant memory gap: open your camera roll immediately. Review photos in order. Timestamped. Before any external account of the night is received.
