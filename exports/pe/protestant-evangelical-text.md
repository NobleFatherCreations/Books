## 2. A day inside {#a-day-inside}

*Tyler · A Tuesday in August · A megachurch campus in the American Sun Belt*

Staff prayer at 8:15. Twenty-two people in a circle, and Tyler leads it because the campus pastor (the pastor in charge of one site of a multi-site church) asked him to on Sunday, in front of others, in the way that is not really a question.

He is the worship leader. He is twenty-nine and has been on staff four years, and last month he signed a new employment agreement that his fiancée's brother, who is a lawyer, read and then read again and then said *huh*. There is a clause about not making disparaging statements. Tyler signed it because the alternative was not signing it, and this is the only job he has ever had that used what he can do.

Rehearsal. They run the closing song four times because the campus pastor wants the key change to land under the giving talk. Tyler understands the architecture of this completely. He built parts of it. It works because the songs are good and because people are actually meeting God in the room, and both of those things are true at the same time and he has stopped trying to separate them.

At lunch he sees a text from Kayla, who was on the creative team until March. She left after something with the executive pastor (the senior staff member who runs the church's operations) that nobody explains the same way twice. Her text says *hey are you okay*. He types *yeah!!* and then sits looking at the two exclamation marks.

Small group at seven, at his house, eleven people. A guy named Marcus asks, sideways, whether anyone knows what the building fund is actually at. There is a pause of about two seconds that Tyler feels in his neck. He says he'll ask. He knows he will not ask.

Eleven-forty. He is on the back steps with a guitar he isn't playing. He thinks about the fact that he can name every person in his life and that all of them are from here, and that the thought did not use to frighten him, and then he goes inside because tomorrow is Wednesday and Wednesdays start at seven.

### The strongest objection, answered

**The objection.** You are cherry-picking megachurch scandals and calling it the whole movement.

**What is true in it.** Most congregations are small, poor, and locally accountable, and most pastors earn less than the schoolteachers in their pews.

**The answer.** The pattern is what makes it more than cherry-picking: the same architecture appears each time — founder, appointed board, no external oversight. And much of the reporting that exposed it came from the evangelical press itself, which is why the claim is not an outside attack. The Southern Baptist record was first documented at scale by the secular *Houston Chronicle* and *San Antonio Express-News* in 2019 [33].

## 4. What healthy looks like here {#healthy}

::: lede
Protestant and evangelical Christianity is a vast family, from mainline denominations to megachurches, Pentecostal networks and independent congregations. It numbers roughly 800M–1B people globally, and its fastest-growing wing is Pentecostal/charismatic Christianity in the Global South.
:::

Healthy practice here looks like churches with plural elders (authority shared among several elders), open books, outside audits, and pastors on published salaries.

The tradition has its own press, and that press has exposed its own. *Christianity Today* reported the rise and fall of Mars Hill [12] and the investigation of RZIM's founder [13]; MinistryWatch reports on the movement's own institutions [21]; and survivor-led accountability movements (#ChurchToo, in which survivors publicly named abuse in churches) have pressed for accountability.

Some of that standard is already on the record. Southern Baptist messengers voted for an independent investigation of their own Executive Committee; its report was published in 2022, and the Executive Committee then released its list of accused ministers [7][8]. Ravi Zacharias International Ministries commissioned an outside investigation of its late founder and made the finding public [13]. In 2013 Alan Chambers apologized to gay people and shut down Exodus International [16], and in 2018 Joshua Harris discontinued his own bestseller, *I Kissed Dating Goodbye* [17]. In England and Wales, churches that are registered charities file public accounts [6]. Each is a place where members or institutions met the standard this page applies, and each is recorded here as credit.

## 5. History {#history}

The 16th-century Reformation broke the Catholic monopoly with the 'priesthood of all believers' (the teaching that every believer can read, interpret and stand before God without a priest as middleman). Evangelical revivalism in the 18th and 19th centuries built a voluntary, entrepreneurial religion. In the 20th century, Pentecostalism and broadcast media created the megachurch and televangelism models.

### Timeline

```timeline
1517–1555 | Luther's protest; Reformation spreads under princely protection | Breaking Rome's monopoly required state sponsors — new establishments replaced the old.
1530s–1600s | Confessions written; state churches established; radical reformers persecuted | 'Priesthood of all believers' coexists with new coercive orthodoxies.
1730s–1800s | Great Awakenings; revivalism; voluntary societies | Religion becomes entrepreneurial: persuasion, emotion, and audience replace inherited parish.
1800s | Global missions bound to colonial expansion; Bible and tract societies | Mass media religion begins; conversion becomes a measurable output.
1900–1930 | Pentecostal revival (Azusa Street); fundamentalist–modernist controversy | Experience and inerrancy become rival authority bases.
1940s–1970s | Billy Graham crusades; radio and television ministry; parachurch empires | Charisma decouples from any denomination that could discipline it.
1970s–1990s | Religious Right mobilization; prosperity gospel; church growth movement | Congregations become political constituencies and marketing targets.
1980s–2000s | Televangelist scandals; Senate (Grassley) inquiry 2007–2011 | Financial opacity documented at the federal level with no penalties following. [GOVERNMENT REPORT]
2010s–present | Megachurch collapses (Mars Hill, Willow Creek, Hillsong, RZIM); #ChurchToo | Same architecture each time: founder, hand-picked board, no external accountability. [INVESTIGATIVE REPORT]
2019 | Houston Chronicle and San Antonio Express-News publish "Abuse of Faith" | About 380 Southern Baptist leaders and volunteers are shown to have been accused of sexual misconduct since 1998, three years before the denomination's own commissioned investigation. [INVESTIGATIVE REPORT] [33]
Present | Global South Pentecostalism is the growth engine; Western deconstruction wave | The tradition's future is African, Latin American, and Asian — with the same structural risks.
```

### Moments in the room

::: card
#### 1979 — A voter registration list becomes a movement

The Moral Majority (a conservative political organization) organized congregations into a voting bloc, delivering political power to pastors who answered to no bishop and, in the independent churches, to no elected board they had not themselves appointed.

**Why it matters.** Political influence arrived at exactly the point in the structure with the least accountability. That combination — maximum reach, minimum review — is the defining feature of this page.
:::

::: card
#### 2007–2011 — The Senate asks, and cannot compel

Senator Grassley's Finance Committee sought basic financial information from six media ministries. Several declined to provide it fully. The review closed in 2011 with no penalties and a suggestion that the sector self-regulate.

**Why it matters.** It was the most authoritative body that has ever asked, and it asked politely and went away. This is what the church exemption looks like in operation rather than in theory.
:::

::: card
#### May 2022 — The list existed the whole time

Guidepost Solutions, the firm hired to investigate, reported that Southern Baptist executive staff had maintained a private list of accused ministers for years while publicly asserting that the convention's polity (its system of church government) made such a list impossible.

**Why it matters.** The impossibility was a claim, not a fact, and the claim was falsified by the institution's own filing cabinet. Nothing structural prevented it; a decision did.
:::

## 6. Branches & variants {#branches}

The main branches are the mainline churches (the older, historically established Protestant denominations: Lutheran, Anglican, Methodist, Presbyterian and Reformed), Baptists, evangelicals, Pentecostal and charismatic churches, and non-denominational and independent networks. Pew's Protestant total includes Anglicans and Pentecostals, whom this codex also covers in their own volumes, so figures here overlap with those volumes.

| Branch | Example | Where authority sits |
|---|---|---|
| **Mainline** | Examples are the Lutheran, Methodist, Presbyterian and Reformed churches. | Authority sits with bishops or elected assemblies, usually under a published constitution with a church court. |
| **Baptist** | The Southern Baptist Convention had 12.3 million members in 2025, down from 16.3 million in 2006 [20][21]. | Each congregation is autonomous; an annual meeting of messengers (voting delegates sent by member churches) votes on the convention's business [7]. |
| **Pentecostal & Charismatic** | Examples are the Assemblies of God, Hillsong and independent networks. Pentecostal and charismatic Christians numbered about 584 million worldwide in 2011 [19]. | Authority often sits with a founder and a board the founder chose (see the Pentecostal & Charismatic volume). |
| **Non-denominational and independent** | Examples are megachurches and church-planting networks (networks that found new congregations) [12]. | Authority sits with the founding pastor and the elders the pastor appointed, and no one sits above them [12]. |
| **Parachurch ministries** (Christian organizations that work outside any church's structure) | Examples are apologetics (defense of the faith by argument), broadcast, publishing and relief organizations [1][13]. | Authority sits with a board, often of the founder's choosing [1][13]. |

- Formal hierarchy is low, and charismatic authority is highly concentrated. The founder-pastor with 'vision,' an appointed board, and no bishop above him is the modern high-risk configuration.

## 10. Genealogy {#genealogy}

::: card
#### Prosperity and seed-faith giving

**Origin.** 20th-century American revivalism fused New Thought abundance ideas with healing ministry and, from the 1950s, television. [ACADEMIC SOURCE]

**What it was for.** No part of it is older than about a century. This is not an ancient practice with a lapsed rationale — it was engineered as a fundraising mechanism from the start, using Malachi 3's blessing-and-curse framing on a mass audience.

**Why that reason expired.** It was never valid. An investment product that promises returns, keeps no accounts, and blames the buyer's faith when the return does not arrive would be prosecuted as fraud in any other sector.

**Who benefits now.** Named individuals with jets benefit, and so do the broadcast operations around them. The 2007–2011 Senate inquiry examined six ministries, documented the assets and family-controlled boards, and ended without penalties. [GOVERNMENT REPORT]
:::

::: card
#### Purity culture

**Origin.** Purity culture was assembled in the 1990s from abstinence curricula, pledge campaigns, and courtship literature, which makes it roughly thirty years old. [ACADEMIC SOURCE]

**What it was for.** It was presented as a response to STIs (sexually transmitted infections) and teen pregnancy. Its actual function was to make adolescent sexuality a matter of community surveillance and female responsibility.

**Why that reason expired.** Outcome data did not support the abstinence-pledge model, and the movement's own leaders — including the author of its best-known book — have publicly repudiated it. The framework outlived its own architects' belief in it.

**Who benefits now.** The curriculum industry that sold it benefits, and so does the authority structure it created, a system in which adults question children about their sexual thoughts and call it accountability.
:::

::: card
#### Spiritual covering and authority language

**Origin.** It comes from the 1970s shepherding movement (in which each believer submitted to a personal 'shepherd') and later apostolic network models (networks of churches under a leader styled an apostle), so it is decades old, not millennia. [ACADEMIC SOURCE]

**What it was for.** It was sold as protection from deception for a movement with no hierarchy. In practice it manufactured hierarchy while denying it had one.

**Why that reason expired.** The shepherding movement collapsed publicly amid documented abuse of authority, and its own founders apologized. The vocabulary survived the movement that discredited it.

**Who benefits now.** Founder-pastors benefit, gaining the deference of a bishop with none of the oversight of one. 'Covering' means you may be corrected and may not correct.
:::

::: card
#### The unaccountable founder-pastor structure

**Origin.** Reformation congregationalism, radicalized by American voluntarism and 20th-century church-growth strategy. [ACADEMIC SOURCE]

**What it was for.** Congregational independence was a genuine protest against distant hierarchies imposing clergy on unwilling communities. It was a check on power.

**Why that reason expired.** The reason was inverted completely. A church with a founder, a board he appointed, and no denomination above him has removed every check — the protest against unaccountable authority became the most unaccountable structure in modern Christianity. The collapses repeat with identical architecture: Mars Hill, Willow Creek, Hillsong, RZIM.

**Who benefits now.** The founder benefits personally, through compensation, book royalties from a captive market, real estate, and a platform that outlives any single scandal.
:::

## 11. Reach {#reach}

### Information

- 'Don't read the critics, read your Bible' functions as source restriction while sounding like piety; discernment blogs (sites that police teachers for doctrinal error) and approved-teacher lists channel information. [PATTERN OBSERVED]
- Non-disclosure agreements and severance conditions have been documented at multiple large churches and ministries, converting internal criticism into a legal risk [32]. [INVESTIGATIVE REPORT]
- 'Touch not the Lord's anointed' (a scriptural phrase used to shield leaders from criticism) and 'spirit of Jezebel/Absalom' labels (names of biblical rebels applied to a critic) convert accountability attempts into spiritual offenses. [LEADERSHIP STATEMENT / PATTERN OBSERVED]

### Children

- Child dedication (a ceremony presenting an infant to God and the congregation), children's church, and altar calls (invitations to come forward at the end of a service and make a commitment) aimed at young children secure decisions before the capacity for informed consent.
- Purity pledges, abstinence curricula, and courtship rules place adolescent sexuality under adult community surveillance. [ACADEMIC SOURCE]
- Youth camps and mission trips produce peak emotional experiences deliberately timed to conversion decisions; homeschool networks in some circles restrict outside information entirely.
- Hell teaching for children, and in some circles corporal-punishment teaching sold as biblical parenting, have documented harms. [INVESTIGATIVE REPORT]

### Bodies

- Complementarian statements (teaching that men and women have distinct roles, with church and family leadership reserved to men) bar women from preaching and eldership across large denominations and networks; where headship (a husband's authority over his wife) is taught, marital refusal has no doctrinal standing. [OFFICIAL POLICY]
- Purity culture's asymmetry is well documented: female modesty is framed as responsibility for male behavior. [ACADEMIC SOURCE / FORMER MEMBER TESTIMONY]
- Abuse is handled through pastor-counseled reconciliation instead of reporting, and independent investigations (e.g., the SBC's 2022 Guidepost report) found survivor allegations tracked and resisted rather than addressed. [INVESTIGATIVE REPORT]
- LGBTQ exclusion from membership and leadership is standard in conservative bodies; ex-gay ministries (programs that claimed to change sexual orientation) operated for decades before high-profile closures and apologies. [INVESTIGATIVE REPORT]

## 12. The 30 techniques {#techniques}

Thirty named techniques from domestic-abuse and social-psychology research, applied to institutions, in the eight stages of the cycle. Each carries an evidence grade for this tradition.

### Stage 1 · Idealize {#stage-1}

::: stage
**You arrive with a need and are met with more warmth than you have had in years.**

There are parking volunteers, coffee, lights, a band, and within a week a stranger crying with you at the altar. You are assigned a small group, then a serving team, then a membership class. The intimacy is manufactured fast and on purpose.

*What it asks of you:* Within a month you had confessed things to people whose last names you didn't know. Ask what that access was for.
:::

::: tactic n=1
#### 1 · Love Bombing {#t-1}

*Overwhelming affection on arrival to create emotional dependency before terms are revealed.*

**How it shows here**

- The newcomer is greeted by parking volunteers, coffee teams, worship music, emotional sermons, altar calls, small group invitations, and instant “family” language.
- Evangelical spaces often specialize in rapid intimacy: strangers ask about your trauma, your marriage, your sins, your “walk with God,” and your prayer requests within days.
- The system creates an emotional high through worship, lights, music, collective singing, and public vulnerability — then interprets that high as the Holy Spirit confirming the church’s message.

**The strongest defense.** People are just excited to see someone come to Jesus.

**The counter.** Excitement is not the issue. The issue is emotional acceleration before informed consent. If a person is pushed to confess, convert, join, serve, tithe, or submit while flooded with music, tears, applause, and belonging, that is not just welcome. That is pressure disguised as revival.

**Evidence grade.** [[Cultural]] Assimilation pipelines are church-growth practice, copied between congregations and mandated by nobody.
:::

::: tactic n=2
#### 2 · Weaponized Generosity {#t-2}

*Giving help that installs unspoken obligation.*

**How it shows here**

- Evangelical churches often offer free childcare, youth groups, counseling, coffee, meals, recovery programs, men’s groups, women’s groups, worship nights, and emotional crisis support.
- The newcomer receives not just help but instant assignment: join a small group, serve on a team, attend membership class, give testimony, tithe, volunteer, invite others.
- A church may help someone through addiction, divorce, unemployment, or grief, then later treat disagreement as betrayal: “We walked with you through your darkest season.”
- Volunteer-heavy churches often turn gratitude into labor. The person who was helped becomes the person expected to help set up chairs, lead youth, sing on the worship team, host small group, or give money.

**The strongest defense.** Healthy churches build community. People serve because they want to give back.

**The counter.** Giving back is beautiful when it is voluntary. It becomes extraction when gratitude is converted into obligation. If a person cannot say no without being called selfish, lukewarm, rebellious, or spiritually immature, then the church did not create community. It created a debt economy with worship music.

**Evidence grade.** [[Cultural]] Free help (childcare, counseling, recovery programs) and the later expectation to serve are congregational practice; no denomination sets a rule for either.
:::

### Stage 2 · Hook {#stage-2}

::: stage
**You are given a future that cannot be verified, and a rope for whenever you drift toward the door.**

Revival, breakthrough, your best life, God's promises over your household and prophetic words about your calling are never measured and always deferred. And when you drift: a text from the pastor, we miss you, God isn't finished with you.

*What it asks of you:* Count the prophecies spoken over you that had a date. Now count the ones that came true. Then ask why the second number is never discussed.
:::

::: tactic n=3
#### 3 · Future Faking {#t-3}

*Promising a future that keeps you invested but never has to arrive.*

**How it shows here**

- Evangelicalism often sells a personalized future: God has a plan for your life, a spouse prepared for you, a ministry calling, a breakthrough, a season of favor, a restored family, a revival, a destiny.
- Altar calls and conferences create immediate emotional certainty about future transformation: your addiction will break, your depression will lift, your marriage will heal, your children will come back, your purpose will be revealed.
- Prosperity-adjacent versions promise harvest after seed: give money now, expect blessing later. If the blessing fails to appear, the believer is told to examine faith, obedience, hidden sin, or timing.
- Purity culture future-fakes marriage: stay sexually pure now and God will reward you with a beautiful covenant marriage later. When the marriage is painful, sex is traumatic, or no spouse appears, the system rarely refunds the lost years.
- Revival culture future-fakes national transformation: one more prayer meeting, one more fast, one more election, one more youth movement, one more prophetic word, and the nation will turn back to God.

**The strongest defense.** Christians believe God really does have plans, callings, and promises. Encouraging faith is not deception.

**The counter.** Encouragement becomes manipulation when leaders make emotionally specific promises they cannot verify. “God has someone for you,” “your breakthrough is coming,” “this seed will release blessing,” and “revival is about to happen” keep people invested in systems that fail repeatedly. When failure is always blamed on the believer and never on the promise-maker, the future is fake by design.

**Evidence grade.** [[Taught]] Prosperity and seed-faith promises are taught from platform and broadcast.
:::

::: tactic n=4
#### 4 · Hoovering {#t-4}

*Pulling someone back after they've started to leave, through guilt, love, or fear.*

**How it shows here**

- The hoover often begins softly: “We just miss your face,” “You’re still family,” “No pressure, but we saved you a seat.”
- Then it turns theological: “Don’t let church hurt separate you from God,” “The enemy wants you isolated,” “You’re in a dangerous season.”
- Former volunteers are often pulled through usefulness: “The youth miss you,” “The worship team isn’t the same,” “You have a calling,” “God gave you gifts for the body.”
- Evangelical leaders may turn your exit into a testimony-in-waiting: “This is a wilderness season. God will bring you back stronger.”
- If you left over leadership abuse, they may say, “No church is perfect,” converting accountability into a maturity test.

**The strongest defense.** Evangelicals genuinely believe community is essential and isolation is spiritually harmful.

**The counter.** Community is not the same as captivity. If the solution to harm caused by the church is always “come back to church,” then the institution is prescribing the injury as the cure.

**Evidence grade.** [[Taught]] The warnings used to call a leaver back (the enemy wants you isolated, a wilderness season, gifts owed to the body) are taught from the pulpit; the follow-up contact itself is congregational practice.
:::

### Stage 3 · Devalue {#stage-3}

::: stage
**You are taught that you are broken, that your perception is unreliable, and that both exits from the trap lead back inside.**

Tithe, then tithe cheerfully. Serve, then serve without recognition. You are lukewarm, unsubmitted, not all in. The vision is always bigger and your contribution is always the gap.

*What it asks of you:* You have never been told you are doing enough. There is a reason the standard is a moving target.
:::

::: tactic n=5
#### 5 · Devaluation {#t-5}

*Reducing your sense of worth so you become dependent on the institution for identity.*

**How it shows here**

- Evangelicalism often devalues through the language of being lost, broken, dead in sin, rebellious, backslidden, lukewarm, worldly, impure, or outside God’s will.
- Testimony culture rewards self-denigration: “I was nothing,” “I was filthy,” “I was lost,” “I destroyed everything,” “Only Jesus made me worth anything.”
- Purity culture teaches especially women and girls that their bodies can make men stumble, that sexual history damages value, and that modesty is proof of spiritual seriousness.
- Calvinist forms intensify this with total depravity: the teaching that “you cannot even choose good without God choosing you first” can function as devaluation.
- Worship lyrics and sermons often rehearse unworthiness until the believer feels guilty for having self-respect outside grace.

**The strongest defense.** Evangelicals are emphasizing grace. The point is that God loves people despite sin.

**The counter.** Grace that first requires psychological collapse is not merely grace. If the system has to convince people they are worthless so that its rescue feels beautiful, then salvation is being staged like an emotional hostage release.

**Evidence grade.** [[Taught]] Total depravity and unworthiness are preached doctrine; how hard they are pressed is a matter of preaching style.
:::

::: tactic n=6
#### 6 · Gaslighting {#t-6}

*Making you doubt your own experience so you trust the institution's interpretation instead.*

**How it shows here**

- A member says, “The pastor manipulated me,” and leaders answer, “Touch not the Lord’s anointed,” “You have a critical spirit,” or “You are causing division.”
- A woman says purity culture damaged her, and the church says, “You misunderstood biblical modesty.”
- A gay or trans person says the teaching made them hate themselves, and the response is, “That is conviction, not shame.”
- A doubter says, “The Bible seems contradictory,” and the answer becomes, “You just don’t want to submit to God.”
- Emotional worship experiences are used as proof: “You felt the Spirit. You know this is true.” Later, if the person reinterprets that experience, they are told they are hardening their heart.

**The strongest defense.** Churches have to distinguish genuine conviction from worldly rebellion.

**The counter.** That distinction becomes abuse when the church always gets to define which is which. If pain means conviction, disagreement means rebellion, and leaving means deception, the system has made itself unfalsifiable.

**Evidence grade.** [[Taught]] The replies used to reinterpret a member's experience ('touch not the Lord's anointed', 'a critical spirit', 'conviction, not shame') are taught from the pulpit and in counsel; no written rule directs them.
:::

::: tactic n=7
#### 7 · Double Bind {#t-7}

*A trap where both options lead to compliance. No exit serves you.*

**How it shows here**

- Evangelicals are told salvation is by grace alone, not works — yet their standing is continuously measured by attendance, tithing, serving, witnessing, and 'fruit.' Rest in grace and you are lukewarm; strive and you are reminded works save no one.
- Members must be 'authentic and vulnerable' in small group — and what is shared becomes prayer-chain (a network that passes prayer requests among members) information and leadership leverage. Withhold and you are guarded; share and you are exposed.
- Questioning the pastor's 'vision' is quenching the Spirit; following your own leading is deception. The Spirit, somehow, only ever speaks through the platform.

**The strongest defense.** We preach grace; discipleship simply produces visible fruit.

**The counter.** If grace is free but belonging is billed weekly in visible performance, the system runs on works while advertising grace. The bind is the business model: the anxiety it creates is resolved only by more participation.

**Evidence grade.** [[Taught]] Both halves of the bind are taught: salvation by grace alone, and standing measured by attendance, giving, serving and 'fruit'. How the two are combined is congregational practice.
:::

### Stage 4 · Confuse {#stage-4}

::: stage
**The rewards become unpredictable, the standard keeps moving, the answers stop meaning anything, and the accusation gets turned around.**

Ask about the budget and you have a spirit of division. Ask about the allegation and you are touching the anointed. Departing staff sign severance conditioned on silence, then are described from the platform as having left in sin. [INVESTIGATIVE REPORT]

*What it asks of you:* The NDA is not a spiritual practice. Ask why a church needs one.
:::

::: tactic n=8
#### 8 · Intermittent Reinforcement {#t-8}

*Unpredictable reward that creates addictive emotional dependency.*

**How it shows here**

- Production-grade worship delivers a reliable Sunday high that is never quite reliable — and the weeks it fails, the message is 'come expectant,' 'press in,' 'get your heart right.' Attendance becomes a lever pressed for a jackpot God.
- Platform access — worship team, leadership pipeline, the pastor's circle — is granted, revoked, and re-dangled without stated criteria; volunteers double their hours to decode it.
- Prophecy culture pays out intermittently by design: the words that land are testimonies forever; the misses are timing, faith, or your blockage. The hit rate never has to improve, because the near-misses keep the room.

**The strongest defense.** We can't manufacture the Holy Spirit; some services simply carry more anointing.

**The counter.** Fog machines, key changes, and build-drop set lists are manufacturing — skillful, budgeted, rehearsed. The honest question is not whether God moves, but why the system takes credit for the peaks and assigns you the valleys.

**Evidence grade.** [[Cultural]] Production-grade worship, unstated criteria for platform access, and the reading of prophetic hits and misses are congregational practice; no governing document sets them.
:::

::: tactic n=9
#### 9 · Moving the Goalposts {#t-9}

*The standard of success keeps shifting so you can never arrive.*

**How it shows here**

- Salvation is preached as a free gift. Then the believer must prove it through quiet time (daily private prayer and Bible reading), purity, church attendance, doctrinal correctness, evangelism, giving, serving, emotional worship, and public testimony.
- Purity culture says, “Save sex for marriage.” Then marriage comes and the person is told they must now be sexually available, emotionally healed, submissive, fruitful, modest, and grateful.
- Someone raises concerns about church abuse. Leaders first demand evidence. Evidence appears. Then they demand tone. Tone improves. Then they demand process. Process is followed. Then they say the person’s heart is wrong.

**The strongest defense.** True faith produces fruit; leaders are discerning spiritual maturity.

**The counter.** Discernment becomes goalpost-moving when the evidence is never enough because the institution can always inspect your motives. Once motives become the final battlefield, no one can ever satisfy the court.

**Evidence grade.** [[Cultural]] Escalating expectations of giving and service are cultural rather than codified, which is what makes them deniable.
:::

::: tactic n=10
#### 10 · Strategic Ambiguity {#t-10}

*Language that sounds profound but means nothing specific — functioning as a shutdown to real questions.*

**How it shows here**

- Evangelical churches say “relationship, not religion,” but build detailed expectations around attendance, giving, purity, doctrine, politics, gender, and service.
- Leaders say membership is voluntary, but leaving is framed as drifting, rebellion, offense, or spiritual danger.
- “Biblical leadership” is vague enough to mean servant leadership in public and authoritarian control in private.

**The strongest defense.** Churches need flexibility because every person’s spiritual situation is different.

**The counter.** Flexibility becomes strategic ambiguity when leaders refuse definitions until definitions would limit them. If no one can say where pastoral care ends and control begins, the ambiguity is not accidental.

**Evidence grade.** [[Cultural]] The undefined terms described here ('relationship, not religion', voluntary membership, 'biblical leadership') are congregational vocabulary; no governing document defines them.
:::

::: tactic n=11
#### 11 · Projection {#t-11}

*Accusing the outside world of the exact thing the institution practices.*

**How it shows here**

- Evangelical churches accuse leavers of being offended, rebellious, or wanting to sin, while refusing to examine manipulation, purity shame, authoritarian leadership, or cover-ups.
- Leaders call critics divisive after their own secrecy fractured trust.
- Purity culture accuses women of making men stumble, projecting male responsibility onto female bodies.
- Prosperity or revival preachers blame failed promises on the believer’s lack of faith, projecting the failure of the system onto the follower.

**The strongest defense.** Churches must correct sin and protect biblical truth.

**The counter.** Correction becomes projection when it only travels downward. If pastors can diagnose everyone else’s sin but cannot name their own exploitation, “biblical correction” is just accusation with a microphone.

**Evidence grade.** [[Cultural]] Accusing leavers and critics of offense and division, and blaming failed promises on the believer's faith, is observed practice; no governing document directs it.
:::

::: tactic n=12
#### 12 · DARVO {#t-12}

*Deny the wrongdoing. Attack the person who raised it. Reverse victim and offender.*

**How it shows here**

- A church member reports manipulation, financial secrecy, sexual misconduct, abusive counseling, or authoritarian leadership. Leaders deny wrongdoing, then accuse the member of gossip, slander, bitterness, or rebellion.
- A pastor caught in misconduct may be framed as “under attack” because of his anointing or influence.
- The victim is pressured to forgive quickly, while the leader receives public sympathy, restoration language, and protective prayer.
- If the person leaves, the church says, “They were offended,” “They wanted to sin,” or “They could not submit to authority.”
- Abuse exposure becomes “division,” while cover-up becomes “protecting the church.”

**The strongest defense.** Church discipline requires care, confidentiality, and avoiding gossip.

**The counter.** Confidentiality protects victims. Secrecy protects institutions. If “do not gossip” means “do not warn people,” then the church has made silence sound biblical.

**Evidence grade.** [[Documented]] The Southern Baptist Convention's 2022 independent report found survivor allegations tracked and resisted. *(sourced)*
:::

::: tactic n=13
#### 13 · Normalization / Desensitization {#t-13}

*Practices that would seem extreme to an outsider feel normal to insiders because they were installed before critical evaluation developed.*

**How it shows here**

- Emotional manipulation becomes normal through worship music, altar calls, dim lighting, repeated choruses, public tears, and sermons that intensify vulnerability before asking for commitment.
- Purity culture desensitizes young people to sexual surveillance: accountability questions, modesty checks, pornography confessions, dating rules, and shame around desire.
- Members become used to pastors speaking with divine certainty: “God told me,” “the Lord put this on my heart,” “this is a word for someone.”
- Repeated volunteer pressure makes burnout feel holy. People stop asking why a church with paid leaders runs on unpaid exhaustion.

**The strongest defense.** Evangelical churches encourage passionate worship, holiness, accountability, and service.

**The counter.** Passion is not the issue. The issue is conditioning. If people are repeatedly placed in emotionally heightened environments and then asked to surrender, confess, give, serve, or obey, their consent is being softened through ritualized intensity.

**Evidence grade.** [[Cultural]] Worship conditioning, claims of divine certainty and volunteer burnout are congregational practice that no body sets and none has reformed. The repudiation of purity culture by its own authors and the closing of ex-gay ministries (section 20) bear only on the purity bullet.
:::

### Stage 5 · Isolate {#stage-5}

::: stage
**Your world narrows until every voice you hear is inside the system, and everything you came for now runs through a middleman.**

The church holds your friends, your childcare, your kids' school, sometimes your paycheck. Critics are 'discernment blogs,' therapy is 'worldly wisdom,' former members are bitter. The only place to process a doubt about the church is inside the church.

*What it asks of you:* Write down everyone you'd still have coffee with in a year if you left tomorrow. Look at the list.
:::

::: tactic n=14
#### 14 · Isolation {#t-14}

*Shrinking your world until the only voices you hear are inside the system.*

**How it shows here**

- Evangelical churches isolate through small groups, purity culture, Christian schooling, homeschooling, youth groups, accountability partners, Christian media ecosystems, and warnings about “worldly” influence.
- Young people may be told not to date unbelievers, not to consume secular music or entertainment, not to trust secular universities, and not to build intimate friendships with people who could weaken their faith.
- Deconstruction is treated as contagion. People questioning faith are warned not to listen to exvangelicals (former evangelicals), progressive Christians, atheists, LGBTQ-affirming theologians, or “bitter” former members.
- The church may say it is not isolating you because you still go to work or school — but emotionally, spiritually, sexually, intellectually, and socially, it narrows your trusted world to approved believers.

**The strongest defense.** Evangelicals are building strong Christian community and protecting believers from harmful ideologies.

**The counter.** Community becomes isolation when outside perspectives are treated as infection. If members are safe only when surrounded by church-approved people, church-approved books, church-approved politics, and church-approved relationships, then the community is not strengthening faith. It is reducing oxygen.

**Evidence grade.** [[Taught]] Warnings against critics, secular therapy, and former members are taught continuously; the NDA practice is separately documented.
:::

::: tactic n=15
#### 15 · Triangulation {#t-15}

*Inserting intermediaries between you and the thing you came for, so the institution is always in the middle.*

**How it shows here**

- Evangelical churches triangulate through small groups, accountability partners, pastors, spouses, ministry teams, prayer chains, and “concerned friends.”
- If you question leadership, suddenly several people want coffee with you. Each meeting feels personal, but the message is coordinated: forgive, stop gossiping, trust leadership, don’t divide the church.
- Marriage is commonly used: “Your wife needs you to lead spiritually,” “Your husband is concerned,” “Your children need to see you in church.”
- Volunteer roles become triangulation: “The youth look up to you,” “The worship team needs you,” “The church family misses your gifts.”
- Evangelicalism triangulates with “the world”: if you leave, you are warned that secular friends, liberal media, sexual temptation, bitterness, or deconstruction communities will pull you away from God.
- It triangulates through testimonies: former doubters are showcased as proof that “faithful people come back.”

**The strongest defense.** Healthy churches use community to care for people who are struggling.

**The counter.** Care listens. Triangulation coordinates. If multiple people approach you with the same spiritual script and none of them can tolerate the possibility that the church is wrong, then the community is not caring for you. It is managing you.

**Evidence grade.** [[Taught]] Coordinated counsel from small groups, spouses, accountability partners and ministry teams is taught as care and carried out at congregational discretion; no written rule requires it.
:::

### Stage 6 · Extract {#stage-6}

::: stage
**Now the harvest: your labor, your money, your identity, your silence, your children's schooling, your capacity to trust yourself.**

Withholding the tithe is framed as robbing God. Seed-faith giving is sold as an investment. Building pledges run for years. Volunteers work full-time-equivalent hours with no wage. Your small-group leader informs leadership about your doubts. Departing staff sign non-disclosure agreements. Ask about the budget and you become a spirit of division.

*What it asks of you:* A church that requires legal silence from the people who leave has told you exactly what it believes about its own conduct.
:::

::: tactic n=16
#### 16 · Flying Monkeys {#t-16}

*The community enforces the institution's norms without being asked — mobilized by culture, not memos.*

**How it shows here**

- After someone leaves an evangelical church, former friends, ministry teammates, worship leaders, youth pastors, and small-group members begin “checking in.”
- The person who reports abuse is approached by people saying: “You’re hurting the church,” “You need to forgive,” “Have you gone through the proper process?” “This is gossip.”
- If a pastor is criticized, loyal members defend him publicly and privately: “He has done so much good,” “You don’t know the whole story,” “Touch not the Lord’s anointed.”
- Prayer chains become surveillance networks: private pain is reframed as a “prayer concern” and circulated.

**The strongest defense.** Church members naturally care when someone is struggling or hurt.

**The counter.** Caring people ask what happened. FLYING MONKEYS ask why you are not over it yet. If the entire concern network protects leadership more than the harmed person, the love is being routed through loyalty.

**Evidence grade.** [[Taught]] Members pressing a critic or survivor to forgive, stop 'gossiping' and protect the pastor repeat taught phrases ('touch not the Lord's anointed'); the pressure itself is congregational practice with no written rule.
:::

::: tactic n=17
#### 17 · Smear Campaign {#t-17}

*Rewriting your character after departure to protect the institution from the implications of your leaving.*

**How it shows here**

- Former members are described as offended, deconstructing, worldly, progressive, deceived, rebellious, or wanting sexual freedom.
- Abuse survivors may be accused of exaggerating, gossiping, refusing forgiveness, or attacking a man of God.
- Churches may imply that the person left because they did not want accountability.
- Leaders may tell staff, volunteers, or small-group leaders a sanitized version of events to make the dissenter look unstable.

**The strongest defense.** Church leaders must warn against false teaching, gossip, and spiritual danger.

**The counter.** Warning becomes SMEAR CAMPAIGN when it shifts attention from evidence to character. If the institution cannot answer the claim, it attacks the claimant.

**Evidence grade.** [[Taught]] Warnings against critics and former members are taught from the pulpit. The Mars Hill, Hillsong and RZIM case entries (section 19) do not record a smear of a named person, so the technique is not graded on them.
:::

::: tactic n=18
#### 18 · Silent Treatment / Stonewalling {#t-18}

*Refusing to engage with legitimate questions in order to maintain control.*

**How it shows here**

- A church says it has handled a complaint “biblically,” but refuses to disclose what happened, who investigated, what consequences occurred, or whether victims are safe.
- Leaders ask the harmed person to meet privately, then use the private meeting to pressure forgiveness without changing anything.
- Emails go unanswered. Requests for financial transparency are ignored. Abuse concerns are redirected to elders loyal to the pastor.
- The church keeps worship services upbeat while victims wait in silence.

**The strongest defense.** Churches must avoid gossip and protect privacy.

**The counter.** Privacy is not the same as secrecy. If the only people protected by silence are leaders, then “biblical process” has become a curtain over institutional cowardice.

**Evidence grade.** [[Documented]] Non-disclosure agreements imposed on departing staff at Mars Hill, Harvest Bible Chapel, Hillsong, Willow Creek, Acts 29 and RZIM, among others [32]. *(sourced)*
:::

::: tactic n=19
#### 19 · Manufactured Consent {#t-19}

*Creating the appearance of choice when the conditions of choice were shaped before you could evaluate them.*

**How it shows here**

- Altar calls manufacture consent through music, dim lighting, emotional sermons, group pressure, public invitation, and fear of eternal consequences.
- “Membership covenants” (written agreements setting out a member's obligations) are signed in communities where leaving later is framed as unfaithfulness or spiritual danger.
- Volunteers “choose” to serve after repeated messages that real Christians sacrifice, use their gifts, and do not consume without contributing.
- Youth purity pledges are made before young people can understand adult sexuality, marriage, trauma, consent, or long-term consequences.

**The strongest defense.** Evangelical churches invite people to respond freely to God.

**The counter.** Emotional intensity plus eternal stakes plus public pressure is not a neutral invitation. If the environment is engineered to make refusal feel like rejecting God, then consent has been manufactured with worship music.

**Evidence grade.** [[Taught]] The appeals that shape these choices (altar calls, membership covenants, calls to serve, purity pledges) are taught and staged from the platform; the choices are then presented as free.
:::

::: tactic n=20
#### 20 · Trauma Bonding {#t-20}

*The wound and the bandage come from the same hand.*

**How it shows here**

- Evangelical spaces alternate intense fear and intense comfort: hell, demons, purity shame, end-times panic — then worship highs, altar prayer, small-group affection, and “God loves you.”
- Youth camps can create emotional whiplash: sermons about sin and damnation followed by music, tears, hugs, and public salvation moments.
- Purity culture shames sexuality, then offers restoration through accountability, confession, marriage, or “redeemed” identity.
- A church hurts someone, then surrounds them with prayer and emotional warmth if they agree not to leave.

**The strongest defense.** Evangelicals preach both sin and grace because both are central to the gospel.

**The counter.** Sin and grace become TRAUMA BONDING when terror is used to intensify dependence on the institution’s comfort. If the church creates the emotional crisis and then interprets the relief as proof of truth, it has engineered attachment.

**Evidence grade.** [[Taught]] The alternation of fear (hell, demons, purity shame, end-times) and comfort (worship, altar prayer, 'God loves you') is preached and staged in services and camps; no written rule directs it.
:::

::: tactic n=21
#### 21 · Learned Helplessness {#t-21}

*Teaching you that your own judgment is unreliable until you stop trusting it entirely.*

**How it shows here**

- A believer prays for healing, breakthrough, marriage, purity, or deliverance. When nothing changes, they are told to pray harder, have more faith, repent more deeply, or wait on God.
- Church volunteers burn out, ask for relief, and are told the harvest is plentiful but workers are few.
- Abuse reports disappear into elder meetings, private processes, and forgiveness language.
- The person learns that effort does not produce change; it only produces another demand.

**The strongest defense.** Faith requires patience and perseverance.

**The counter.** Patience becomes LEARNED HELPLESSNESS when it is demanded only from the harmed while leaders remain unaccountable. If nothing changes except the victim’s tolerance, perseverance has become captivity.

**Evidence grade.** [[Taught]] The counsel to pray harder, repent more deeply and wait on God, and to take abuse reports to the elders, is taught from the pulpit and in pastoral counsel; no written rule requires it.
:::

::: tactic n=22
#### 22 · Benevolent Control {#t-22}

*Control wrapped in the language of care, so that resisting the control feels like rejecting the love.*

**How it shows here**

- Evangelical churches often frame control as accountability: porn accountability, dating accountability, purity accountability, giving accountability, leadership accountability.
- Small groups may monitor members’ emotional lives, marriages, doubts, habits, and “walk with God.”
- Pastors may say they are protecting the flock from false teaching when discouraging members from reading critics or former members.
- Youth ministries may control sexuality, friendships, media, clothing, and dating under the banner of “guarding purity.”

**The strongest defense.** Accountability helps believers grow and avoid destructive choices.

**The counter.** Accountability becomes BENEVOLENT CONTROL when it flows upward as confession and downward as surveillance. If the member is transparent but leadership is opaque, the system is not mutual care. It is spiritual monitoring.

**Evidence grade.** [[Taught]] Accountability for dating, purity, pornography and giving is taught as discipleship and run through small groups and youth ministries; it is written into almost no governing document.
:::

::: tactic n=23
#### 23 · Infantilization {#t-23}

*Positioning the adult believer as permanently childlike and dependent on institutional authority.*

**How it shows here**

- Adults are told to seek pastoral covering, submit to male headship, obey elders, join accountability groups, and avoid independent interpretation.
- Women may be treated as spiritually equal but practically managed through complementarian roles.
- Youth-group logic follows adults into adulthood: purity rules, accountability questions, emotional dependence on leaders.
- Doubters are called baby Christians, backsliders, or spiritually immature.

**The strongest defense.** Spiritual maturity requires accountability and discipleship.

**The counter.** Accountability becomes INFANTILIZATION when it prevents autonomy. If maturity always means agreeing with leadership, and disagreement proves immaturity, adulthood has been rigged.

**Evidence grade.** [[Taught]] Submission to male headship and to elders is taught, and some large denominations write complementarian roles into policy (section 11). Treating adults as dependants is congregational practice with no written rule.
:::

::: tactic n=24
#### 24 · Identity Erosion {#t-24}

*Gradually replacing your identity with a role the institution provides, until you can't separate who you are from where you belong.*

**How it shows here**

- Evangelical identity can become total: quiet time, purity, testimony, church attendance, spiritual gifts, mission, calling, accountability, worship style, politics, dating, marriage, and parenting.
- “God has a plan for your life” can erase the person’s own plan.
- Purity culture erodes sexual identity by making the body a public moral object.
- Testimony culture rewards people for narrating their lives as “before Jesus I was broken; after Jesus I am useful.”
- The person’s emotions are sorted into spiritual categories: peace means God approves, anxiety means lack of trust, desire means flesh, doubt means attack.

**The strongest defense.** Evangelical faith helps people find purpose and transformation.

**The counter.** Purpose becomes IDENTITY EROSION when every part of the person must be converted into ministry material. If your trauma becomes testimony, your body becomes purity evidence, your labor becomes calling, and your doubt becomes enemy attack, the institution has colonized the self.

**Evidence grade.** [[Taught]] The replacement identities (testimony, calling, purity, spiritual categories for emotions) are taught in preaching and discipleship; no rule imposes them.
:::

::: tactic n=25
#### 25 · Spiritual Bypassing {#t-25}

*Using spiritual language to avoid engaging with real pain, real questions, and real accountability.*

**How it shows here**

- Trauma is bypassed into testimony: “God allowed this so you can help others.”
- Abuse is bypassed through “forgive,” “pray for them,” “don’t gossip,” and “no church is perfect.”
- Depression or anxiety may be spiritualized as lack of faith, demonic attack, or insufficient prayer.
- Racism, sexism, homophobia, and abuse may be bypassed through “the gospel is the answer” without structural change.
- Worship highs can create temporary relief that substitutes for repair.

**The strongest defense.** Evangelicals believe God redeems pain and transforms lives.

**The counter.** Redemption becomes SPIRITUAL BYPASSING when transformation is demanded from the victim while the system avoids change. If the wound becomes a sermon before it becomes evidence, the church is using spirituality to escape accountability.

**Evidence grade.** [[Taught]] The phrases used to meet harm with forgiveness and hope ('forgive', 'no church is perfect', 'God allowed this so you can help others') are taught from the pulpit and in counsel; no written rule directs their use.
:::

::: tactic n=26
#### 26 · Financial Control {#t-26}

*Extracting money through spiritual obligation while offering no transparency about where it goes.*

**How it shows here**

- Evangelical churches may push tithing, offerings, missions pledges, church plants, conferences, youth camps, books, merch, and paid leadership training.
- Members may be told that failing to tithe robs God, blocks blessing, reveals distrust, or shows a consumer attitude.
- Churches may depend on unpaid volunteer labor while leaders receive salaries, housing allowances, speaking fees, book deals, or platform benefits.
- Financial transparency may be limited while members are expected to give sacrificially.

**The strongest defense.** Churches need money and volunteers to serve people and spread the gospel.

**The counter.** Mission becomes FINANCIAL CONTROL when the institution demands sacrifice from members but hides its own finances. If transparency flows upward as giving but not downward as accountability, the offering plate is also a power structure.

**Evidence grade.** [[Taught]] Tithing is taught as a covenant obligation with blessing-and-curse framing from the platform.
:::

### Stage 7 · Discard {#stage-7}

::: stage
**You become expensive — too many questions, too much independence — and the urgency ramps up until you are removed.**

You are named from the pulpit, or described precisely enough not to need naming. You are called divisive, bitter, in rebellion. Friends stop replying — not because they were instructed to, but because the story arrived before you did.

*What it asks of you:* You have believed one of these stories about someone else. Consider that yours is being told right now, in the same voice.
:::

::: tactic n=27
#### 27 · Manufactured Crisis {#t-27}

*Creating urgency to suppress critical thinking and generate loyalty.*

**How it shows here**

- Evangelical spaces may manufacture crisis through revival urgency, altar-call pressure, rapture anxiety, anti-LGBTQ panic, persecution narratives, demonic attack, and “lost generation” rhetoric.
- Youth are told that one night, one prayer, one choice, one sexual mistake, or one doubt could alter eternity.
- Fundraising often uses urgency: “We must act now,” “souls are at stake,” “this building will reach the city.”
- Leadership scandals become crises of division rather than crises of accountability.

**The strongest defense.** Evangelicals believe eternal stakes are real and urgent.

**The counter.** Urgency becomes MANUFACTURED CRISIS when it is used to override consent, evidence, and time. If people are asked to give, confess, convert, vote, or obey while emotionally flooded, urgency has become a manipulation tool.

**Evidence grade.** [[Taught]] Crisis is preached from the platform: revival urgency, rapture anxiety, persecution narratives and fundraising deadlines are repeated in sermons and appeals. No governing body sets them.
:::

::: tactic n=28
#### 28 · Discard {#t-28}

*Removing members who become too costly — too many questions, too much independence, too visible a contradiction.*

**How it shows here**

- Someone is praised as called, gifted, anointed, or “on fire” until they burn out or question leadership.
- A worship leader, youth worker, small-group host, or ministry volunteer may be removed overnight after expressing doubt or naming abuse.
- Churches may say, “They left because they were offended,” turning discard into a spiritual diagnosis.
- Former members become sermon illustrations: people who drifted, compromised, deconstructed, or chose the world.

**The strongest defense.** Leaders must protect the flock from confusion and false teaching.

**The counter.** Protection becomes DISCARD when the flock is protected from the truth-teller. If someone’s years of service disappear the moment they become inconvenient, the church did not love them. It loved their usefulness.

**Evidence grade.** [[Cultural]] Removal is congregational and discretionary; independent polity means there is no appeal and no external standard.
:::

### Stage 8 · Replace {#stage-8}

::: stage
**Your seat is filled before the door shuts, and nobody is responsible for any of it because the authority was attributed to God.**

Your seat, your team slot, and your row are filled within weeks, and your leaving becomes a sermon illustration about bitterness. The rules were never leadership's: this is what the Bible says, take it up with God.

*What it asks of you:* Nobody who says take it up with God has ever handed you God's phone number. They have, however, handed you a giving envelope.
:::

::: tactic n=29
#### 29 · Replacement {#t-29}

*Your seat is filled before the door closes behind you.*

**How it shows here**

- A burned-out worship leader is replaced by a younger singer with more enthusiasm.
- A staff member who questions leadership is replaced by someone more loyal and less informed.
- A family leaves over abuse; the church highlights new baptisms and growth statistics.
- A deconstructing member becomes a warning while a new convert becomes proof God is moving.

**The strongest defense.** Churches should celebrate new life and continue ministry.

**The counter.** Celebration becomes REPLACEMENT when new growth is used to bury old harm. If the church points to new members to avoid answering former members, revival has become a distraction from wreckage.

**Evidence grade.** [[Cultural]] Filling a departed member's roles and pointing to new growth is congregational practice; no written rule directs it.
:::

::: tactic n=30
#### 30 · Plausible Deniability {#t-30}

*The institution is never responsible, because the authority is attributed to God — who isn't available for cross-examination.*

**How it shows here**

- A church says membership is voluntary, but membership covenants, small groups, accountability structures, and leadership culture make exit feel like rebellion.
- Pastors preach against “gossip” and “division” after abuse allegations, then deny they were referring to the survivor.
- Purity culture is framed as biblical wisdom, but when adults describe sexual trauma from it, leaders say, “You misunderstood purity.”
- Churches say giving is voluntary, then preach that not tithing robs God or reveals distrust.
- Leaders say they are not political, then use sermons, voter guides, culture-war language, and “biblical values” to guide political behavior without officially endorsing candidates.

**The strongest defense.** Churches teach principles; people apply them freely. Accountability is healthy discipleship. No church is perfect.

**The counter.** Teaching “principles” becomes PLAUSIBLE DENIABILITY when every member knows the expected application but leadership denies commanding it. Accountability is not healthy when members confess upward while leadership remains opaque. “No church is perfect” is not a defense against patterned harm. It is a slogan used to lower expectations.

**Evidence grade.** [[Cultural]] Authority is attributed to Scripture by leaders answerable to boards they appointed, and the pressure described here is informal by design, so no body can be held to it.
:::

## 13. The loops {#loops}

::: lede
The seven loops below show how the practices connect, so that each step makes the next one easier and the last step feeds the first. The loops are analysis built from findings recorded elsewhere on this page [PATTERN OBSERVED]; each step names the section or technique where its fact is recorded.
:::

::: card
#### 1 · Money to Doctrine to Money

Tithing is framed as robbing God if withheld, it is interpreted by the man whose compensation it sets, and it funds the conference circuit that teaches it.

**How it runs.**

1. Tithing is taught as a covenant obligation, with Malachi 3's blessing-and-curse framing, so that withholding reads as robbing God (section 9; technique 26).
2. The teaching is preached by a pastor whose compensation is set by a board he appointed (sections 7 and 16).
3. Members give, and prosperity teaching adds seed-faith gifts that promise a divine return (section 9).
4. The money pays senior staff, building campaigns, and the media, book and conference operations that carry the same teaching (section 9, Follow the money).
5. The congregation is the guaranteed first market for those books and conferences, and the teaching returns to the pulpit (section 9).

**Techniques that feed it.** [26 · Financial Control](#t-26), [3 · Future Faking](#t-3), [9 · Moving the Goalposts](#t-9), [30 · Plausible Deniability](#t-30).

**Why it closes.** The pastor who teaches the obligation is paid from it, and the board that sets his pay is one he chose (section 7). In the United States a church files no annual return (section 8), so a giver cannot see how much of the tithe returns to the teacher. When the promised blessing fails to appear, the believer is told to examine faith, obedience, hidden sin or timing (technique 3), so the failure never reaches the teaching.

**Where it could be broken, and by whom.** It weakens wherever the books are opened: plural elders, outside audits and published salaries (section 4). In England and Wales a church that is a registered charity files public accounts (section 8). A member can ask for the budget, the pastor's salary and the housing allowance before giving (section 23, question 1). This paragraph is analysis, not a documented finding.

**An example from this page.** The Senate inquiry into six television ministries documented private aircraft, high-value parsonages and boards controlled by the founders' families; most of the ministries declined full disclosure, and the inquiry closed without penalties (section 19).
:::

::: card
#### 2 · Fear to Dependence to Fear

Hell and rapture anxiety are installed at camp, relief is supplied at the altar, and the altar is inside the building.

**How it runs.**

1. Hell teaching reaches children, and youth camps produce peak emotional experiences timed to conversion decisions (section 11).
2. The fear is kept live through rapture anxiety, demonic attack, and the warning that one night, one choice or one doubt could alter eternity (technique 27).
3. Relief comes at the altar through music, tears, hugs and public salvation moments (technique 20), and the emotional high is read as the Holy Spirit confirming the church's message (technique 1).
4. The high does not last. In the weeks worship fails to deliver, the message is 'come expectant' and 'get your heart right' (technique 8).
5. The next fear sends the member back to the same altar, inside the same building (techniques 20 and 21).

**Techniques that feed it.** [27 · Manufactured Crisis](#t-27), [20 · Trauma Bonding](#t-20), [8 · Intermittent Reinforcement](#t-8), [5 · Devaluation](#t-5).

**Why it closes.** The church that names the danger also runs the room where relief is offered, and it interprets the relief as proof that the teaching is true (technique 20). A failed Sunday is assigned to the worshipper, never to the service (technique 8).

**Where it could be broken, and by whom.** It weakens where the call to commit is separated from the emotional peak. Section 18 names worship leaders as the tier that could refuse to place the appeal at that peak. It also weakens when a member finds relief outside the building that supplied the fear. This paragraph is analysis, not a documented finding.

**An example from this page.** Technique 20 describes youth camps where sermons on sin and damnation are followed by music, tears, hugs and public salvation moments.
:::

::: card
#### 3 · Children to Members to Children

Youth programs, Christian schools, and homeschool curricula produce adults whose only framework is the institution's.

**How it runs.**

1. Child dedication, children's church and altar calls aimed at young children secure decisions before the capacity for informed consent (section 11; technique 19).
2. Purity pledges, abstinence curricula and courtship rules place adolescent sexuality under adult surveillance (section 11; technique 22).
3. Youth camps and mission trips time peak emotional experiences to conversion decisions, and homeschool networks in some circles restrict outside information entirely (section 11; technique 14).
4. By adulthood the church supplies friends, childcare, school, a small group and often employment, so leaving costs a total social world (sections 3 and 15).
5. Adults formed this way raise their own children inside the same programs, and the cycle begins again.

**Techniques that feed it.** [19 · Manufactured Consent](#t-19), [13 · Normalization / Desensitization](#t-13), [14 · Isolation](#t-14), [22 · Benevolent Control](#t-22).

**Why it closes.** Each stage arrives before the person can weigh it, and by adulthood the institution supplies the framework used to judge the institution. No one has to decide to continue the loop; it continues unless someone decides to stop it.

**Where it could be broken, and by whom.** It weakens where decisions wait for consent and children hear outside voices. Parents decide enrolment, and section 18 names Christian school teachers as able to refuse to teach a curriculum they know to be false. The authors who repudiated purity culture (section 20) show the curriculum can be withdrawn from inside. This paragraph is analysis, not a documented finding.

**An example from this page.** Section 17 records the deconstruction generation, who absorbed purity culture and hell teaching as children and process them as adults, often at the cost of family estrangement.
:::

::: card
#### 4 · Aid to Legitimacy to Leverage to Aid

Relief and mission work buy standing, standing sustains the exemption and political access, and that access protects the exemption.

**How it runs.**

1. Congregations fund relief, mission and development work, and short-term mission trips move billions in aggregate (section 16).
2. Faith-based development organizations carry donor and public-grant money to recipient governments and communities (section 9, Aid to policy leverage).
3. The standing becomes leverage abroad: 28 US Christian-right groups spent at least $280 million outside the US between 2007 and 2018, on campaigns framed as pro-family aid (sections 9 and 16).
4. At home, congregational trust is organized into voter guides, donor lists and turnout operations (section 16), as the Moral Majority first did in 1979 (section 5).
5. The exemption holds. US churches are exempt without applying and file no annual return, and in 2025 the IRS agreed in a court filing that a church could endorse candidates to its own congregation (section 8).

**Techniques that feed it.** [2 · Weaponized Generosity](#t-2), [30 · Plausible Deniability](#t-30), [27 · Manufactured Crisis](#t-27), [26 · Financial Control](#t-26).

**Why it closes.** The aid earns the standing, the standing secures the exemption and the access, and neither the conditions attached to aid nor the advocacy spending has to be disclosed (section 9, Aid to policy leverage).

**Where it could be broken, and by whom.** It weakens where courts and legislators, not settlements, decide the terms of the exemption: in March 2026 a federal judge dismissed the case in which the IRS had agreed to let churches endorse candidates (section 8). It also weakens under a disclosure statute; the United Kingdom card in section 22 names that as the variable. This paragraph is analysis, not a documented finding.

**An example from this page.** Section 16 records American evangelical money funding campaigns abroad against LGBTQ rights and reproductive access: "Recipients get resources; recipient countries get laws their citizens never debated."
:::

::: card
#### 5 · Unpaid Labor to Assets to Power to More Labor

Volunteers build the campus, the corporation holds it, and the asset base underwrites the next vision.

**How it runs.**

1. Volunteers give full-time-equivalent labor with no wage, benefits or equity while leaders are salaried (section 9; technique 26).
2. Gratitude for help received is turned into service: setting up chairs, leading youth, singing on the worship team, hosting small groups (technique 2).
3. Building and vision campaigns bind members through debt and pledges, and the assets are titled to the institution (sections 9 and 16).
4. A failed vision is never audited (section 9), and the next campaign asks for more pledges and more hours.
5. Burnout is made to feel holy, and volunteers who ask for relief are told the harvest is plentiful but the workers are few (techniques 13 and 21).

**Techniques that feed it.** [2 · Weaponized Generosity](#t-2), [26 · Financial Control](#t-26), [13 · Normalization / Desensitization](#t-13), [21 · Learned Helplessness](#t-21), [29 · Replacement](#t-29).

**Why it closes.** The people who give the labor hold no stake in what it builds. Section 16 lists volunteers "whose unpaid labor funded assets titled to the institution" among those who pay, and a volunteer who burns out is replaced (technique 29).

**Where it could be broken, and by whom.** It weakens when someone asks what the money built and is answered. Section 18 names elders and deacons as able to refuse to approve a budget line they cannot explain, and to say so publicly. This paragraph is analysis, not a documented finding.

**An example from this page.** In section 2, Marcus asks his small group whether anyone knows what the building fund is actually at. Tyler says he will ask, and knows he will not.
:::

::: card
#### 6 · Scandal to Removal to Reform Theatre to Continuity

The founder falls, the church rebrands or closes, the network platforms him again, and the model is copied by the next church plant (a newly founded congregation).

**How it runs.**

1. A founder-pastor holds authority with a board he appointed and no one above him (sections 6 and 7).
2. Misconduct comes to light through outside investigations and reporting rather than through the board, as at Mars Hill, RZIM and Hillsong (section 19).
3. The founder resigns or the church dissolves. Mars Hill dissolved in 2014, and the RZIM finding came only after the founder's death (section 19).
4. A restoration process, administered by the leader's own peers on a timeline nobody outside chose, can return him to ministry (section 14, Words used here).
5. The architecture stays in place and recurs: founder, hand-picked board, no external accountability (sections 3 and 10).

**Techniques that feed it.** [18 · Silent Treatment / Stonewalling](#t-18), [30 · Plausible Deniability](#t-30), [25 · Spiritual Bypassing](#t-25), [29 · Replacement](#t-29), [17 · Smear Campaign](#t-17).

**Why it closes.** Removing the person meets the demand for action. The structure that allowed the case, a founder with a board he chose and no one above either, survives the case and is copied.

**Where it could be broken, and by whom.** Section 20 names one change: releasing departing staff from their non-disclosure agreements. Outside the church, the Charity Commission in England and Wales can open statutory inquiries and remove trustees (section 22); inside it, the Southern Baptist messengers' annual vote has shown it can act (section 8). This paragraph is analysis, not a documented finding.

**An example from this page.** Mars Hill Church in Seattle, with about 15 locations, dissolved after its founding pastor resigned, and its structure gave no body above the founder the power to act sooner (section 19).
:::

::: card
#### 7 · Persecution to Solidarity to Insulation to Unaccountability

Genuine cultural hostility toward evangelicals is cited to convert a question about the budget into an attack on Jesus.

**How it runs.**

1. Persecution narratives and culture-war panic are preached as spiritual danger (technique 27).
2. A question is recast as division. Ask about the budget and you have a spirit of division; ask about the allegation and you are touching the anointed (section 12, stage 4).
3. The person asking is labeled divisive, bitter or in rebellion, often from the platform (technique 17; section 15).
4. Members close ranks. Loyal members defend the pastor, and several people ask the questioner for coffee with the same message: forgive, stop gossiping, trust leadership (techniques 15 and 16).
5. The question goes unanswered, and "We are financially accountable" stands beside a Senate inquiry that most of the ministries it examined declined to answer in full (section 14).

**Techniques that feed it.** [27 · Manufactured Crisis](#t-27), [17 · Smear Campaign](#t-17), [16 · Flying Monkeys](#t-16), [11 · Projection](#t-11), [12 · DARVO](#t-12).

**Why it closes.** Each new question can be read as more proof of hostility, so the questions strengthen the solidarity that shields the institution from them.

**Where it could be broken, and by whom.** It weakens when the question comes from inside. Section 3 notes that much of the reporting that exposed the pattern came from the evangelical press itself, and section 23 closes on the tradition's own principle, the priesthood of all believers, as a warrant to examine the books. This paragraph is analysis, not a documented finding.

**An example from this page.** The Guidepost report found that the Southern Baptist Executive Committee had treated survivors as adversaries and liabilities while it kept a private list of accused ministers (section 19).
:::

## 22. Regional variants {#regional}

::: card
### United States

- **apex:** There is no single apex. Polity is congregational and independent, and the SBC president is elected annually while the executive staff are not.
- **law:** Churches file no Form 990 (the annual public return other US charities must file). State attorneys general have jurisdiction over charitable assets. Many states exempt clergy from mandatory reporting.
- **documented:** The Guidepost report (2022) examined the SBC. The Grassley Senate inquiry (2007–2011) closed without full disclosure. Megachurch NDA controversies have recurred [32].
- **exit:** Exit costs are low socially in most of the country, and high where the church is also the school, employer, and social world, which is common in the South and Midwest.
- **regulator:** Complaints go to state attorneys general, and to the IRS for affiliated entities that do file. The SBC messenger vote is a genuine internal lever.
- **tell:** The country with the largest evangelical population has among the weakest religious financial disclosure in the developed world. Those two facts are related.
:::

::: card
### United Kingdom

- **apex:** Authority sits in denominational structures and independent churches. Most are charities, though smaller churches in several denominations are excepted from registration until 2031 [34].
- **law:** The Charity Commission requires annual accounts, publishes trustee names, and can open statutory inquiries and remove trustees. Serious incident reporting is mandatory.
- **documented:** The Commission has opened statutory inquiries into churches over safeguarding and financial management, and has removed trustees.
- **exit:** Exit costs are generally low, and higher in tightly-knit immigrant and diaspora congregations.
- **regulator:** The Charity Commission is the most reader-usable regulator in this entire volume. It is free and searchable, and it acts.
- **tell:** The theology and the practices are the same, and the accounts are published. The variable is a disclosure statute.
:::

::: card
### Australia

- **apex:** Authority sits with denominational bodies and independent megachurches, notably in the Hillsong network.
- **law:** Churches register with the ACNC (the Australian Charities and Not-for-profits Commission), though churches that qualify as "basic religious charities" are exempt from financial reporting. The Royal Commission (2013–2017) covered institutions across denominations. Mandatory reporting varies by state.
- **documented:** The record includes Royal Commission case studies, the Hillsong disclosures, and Brian Houston's 2022 resignation, followed by a 2023 acquittal on the criminal charge.
- **exit:** Exit costs are low socially, and higher for those employed in church-linked enterprises.
- **regulator:** The ACNC oversees finances and governance. State child-protection authorities and state police are the other routes.
- **tell:** Australia produced the most thorough statutory examination of institutional child abuse anywhere. Its findings are usable by readers in every other jurisdiction.
:::

## 23. The questions {#questions}

1. If there is nothing to hide, why won't your church publish its budget, your pastor's salary, and its housing allowance — on the website, this week?
2. Why does a church that preaches truth-telling require departing staff to sign non-disclosure agreements?
3. The last time a leader was accused, who conducted the investigation, who paid them, and did the report get published in full or summarized by the people it examined?
4. If the door is unlocked, why does everyone who walks through it lose every friend they had?
5. Prosperity teaching says giving produces return. Ask for the data. Then ask why the only reliably enriched party is the person teaching it.
6. You were told doubt is dangerous and criticism is divisive. Ask who is protected by those two rules, and notice it is never you.

### In closing

Your tradition's founding claim is the priesthood of all believers — that you can read, interpret, and stand before God without a middleman. Take that seriously and the conclusion is unavoidable: you do not need permission to examine your church's books, its process, or its pastor. That is not rebellion against Protestant principle. It is Protestant principle.

The people who exposed Mars Hill, Hillsong, and the SBC's own abuse records were not atheists. They were believers who refused to let loyalty to an institution stand in for loyalty to God. You are allowed to be one of them. You may find it is the most evangelical thing you ever do.

## 24. Leaving safely here {#leaving}

Practical guidance, not legal advice.

1. **Leaving is legally free.** The costs are social, family and sometimes economic, where the church is also the school, employer or social world.
2. **If you signed a non-disclosure agreement as staff,** it generally cannot stop you reporting a crime to police; take the agreement to a lawyer.
3. **Membership covenants.** Some churches ask members to sign one; you can resign membership in writing.
4. **If you were abused,** go to the police or child-protection services first, not the church.
5. **Find support** that is not run by a church [26][27][28].
